# Environmental Conventions — Questions

@qenv.conventions.q1 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=5
The **Stockholm Conference** (1972) is historically significant because it was:

- A) The first major UN conference on the human environment and led to the creation of UNEP
- B) The treaty that phased out CFCs and other ozone-depleting substances
- C) The first legally binding climate treaty requiring developed countries to cut emissions
- D) The convention that introduced the prior-informed-consent procedure for hazardous chemicals
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — The **Stockholm Conference** (1972) was the first major UN conference on the human environment, raised global environmental awareness, and led directly to the creation of UNEP. The CFC phase-out belongs to the Montreal Protocol (1987); binding GHG cuts belong to the Kyoto Protocol (1997); prior-informed consent belongs to the Rotterdam Convention (1998).

**Trap:** B, C and D each describe real environmental treaties — students must match the *specific* achievement to the right conference.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#2. Stockholm Conference]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q2 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=5
The **Montreal Protocol** (1987) was adopted to:

- A) Control transboundary movements of hazardous wastes
- B) Phase out production and consumption of ozone-depleting substances
- C) Limit global warming to well below 2°C
- D) Protect human health from mercury emissions
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The **Montreal Protocol** (1987) is the landmark treaty to phase out ozone-depleting substances (CFCs, halons, carbon tetrachloride, etc.). The Kigali Amendment (2016) added HFCs. Hazardous-waste control is the Basel Convention; the 2°C target is in the Paris Agreement; mercury is the Minamata Convention.

**Trap:** A, C and D are real environmental treaties — and Montreal Protocol is sometimes confused with the Kyoto Protocol because both are climate/environment protocols.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#5. Montreal Protocol]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q3 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=5
The **Kyoto Protocol** (1997) is best described as:

- A) A non-binding declaration on sustainable development
- B) The first legally binding commitment for developed countries to reduce greenhouse-gas emissions
- C) An agreement to phase out ozone-depleting substances
- D) An agreement to share benefits from genetic resources
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The **Kyoto Protocol** (1997) imposed *legally binding* emission-reduction commitments on developed (Annex-I) countries. It is the operational first period of the UNFCCC. Declarations (Rio, Johannesburg, Nairobi) are non-binding; the Montreal Protocol handles ODS; the Nagoya Protocol handles genetic resources.

**Trap:** A is a common wrong answer — "Kyoto" sounds similar to other declarations, but Kyoto is *legally binding*, unlike declarations.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#10. Kyoto Protocol]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q4 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=5
The **Paris Agreement** (2015) aims to:

- A) Phase out CFCs globally
- B) Control transboundary movements of hazardous wastes
- C) Strengthen the global response to climate change by limiting warming to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels
- D) Protect biological diversity from risks posed by living modified organisms (LMOs)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — The **Paris Agreement** (adopted 2015, in force 2016; linked to COP21) sets the well-below-2°C target (with pursuit of 1.5°C) and unites all countries under NDCs. CFC phase-out = Montreal; hazardous waste = Basel; LMOs = Cartagena Protocol.

**Trap:** A is the classic confusion between Paris and Montreal — both are famous environmental agreements, but they target completely different problems (climate vs ozone).

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#17. Paris Agreement]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q5 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=5
A customs officer intercepts a shipment of e-waste being shipped illegally from one country to another under the guise of "recycling." Which international convention primarily empowers the officer to act against such transboundary hazardous-waste movements?

- A) Rotterdam Convention
- B) Basel Convention
- C) Stockholm Convention (on POPs)
- D) Minamata Convention
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The **Basel Convention** (1989) controls transboundary movements of hazardous wastes and ensures their environmentally sound disposal. Rotterdam handles hazardous *chemicals and pesticides* via prior-informed consent; the Stockholm POPs Convention bans persistent organic pollutants; Minamata addresses mercury.

**Trap:** A is the most common wrong answer — Rotterdam also deals with hazardous materials but only *chemicals/pesticides*, not *waste shipments*.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#7. Basel Convention]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q6 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=5
A researcher wants to commercially use a compound found in a plant species collected from a tropical rainforest, and the provider country demands a fair share of the benefits. Which international instrument provides the legal framework for *Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS)* of genetic resources?

- A) Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety
- B) Nagoya Protocol
- C) Bonn Convention (CMS)
- D) Rotterdam Convention
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The **Nagoya Protocol** (adopted 2010, in force 2014) is the ABS supplement to the CBD: it ensures *fair and equitable sharing of benefits* arising from the utilisation of genetic resources. Cartagena addresses LMOs (biosafety); Bonn addresses migratory species; Rotterdam addresses hazardous chemicals/pesticides.

**Trap:** A is the most common wrong answer — Cartagena is also a *protocol to the CBD*, but it is about biosafety, not access and benefit sharing.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#16. Nagoya Protocol]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q7 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=5
A nation releases large amounts of mercury from coal-fired power plants and artisanal gold mining, contaminating local fish and harming human health. Which international convention directly targets this pollutant?

- A) Minamata Convention
- B) Stockholm Convention
- C) Basel Convention
- D) Vienna Convention
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — The **Minamata Convention** (adopted 2013, in force 2017) protects human health and the environment from anthropogenic mercury emissions and releases. Stockholm targets POPs; Basel targets hazardous *waste*; Vienna targets the ozone layer.

**Trap:** B (Stockholm) is the most common wrong answer — Stockholm is *also* a convention on pollutants, but the *2001* Stockholm Convention targets POPs, not mercury. There are two different "Stockholm" entries in the chapter — Stockholm Conference (1972) and Stockholm Convention (POPs, 2001).

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#1. Minamata Convention]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q8 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=5
A new variety of genetically modified corn is being imported into a country. The country wants a legal framework to assess and manage the potential risks of the living modified organism (LMO) before allowing entry. Which instrument provides this framework?

- A) Nagoya Protocol
- B) Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety
- C) Rotterdam Convention
- D) Bonn Convention
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The **Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety** (adopted 2000, in force 2003) specifically addresses the safe handling, transfer and use of *living modified organisms (LMOs)* that may have adverse effects on biological diversity or human health. Nagoya handles *genetic-resource benefit sharing*, not biosafety; Rotterdam covers hazardous chemicals/pesticides; Bonn covers migratory species.

**Trap:** A is the most common wrong answer — both Nagoya and Cartagena are protocols to the CBD, but they target very different issues (ABS vs biosafety).

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#14. Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q9 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=5
A migratory bird species breeding in Siberia passes through India each winter on its way to sub-Saharan Africa. Which international framework aims to conserve migratory species across their entire range?

- A) Ramsar Convention
- B) Bonn Convention (CMS)
- C) Basel Convention
- D) UNCCD
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The **Bonn Convention** (Convention on Migratory Species, adopted 1979, in force 1983) conserves migratory species of wild animals across their entire range. Ramsar deals with wetlands; Basel with hazardous waste; UNCCD with desertification.

**Trap:** A is tempting because wetlands host many migratory birds, but Ramsar protects *sites*, not species ranges.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#15. Bonn Convention (Convention on Migratory Species - CMS)]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=5
A student is given a list of four pairs of (Convention / Year adopted) and is asked to identify the pair that is **incorrectly matched**:

- A) Kyoto Protocol — 1997
- B) Paris Agreement — 2015
- C) Vienna Convention — 1985
- D) Stockholm Convention (POPs) — 1989
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D** — The **Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)** was adopted in **2001** (entered into force in 2004), not 1989. The 1989 Stockholm-related item is the Helsinki Declaration on the Protection of the Ozone Layer (also 1989). Kyoto (1997), Paris (2015) and Vienna (1985) are all correctly matched.

**Trap:** Students confuse the *1972* Stockholm Conference, the *1989* Basel Convention, the *1989* Helsinki Declaration, and the *2001* Stockholm (POPs) Convention because of the repeated "Stockholm" and "1989" labels in the chapter.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#11. Stockholm Convention]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=5
The **Helsinki Declaration on the Protection of the Ozone Layer (1989)** is sometimes confused with another famous *Helsinki Declaration*. The other declaration, *not* an environmental convention, is:

- A) The WMA Declaration of Helsinki (1964) — on medical research ethics
- B) The Rio Declaration on Environment and Development (1992)
- C) The Johannesburg Declaration on Sustainable Development (2002)
- D) The Geneva Declaration on the Rights of the Child (1924)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — The chapter explicitly notes that the **Helsinki Declaration on the Protection of the Ozone Layer (1989)** is a *different* document from the **WMA Declaration of Helsinki (1964)** — which addresses medical research ethics, not environmental protection.

**Trap:** B and C are environmental declarations but were adopted in Rio and Johannesburg, not Helsinki. D is unrelated entirely. Only A is the matching "other Helsinki Declaration."

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#6. Helsinki Declaration (on the Protection of the Ozone Layer)]]
</details>

@qenv.conventions.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=5
Match each convention with the pollutant or environmental issue it primarily addresses, and identify which pair is **correctly matched**:

- A) Minamata Convention — Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)
- B) Basel Convention — Mercury emissions
- C) Rotterdam Convention — Transboundary hazardous-waste movements
- D) Stockholm Convention (POPs, 2001) — Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D** — The **Stockholm Convention** (adopted 2001) targets Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs) — that is the matching. A is wrong (Minamata targets mercury, not POPs); B is wrong (Basel targets *hazardous waste*, not mercury); C is wrong (Rotterdam targets hazardous *chemicals and pesticides* via PIC, not waste shipments).

**Trap:** A, B and C all flip the convention and its target — these are the easiest trap pattern in this chapter because all four are real environmental conventions with similar-sounding but distinct mandates.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/05 - Environmental Conventions#11. Stockholm Convention]]
</details>
