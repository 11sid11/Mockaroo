# Biogeochemical Cycles — Questions

@qenv.cycles.q1 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=4
Which biogeochemical cycle is described as the movement of carbon among the biosphere, geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere and pedosphere, driven mainly by photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition and fossil-fuel combustion?

- A) Nitrogen cycle
- B) Water (hydrological) cycle
- C) Carbon cycle
- D) Phosphorus cycle
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — The **carbon cycle** is precisely the exchange of carbon across those five spheres, anchored by photosynthesis (uptake) and respiration / combustion / decomposition (release).

**Trap:** B is the most common wrong answer because the water cycle is the most famous "everywhere" cycle; but the question describes *carbon's* pathways.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#CARBON CYCLE]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q2 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=4
The water cycle (hydrological cycle) is driven by evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration and:

- A) Nitrogen fixation
- B) Photosynthesis
- C) Transpiration
- D) Denitrification
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — **Transpiration** (release of water vapour from plants) is one of the two major *fluxes* of water to the atmosphere alongside evaporation; together they are called "evapotranspiration." The other processes listed (fixation, photosynthesis, denitrification) are part of the *nitrogen* and *carbon* cycles, not water.

**Trap:** A and D are nitrogen-cycle terms; B is from the carbon cycle. They are common distractors in cross-cycle questions.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#WATER CYCLE (HYDROLOGICAL CYCLE)]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q3 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=4
Which biogeochemical cycle has **no atmospheric component** — i.e., the element does not cycle significantly through the atmosphere?

- A) Carbon cycle
- B) Nitrogen cycle
- C) Phosphorus cycle
- D) Sulphur cycle
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — The **phosphorus cycle** is essentially a *sedimentary* cycle: phosphorus is released from rocks by weathering, taken up by plants, passed through the food web, and returned to soil/sediment. There is no significant gaseous/atmospheric phase. Carbon, nitrogen and sulphur all have important atmospheric reservoirs.

**Trap:** B is tempting because the chapter emphasises nitrogen's atmospheric reservoir (N₂); but the question asks for the cycle with *no* atmospheric phase, which uniquely fits phosphorus.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#PHOSPHORUS CYCLE]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q4 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=4
In the nitrogen cycle, atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) is converted into usable forms like ammonia and nitrates primarily by:

- A) Photosynthesis
- B) Lightning and nitrogen-fixing bacteria
- C) Volcanic eruptions
- D) Combustion of fossil fuels
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Atmospheric N₂ is *fixed* into ammonia/nitrates by **lightning** (which provides the energy to break the N≡N bond) and by **nitrogen-fixing bacteria** (e.g., *Rhizobium* in legume root nodules, free-living *Azotobacter*). Photosynthesis fixes carbon, not nitrogen; volcanoes release sulphur and CO₂, not usable nitrogen; fossil-fuel combustion does release nitrogen oxides but does not *fix* N₂ into usable forms.

**Trap:** C and D both involve atmospheric processes but neither is a fixation pathway.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#NITROGEN CYCLE]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q5 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=4
A field of fossil-fuel-burning power plants releases huge amounts of CO₂ into the atmosphere, while on the other side of the planet vast tracts of forest are being cut down — reducing the planet's capacity to absorb CO₂. The biogeochemical cycle most directly affected by both activities is:

- A) Water cycle
- B) Nitrogen cycle
- C) Carbon cycle
- D) Phosphorus cycle
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — Fossil-fuel combustion releases *stored* carbon as CO₂, and forests act as *carbon sinks* through photosynthesis. Both activities directly perturb the **carbon cycle**. The water cycle is affected indirectly (e.g., via climate); nitrogen and phosphorus cycles are not directly driven by combustion or deforestation.

**Trap:** D is plausible because deforestation increases soil phosphorus runoff, but the *primary* carbon-cycle link is the one the chapter emphasises.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#CARBON CYCLE]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q6 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=4
In a wet tropical forest, plants absorb large amounts of water through their roots and release water vapour into the atmosphere through their leaves. This plant-driven component of the water cycle is called:

- A) Evaporation
- B) Condensation
- C) Transpiration
- D) Infiltration
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — **Transpiration** is the loss of water vapour from plants (mainly through stomata in leaves). It pairs with evaporation to form "evapotranspiration," the total flux of water from the land surface to the atmosphere.

**Trap:** A is the same physical process (water → vapour) but occurs from *non-living* surfaces like oceans and lakes, not plants.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#WATER CYCLE (HYDROLOGICAL CYCLE)]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q7 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=4
A farmer grows a leguminous crop (e.g., soybean or groundnut) and notices that soil nitrogen levels remain relatively high without heavy fertiliser application. The most important biological reason is:

- A) Legumes release nitrogen gas into the soil through their leaves
- B) Legumes host nitrogen-fixing bacteria in their root nodules
- C) Legumes absorb nitrogen directly from the atmosphere through their stomata
- D) Legumes convert soil phosphorus into nitrogen
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Legumes form symbiotic root nodules with **nitrogen-fixing bacteria** (e.g., *Rhizobium*), which convert atmospheric N₂ into ammonia that the plant can use. This enriches soil nitrogen when nodules decompose.

**Trap:** A is wrong — plants don't release N₂. C is wrong — plants *cannot* directly absorb N₂; only certain prokaryotes can fix it. D describes a non-existent transformation.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#NITROGEN CYCLE]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q8 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=4
Volcanic eruptions release sulphur-containing gases (e.g., SO₂) into the atmosphere. These gases eventually fall back to the surface as dilute sulphuric acid in rain. This phenomenon links the sulphur cycle to:

- A) Carbon cycle
- B) Acid rain
- C) Ozone depletion
- D) Eutrophication
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Sulphur oxides (SO₂) react with atmospheric water to form sulphuric acid, which falls as **acid rain**, damaging ecosystems, acidifying soils, and corroding structures. The chapter explicitly links the sulphur cycle to "acid rain formation."

**Trap:** C (ozone depletion) is driven by CFCs and halons, not sulphur. D (eutrophication) is a nitrogen/phosphorus problem, not sulphur.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#SULPHUR CYCLE]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q9 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=4
Phosphorus is essential for which class of biological molecules in living cells?

- A) Carbohydrates and lipids
- B) Proteins and carbohydrates
- C) DNA, RNA and ATP (energy transfer)
- D) Steroid hormones only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — The chapter explicitly states that phosphorus is essential for **DNA, RNA and energy transfer** (ATP is the universal energy currency and contains phosphate groups). Carbohydrates, lipids and proteins do not contain phosphorus in their core backbone.

**Trap:** A and B are plausible because phosphorus is needed for *some* phospholipids and phosphorylated proteins, but DNA/RNA/ATP are the textbook trio.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#PHOSPHORUS CYCLE]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=4
Consider the following statements about biogeochemical cycles:
1. The **carbon cycle** is the only cycle that involves the atmosphere, biosphere, geosphere, hydrosphere and pedosphere.
2. The **phosphorus cycle** has no significant atmospheric phase.
3. The **sulphur cycle** is linked to acid rain formation.
4. The **water cycle** is driven only by evaporation and condensation.

Which of the above statements are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1, 3 and 4 only
- D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Statement 1 ✗ (the chapter lists five spheres for the *carbon* cycle but doesn't claim it is the *only* multi-sphere cycle — nitrogen and sulphur also involve multiple spheres). Statement 2 ✓ (phosphorus is a sedimentary cycle with no major atmospheric phase). Statement 3 ✓ (sulphur oxides → sulphuric acid → acid rain). Statement 4 ✗ (the water cycle also involves precipitation, infiltration, runoff and **transpiration**, not just evaporation and condensation).

**Trap:** Statement 1 is tempting because the chapter quotes all five spheres in the carbon-cycle paragraph; but that is a description of the carbon cycle, not an exclusivity claim.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#MAJOR BIOGEOCHEMICAL CYCLES]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=4
A textbook asks: "Why is the **phosphorus cycle** considered *slow* and *local*, while the **carbon cycle** is *fast* and *global*?" The best answer, consistent with the chapter, is:

- A) Phosphorus atoms are heavier than carbon atoms and therefore move more slowly.
- B) Phosphorus cycles mainly through rocks, soil and organisms (no atmospheric phase), whereas carbon moves rapidly through the atmosphere as CO₂.
- C) Phosphorus is radioactive and therefore cycles slowly.
- D) Carbon is produced by respiration but phosphorus is not produced by any organism.
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Phosphorus has no significant gaseous phase, so it must be transported by physical processes (weathering, erosion, runoff) — which are slow and local. Carbon, by contrast, has a major gaseous phase (CO₂) that moves quickly via atmospheric circulation, making the carbon cycle fast and global.

**Trap:** A is a tempting "physical" answer but atomic mass doesn't determine biogeochemical cycle speed. C is false — phosphorus is not significantly radioactive. D confuses *flux* with *production*.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#PHOSPHORUS CYCLE]]
</details>

@qenv.cycles.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=4
A multiple-choice question in a textbook lists four cycles. Which option correctly matches the cycle to a *signature* process or pathway that is unique to that cycle (i.e., not shared with the other three)?

- A) Carbon cycle — nitrogen fixation by bacteria
- B) Water cycle — transpiration by plants
- C) Nitrogen cycle — release of CO₂ by respiration
- D) Phosphorus cycle — weathering of phosphate-bearing rocks
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D** — **Weathering of phosphate-bearing rocks** is the signature entry point of phosphorus into the cycle — unique to the phosphorus cycle among the options. Nitrogen fixation belongs to the nitrogen cycle (A is misattributed); transpiration is a water-cycle process but is also an *evaporation analogue*, not unique (B is misattributed); respiration releases CO₂ and is a carbon-cycle process, not nitrogen-cycle (C is misattributed).

**Trap:** A, B and C each correctly name a real process but attach it to the wrong cycle — testing whether students can match process *and* cycle together.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/04 - Biogeochemical Cycles#PHOSPHORUS CYCLE]]
</details>
