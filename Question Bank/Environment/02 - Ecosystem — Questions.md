@qenv.ecosystem.q1 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=2
The term "Ecosystem" was first coined by:

- A) Ernst Haeckel
- B) Eugene Odum
- C) Sir Arthur George Tansley
- D) Charles Elton
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — **Sir Arthur George Tansley** coined "Ecosystem" in **1935**. Haeckel coined "Ecology" (1866); Odum is the Father of Modern Ecology; Elton developed the ecological-pyramid concept (1927).

**Trap:** B is a frequent wrong answer because Odum's name is widely associated with ecosystem ecology — but he did not coin the *term*.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#ECOSYSTEM]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q2 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=2
Which four broad ecosystem services are recognised by ecology?

- A) Provisioning, regulating, supporting, cultural
- B) Direct, indirect, tertiary, cultural
- C) Material, energetic, informational, cultural
- D) Primary, secondary, tertiary, cultural
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — The standard typology is **Provisioning** (food, water, wood), **Regulating** (climate, flood, pollination), **Supporting** (habitat, nutrient cycles) and **Cultural** (recreation, spiritual).

**Trap:** D echoes the *trophic-level* terminology (primary, secondary, tertiary consumers) and is a strong distractor.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#ECOSYSTEM]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q3 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=2
The maximum sustainable size of a population that an environment can support indefinitely is called the:

- A) Biotic potential
- B) Natality
- C) Carrying capacity
- D) Population density
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — **Carrying capacity (K)** is the maximum population size that the environment can sustain. Biotic potential is the *maximum reproductive capacity* under ideal conditions; natality is the birth rate.

**Trap:** A sounds similar and is the most common wrong answer — biotic potential is *capacity to grow*, not the *limit on growth*.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#POPULATION ECOLOGY]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q4 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=2
Which of the following correctly describes **r-selected** species?

- A) Large body size, few offspring, long lifespan
- B) Small body size, many offspring, short lifespan
- C) Long gestation, parental care, stable populations
- D) Found only in terrestrial ecosystems
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — r-strategists are **small**, produce **many offspring** in a **short time**, and have **short lifespans** (insects, frogs, weeds, bacteria). K-strategists show the opposite traits (large, few offspring, long-lived — humans, elephants, whales).

**Trap:** A describes K-strategists and is a very common distractor.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#Life history Strategies]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q5 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=2
A patch of forest in the Western Ghats is clear-cut for timber and a new plantation of the same species is established on the cleared land. Compared to a forest recovering after a fire, this new plantation will most likely:

- A) Support more native biodiversity than the fire-recovered forest
- B) Be a monoculture with reduced structural complexity
- C) Have a higher carrying capacity than the original forest
- D) Show a classic inverted pyramid of numbers
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — A plantation of a single species is a **monoculture**, with a single canopy layer and very low structural and species diversity, unlike a fire-recovered forest which regenerates through natural succession with many species.

**Trap:** A reverses the typical outcome — plantations are *less* biodiverse than naturally regenerating forests.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#DEFORESTATION]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q6 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=2
A lake used to be clear and supported diverse fish. After years of fertiliser runoff from nearby farms, dense algal mats appear on the surface, oxygen levels drop sharply, and fish begin to die. This process is called:

- A) Bioaccumulation
- B) Eutrophication
- C) Biomagnification
- D) Bioremediation
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — **Eutrophication** is the over-enrichment of a water body with nutrients (often nitrogen/phosphorus from fertilisers), leading to algal blooms, oxygen depletion (high BOD), and fish kills.

**Trap:** A and C describe concentration of *toxins* in organisms, not nutrient-driven oxygen depletion. D is the opposite — a clean-up process.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#Eutrophication]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q7 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=2
Match the following aquatic organisms to their correct ecological category:

| Organism | Category |
|---|---|
| (i) Organisms floating on / attached to the upper surface of water | (a) Neuston |
| (ii) Organisms attached to submerged surfaces like stems of water plants | (b) Periphyton |
| (iii) Organisms that swim actively in the water column | (c) Nekton |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-b, ii-a, iii-c
- C) i-a, ii-c, iii-b
- D) i-c, ii-b, iii-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — **Neuston** float on / are attached to the upper surface film; **periphyton** grow on submerged surfaces; **nekton** are active swimmers (fish, turtles). Benthos live on the bottom; plankton drift passively.

**Trap:** B and C swap neuston and periphyton — students confuse "on the surface" with "on submerged surfaces."

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#Aquatic Organisms]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q8 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=2
Coral bleaching occurs when corals lose their symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) due to stress — typically elevated sea temperatures. The most direct consequence of bleaching for the coral animal is:

- A) Increased growth rate due to extra sunlight
- B) Loss of the major source of nutrition from the algae
- C) Strengthening of the calcium carbonate skeleton
- D) Improved resistance to ocean acidification
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Zooxanthellae provide corals with the bulk of their nutrition via photosynthesis; losing them starves the coral and (combined with other stressors) can kill it. Bleaching does *not* increase growth, strengthen the skeleton, or confer resistance to acidification.

**Trap:** A sounds optimistic but is biologically backwards — without the algae, corals actually lose their primary energy source.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#Threats to Coral Reefs]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q9 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=2
A salt-tolerant tree species growing along a coastal tidal zone has roots that project above the mud surface to absorb oxygen from the air. These aerial roots are called:

- A) Prop roots
- B) Pneumatophores
- C) Tap roots
- D) Stilt roots
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — **Pneumatophores** are the aerial "breathing" roots produced by mangroves (e.g., *Avicennia*, *Sonneratia*) to obtain oxygen in anaerobic, waterlogged soils.

**Trap:** A (prop roots, like in banyan) and D (stilt roots, like in maize) are *support* roots — they don't primarily serve in gas exchange.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#MANGROVES]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=2
According to the chapter, which of the following Indian wetlands is on the **Montreux Record** (a register of endangered wetlands under the Ramsar Convention)?

- A) Chilika Lake, Odisha
- B) Wular Lake, Jammu & Kashmir
- C) Keoladeo National Park, Rajasthan
- D) Dal Lake, Jammu & Kashmir
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — The chapter explicitly names **Keoladeo National Park (Rajasthan)** as one of the Indian sites on the Montreux Record (the other being Loktak Lake, Manipur). Chilika, Wular and Dal are Ramsar sites but are not named in the chapter as Montreux Record sites.

**Trap:** A and D are famous wetlands and are often confused with Montreux-listed sites because Ramsar and Montreux records are distinct lists. Only sites on the Montreux Record are specifically endangered.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#WETLANDS]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=2
Which type of coral reef is described as a *ring-shaped* reef encircling a central lagoon, with no central island visible above sea level?

- A) Fringing reef
- B) Barrier reef
- C) Atoll
- D) Patch reef
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — An **atoll** is a ring-shaped coral reef around a central lagoon, formed around a subsided volcanic island with no land visible above sea level. Fringing reefs hug the shoreline; barrier reefs are separated from land by a lagoon.

**Trap:** B is the most common wrong answer because both atolls and barrier reefs involve lagoons; the difference is the *ring shape with no central island* in an atoll.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#TYPES OF CORAL REEFS]]
</details>

@qenv.ecosystem.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=2
Consider the following statements:
1. An **estuary** is a transition zone between freshwater and marine ecosystems.
2. **Mangroves** are salt-tolerant trees that grow in coastal intertidal zones.
3. Both estuaries and mangroves function as **ecotones**.
4. Mangrove seeds germinate only *after* falling into the mud (vivipary is absent).

Which of the above statements are correct?

- A) 1, 2 and 3 only
- B) 1 and 2 only
- C) 2, 3 and 4 only
- D) 1, 2, 3 and 4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — Statements 1, 2 and 3 are correct. Statement 4 is **false** — mangroves are famously **viviparous**: their seeds *germinate while still attached to the parent tree* (the propagule then drops and roots in the mud).

**Trap:** D includes statement 4, which contradicts the chapter's explicit description of mangrove vivipary.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/02 - Ecosystem#ESTUARIES AND MANGROVES]]
</details>
