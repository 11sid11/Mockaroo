# Basics of Environmental Sciences — Questions

@qenv.basics.q1 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=1
The word "Environment" is derived from the French word *environner*, which means:

- A) "to live"
- B) "to surround"
- C) "to breathe"
- D) "to grow"
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — *Environner* literally means "to surround," capturing the idea that the environment comprises everything that *surrounds* and influences an organism.

**Trap:** A and C are plausible because ecology deals with life and air, but the etymological source is specifically "to surround."

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#INTRODUCTION TO ENVIRONMENT]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q2 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=1
Which of the following lists **only** abiotic components of the environment?

- A) Plants, animals, bacteria, fungi
- B) Light, rainfall, temperature, soil
- C) Producers, consumers, decomposers
- D) Plants, rainfall, animals, atmosphere
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Light, rainfall, temperature and soil are non-living (abiotic) factors. A and C list biotic factors only; D mixes biotic (plants, animals) with abiotic (rainfall, atmosphere).

**Trap:** D mixes living and non-living items — students must reject any option that includes even one biotic element.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#Components of the Environment]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q3 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=1
The term "Ecology" was coined in 1866 by:

- A) Charles Darwin
- B) Alexander von Humboldt
- C) Ernst Haeckel
- D) Eugene Odum
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — **Ernst Haeckel** (German zoologist) coined "Ecology" in 1866 from the Greek *oikos* (house). Eugene Odum is the *Father of Modern Ecology*, not the coiner of the term.

**Trap:** D is a common wrong answer because Odum's name is associated with ecology as a discipline; but the *term* is Haeckel's.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#ECOLOGY]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q4 difficulty=recall subject=Environment chapter=1
Match the following "fathers" with their associated fields:

| Column I (Father) | Column II (Field) |
|---|---|
| (i) Ernst Haeckel | (a) Coined the term "Ecology" |
| (ii) Eugene Odum | (b) Father of Modern Ecology |
| (iii) Ramdeo Mishra | (c) Father of Indian Ecology |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-b, ii-a, iii-c
- C) i-c, ii-b, iii-a
- D) i-a, ii-c, iii-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — Haeckel coined "Ecology," Odum is the Father of Modern Ecology, and Ramdeo Mishra is the Father of Indian Ecology. All three are well-attested in Indian competitive-exam material.

**Trap:** B and D mix Odum and Mishra — students often remember only one of the two and swap them.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#ECOLOGY]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q5 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=1
A forest ecologist counts all the tigers in a given tiger reserve. The tigers belong to the same species, live in the same area, and interbreed. This group of tigers is best described as a:

- A) Community
- B) Ecosystem
- C) Population
- D) Biome
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — A **population** is a group of individuals of the *same species* in a defined geographic area. A community would include *multiple species*; an ecosystem would include abiotic components too; a biome is a large regional classification (e.g., tropical rainforest).

**Trap:** B is tempting because tigers live in an ecosystem, but the ecologist is counting *tigers only*, not the entire system.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#Levels of Organization in an Ecosystem]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q6 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=1
A region where two distinct ecosystems meet and interact — for example, the transition between a forest and a grassland — is called an:

- A) Ecotone
- B) Niche
- C) Biome
- D) Biosphere
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — An **ecotone** is the transitional zone between two ecosystems or biomes. Ecotones are known for **maximum biodiversity** (the "edge effect").

**Trap:** B (niche) refers to the *role* of a species, not a physical region; C (biome) is a *whole* ecosystem type, not a transition zone.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#HABITAT]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q7 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=1
A patch of bare rock formed by a recent volcanic eruption has no soil and no life. Over time, lichens colonise the rock, slowly forming a thin layer of organic matter, which is later replaced by mosses and eventually by grasses and shrubs. This entire sequence is an example of:

- A) Secondary succession
- B) Primary succession
- C) Seasonal succession
- D) Climax succession in reverse
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — **Primary succession** occurs on a site where *no previous life existed* (bare rock, lava, newly formed ponds). Lichens are the typical **pioneer community**. Secondary succession would require a pre-existing soil or seed bank (e.g., after a forest fire).

**Trap:** A is the most common wrong answer — students confuse "no soil" with "no previous ecosystem" too loosely. Fire recovery = secondary; volcanic rock = primary.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#ECOLOGICAL SUCCESSION]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q8 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=1
After a forest fire, the area is observed to recover relatively quickly — within a few years, grasses and shrubs reappear, and within decades, a forest stand re-establishes. The most important reason this recovery is faster than primary succession is that:

- A) Fire eliminates all competing species
- B) The climate becomes more favourable after fire
- C) Soil and a seed bank or root stock survive underground
- D) Pioneer lichens immediately recolonise the burned surface
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — Secondary succession is faster than primary succession because the **soil** (and often buried seeds, roots, and microbial communities) survives the disturbance. Pioneer lichens are characteristic of *primary* succession on bare rock, not of fire recovery.

**Trap:** D reverses the two succession types; A and B describe effects of fire that are either false or irrelevant to succession rate.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#ECOLOGICAL SUCCESSION]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q9 difficulty=apply subject=Environment chapter=1
A person migrates from sea level to a high-altitude region (above 3,500 m). Over a few weeks, their body produces more red blood cells and their breathing rate increases at rest. This is an example of:

- A) Adaptation
- B) Acclimatization
- C) Evolution
- D) Mutation
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — **Acclimatization** is a short-term, *reversible* physiological adjustment to a changed environment (high altitude → more RBCs, faster breathing). Adaptation is a *heritable, long-term* feature of a population, not an individual. Evolution and mutation operate across generations.

**Trap:** A is the most common wrong answer — students equate any physiological change with "adaptation," but adaptation is genetic and inherited.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#ADAPTATION AND ACCLIMATIZATION]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=1
Consider the following statements about biotic interactions:
1. In **mutualism**, both interacting species benefit.
2. In **commensalism**, one species benefits and the other is harmed.
3. In **parasitism**, the host is usually killed immediately.
4. In **competition**, both species are negatively affected.

Which of the above statements are correct?

- A) 1 and 4 only
- B) 1, 2 and 4
- C) 2, 3 and 4
- D) 1, 3 and 4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — Statement 1 ✓ (mutualism = +/+). Statement 2 ✗ — in commensalism the other species is *neither helped nor harmed* (+/0), not harmed. Statement 3 ✗ — a parasite typically does *not* kill the host immediately; it feeds on the host over time. Statement 4 ✓ (competition = −/−).

**Trap:** Statement 2 is the classic distractor — students remember "+" and "0" signs and confuse them with parasitism's "+/−".

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#BIOTIC INTERACTIONS]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=1
A cattle egret follows grazing cattle and feeds on the insects stirred up by their movement. The egret clearly benefits (more food); the cattle appear neither helped nor harmed. According to standard ecological classification, this relationship is best described as:

- A) Mutualism
- B) Commensalism
- C) Parasitism
- D) Amensalism
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Cattle egret + grazing cattle is the textbook example of **commensalism** (+/0): one species benefits, the other is unaffected. Mutualism (+/+) requires *both* to benefit; parasitism harms the host; amensalism harms one and leaves the other unaffected.

**Trap:** A is a strong lure because the association *looks* cooperative. Mutualism requires the cattle to gain something tangible, which they do not.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#Categorisation of Biotic Interactions]]
</details>

@qenv.basics.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=Environment chapter=1
Which of the following is the *correct* sequence of increasing complexity of ecological organisation, from the smallest unit to the largest?

- A) Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere
- B) Community → Population → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere
- C) Population → Community → Biome → Ecosystem → Biosphere
- D) Organism → Ecosystem → Population → Community → Biosphere
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — The accepted hierarchy is: **Individual → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere**. Option A correctly orders populations (same species) within communities (multiple species) within ecosystems (with abiotic factors) within biomes (regional climate zones) within the biosphere (global).

**Trap:** B reverses population and community; C swaps biome and ecosystem; D inserts an "Ecosystem" between organism and population, which is illogical.

**See also:** [[../../Environment/01 - Basics of Environmental Sciences#Levels of Organization in an Ecosystem]]
</details>
