# Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies — Questions

> Source: [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies|Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - polity]]

A 15-question MCQ set on constitutional bodies (ECI, UPSC, SPSC, Finance Commission, CAG, Attorney General, NCSC/NCST/NCBC), statutory bodies (NHRC, CIC), and executive bodies (CVC, CBI) — composition, tenure, key articles, and recent changes. Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=13
The Election Commission of India is established under which Article of the Constitution?

- A) Article 280
- B) Article 315
- C) Article 324
- D) Article 338
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Part XV, Articles 324–329 deal with Elections and the Election Commission. Article 324 specifically provides for the establishment of the ECI and gives it the superintendence, direction and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of elections.

**Trap:** A (Article 280) is the Finance Commission; B (Article 315) is the Public Service Commissions (UPSC/SPSC); D (Article 338) is the National Commission for Scheduled Castes.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#Election Commission of India (ECI)|ECI]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=13
The Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners hold office for:

- A) 5 years or until 60 years of age
- B) 6 years or until 65 years of age
- C) 6 years or until 62 years of age
- D) Until the pleasure of the President
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The CEC and other ECs hold office for **6 years or until they attain 65 years of age**, whichever is earlier. Removal is similar to that of a Supreme Court judge (for the CEC); other ECs are removed by the President on the advice of the CEC.

**Trap:** A (5 years) is the term of the President/Governor; C (6 years / 62 years) is the SPSC term. Students confuse the various tenures.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#Tenure|Tenure]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=13
The Finance Commission of India is constituted by the President under which Article?

- A) Article 260
- B) Article 265
- C) Article 280
- D) Article 300
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Article 280 provides for the Finance Commission — a quasi-judicial body constituted by the President every 5 years (or earlier) to recommend the distribution of net proceeds of taxes between Centre and States.

**Trap:** A (260), B (265), D (300) all look like reasonable article numbers but deal with different topics — Article 265 is the prohibition of tax without authority of law. Article 300A is the right to property.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#Finance Commission (FC)|Finance Commission]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q4 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=13
The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India is described by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar as:

- A) The most important officer under the Constitution
- B) The watchdog of the public purse
- C) The guardian of the Constitution
- D) The guide and philosopher of Parliament
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Dr. Ambedkar called the CAG "the most important officer under the Constitution" because of the CAG's crucial role in safeguarding public finances. The CAG is also widely known as the "Guardian of the Public Purse" (option B). B is a popular description but not the original Ambedkar quote.

**Trap:** B (Guardian of the Public Purse) is widely used but is not Ambedkar's exact phrasing. Students confuse the two popular labels.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG)|CAG]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q5 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=13
The CAG of India holds office for a term of:

- A) 5 years or 60 years of age
- B) 6 years or 62 years of age
- C) 6 years or 65 years of age
- D) 4 years or 65 years of age
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The CAG holds office for **6 years or until 65 years of age**, whichever is earlier. Removal is in the manner of a Supreme Court judge (President, after both Houses pass an address by special majority). Same tenure as the Election Commission.

**Trap:** B (6 years or 62) is the SPSC term; D (4 years) doesn't apply. A (60) is the older retirement age for HCs (before the 15th CAA, 1963).

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#Term|Tenure]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q6 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=13
Consider the following statements about the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC):
1. It was established by the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.
2. The Chairperson of NHRC must be a retired Chief Justice of India.
3. The NHRC has constitutional status.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 only
- B) 1 and 2 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- Statement 1 ✓ — NHRC was established by the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. It is a *statutory* body, not constitutional.
- Statement 2 ✗ — The Chairperson of NHRC must be a retired Chief Justice of India *or* a retired Judge of the Supreme Court. "Or SC judge" is the correct formulation.
- Statement 3 ✗ — NHRC has statutory, not constitutional, status.

**Trap:** Statement 2 is the trap — many sources abbreviate "retired CJI or SC judge" as "retired CJI," losing the alternative qualification.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)|NHRC]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=13
The Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) was established on the recommendation of which committee?

- A) Santhanam Committee
- B) Kothari Committee
- C) Sarkaria Commission
- D) Shah Commission
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The CVC was established in 1964 by an executive resolution on the recommendation of the **Santhanam Committee** on Prevention of Corruption. The Santhanam Committee (1962) also recommended the CBI. The CVC got statutory status in 2003 (CVC Act).

**Trap:** B (Kothari) and C (Sarkaria) and D (Shah) are all famous committees/commissions but on different topics — Kothari was on tax reform, Sarkaria on Centre-State relations, Shah on the Emergency excesses.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)|CVC]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=13
Match the body with the article under which it is established:

| Body | Article |
|---|---|
| (i) UPSC | (a) Article 338B |
| (ii) Finance Commission | (b) Article 280 |
| (iii) National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) | (c) Article 315 |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-c, ii-b, iii-a
- C) i-b, ii-c, iii-a
- D) i-c, ii-a, iii-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- UPSC → Article 315 (c).
- Finance Commission → Article 280 (b).
- NCBC → Article 338B (a) — constitutional status conferred by the 102nd CAA, 2018.

**Trap:** D swaps UPSC and Finance Commission articles — students sometimes confuse 315 with 280 because both are "3xx" articles.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#Articles Mapping|Article Mapping]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q9 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=13
The Attorney General of India holds office during the pleasure of the President and must be qualified to be:

- A) A judge of the Supreme Court
- B) A judge of a High Court
- C) A member of the Lok Sabha
- D) A member of the Rajya Sabha
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Article 76 — the Attorney General must be qualified to be appointed a **judge of the Supreme Court** (i.e., a citizen of India who has been an HC judge for 5 years, an advocate of an HC for 10 years, or a distinguished jurist). By contrast, the Advocate General of a state must be qualified to be a **judge of a High Court** (Article 165).

**Trap:** B (HC judge) is the Advocate General's qualification — easy to slip. C and D (parliament membership) are unrelated.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#Attorney General of India|Attorney General]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=13
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) and State Public Service Commissions (SPSCs) are constitutional bodies, but a Joint Public Service Commission is statutory.
**Reason (R):** Articles 315–323 deal with PSCs but only contemplate Union and State commissions; Joint PSCs require a Parliament Act to set up.

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

UPSC and SPSCs are constitutional bodies established under Articles 315–323. A Joint Public Service Commission (covering two or more states) requires a Parliament Act under Article 315(4) — it is therefore *statutory*, not constitutional.

**Trap:** B is the partial-credit trap. R directly explains A — the Constitution only provides for UPSC and SPSCs; Joint PSCs exist only because Parliament has separately legislated for them.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#State Public Service Commission (SPSC)|SPSC]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q12 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=13
Which Commission/Committee is constituted by the President **every five years** to recommend the distribution of taxes between the Centre and States?

- A) Planning Commission
- B) Finance Commission
- C) Law Commission
- D) Election Commission
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 280 — the Finance Commission is constituted by the President every 5 years (or earlier) to recommend the principles for sharing the net proceeds of taxes between the Centre and States. The 15th FC (chaired by N. K. Singh) gave its report in 2020.

**Trap:** A (Planning Commission) was an *extra-constitutional* body (now replaced by NITI Aayog). C (Law Commission) is constituted by the Law Ministry on an ad hoc basis. D (ECI) is permanent.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#Finance Commission (FC)|Every 5 Years]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q13 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=13
The first Chief Election Commissioner of India was:

- A) T. N. Seshan
- B) K. V. K. Sundaram
- C) Sukumar Sen
- D) R. K. Trivedi
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Sukumar Sen was the first Chief Election Commissioner of India (1950–1958) and is credited with conducting India's first general elections (1951–52). The ECI was originally a single-member body; two additional ECs were added in 1989.

**Trap:** A (T. N. Seshan) was the famous 10th CEC (1990–96) who is widely remembered for strengthening the ECI. Students confuse "famous" with "first."

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#1st CEC: Sukumar Sen|First CEC]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q14 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=13
The GST Council was established by the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016 under which Article?

- A) Article 246A
- B) Article 269A
- C) Article 279A
- D) Article 286A
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Article 279A — inserted by the 101st CAA, 2016 — provides for the constitution of the GST Council, chaired by the Union Finance Minister with State Finance Ministers as members.

**Trap:** A (Article 246A) is the GST-specific legislative entry; B (Article 269A) deals with IGST on inter-State supplies; D (Article 286A) is not a provision in the Constitution. Easy to confuse because all three numbers (246A, 269A, 279A) are in the same neighbourhood and all relate to GST.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#GST Council|GST Council]]
</details>

@qpol.constitutional_bodies.q15 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=13
Consider the following:
1. The NCSC and NCST were originally one commission under Article 338; they were bifurcated by the 89th CAA, 2003.
2. The NCBC received constitutional status by the 102nd CAA, 2018, under Article 338B.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 only
- B) 2 only
- C) Both 1 and 2
- D) Neither 1 nor 2
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

- Statement 1 ✓ — The 65th CAA, 1990 first converted the Special Officer for SCs/STs into a multi-member National Commission for SCs and STs. The 89th CAA, 2003 bifurcated it into NCSC (Article 338) and NCST (Article 338A).
- Statement 2 ✓ — The 102nd CAA, 2018 conferred constitutional status on the NCBC (Article 338B), which had been a statutory body since 1993 (post-Mandal Commission, 1992).

**Trap:** Statement 2 is sometimes misremembered as the 92nd or 105th CAA. It is precisely the 102nd CAA, 2018.

**See also:** [[../../polity/13 - Constitutional and Non-Constitutional Bodies#National Commission for SCs & STs|SCs/STs Commissions]]
</details>
