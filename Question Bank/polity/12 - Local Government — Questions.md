# Local Government — Questions

> Source: [[../../polity/12 - Local Government|Local Government]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - polity]]

A 12-question MCQ set on local self-government in India — Part IX (Panchayats, Articles 243–243O), Part IX-A (Municipalities, Articles 243P–243ZH), Part IX-B (Co-operative Societies), the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments, key committees (Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta, Singhvi), and reservation provisions. @qpol.local_government.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=12
Which constitutional amendment gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions in India?

- A) 72nd Amendment, 1992
- B) 73rd Amendment, 1992
- C) 74th Amendment, 1992
- D) 75th Amendment, 1993
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 gave constitutional status to Panchayats by inserting Part IX (Articles 243–243O) and the 11th Schedule (29 functional items). It came into force on 24 April 1993 — celebrated as **Panchayati Raj Day**. The PM at the time was P. V. Narasimha Rao.

**Trap:** C (74th) is the sister amendment for Municipalities — students confuse the two because both came in 1992. A (72nd) doesn't exist; D (75th) deals with the NJDF/SFC relation.

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992|73rd CAA]]
</details>

@qpol.local_government.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=12
Match the committee with its recommendation:

| Committee | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| (i) Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) | (a) Constitutional status for Panchayats |
| (ii) Ashok Mehta Committee (1977) | (b) 3-tier Panchayati Raj (Village, Block, District) |
| (iii) L. M. Singhvi Committee (1986) | (c) 2-tier system with Zila Parishad as main unit |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-b, ii-c, iii-a
- C) i-c, ii-a, iii-b
- D) i-b, ii-a, iii-c
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- Balwant Rai Mehta (1957) → recommended the 3-tier Panchayati Raj system.
- Ashok Mehta (1977) → recommended a 2-tier system with Zila Parishad at the top and Mandal Panchayats below.
- L. M. Singhvi (1986) → first to recommend *constitutional status* for Panchayats — adopted in the 73rd CAA.

**Trap:** A swaps Balwant Rai Mehta and L. M. Singhvi — students who half-remember "Mehta = Panchayati Raj" sometimes assign the wrong "first" claim.

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#Evolution of Panchayat|Evolution of Panchayat]]
</details>

@qpol.local_government.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=12
The 11th Schedule of the Indian Constitution added how many functional items for Panchayats?

- A) 18
- B) 22
- C) 29
- D) 32
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The 11th Schedule, added by the 73rd CAA, 1992, contains **29 functional items** for Panchayats (e.g., agriculture, minor irrigation, drinking water, sanitation, primary education). The 12th Schedule contains 18 items for Municipalities.

**Trap:** D (32) is wrong; A (18) is the 12th Schedule (Municipalities) count. Students mix up which Schedule goes with which tier.

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992|11th Schedule]]
</details>

@qpol.local_government.q4 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=12
Under the 73rd Constitutional Amendment, the reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes / Scheduled Tribes in Panchayats is:

- A) At the discretion of the State Government
- B) In proportion to their population in the Panchayat area
- C) Fixed at 10% of total seats
- D) Only in Panchayats above a certain population
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 243D(1) — seats are reserved for SCs and STs in proportion to their population in the Panchayat area. Article 243D(3) extends reservation of the *Chairperson* post in the same proportion at each tier.

**Trap:** A is the trap — students remember "reservation" and assume the State Government decides. The Amendment makes it *mandatory* and *proportional*.

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#Article 243D|Article 243D]]
</details>

@qpol.local_government.q5 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=12
The first state to implement Panchayati Raj in India was:

- A) Gujarat
- B) Andhra Pradesh
- C) Rajasthan
- D) Karnataka
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Rajasthan implemented Panchayati Raj at Nagaur on 2 October 1959, becoming the first state to do so — based on the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee's recommendation. Andhra Pradesh followed on 11 October 1959.

**Trap:** B (Andhra Pradesh) is the close second (just 9 days later). Both are remembered as "early adopters."

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#Balwant Rai Mehta Committee, 1957|First Implementation]]
</details>

@qpol.local_government.q6 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=12
Consider the following statements about Panchayats:
1. The intermediate tier (block level) Panchayat is mandatory in every state.
2. The Chairperson of a Panchayat at village level is elected by direct election.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 only
- B) 2 only
- C) Both 1 and 2
- D) Neither 1 nor 2
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- Statement 1 ✗ — Article 243B says the intermediate-tier Panchayat is *not* to be constituted in a state with a population not exceeding 20 lakhs (2 million). So in smaller states (like Goa, Sikkim), the intermediate tier may not exist.
- Statement 2 ✓ — Article 243C(2): the Chairperson at the village level is elected in such manner as the State Legislature provides. In practice, this is typically direct election (with some states having indirect elections for intermediate and district level Chairpersons).

**Trap:** Statement 1 is the trap — students assume "3-tier" is mandatory everywhere. The 20-lakh population threshold is the escape hatch.

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#Article 243B|Article 243B]]
</details>

- C) 74th CAA

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Trap:** B (73rd) is for Panchayats. Students confuse the two sister amendments because both came in 1992 and both deal with local government.

@qpol.local_government.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=12
Lord Ripon's Resolution of 1882 is famously known as:

- A) The Charter of Indian Independence
- B) The Magna Carta of Local Self-Government in India
- C) The Foundation of the Republic
- D) The Birth of the Constitution
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Lord Ripon's Resolution of 1882 is called the "Magna Carta of Local Self-Government" — it established elected local bodies for the first time. Ripon is also called the "Father of Local Self-Government in India."

**Trap:** D (Birth of the Constitution) sounds patriotic but refers to the Constituent Assembly. The trap is between "local" and "national" firsts.

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#Municipal Corporation in Calcutta & Bombay|Magna Carta]]
</details>

@qpol.local_government.q9 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=12
Identify the correct types of Municipalities under the 74th CAA, 1992:

- A) Municipal Corporation, Municipal Council, Nagar Panchayat
- B) Municipal Corporation, Notified Area Committee, Cantonment Board
- C) Nagar Panchayat, Town Area Committee, Port Trust
- D) Township, Special Purpose Agency, Municipal Council
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The 74th CAA recognises three types of Municipalities:

- **Nagar Panchayat** — for a transitional area (moving from rural to urban).
- **Municipal Council** — for a small urban area.
- **Municipal Corporation** — for a larger urban area.

Other urban bodies (Notified Area Committee, Cantonment Board, Township, Town Area Committee, Port Trust, Special Purpose Agency) exist but are *not* the three constitutional categories of Municipalities.

**Trap:** B and D include other urban bodies that exist alongside Municipalities but are not the constitutional categories. Students mix them up.

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#3 Types of Municipalities|3 Types of Municipalities]]
</details>

The 97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011 added which Part to the Constitution, dealing with Co-operative Societies?

- A) Part IX
- B) Part IX-A
- C) Part IX-B
- D) Part X

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

@qpol.local_government.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=12
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** Panchayats and Municipalities cannot be said to be part of the "Three-tier" structure in the strict sense because local bodies exist only when states create them.
**Reason (R):** The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments made Panchayats and Municipalities a mandatory feature of the Indian democratic structure, but with a population threshold for the Panchayat intermediate tier.

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

Assertion A is false: the 73rd and 74th Amendments *do* make Panchayats and Municipalities a mandatory constitutional feature (subject to specific exceptions). The State Legislature *must* provide for them. R correctly explains this — Panchayats and Municipalities are now constitutional bodies, with the only flexibility being the 20-lakh population exception for the Panchayat intermediate tier.

**Trap:** A is tempting because pre-1992 local bodies were state-dependent. Post-1992, they are constitutionally mandated. The 20-lakh population exception does not undermine that.

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992|Constitutional Status]]
</details>

@qpol.local_government.q12 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=12
The Panchayat at the intermediate level is NOT to be constituted in a state with a population not exceeding:

- A) 10 lakhs
- B) 20 lakhs
- C) 25 lakhs
- D) 30 lakhs
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 243B — Panchayats at the intermediate level need not be constituted in a state with a population not exceeding 20 lakhs (2 million). This allows smaller states to skip the block-level Panchayat.

**Trap:** C (25 lakhs) is a tempting wrong answer — students misremember "25" as a "nice round number." The Constitution specifically uses 20 lakhs.

**See also:** [[../../polity/12 - Local Government#Article 243B|Article 243B]]
</details>
