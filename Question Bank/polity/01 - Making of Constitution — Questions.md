# Making of Constitution — Questions

> Source: [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution|Making of Constitution]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - polity]]

A 12-question MCQ set on the making of the Indian Constitution — the Constituent Assembly, its committees, key drafters, timelines, and the difference between adoption (26 Nov 1949) and commencement (26 Jan 1950). Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

Scoring: SSC CGL rules — **+2 correct, -0.50 wrong, 0 unattempted**.

---

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=1
Who chaired the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly, which prepared the final draft of the Indian Constitution?

- A) Jawaharlal Nehru
- B) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
- C) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar
- D) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar chaired the 7-member Drafting Committee and is therefore known as the chief architect of the Constitution. Nehru headed three committees (Union Powers, Union Constitution, States), Patel headed two (Provincial Constitution, Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights etc.), and Rajendra Prasad headed Rules of Procedure and Steering.

**Trap:** Many students pick Nehru because he chaired the most committees overall (3). But the *Drafting* Committee — the one that actually drafted the Constitution — was Ambedkar's.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#DRAFTING COMMITTEE|Drafting Committee]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=1
A candidate appearing for SSC CGL confuses two important dates of the Indian Constitution: 26 November 1949 and 26 January 1950. Which of the following correctly pairs these dates with the events that occurred?

- A) 26 Nov 1949 — Constitution was commenced; 26 Jan 1950 — Constitution was adopted
- B) 26 Nov 1949 — Constitution was adopted; 26 Jan 1950 — Constitution was commenced
- C) Both dates mark the commencement of the Constitution
- D) Both dates mark the adoption of the Constitution
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Constitution was *adopted* on 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day) but *commenced / enforced* on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day). On 26 Nov 1949 itself, certain provisions — Citizenship (Articles 5–9), Elections (Articles 324–329) and the Provisional Parliament — were brought into force; the rest of the Constitution came into force on 26 Jan 1950.

**Trap:** Option A reverses the two events — a very common SSC trap because both dates fall in the same fortnight of late-November-to-late-January.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#Important Sections|Important Sections]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=1
Who served as the Constitutional Advisor to the Constituent Assembly, helping to frame the Constitution?

- A) H. V. Iyengar
- B) S. N. Mukherjee
- C) B. N. Rau
- D) Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

B. N. Rau was the Constitutional Advisor. H. V. Iyengar was the Secretary of the Constituent Assembly, S. N. Mukherjee was the Chief Draftsman, and Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar was a member of the Drafting Committee.

**Trap:** B (S. N. Mukherjee, Chief Draftsman) is a very tempting wrong answer because drafting and advising are easily confused.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#Some more Significant facts to know|Significant facts]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q4 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=1
Consider the following statements about the Constituent Assembly of India:
1. It originally formed 8 major and 13 minor committees.
2. Sachchidananda Sinha was the permanent President of the Assembly from its very first sitting.
3. The Assembly also functioned as the first Provisional Parliament of independent India.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 1 and 3 only
- C) 2 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- Statement 1 ✓ — 8 major + 13 minor committees.
- Statement 2 ✗ — Sachchidananda Sinha was only the *temporary* President (before elections); Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President afterwards.
- Statement 3 ✓ — The Assembly did double as the first Provisional Parliament of independent India.

**Trap:** Statement 2 is the trap. Sinha is well-known as the first presiding officer of the Assembly, but he was *temporary*, not permanent. Many students elevate him to permanent President because he was the *first*.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#Functions of the Constituent Assembly|Functions of the Constituent Assembly]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q5 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=1
The Constitution of India was signed by the members of the Constituent Assembly on:

- A) 26 November 1949
- B) 24 January 1950
- C) 26 January 1950
- D) 15 August 1947
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Constitution was signed by 284 members (including 15 women) on 24 January 1950 — two days before Republic Day. The Constitution was *adopted* on 26 November 1949 and *commenced* on 26 January 1950; the signing ceremony falls between those two milestones.

**Trap:** A and C are the "big" Constitution dates and dominate SSC MCQs. Students who only remember 26 Nov / 26 Jan almost never pick 24 Jan — but the signing date is its own distinct event.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#MAKING OF CONSTITUTION|Making of Constitution — signatory facts]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q6 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=1
Match the Constitution calligrapher with the language in which they wrote the original Constitution of India:

| Calligrapher | Language |
|---|---|
| (i) Prem Bihari Narain Raizada | (a) Hindi |
| (ii) Vasant Krishan Vaidya | (b) English |

- A) i-a, ii-b
- B) i-b, ii-a
- C) Both wrote the English version
- D) Both wrote the Hindi version
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Prem Bihari Narain Raizada calligraphed the English copy and Vasant Krishan Vaidya did the Hindi copy. Nand Lal Bose and Beohar Rammanohar Sinha decorated (painted) the copies.

**Trap:** A is the inversion — students who half-remember the names tend to swap the language each one did.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#Constitution Calligraphers|Constitution Calligraphers]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=1
Why was 26 January chosen as the date on which the Constitution of India came into force?

- A) It was the date the Constituent Assembly first met
- B) It commemorated the Purna Swaraj Day celebrated after the 1929 Lahore Session of the Indian National Congress
- C) It marked the anniversary of the Cabinet Mission Plan
- D) It was the date of India's independence from British rule
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

26 January was chosen to mark the anniversary of Purna Swaraj Day (complete independence), which was celebrated on 26 January 1930 following the Lahore Session (1929) of the INC. D is wrong — independence was 15 August 1947.

**Trap:** D is the dominant wrong answer. Students who do not remember the Lahore Session / Purna Swaraj connection often pick independence day because "26 January is a big national day."

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#Important Sections|Important Sections]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=1
Which committee among the following was chaired by Dr. Rajendra Prasad?

- A) Drafting Committee
- B) Rules of Procedure Committee
- C) Union Powers Committee
- D) Provincial Constitution Committee
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Dr. Rajendra Prasad chaired the Rules of Procedure Committee and the Steering Committee among the major committees, and three minor committees (Finance & Staff, Ad-hoc National Flag, plus credentials-related work). Ambedkar chaired Drafting, Nehru chaired Union Powers, Patel chaired Provincial Constitution.

**Trap:** A (Drafting Committee) is the most-tempted wrong answer because Ambedkar's chairmanship is famous — students assume Prasad also chaired an equally famous one.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#Major Committee of Constituent Assembly|Major Committees]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=1
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** B. R. Ambedkar is referred to as the "Modern Manu" of the Indian Constitution.
**Reason (R):** He chaired the Drafting Committee and is considered the chief architect of the Constitution.

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The epithet "Modern Manu" was given to Ambedkar because, just as Manu is credited with codifying ancient Hindu law, Ambedkar codified modern India's constitutional law. His chairmanship of the Drafting Committee (R) is exactly why the "Modern Manu" label fits him — so R correctly explains A.

**Trap:** B is the partial-credit trap. Both statements are individually true, but R does directly explain A — they aren't just coincidentally true facts.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#DRAFTING COMMITTEE|Drafting Committee]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q10 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=1
Arrange the following events in chronological order (earliest to latest):
1. First sitting of the Constituent Assembly
2. Objective Resolution moved by Nehru in the Assembly
3. Formation of the Drafting Committee
4. Adoption of the Constitution

- A) 1 → 2 → 3 → 4
- B) 2 → 1 → 3 → 4
- C) 1 → 3 → 2 → 4
- D) 3 → 1 → 2 → 4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- 1 — First sitting: 9 December 1946
- 2 — Objective Resolution moved: 13 December 1946 (adopted 22 January 1947)
- 3 — Drafting Committee set up: 29 August 1947
- 4 — Constitution adopted: 26 November 1949

**Trap:** C swaps the Objective Resolution and the Drafting Committee. Students often think Nehru moved the Resolution before the Assembly even sat — in fact, the Resolution came just 4 days *after* the first sitting.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#Drafts of Constituent Assembly|Drafts of Constituent Assembly]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=1
Which of the following correctly matches a member of the Constituent Assembly with the post she is best known for holding later?

- A) Sucheta Kriplani — First female Governor of an Indian state
- B) Sarojini Naidu — First female Chief Minister of an Indian state
- C) Rajkumari Amrit Kaur — First female Health Minister of independent India
- D) All three pairings are correct
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

- Rajkumari Amrit Kaur ✓ — First Health Minister.
- Sucheta Kriplani → she was the first female *Chief Minister* (UP), not Governor.
- Sarojini Naidu → she was the first female *Governor* (UP), not Chief Minister.

A and B are deliberately swapped to trap students who remember only "first woman" + "UP."

**Trap:** D sounds safe but is wrong because A and B swap the post. Remember: Kriplani = CM, Naidu = Governor.

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#Important Female members of The Constituent Assembly|Female Members]]
</details>

@qpol.making_of_constitution.q12 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=1
The final draft of the Constitution was introduced by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar before the Constituent Assembly on 4 November 1948. How many days did the first reading of the Constitution last?

- A) 3 days
- B) 5 days
- C) 7 days
- D) 10 days
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The first reading ran from 4 November 1948 to 9 November 1948 — exactly 5 days. The second reading stretched over 10 months and 3 days (15 Nov 1948 – 17 Oct 1949) and the third reading was completed on 26 November 1949.

**Trap:** D (10 days) is tempting because the second reading lasted 10 months — students confuse "10 months" with "10 days."

**See also:** [[../../polity/01 - Making of Constitution#Drafts of Constituent Assembly|Drafts of Constituent Assembly]]
</details>
