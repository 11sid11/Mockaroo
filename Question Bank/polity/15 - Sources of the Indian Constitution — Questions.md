# Sources of the Indian Constitution — Questions

> Source: [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution|Sources of the Indian Constitution]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - polity]]

A 10-question MCQ set on the foreign constitutions from which the Indian Constitution draws — UK (Parliamentary system, Rule of Law), USA (Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review), Ireland (DPSP), GOI Act 1935, and others. Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

Scoring: SSC CGL rules — **+2 correct, -0.50 wrong, 0 unattempted**.

---

@qpol.sources.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=15
The Parliamentary system of government in India is borrowed from:

- A) USA
- B) UK (British Constitution)
- C) Ireland
- D) Canada
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Parliamentary system of government, along with the Rule of Law, Cabinet system, bicameralism (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha), Speaker's role, single citizenship, parliamentary privileges, and prerogative writs, is borrowed from the **British (UK) Constitution**.

**Trap:** A (USA) is the dominant wrong answer because India is also a federal republic — but the *form of executive* (parliamentary, not presidential) is British.

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#British Constitution (United Kingdom)|UK Sources]]
</details>

@qpol.sources.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=15
The Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) in the Indian Constitution are borrowed from:

- A) US Constitution
- B) USSR (Soviet) Constitution
- C) Irish Constitution
- D) French Constitution
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The DPSP are borrowed from the **Irish Constitution**, which itself drew inspiration from the Spanish Constitution. The nomination of members to the Rajya Sabha and the method of election of the President are also Irish-influenced.

**Trap:** A (US) is the dominant wrong answer — students associate constitutional borrowing with the US because of Fundamental Rights. But DPSP specifically come from Ireland.

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#Irish Constitution|Irish Sources]]
</details>

@qpol.sources.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=15
Which feature of the Indian Constitution is borrowed from the Constitution of the USA?

- A) Parliamentary system
- B) Concurrent List and freedom of trade
- C) Fundamental Rights and Judicial Review
- D) Procedure established by law
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The USA contributed **Fundamental Rights**, **Judicial Review**, independence of the judiciary, written Constitution, the offices of President and Vice President, impeachment procedure, removal of SC/HC judges, and separation of powers among the three organs.

**Trap:** B (Concurrent List) is Australian; D (procedure established by law) is Japanese. A (Parliamentary system) is British. All are easily confused.

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#United States Constitution (USA)|US Sources]]
</details>

@qpol.sources.q4 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=15
Match the feature with the country from which it is borrowed:

| Feature | Country |
|---|---|
| (i) Federal structure, Office of Governor, Emergency Provisions | (a) USA |
| (ii) Procedure established by law (Article 21) | (b) Government of India Act, 1935 |
| (iii) Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review | (c) Japan |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-b, ii-c, iii-a
- C) i-c, ii-a, iii-b
- D) i-b, ii-a, iii-c
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- Federal structure, Office of Governor, Emergency Provisions, Public Service Commissions, integrated judiciary, administrative details → borrowed from the **Government of India Act, 1935**.
- "Procedure established by law" → borrowed from **Japan**.
- Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, written Constitution, Preamble, President/VP, impeachment, removal of judges, separation of powers → borrowed from the **USA**.

**Trap:** D is the trap — it places "procedure established by law" with USA. The US has "due process of law," which is broader; India deliberately chose the narrower Japanese phrasing.

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#Government of India Act, 1935|Source Mapping]]
</details>

@qpol.sources.q5 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=15
The ideals of Liberty, Equality and Fraternity in the Preamble are borrowed from:

- A) USA
- B) UK
- C) French Constitution
- D) Ireland
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The French Constitution contributed the **republican form of government** and the ideals of **Liberty, Equality, Fraternity** (which appear in the Preamble). It also influenced the ideals of justice (social, economic, political) via the Soviet Constitution.

**Trap:** A (USA) is the dominant wrong answer — students associate the US with the slogan "Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness" and confuse it with the French "Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité."

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#French Constitution|French Sources]]
</details>

@qpol.sources.q6 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=15
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** The Indian Constitution is often described as a "borrowed constitution."
**Reason (R):** Almost every provision of the Constitution can be traced to some existing constitution or governmental document — but the specific combination and adaptation to India's needs is unique.

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Constitution draws from many sources — UK (Parliamentary system), USA (Fundamental Rights), Ireland (DPSP), GOI Act 1935 (federal structure), etc. — so calling it "borrowed" is justified. But R adds the crucial nuance: the *combination* and the *adaptation* are original, which is why the Constitution is also called "a patchwork that works" or "an eclectic document."

**Trap:** B is the partial-credit trap. R doesn't just complement A — it gives the substantive context that makes A meaningful.

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#Sources summary|Borrowed Constitution]]
</details>

@qpol.sources.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=15
Concurrent List, freedom of trade and commerce, and joint sitting of Parliament are features borrowed from:

- A) USA
- B) UK
- C) Australian Constitution
- D) Canadian Constitution
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **Australian Constitution** contributed the **Concurrent List**, freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse within the country, and the joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament to resolve deadlocks.

**Trap:** D (Canada) contributed the federal structure with strong centre, residuary powers to the Centre, appointment of Governors by the Centre, and the advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court. Students often confuse Australian with Canadian because both are Commonwealth federations.

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#Australian Constitution|Australian Sources]]
</details>

@qpol.sources.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=15
The Fundamental Duties added to the Indian Constitution are borrowed from:

- A) USA
- B) USSR (Soviet) Constitution
- C) Ireland
- D) France
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Fundamental Duties (Article 51-A) are borrowed from the **USSR (Soviet) Constitution** of 1977. India added them via the 42nd CAA, 1976. The ideals of justice (social, economic, political) in the Preamble also derive from the USSR.

**Trap:** C (Ireland) is the DPSP trap. Many students associate "duties" with Ireland by extension, but FD specifically come from the USSR.

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#Constitution of the Soviet Union (now Russia)|USSR Sources]]
</details>

@qpol.sources.q9 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=15
The procedure for amendment of the Constitution and the election of Rajya Sabha members by proportional representation are features borrowed from:

- A) USA
- B) South Africa
- C) Australia
- D) Japan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **South African Constitution** contributed the procedure for amendment of the Constitution and the election of Rajya Sabha members using proportional representation with the single transferable vote (STV).

**Trap:** A (USA) is the dominant wrong answer — students associate amendment procedures with the US. But India's Article 368 amendment procedure is South African in inspiration.

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#South African Constitution|South African Sources]]
</details>

@qpol.sources.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=15
The emergency provisions (suspension of Fundamental Rights) in the Indian Constitution are borrowed from:

- A) USA
- B) UK
- C) Weimar Constitution of Germany
- D) USSR
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Emergency provisions (Articles 352, 356, 360) — including the suspension of Fundamental Rights — are borrowed from the **Weimar Constitution of Germany** (1919). The Weimar Constitution had elaborate emergency provisions that influenced the framers of the Indian Constitution.

**Trap:** D (USSR) is the trap — students associate "emergency" with the Soviet Union. But the specific *suspension of Fundamental Rights during emergency* is a German (Weimar) borrowing.

**See also:** [[../../polity/15 - Sources of the Indian Constitution#Weimar Constitution of Germany|Weimar Sources]]
</details>
