# DPSP and Fundamental Duties — Questions

> Source: [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties|DPSP and Fundamental Duties]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - polity]]

A 12-question MCQ set on Part IV (Directive Principles, Articles 36–51) and Part IV-A (Fundamental Duties, Article 51-A) — their justiciability, the Swaran Singh Committee, classifications of DPSP, and the 11 Fundamental Duties (including the 86th CAA addition). Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

Scoring: SSC CGL rules — **+2 correct, -0.50 wrong, 0 unattempted**.

---

@qpol.dpsp.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=6
Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are contained in which Part and Articles of the Constitution?

- A) Part III — Articles 12–35
- B) Part IV — Articles 36–51
- C) Part IV-A — Article 51-A
- D) Part V — Articles 52–78
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

DPSP are in Part IV, Articles 36 to 51. Part III is Fundamental Rights; Part IV-A is Fundamental Duties (Article 51-A); Part V is The Union (executive).

**Trap:** C (Part IV-A) is the dominant wrong answer — students confuse DPSP (Part IV) with Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A). Both were influenced by foreign constitutions and are "non-justiciable," which adds to the confusion.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#PART IV - DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES OF STATE POLICY [Article 36-51]|Part IV]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=6
Which of the following statements BEST captures the legal status of the Directive Principles of State Policy?

- A) They are enforceable in a court of law
- B) They are non-justiciable but fundamental to governance
- C) They are merely advisory and can be ignored by the State
- D) They have the same legal force as Fundamental Rights
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 37 explicitly says DPSPs are **fundamental to the governance of the country** and the State has a duty to apply them while making laws. They are *non-justiciable* (not legally enforceable in court) but they are *not* "merely advisory" — they are constitutional obligations on the State.

**Trap:** C is the trap — K. T. Shah called them a "cheque payable by the bank at its own convenience," which sounds dismissive, but Article 37 elevates them above mere advice.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#Article 37|Article 37]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=6
The Directive Principles of State Policy were borrowed from the Constitution of which country?

- A) USA
- B) UK
- C) Ireland
- D) USSR
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

DPSP are borrowed from the **Irish Constitution**, which itself derived them from the Spanish Constitution. B. R. Ambedkar noted that DPSP resemble the "Instrument of Instructions" in the GOI Act, 1935.

**Trap:** A (USA) is the dominant wrong answer — students associate the US with Bill of Rights / Fundamental Rights. But DPSP are Irish in origin.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#Salient Features of DPSP|Salient Features of DPSP]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q4 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=6
Match the following DPSP comments with their authors:

| Comment | Author |
|---|---|
| (i) "Conscience of the Constitution" | (a) K. T. Shah |
| (ii) "Cheque payable by the bank at its own convenience" | (b) Granville Austin |
| (iii) "Moral Percepts for the authorities of state" | (c) B. N. Rau |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-b, ii-a, iii-c
- C) i-b, ii-c, iii-a
- D) i-c, ii-a, iii-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- Granville Austin → "Conscience of the Constitution."
- K. T. Shah → "Cheque payable by the bank at its own convenience."

**Trap:** A swaps (i) and (ii). Students remember Austin and Shah as "famous commentators" but mix which quote belongs to whom.

@qpol.dpsp.q5 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=6
Fundamental Duties are contained in:

- A) Part IV, Article 36
- B) Part IV-A, Article 51-A
- C) Part III, Article 32
- D) Schedule III of the Constitution
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Fundamental Duties are in Part IV-A, Article 51-A — added by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976**, on the recommendations of the **Swaran Singh Committee (1976)**. Originally 10 duties; the 86th CAA, 2002 added the 11th (51A(k) — parent/guardian's duty to provide education to children aged 6–14).

**Trap:** A (Part IV, Article 36) is the DPSP trap — students confuse Part IV and Part IV-A. Remember: "IV-A" is the appendix added later for duties.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#PART IVA - FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES [Article 51A]|Part IV-A]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q6 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=6
Consider the following statements about Fundamental Duties:
1. They are enforceable by courts like Fundamental Rights.
2. They are applicable to both citizens and foreigners.
3. They were added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976, on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 1 and 3 only
- C) 3 only
- D) 2 and 3 only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

- Statement 1 ✗ — Fundamental Duties are *non-justiciable* (not legally enforceable). They are moral obligations.
- Statement 2 ✗ — They apply only to **citizens**, not foreigners.
- Statement 3 ✓ — Added by the 42nd CAA, 1976, on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation.

**Trap:** Statements 1 and 2 are the dual traps. Students extend the principle of "rights" to "duties" — but rights are enforceable and available against the State; duties are moral and rest on citizens.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#Fundamental duties are provided for Indian citizens only and not for Foreigners.|Fundamental Duties]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=6
The DPSP are broadly classified into three categories: Socialist, Gandhian and Liberal-Intellectual. Which of the following is a **Gandhian** DPSP?

- A) Article 38 — Promotion of welfare of the people
- B) Article 39 — Equal pay for equal work
- C) Article 40 — Organisation of Village Panchayats
- D) Article 41 — Right to work and education
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Article 40 (organisation of Village Panchayats), Article 43 (living wages for workers), Article 46 (promotion of education of SC/ST/weaker sections), Article 47 (prohibition of intoxicating drinks) are Gandhian — they echo Gandhiji's vision of village self-governance and rural uplift. Article 38 and 39 are Socialist; Article 41 is Liberal-Intellectual.

**Trap:** D (Article 41) sounds "Gandhian" because Gandhi championed village welfare, but Article 41 — Right to Work, Education, Public Assistance — is the Liberal-Intellectual category.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#Classification of DPSP|Classification]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=6
Which DPSP Article specifically mandates the State to provide free legal aid to ensure equal justice?

- A) Article 38
- B) Article 39
- C) Article 39A
- D) Article 41
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Article 39A directs the State to ensure equal justice and free legal aid to the poor and weaker sections. It was inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**.

**Trap:** B (Article 39) is the dominant wrong answer — Article 39A is a sub-clause added later, and students who remember "Article 39 = equal pay" can confuse the two. The "A" sub-clause is the free-legal-aid one.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#Article 39A|Article 39A]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q9 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=6
Which Fundamental Duty mandates a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the ages of six and fourteen?

- A) 51A(a)
- B) 51A(e)
- C) 51A(j)
- D) 51A(k)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

51A(k) is the eleventh Fundamental Duty, inserted by the 86th CAA, 2002 — the only FD added after the original 1976 list. It complements Article 21-A (Right to Education).

**Trap:** A (51A(a)) is the duty to abide by the Constitution and respect the national flag/anthem — the most "famous" FD and the obvious trap. Students default to it.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#(k) duty of a parent or guardian|(k) duty of a parent]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q10 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=6
Which Constitutional Amendment added the words "Socialist" and "Secular" to the Preamble AND inserted the Fundamental Duties chapter in the same year?

- A) 42nd Amendment, 1976
- B) 44th Amendment, 1978
- C) 86th Amendment, 2002
- D) 91st Amendment, 2003
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The **42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976** — known as the "Mini-Constitution" — did both: added "Socialist," "Secular" and "Integrity" to the Preamble, and added Part IV-A (Fundamental Duties, Article 51-A). The Swaran Singh Committee's report (1976) recommended the duties, drawing inspiration from the USSR Constitution.

**Trap:** B (44th) is the trap — the 44th *restored* some liberties curtailed by the 42nd. Many students wrongly credit the 44th with adding duties.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#Recent Amendments and Importance|Recent Amendments]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=6
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** In case of conflict between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles, the Courts generally give precedence to Fundamental Rights.
**Reason (R):** DPSP are non-justiciable whereas Fundamental Rights are legally enforceable.

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

This is the classical position (post-Minerva Mills). The Supreme Court held that the harmony between FR and DPSP should be sought by reading them complementarily. But where they conflict directly and cannot be reconciled, FR prevails *because* it is justiciable. In *Minerva Mills v. Union of India* (1980), the Court struck down parts of the 42nd Amendment that gave DPSP primacy over FR.

**Trap:** Some students wrongly think DPSP always prevail (Article 31C as amended in 1976 *did* give them primacy in some sectors, but this was struck down in Minerva Mills). The current constitutional position is FR prevails in conflict.

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#DPSP vs FR|DPSP vs FR]]
</details>

@qpol.dpsp.q12 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=6
The DPSP and Fundamental Duties are both inspired by foreign constitutions. Which of the following correctly pairs them with their sources?

- A) DPSP — USSR; Fundamental Duties — Ireland
- B) DPSP — Ireland; Fundamental Duties — USSR
- C) DPSP — USA; Fundamental Duties — Ireland
- D) DPSP — UK; Fundamental Duties — USA
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

DPSP are borrowed from the **Irish** Constitution; Fundamental Duties are borrowed from the **USSR (Soviet)** Constitution. The Irish Constitution itself took them from Spain.

**Trap:** A is the inversion — students reverse the sources. Remember: Ireland gave India "principles" (DPSP), USSR gave India "duties" (FD).

**See also:** [[../../polity/06 - DPSP and Fundamental Duties#Salient Features of DPSP|DPSP Sources]]
</details>
