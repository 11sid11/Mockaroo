@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=8
Which article provides that the Council of Ministers shall aid and advise the President, who shall act in accordance with such advice?

- A) Article 72
- B) Article 74
- C) Article 76
- D) Article 78
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 74(1) says there shall be a Council of Ministers with the PM as head to aid and advise the President, who shall act in accordance with such advice. Article 74(2) bars any court from inquiring into what advice was tendered.

**Trap:** A (Article 72) is the pardoning power; C (Article 76) is the Attorney General; D (Article 78) is the PM's specific duties to the President. All relate to the executive but only Article 74 establishes the aid-and-advise relationship.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#Article 74|Article 74]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=8
The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to:

- A) The President
- B) The Rajya Sabha
- C) The Lok Sabha
- D) Both Houses of Parliament jointly
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Article 75(3) makes the Council of Ministers **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (House of the People). This is the basis of the parliamentary system — the government must retain the confidence of the Lok Sabha to remain in office.

**Trap:** D (both Houses jointly) sounds "parliamentary" but is wrong — collective responsibility is specifically to the Lok Sabha because it is the directly elected House.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#Article 75|Article 75]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=8
The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003, limited the total number of Ministers (including the Prime Minister) in the Council of Ministers to what percentage of the total strength of the Lok Sabha?

- A) 10%
- B) 15%
- C) 20%
- D) 25%
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The 91st CAA, 2003 capped the Council of Ministers (including the PM) at **15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha**. It also disqualified anyone convicted of defection from becoming a Minister.

**Trap:** A (10%) is the trap — students confuse the 15% rule with the Lok Sabha quorum requirement (Article 100, 1/10th of total strength).

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#Article 75|Article 75 - 91st CAA]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q4 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=8
Which type of emergency can be proclaimed by the President under Article 352 of the Indian Constitution?

- A) Financial Emergency
- B) President's Rule in a State
- C) National Emergency on grounds of war, external aggression or armed rebellion
- D) Constitutional Emergency
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Article 352 provides for **National Emergency** on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion (the last was substituted for "internal disturbance" by the 44th Amendment, 1978). Originally invoked in 1962 (Sino-Indian war), 1971 (Indo-Pak war), and 1975–77 (internal emergency).

**Trap:** B (President's Rule) is Article 356 — different. D (Constitutional Emergency) is not a defined category — it's a distractor. A (Financial Emergency) is Article 360 — declared only once (not really) in 1991 consideration.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#National Emergency|National Emergency]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q5 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=8
Under which article does the President impose President's Rule in a State when the constitutional machinery in that State breaks down?

- A) Article 352
- B) Article 356
- C) Article 360
- D) Article 365
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 356 — President's Rule — is invoked when the President is satisfied that the constitutional machinery in a State has broken down. The State Legislature is suspended and the Governor administers the State with the help of the Centre. It cannot normally continue beyond 3 years without specific conditions.

**Trap:** A (352) and C (360) are the National Emergency and Financial Emergency respectively — students confuse the three emergency articles.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#President's Rule (Article 356)|Article 356]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q6 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=8
Consider the following about Emergency provisions:
1. During a National Emergency, Fundamental Rights under Articles 19 and 32 can be suspended.
2. Articles 20 and 21 can NEVER be suspended even during an Emergency.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 only
- B) 2 only
- C) Both 1 and 2
- D) Neither 1 nor 2
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

- Statement 1 ✓ — Article 358 (during Emergency) automatically suspends Article 19, and Article 359 lets the President suspend the right to move courts for enforcement of other Fundamental Rights (including Article 32).
- Statement 2 ✓ — Articles 20 (protection in respect of conviction for offences) and 21 (right to life and personal liberty) are excepted from suspension — they cannot be taken away even during Emergency. This was confirmed in *ADM Jabalpur* (1976, though that decision was overturned post-Emergency) and the constitutional position was reaffirmed by the 44th Amendment.

**Trap:** Statement 1 is partially right but easily over-stated — only some rights are auto-suspended. Statement 2 is the important safety-valve.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#National Emergency|Effects of Emergency]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=8
Constitutional amendments under Article 368 require different types of majorities depending on the subject matter. Consider the following statements:
1. An amendment to Article 368 itself (which governs the amendment procedure) requires a special majority + ratification by half the state legislatures.
2. An amendment creating a new state (Article 2/3) requires a special majority.
3. An amendment to the 7th Schedule (legislative lists) requires a simple majority.

Which of the above statements is/are INCORRECT?

- A) 1 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 2 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Statements 2 and 3 are INCORRECT.
- Statement 2 ✗ — Creating new states under Article 2/3 requires only a **simple majority** (not a special majority). These amendments do NOT follow the Article 368 procedure at all (Article 4 says so explicitly).
- Statement 3 ✗ — Amendments to the 7th Schedule (lists) require **special majority + half-state ratification** (not simple majority), because they affect the federal distribution of power.
- Statement 1 ✓ — Amending Article 368 itself requires the most stringent procedure: special majority in each House + ratification by at least half the state legislatures.

**Trap:** Statement 2 is the dominant trap — students assume "anything to do with the Constitution" needs the heavy Article 368 procedure. But state formation is a notable exception that uses simple majority under Article 4.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#Methods of Constitutional Amendment|Methods of Amendment]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=8
The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976) is often called the "Mini-Constitution" because:

- A) It established a wholly new Constitution
- B) It made the largest set of amendments to the Constitution, including adding "Socialist" and "Secular" to the Preamble
- C) It reduced the number of Fundamental Rights
- D) It abolished the Supreme Court
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The 42nd CAA, 1976 made sweeping changes during the Emergency: added "Socialist," "Secular," "Integrity" to the Preamble; transferred five subjects from State to Concurrent List; reduced the Supreme Court's and High Courts' jurisdictions over certain cases; gave DPSP primacy over FR; added Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A); curtailed Lok Sabha's power over the PM's salary.

**Trap:** A and C are extreme-sounding distractors — the 42nd was a heavy amendment, but it did not replace the Constitution or remove Fundamental Rights.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#Recent Constitutional Amendments (CA)|42nd Amendment]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q9 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=8
In which landmark case did the Supreme Court propound the "Basic Structure Doctrine" — that Parliament cannot amend the Constitution in a way that destroys its basic structure?

- A) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967)
- B) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
- C) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)
- D) Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

In *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973), a 13-judge bench (largest ever) held by a thin majority that Parliament's power to amend the Constitution under Article 368 is *not unlimited* — it cannot destroy the "basic structure" of the Constitution. The doctrine was reaffirmed in *Minerva Mills* (1980).

**Trap:** A (Golaknath) earlier held that Parliament could not amend Fundamental Rights at all — that position was overturned in Kesavananda. C (Minerva Mills) applied the basic structure doctrine but did not *propound* it.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#Basic Structure|Basic Structure Doctrine]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q10 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=8
The Attorney General of India is appointed under which article?

- A) Article 72
- B) Article 74
- C) Article 76
- D) Article 78
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Article 76 provides for the appointment of the Attorney General of India — the highest law officer in the country. The AG is appointed by the President and holds office during the pleasure of the President. Must be qualified to be a SC judge.

**Trap:** A (Article 72) is pardoning power; B (Article 74) is aid-and-advise; D (Article 78) is PM's duties. AG is Article 76.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#Attorney General|Attorney General]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=8
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** The 42nd Constitutional Amendment made the advice of the Council of Ministers *binding* on the President of India.
**Reason (R):** Before the 42nd Amendment, the President could refuse to act on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers.

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The 42nd CAA, 1976 added "and shall act in accordance with such advice" to Article 74(1), making the advice of the Council of Ministers binding on the President. The 44th CAA, 1978 added a small exception — the President can ask the Council to *reconsider* the advice, but after such reconsideration the President must act on it. Before the 42nd, the constitutional text said only "aid and advise," leaving ambiguity about whether the President could disregard the advice.

**Trap:** B is the partial-credit trap. R directly explains A — the very reason the 42nd added the binding language was to remove the pre-existing ambiguity.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#Article 74|Article 74]]
</details>

@qpol.pm_and_emergency.q12 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=8
The 103rd Constitutional Amendment, 2019, provided for what reservation?

- A) Reservation of seats for women in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies
- B) 10% reservation in educational institutions and government jobs for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS)
- C) Constitutional status to the National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)
- D) Restoration of state power to identify Socially and Educationally Backward Classes
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The 103rd CAA, 2019 (also called the EWS Reservation Act) amended Article 15(6) and Article 16(6) to provide 10% reservation in educational institutions and government jobs for EWS who are not covered by existing SC/ST/OBC reservations.

**Trap:** A is the 106th CAA, 2023 (Nari Shakti). C is the 102nd CAA, 2018 (NCBC constitutional status). D is the 105th CAA, 2021 (SEBC restoration). All recent amendments, easy to confuse.

**See also:** [[../../polity/08 - PM, Council of Ministers, Emergency, and Constitutional Amendment#Recent Constitutional Amendments (CA)|Recent Amendments]]
</details>
