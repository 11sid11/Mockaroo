# Important Acts — Questions

@qpol.important_acts.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=14
The Indian Councils Act, 1909 is also known as:

- A) Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms
- B) Morley-Minto Reforms
- C) Charter Act
- D) Government of India Act, 1935
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Indian Councils Act, 1909 is called the **Morley-Minto Reforms** — after Lord Morley (Secretary of State for India) and Lord Minto (Viceroy of India). It introduced separate electorates for Muslims, expanding the central legislative council from 16 to 60 members. Lord Minto is called the "Father of Communal Electorate."

**Trap:** A (Montagu-Chelmsford) is the 1919 Act; D (GOI Act 1935) is the 1935 Act. Both are sister reforms; students confuse them.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Indian Councils Act, 1909|1909 Act]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=14
The Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms) introduced which of the following for the first time in India?

- A) Separate electorates for Muslims
- B) Dyarchy in the provinces
- C) A federal structure for India
- D) Bicameralism at the Centre
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The 1919 Act introduced **Dyarchy (dual governance) in the provinces**: subjects were divided into "Transferred" (governed by the Governor with ministers responsible to the legislature) and "Reserved" (governed by the Governor and his executive council, not responsible to the legislature). It also introduced bicameralism at the Centre (Council of State + Legislative Assembly).

**Trap:** D (bicameralism at the Centre) is half-right — the 1919 Act did introduce bicameralism, but *dyarchy* is the more famous innovation. A (separate electorates) is from the 1909 Act.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Government of India Act, 1919|1919 Act]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=14
The Government of India Act, 1935 provided for the establishment of:

- A) Supreme Court of India
- B) Federal Court in 1937 and Reserve Bank of India
- C) Election Commission
- D) Planning Commission
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The GOI Act, 1935 established the Federal Court (1937) and the Reserve Bank of India. It also divided powers into three lists (Federal, Provincial, Concurrent), provided for dyarchy at the Centre, abolished dyarchy in the provinces, and extended adult franchise (about 14% of the population).

**Trap:** A (Supreme Court) was established by the Constitution of India in 1950, replacing the Federal Court. C (Election Commission) was also established by the Constitution in 1950.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Government of India Act, 1935|1935 Act]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q4 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=14
Which Act abolished the office of the Secretary of State for India and transferred his functions to the Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs?

- A) Indian Councils Act, 1909
- B) Government of India Act, 1919
- C) Government of India Act, 1935
- D) Indian Independence Act, 1947
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The Indian Independence Act, 1947 abolished the office of the Secretary of State for India, transferred his functions to the Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs, abolished the office of Viceroy (replaced by Governor General), declared two independent dominions (India and Pakistan), and dropped the title of "Emperor of India" from the British Crown.

**Trap:** C (GOI Act, 1935) is the most famous "reforms" act but did not abolish the Secretary of State.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Indian Independence Act, 1947|Independence Act]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q5 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=14
Who was the first and last Indian Governor General of independent India?

- A) Lord Mountbatten
- B) Jawaharlal Nehru
- C) C. Rajagopalachari
- D) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**C. Rajagopalachari** was the first and last *Indian* Governor General of independent India (1948–50), succeeding Lord Mountbatten. He was the last Governor General before the office was replaced by the President of India (Rajendra Prasad).

**Trap:** A (Lord Mountbatten) was the first Governor General of independent India, but he was British, not Indian. D (Rajendra Prasad) was the first President, not the last Governor General.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#1st and last Indian Governor General|Rajagopalachari]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q6 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=14
Consider the following statements about the Government of India Act, 1935:
1. It introduced dyarchy at the Centre.
2. It abolished dyarchy in the provinces.
3. It provided for an All-India Federation including British provinces and princely states.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 1 and 3 only
- C) 2 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. The 1935 Act:

- Introduced dyarchy at the Centre (provincial dyarchy from the 1919 Act was abolished).
- Provided for an All-India Federation of British provinces and princely states (though the federation never actually came into being because princely states did not accede).

**Trap:** Statement 2 is sometimes missed — students remember "dyarchy" but not that the 1935 Act both abolished it (in provinces) and reintroduced it (at the Centre). Net result: dyarchy at the Centre.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Government of India Act, 1935|1935 Act Features]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=14
The Indian Independence Act, 1947 is also known as:

- A) Morley-Minto Reforms
- B) Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms
- C) Mountbatten Plan / June 3rd Plan / Partition Plan
- D) Cabinet Mission Plan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Indian Independence Act, 1947 is also called the **Mountbatten Plan**, the **June 3rd Plan**, or the **Partition Plan** — after Lord Mountbatten, the last Viceroy, who announced the partition plan on 3 June 1947. The Act itself was passed by the British Parliament on 18 July 1947.

**Trap:** D (Cabinet Mission Plan) is the trap — the Cabinet Mission (1946) was an earlier plan to keep India united; it was rejected by the Muslim League, leading eventually to the Mountbatten Plan.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Indian Independence Act, 1947|Independence Act Names]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=14
Who was the first Indian to join the Viceroy's Executive Council (under the Indian Councils Act, 1909)?

- A) C. Rajagopalachari
- B) Mahatma Gandhi
- C) Satyendra Prasad Sinha
- D) Jawaharlal Nehru
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Satyendra Prasad Sinha** (also called Satyendra Sinha) was the first Indian to join the Viceroy's Executive Council, appointed as the Law Member in 1909. He later became Baron Sinha of Raipur.

**Trap:** A (Rajagopalachari) was the last Governor General; B (Gandhi) was never in the Viceroy's Executive Council. D (Nehru) became a member only after the 1919 reforms broadened representation.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Indian Councils Act, 1909|First Indian]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q9 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=14
The Indian Councils Act, 1892 introduced which feature for the first time?

- A) Indirect elections
- B) Direct elections
- C) Separate electorates
- D) Adult suffrage
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Indian Councils Act, 1892 introduced **indirect elections** — members of legislative councils were now chosen in part by electoral bodies (e.g., municipal committees, district boards, chambers of commerce), rather than being directly nominated. It also permitted discussions on financial matters and questions to the executive.

**Trap:** B (Direct elections) was first introduced by the Government of India Act, 1919. C (Separate electorates) by the 1909 Act. D (Adult suffrage) by the 1935 Act (extended to 14%).

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Indian Councils Act, 1892|1892 Act]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q10 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=14
The Indian Independence Act, 1947 declared independence for India on:

- A) 15 August 1947
- B) 26 January 1950
- C) 26 November 1949
- D) 1 April 1947
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Indian Independence Act, 1947 declared India (and Pakistan) independent on **15 August 1947**. The Act itself was passed by the British Parliament on 18 July 1947, but the operative date for independence was 15 August. At the stroke of midnight of 14–15 August 1947, the British rule ended.

**Trap:** B (26 January 1950) is the commencement of the Constitution. C (26 November 1949) is the adoption of the Constitution. D (1 April) is the typical date for new Acts/Regulations.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Indian Independence Act, 1947|Independence Day]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=14
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** Lord Minto is called the "Father of Communal Electorate" in India.
**Reason (R):** The Indian Councils Act, 1909 introduced separate electorates for Muslims.

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms) introduced the **separate electorate system** for Muslims — Muslims would vote only for Muslim candidates in reserved constituencies. Lord Minto, as Viceroy, was the architect of this and is called the "Father of Communal Electorate" because of this specific contribution.

**Trap:** B is the partial-credit trap. R directly explains A — the very reason Minto is called "Father of Communal Electorate" is the 1909 Act.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Indian Councils Act, 1909|Father of Communal Electorate]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q12 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=14
Which of the following Acts led to the separation of Burma (now Myanmar) from India?

- A) Indian Councils Act, 1909
- B) Government of India Act, 1919
- C) Government of India Act, 1935
- D) Indian Independence Act, 1947
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The GOI Act, 1935 separated Burma (now Myanmar) from British India — Burma was administered as a separate colony. The Indian Independence Act, 1947 later confirmed Burma's separate status as part of the British Commonwealth.

**Trap:** D (Indian Independence Act, 1947) is the trap because Burma's independence came later (1948), but the *separation* from India had already happened under the 1935 Act.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Government of India Act, 1935|Burma Separation]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q13 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=14
The Government of India Act, 1935 divided powers between the Centre and units into how many lists?

- A) Two lists (Federal and Provincial)
- B) Three lists (Federal, Provincial, Concurrent)
- C) Four lists
- D) Five lists
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The 1935 Act introduced the **three-list system**: Federal List (59 items), Provincial List (54 items), and Concurrent List (36 items). Residuary powers were given to the Viceroy. The Indian Constitution adopted this three-list structure in its Seventh Schedule.

**Trap:** A (Two lists) was the structure of earlier Acts; D (five lists) is wrong — five-list is sometimes mistakenly applied to the Constitution but it has only three lists plus the residuary.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Government of India Act, 1935|Three Lists]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q14 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=14
Which Act established the Public Service Commission at the Centre?

- A) Indian Councils Act, 1892
- B) Indian Councils Act, 1909
- C) Government of India Act, 1919
- D) Government of India Act, 1935
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The GOI Act, 1919 provided for the establishment of a Public Service Commission, and the Central Public Service Commission (CPSC) was set up in 1926 for recruiting civil servants. The 1935 Act later established Federal, Provincial, and Joint Public Service Commissions.

**Trap:** D (1935 Act) is the trap — students confuse the *general* Public Service Commission (1919) with the *Federal/Provincial/Joint* PSCs (1935). Both are correct historically, but the original institution traces to the 1919 Act.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Government of India Act, 1919|PSC Origin]]
</details>

@qpol.important_acts.q15 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=14
Three landmark Acts shaped the British-era constitutional evolution of India — the Indian Councils Act 1909, the Government of India Act 1919, and the Government of India Act 1935. Consider the following statements about their year and key innovation:
1. The Indian Councils Act 1909 introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
2. The Government of India Act 1919 introduced dyarchy in the provinces.
3. The Government of India Act 1935 introduced the three-list structure and the Federal Court.

Which of the above statements is/are INCORRECT?

- A) 1 only
- B) 2 only
- C) 1 and 2 only
- D) None — all three statements are correct
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct, so none is incorrect. Therefore the answer is **D (None — all three are correct)**.

- Statement 1 ✓ — Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms) introduced separate electorates for Muslims; Lord Minto is called the "Father of Communal Electorate" in India.
- Statement 2 ✓ — GOI Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms) introduced dyarchy in the provinces (transferred and reserved subjects) and bicameralism at the Centre.
- Statement 3 ✓ — GOI Act 1935 introduced the three-list structure (Federal, Provincial, Concurrent) — adopted by the Indian Constitution as the Seventh Schedule — and provided for the Federal Court (1937) and the RBI.

**Trap:** The framing "which is INCORRECT" tempts students to find an error. But all three statements are textbook-verified. Students who second-guess themselves and pick one of A/B/C will get this wrong. The lesson: trust the verified facts.

**See also:** [[../../polity/14 - Important Acts#Important Acts Timeline|Important Acts]]
</details>
