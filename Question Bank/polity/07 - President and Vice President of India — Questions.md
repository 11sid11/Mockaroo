# President and Vice President of India — Questions

@qpol.president.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=7
The President of India is elected by:

- A) Direct popular vote
- B) An electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (including Delhi and Puducherry)
- C) Members of both Houses of Parliament only
- D) The Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The President is elected by an electoral college consisting of the *elected* members of both Houses of Parliament and the *elected* members of the State Legislative Assemblies of all States (and the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry). Nominated members of Parliament and members of State Legislative Councils do **not** participate.

**Trap:** C (members of both Houses of Parliament only) is the dominant wrong answer — students forget the State Assembly members. India does not have direct election for the President (A is wrong).

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#PRESIDENT OF INDIA|Electoral College]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=7
What is the method of election of the President of India?

- A) First-past-the-post
- B) Proportional Representation with Single Transferable Vote (STV), secret ballot
- C) Open ballot
- D) Proportional Representation with simple majority
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The President is elected by **Proportional Representation with Single Transferable Vote** (STV) in a **secret ballot**. This is also the method used for Vice President and Rajya Sabha elections. The system ensures the winner has a majority of preferences.

**Trap:** D is the trap — it correctly says "Proportional Representation" but wrongly says "simple majority." STV specifically requires a majority of *preferences*, not just a simple majority.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#Article 54|Article 54]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=7
Which of the following is the minimum age required to be eligible for election as President of India?

- A) 25 years
- B) 30 years
- C) 35 years
- D) 40 years
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

A candidate for the Presidency must be at least **35 years old**, a citizen of India, and qualified to be a member of the Lok Sabha. (The same 35-year minimum applies to the Vice President and the Governor.)

**Trap:** B (30 years) is the lower threshold for Rajya Sabha membership and is a common distractor.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#Article 58|Article 58]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q4 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=7
Match the type of veto with the correct description:

| Veto | Description |
|---|---|
| (i) Absolute Veto | (a) President withholds assent indefinitely without acting |
| (ii) Suspensive Veto | (b) President rejects the bill outright |
| (iii) Pocket Veto | (c) President returns the bill for reconsideration; Parliament can repass |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-b, ii-c, iii-a
- C) i-c, ii-a, iii-b
- D) i-b, ii-a, iii-c
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- Absolute Veto — rejects the bill outright.
- Suspensive Veto — returns for reconsideration; Parliament can repass and the President must then assent.
- Pocket Veto — President sits on the bill and does nothing for an indefinite period (no time limit in the Indian Constitution).

**Trap:** A swaps (i) and (ii) — many students confuse "absolute" with "pocket" because both involve the President withholding assent. The difference: absolute veto is a *decision* (rejected); pocket veto is *inaction*.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#Veto Powers|Veto Powers]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q5 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=7
The President of India holds office for a term of five years. Which article provides for the procedure of impeachment of the President?

- A) Article 61
- B) Article 65
- C) Article 72
- D) Article 74
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Article 61 provides for impeachment. The charge can be initiated by either House of Parliament (notice signed by ¼ of total members), and the charge must be investigated by the other House. A two-thirds majority of total membership of that House is required to sustain the charge. No President has ever been impeached.

**Trap:** B (Article 65) is the "Acting President" article — it lets the Vice President act when the President is unavailable. Many students confuse it with impeachment.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#Article 61|Article 61]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q6 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=7
The Vice President of India is the *ex-officio* Chairman of which House?

- A) Lok Sabha
- B) Rajya Sabha
- C) Both Houses
- D) Neither House (he is only Acting President when needed)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 64 makes the Vice President the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States). The Speaker is the presiding officer of the Lok Sabha. The Vice President is also the second-highest constitutional office in India.

**Trap:** A (Lok Sabha) is the dominant wrong answer — students confuse Chairman (RS) with Speaker (LS). They are different posts and different occupants.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#Article 64|Article 64]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=7
Identify the type of executive power the President can exercise which is NOT available to a State Governor:

- A) Pardoning a person convicted of an offence under IPC
- B) Remission of a sentence
- C) Pardoning a death sentence and court-martial sentences
- D) Reprieve of a sentence
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Under Article 72, the President can pardon, commute, remit, respite, or reprieve sentences, **including death sentences and court-martial sentences**. The Governor (Article 161) can do all of these *except* pardon a death sentence or a court-martial sentence. So the differentiating power is C.

**Trap:** A and B are common powers shared with the Governor — students who half-remember the difference pick a partial-credit option.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#Q. What is the difference between the pardoning power of President and Governor?|Pardoning Power]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=7
Who administers the oath of office to the President of India?

- A) The Vice President
- B) The Speaker of Lok Sabha
- C) The Chief Justice of India
- D) The Prime Minister
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The oath of office to the President of India is administered by the **Chief Justice of India** (Article 60). If the CJI is unavailable, the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court can administer it.

**Trap:** A (Vice President) administers the oath to the *Governor* of a state — common confusion. The PM (D) administers the oath to the Council of Ministers.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#Article 60|Article 60]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q9 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=7
Which President of India was the only one elected unopposed?

- A) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
- B) Dr. S. Radhakrishnan
- C) Neelam Sanjiva Reddy
- D) K. R. Narayanan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Neelam Sanjiva Reddy (1977–82) was the only President to be elected unopposed. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the longest-serving (1950–62). K. R. Narayanan was the first Dalit President (1997–2002).

**Trap:** A (Rajendra Prasad) is the dominant wrong answer because he was the first and longest-serving President — students assume "first" = "unopposed."

@qpol.president.q10 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=7
In which case did the Supreme Court hold that the President's pardoning power (Article 72) is not subject to judicial review, unless exercised arbitrarily or mala fide?

- A) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
- B) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967)
- C) Kehar Singh v. Union of India (1988)
- D) S. R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

In *Kehar Singh v. Union of India* (1988), arising from the Indira Gandhi assassination case, the Supreme Court held that the President's pardoning power is not justiciable *except* when it is arbitrary, mala fide, irrational, or discriminatory — in which case judicial review is available.

**Trap:** A (Kesavananda Bharati) is the famous Basic Structure Doctrine case — students confuse landmark SC judgments.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#Kehar Singh vs Union of India Case (1988)|Kehar Singh Case]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=7
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** The President of India is described as the *de jure* (nominal) head of the Indian state, while the Prime Minister is the *de facto* (real) head.
**Reason (R):** The President acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers and is bound by such advice (subject to limited exceptions).

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The President is *de jure* (in law) the head of state but acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the PM. The PM and CoM are *de facto* (in practice) wielding executive power. The 42nd Amendment (1976) made the advice binding on the President (Article 74); the 44th (1978) restored a limited reconsideration power but ultimately the President acts on advice.

**Trap:** B is the partial-credit trap. R *does* directly explain A — the President's nominal role exists precisely because he acts on advice.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#Article 74|Article 74]]
</details>

@qpol.president.q12 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=7
The Vice President can act as President for a maximum period of how many months, in case of a vacancy?

- A) 3 months
- B) 6 months
- C) 1 year
- D) Until the next general election
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 65 says the Vice President can act as President (or discharge the President's functions) for a maximum period of **6 months** during a vacancy in the Presidential office. A new President must be elected within that period.

**Trap:** D (until the next general election) is the trap — students assume "until a new President is elected" without the 6-month cap. The cap exists specifically to prevent prolonged acting-Presidents.

**See also:** [[../../polity/07 - President and Vice President of India#VP can act as President|Acting President]]
</details>
