# State Legislature — Questions

> Source: [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature|State Legislature]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - polity]]

A 12-question MCQ set on Part VI of the Constitution — the Governor (Articles 153–167), the State Legislature (bicameral in 6 states), Chief Minister and Council of Ministers, Advocate General, Money Bill in the state, and the Governor's Ordinance power (Article 213). Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

Scoring: SSC CGL rules — **+2 correct, -0.50 wrong, 0 unattempted**.

@qpol.state_legislature.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=10
The Governor of a state is appointed by:

- A) Direct popular vote
- B) The President of India
- C) The Chief Minister
- D) The Chief Justice of the High Court
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 155 — the Governor is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal, and holds office during the pleasure of the President (Article 156). The 7th Constitutional Amendment allows the same person to be Governor of two or more states.

**Trap:** C (Chief Minister) is the dominant wrong answer — students confuse the appointment (by the President) with the recommendation (the CM often has informal influence in choosing the Governor).

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 155|Article 155]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=10
Match the Central executive counterpart to its State equivalent:

| Central | State |
|---|---|
| (i) President | (a) Chief Minister |
| (ii) Prime Minister | (b) Governor |
| (iii) Attorney General of India | (c) Advocate General |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-b, ii-a, iii-c
- C) i-b, ii-c, iii-a
- D) i-c, ii-a, iii-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- President → Governor (head of state, ceremonial).
- Prime Minister → Chief Minister (head of government, executive).
- Attorney General of India → Advocate General (highest law officer at state level).

**Trap:** A is the inversion — students swap (i) and (ii). Remember: the President is a *head of state*, the PM is a *head of government*; the same distinction holds at state level.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Key Replacements|Replacements]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=10
The oath of office to the Governor of a state is administered by:

- A) The President of India
- B) The Chief Justice of the High Court
- C) The Chief Minister
- D) The Speaker of the State Legislative Assembly
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 159 — the oath to the Governor is administered by the **Chief Justice of the High Court**, and in the Chief Justice's absence, by the senior-most judge of that High Court. (At the Centre, the CJI administers the oath to the President.)

**Trap:** A (President) is the dominant wrong answer — students assume the senior person administers all oaths. The President appoints the Governor but does not swear him in.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 159|Article 159]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q4 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=10
Which of the following Indian states has a bicameral legislature?

- A) Punjab
- B) Maharashtra
- C) Rajasthan
- D) West Bengal
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Currently six states have a Legislative Council (the second chamber): Karnataka, Maharashtra, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Uttar Pradesh. The other four states listed — Punjab, Rajasthan, West Bengal — have only a Legislative Assembly. Article 168(1) lists the states which can have a Legislative Council.

**Trap:** A (Punjab) and C (Rajasthan) are popular states but unicameral. D (West Bengal) is the classic SSC trap because it was one of the original states with a Legislative Council — but the Council was *abolished* in 1969.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 168|Article 168]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q5 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=10
Which article empowers the Governor to promulgate an Ordinance when the State Legislature is not in session?

- A) Article 123
- B) Article 213
- C) Article 356
- D) Article 200
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 213 — the Governor's Ordinance-making power is the state counterpart of Article 123 (President's Ordinance). It has the same force as a state Act, must be laid before the Legislature, and lapses within 6 weeks of reassembly.

**Trap:** A (Article 123) is the President's Ordinance power; C (Article 356) is President's Rule. Students who know only one Ordinance article default to Article 123.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 213|Article 213]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q6 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=10
Consider the following statements about the pardoning power of the Governor (Article 161):
1. The Governor can pardon a death sentence.
2. The Governor can pardon sentences by a court-martial.
3. The Governor can pardon, remit, commute, respite and reprieve sentences other than death and court-martial.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 only
- B) 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 2 and 3 only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Only Statement 3 is correct. The Governor's pardoning power under Article 161 is *wider* than the President's on most counts but *narrower* on death sentences and court-martial sentences — the Governor cannot pardon those. The President (Article 72) can.

**Trap:** Statements 1 and 2 are the dual traps — students assume "more heads of state = more power." For clemency, the opposite is true at the state level.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 161|Article 161]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=10
In a state with a bicameral legislature (Legislative Assembly and Legislative Council), a Money Bill can be introduced in:

- A) Either House
- B) Only the Legislative Assembly
- C) Only the Legislative Council
- D) Joint sitting of both Houses
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 198 — a Money Bill in a state can be introduced only in the **Legislative Assembly**. The Legislative Council can only make recommendations within 14 days; if it fails to act, the Bill is deemed passed by both Houses.

**Trap:** A (either House) is the dominant wrong answer — the same trap as the Parliament Money Bill question. Money Bill procedure is consistently an LA-only prerogative at both central and state levels.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 198|Article 198]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=10
The Advocate General of a state is appointed by:

- A) The President
- B) The Governor
- C) The Chief Minister
- D) The Chief Justice of the High Court
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 165 — the Advocate General is appointed by the **Governor** and holds office during the pleasure of the Governor. The AG must be qualified to be a High Court judge.

**Trap:** A (President) is the dominant wrong answer — students remember that the *Attorney General of India* is appointed by the President and assume the same for the state AG.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 165|Article 165]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q9 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=10
The Council of Ministers in a state is collectively responsible to:

- A) The Governor
- B) The Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)
- C) The Legislative Council
- D) Both Houses jointly
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 164(2) — the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) of the state. This mirrors the Lok Sabha's relationship with the Union Council of Ministers.

**Trap:** A (Governor) is the trap — students remember the Governor appoints the CM and assume the CoM is "responsible" to him. But the CoM is responsible to the elected House.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 164|Article 164]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q10 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=10
What is the normal term of office of the Governor of a state?

- A) 4 years
- B) 5 years
- C) 6 years
- D) During the pleasure of the President
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 156 — the Governor holds office for a term of **5 years** from the date of assuming office, but he holds office during the pleasure of the President. So in practice, the President can remove a Governor at any time.

**Trap:** D (during the pleasure of the President) is technically also true but not the *fixed term* answer — the question asks for the *normal term*, which is 5 years.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 156|Article 156]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=10
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** A bill passed by the state Legislature can be reserved by the Governor for the consideration of the President.
**Reason (R):** Article 201 empowers the Governor to reserve certain bills for the President's consideration, particularly those affecting the Centre-State relationship.

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Article 201 allows the Governor to reserve certain bills for the President's consideration (and assent). This includes bills that would affect the powers of the High Court, encroach on the Union List, or compromise the position of the State in the federal structure. The President can then give assent, withhold, or direct the State to reconsider.

**Trap:** B is the partial-credit trap. R directly explains A — the reservation power exists precisely because of the federal concerns R mentions.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 201|Article 201]]
</details>

@qpol.state_legislature.q12 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=10
The Legislative Council of a state can delay an Ordinary Bill (not Money Bill) for a maximum period of:

- A) 1 month
- B) 3 months
- C) 4 months (3 months in first instance + 1 month on second transmission)
- D) 6 months
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Article 197 — the Legislative Council can hold an Ordinary Bill for a maximum of 4 months (3 months on first transmission + 1 month on second transmission). Beyond that, the Legislative Assembly can pass the Bill without the Council's concurrence. (Money Bills have only 14 days.)

**Trap:** D (6 months) is the dominant wrong answer — students apply the Ordinance's "6 months + 6 weeks" rule to the Council delay power. The Council's max delay is just 4 months.

**See also:** [[../../polity/10 - State Legislature#Article 197|Article 197]]
</details>
