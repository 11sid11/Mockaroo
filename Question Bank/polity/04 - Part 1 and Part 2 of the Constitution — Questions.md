# Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution — Questions

> Source: [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution|Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - polity]]

A 12-question MCQ set on Articles 1–11 — India's identity as a "Union of States" (not a federation), admission of new states under Article 2, reorganisation under Article 3, the Berubari precedent, and citizenship acquisition/loss under the Citizenship Act, 1955 (including CAA 2019). Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

Scoring: SSC CGL rules — **+2 correct, -0.50 wrong, 0 unattempted**.

---

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=4
Article 1 of the Indian Constitution describes India as:

- A) A Federation of States
- B) A Union of States
- C) A Confederation of States
- D) A Commonwealth of States
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 1(1) says "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States." India is not a federation because the states have no right to secede from the Union. The US is described as a "Federation of indestructible states," whereas India is an "Indestructible Union of destructible states."

**Trap:** A is the dominant wrong answer because "federation" is the more familiar term. The choice of "Union" over "Federation" was deliberate to emphasise that the Indian Union is not the result of a treaty.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#PART I - UNION AND IT'S TERRITORY [Article 1-4]|Part I]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=4
If Parliament wishes to form a new state by separating territory from an existing state, which article gives it the power to do so?

- A) Article 2
- B) Article 3
- C) Article 4
- D) Article 368
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Article 3 empowers Parliament to form a new state by separation of territory from any state, by uniting two or more states, by uniting any territory to a part of any state, by increasing or diminishing any state's area, by altering boundaries, or by altering names. Article 2 deals with admission of *foreign* territory or establishment of new states outside existing territory; Article 4 merely says such laws under Articles 2 and 3 do not require Article 368 amendment procedure.

**Trap:** A (Article 2) is the trap because students confuse "admitting a new state" with "forming a new state from existing Indian territory." Article 2 is for external addition; Article 3 is for internal reorganisation.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#Article 3|Article 3]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=4
The provisions for citizenship of India at the commencement of the Constitution (26 January 1950) are contained in which Articles?

- A) Articles 1–4
- B) Articles 5–11
- C) Articles 12–35
- D) Articles 36–51
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Part II of the Constitution (Articles 5–11) deals with citizenship. Article 5 defines who was a citizen on 26 January 1950; Articles 6–8 deal with migrants and persons of Indian origin abroad; Article 9 strips citizenship from those who voluntarily acquired a foreign nationality; Article 10 ensures continuity of citizenship; Article 11 empowers Parliament to regulate citizenship by law (which it did via the Citizenship Act, 1955).

**Trap:** C (Articles 12–35) covers Fundamental Rights and is a frequent SSC trap.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#PART II - CITIZENSHIP [Article 5-11]|Part II Citizenship]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q4 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=4
Match the mode of acquiring Indian citizenship with its key requirement under the Citizenship Act, 1955:

| Mode | Key requirement |
|---|---|
| (i) By Birth | (a) 12 years of residence (or 11 of preceding 14 + 1 immediate) |
| (ii) By Naturalisation | (b) Must be of Indian origin currently living abroad |
| (iii) By Descent | (c) Born in India |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-c, ii-a, iii-b
- C) i-b, ii-c, iii-a
- D) i-c, ii-b, iii-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- By Birth — born in India.
- By Naturalisation — 12 years residence rule (relaxed to 6 years for certain communities by CAA 2019) plus knowledge of an Indian language in the 8th Schedule.
- By Descent — a person of Indian origin currently living abroad.

**Trap:** A is a tempting mis-pair — students confuse "By Descent" (for NRIs of Indian origin) with "By Naturalisation" (long residence rule). Remember: Descent = blood/lineage, Naturalisation = years lived in India.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#Citizenship Act 1955|Citizenship Act 1955]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q5 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=4
Which article of the Constitution provides that if a person voluntarily acquires the citizenship of a foreign country, they cease to be an Indian citizen?

- A) Article 5
- B) Article 7
- C) Article 9
- D) Article 11
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Article 9 says a person who voluntarily acquires citizenship of any foreign State shall not be an Indian citizen. Article 11 lets Parliament frame the detailed law on citizenship (which it did via the Citizenship Act, 1955).

**Trap:** D (Article 11) is the trap because students remember "Parliament makes citizenship laws" and jump to Article 11. But Article 11 is enabling; Article 9 is the operative clause on loss of citizenship.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#Article 9|Article 9]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q6 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=4
Consider the following Assertion–Reason pair:

**Assertion (A):** Article 2 deals with the admission of new states formed out of existing Indian territory.
**Reason (R):** Article 3 deals with the admission or establishment of new states *outside* the existing territory of India.

- A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are false
- C) A is false but R is true
- D) A is true but R is false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Both statements are false — but with their roles *reversed*.

- In reality, Article 2 deals with admission of new states *outside* the existing territory (i.e., foreign territory), while Article 3 deals with reorganisation *inside* the existing Indian territory (forming new states, altering boundaries, names, areas of existing states).

So A and R have their subjects swapped, which is why both end up false.

**Trap:** The assertion-reason framing makes it look like "both true" — students pick A without checking which article does what.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#Article 2|Article 2 vs Article 3]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=4
Under the Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019, persons from Hindu, Sikh, Parsi, Jain, Christian and Buddhist communities of which three countries can apply for Indian citizenship on a relaxed basis?

- A) Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka
- B) Pakistan, Bangladesh, Afghanistan
- C) Pakistan, Afghanistan, Myanmar
- D) Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri Lanka
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The CAA 2019 applies to six communities (Hindu, Sikh, Parsi, Jain, Christian, Buddhist — notably excluding Muslim) from three specific countries: Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan. The applicant must have entered India on or before 31 December 2014 and lived here for at least 6 years (down from the standard 12).

**Trap:** A includes Nepal and Sri Lanka — neither is in the CAA list. C includes Myanmar — which has a large Hindu minority in distress, but CAA does not cover Myanmar.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#Recent Amendment in Citizenship Act, 1955|CAA 2019]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=4
Which Constitutional Amendment ratified the India-Bangladesh boundary agreement and the exchange of enclaves between the two countries?

- A) 84th Amendment
- B) 91st Amendment
- C) 100th Amendment
- D) 104th Amendment
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The 100th Constitutional Amendment (2015) implemented the India-Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement. India transferred 111 enclaves to Bangladesh; Bangladesh transferred 51 enclaves to India. This required a constitutional amendment because it involved ceding Indian territory (unlike the Article 3 reorganisation, which deals with internal reorganisation).

**Trap:** D (104th) abolished the Anglo-Indian nomination to Lok Sabha — also a recent amendment and an easy year-confusion.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#100th CA (2015)|100th CA]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q9 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=4
The linguistic reorganisation of Indian states was based on the recommendations of which commission/committee?

- A) Dhar Commission
- B) Fazl Ali Commission (States Reorganisation Commission)
- C) Simon Commission
- D) Sarkaria Commission
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The States Reorganisation Commission (1953), headed by Justice Fazal Ali (with K. M. Panikkar and H. N. Kunzru), recommended the reorganisation of states primarily on linguistic and administrative lines. This led to the formation of Andhra Pradesh (the first linguistic state, 1953), Kerala, Karnataka and other linguistic states in 1956.

**Trap:** A (Dhar Commission) is the trap — the Dhar Commission (1948) had earlier recommended that reorganisation on linguistic lines was *not* advisable. Fazal Ali later reversed that. The two commissions are easy to confuse because both deal with state reorganisation.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#LINGUISTIC REORGANISATION|Linguistic Reorganisation]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q10 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=4
India follows the principle of "single citizenship" borrowed from which country's constitution?

- A) USA
- B) UK
- C) Canada
- D) Australia
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

India has a single citizenship — every Indian is a citizen of India only, not of any state. This is in contrast to the US, where citizens are simultaneously citizens of the USA *and* of the state they reside in. The single-citizenship feature was borrowed from the British (UK) Constitution.

**Trap:** A is the dominant wrong answer — students associate "federalism" with the US and assume India's citizenship must mirror the US model. But the borrow for citizenship specifically came from the UK.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#Single citizenship|Single citizenship]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=4
Consider the following ways of *losing* Indian citizenship under the Citizenship Act, 1955:
1. Renunciation
2. Termination
3. Deprivation

Which of the following correctly pairs each with its trigger?

- A) 1 — voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship; 2 — voluntarily giving up Indian citizenship; 3 — fraud / wartime disloyalty
- B) 1 — voluntarily giving up Indian citizenship; 2 — voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship without informing GOI; 3 — fraud / wartime disloyalty
- C) 1 — fraud / wartime disloyalty; 2 — voluntarily giving up; 3 — voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship
- D) All three are identical in trigger
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- Renunciation (1): When a citizen voluntarily gives up Indian citizenship.
- Termination (2): When a citizen voluntarily acquires citizenship of another country *without informing* the GOI.
- Deprivation (3): When citizenship is taken away by the government — for fraud, false representation, or having helped an enemy during wartime.

**Trap:** A swaps the definitions of Renunciation and Termination — easy to slip because both involve "voluntary" foreign citizenship. The distinguishing element: Termination is a *consequence* the law attaches automatically; Renunciation is a *declaration* the citizen files.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#Can lose Citizenship through|Loss of Citizenship]]
</details>

@qpol.part_1_and_part_2.q12 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=4
The integration of which princely state into India was code-named "Operation Polo"?

- A) Hyderabad
- B) Junagadh
- C) Jammu & Kashmir
- D) Travancore
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Operation Polo (September 1948) was the Indian Army's action to integrate the princely state of Hyderabad into the Indian Union, after the Nizam refused to join. Junagadh was integrated via a plebiscite (1948). J&K acceded via the Instrument of Accession in October 1947.

**Trap:** B (Junagadh) is a close trap because it is also a princely-state integration from the same era — but the operation name "Polo" specifically refers to Hyderabad.

**See also:** [[../../polity/04 - Part 1 and Part 2 of the Constitution#INTEGRATION AND FORMATION OF STATES|Integration of States]]
</details>
