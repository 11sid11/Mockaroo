# Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution — Questions

@qpol.preamble.q1 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=3
Who moved the Objective Resolution in the Constituent Assembly, which later became the basis of the Preamble?

- A) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar
- B) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
- C) Jawaharlal Nehru
- D) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objective Resolution on 13 December 1946; it was adopted on 22 January 1947. The Preamble, drafted later, is described as a modified version of this Resolution.

**Trap:** A is a tempting wrong answer because Ambedkar is the most famous constitution-maker — but he chaired the Drafting Committee, not the Resolution.

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#PREAMBLE|Preamble]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q2 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=3
Consider the following statements about the word "Socialist" in the Preamble:
1. It was a part of the original Preamble adopted in 1949.
2. It was added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 only
- B) 2 only
- C) Both 1 and 2
- D) Neither 1 nor 2
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The original 1949 Preamble described India as a "Sovereign Democratic Republic." "Socialist" and "Secular" were both inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976 (during the Emergency). Hence statement 1 is wrong and statement 2 is correct.

**Trap:** Students assume "Socialist" was always in the Preamble because India's socialist economic policies go back to the 1950s (Five-Year Plans). But the *word* "Socialist" was only added in 1976.

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#SOCIALIST|Socialist]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q3 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=3
The phrase "We, the People of India" in the Preamble signifies that the Constitution's authority is drawn from:

- A) The British Crown
- B) The Constituent Assembly only
- C) The people of India, through their representatives
- D) The President and the Parliament
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

"We, the People" establishes that the Constitution derives its authority from the people themselves, exercised through their representatives — not from a monarch or external power. This is the bedrock of popular sovereignty.

**Trap:** B sounds plausible (the Assembly did adopt the Constitution), but the Assembly was acting on behalf of the people, not on its own sovereign authority.

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#Understanding the Preamble|Understanding the Preamble]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q4 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=3
Match the following commentaries on the Preamble with their authors:

| Comment | Author |
|---|---|
| (i) "ID Card of the Constitution" | (a) K. M. Munshi |
| (ii) "Political Horoscope of the Constitution" | (b) N. A. Palkhivala |
| (iii) "Jewel set" | (c) Pt. Thakur Das |

- A) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- B) i-b, ii-a, iii-c
- C) i-c, ii-b, iii-a
- D) i-b, ii-c, iii-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- "ID Card of the Constitution" → N. A. Palkhivala
- "Political Horoscope of the Constitution" → K. M. Munshi
- "Jewel set" → Pt. Thakur Das

**Trap:** A swaps (i) and (ii) — students who half-remember Munshi's name confuse which label belongs to whom. The "Horoscope" is the predictive / political reading — that fits Munshi; the "ID Card" is the descriptive / identity reading — that fits Palkhivala.

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#Comments on Preamble|Comments on Preamble]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q5 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=3
Which of the following ideals is NOT mentioned in the Preamble to the Indian Constitution?

- A) Justice
- B) Liberty
- C) Equality
- D) Privacy
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The Preamble lists JUSTICE (social, economic, political), LIBERTY (of thought, expression, belief, faith, worship), EQUALITY (of status and opportunity), and FRATERNITY (assuring dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the Nation). "Privacy" is not in the Preamble — it was recognised as a Fundamental Right under Article 21 by the Supreme Court in *K. S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India* (2017).

**Trap:** Privacy is a buzzword in recent MCQs. Many students assume it is in the Preamble; it is not. The four ideals are J-L-E-F (Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity).

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#Objectives of the Constitution|Objectives of the Constitution]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q6 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=3
In which schedule of the Indian Constitution are the subjects of the Union List, State List and Concurrent List enumerated?

- A) Third Schedule
- B) Fifth Schedule
- C) Seventh Schedule
- D) Ninth Schedule
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Seventh Schedule contains the three legislative lists (Union, State, Concurrent). It is based on the Government of India Act, 1935. The trick "TEARS OF OLD PM" covers all 12 schedules.

**Trap:** A (Third Schedule) deals with oaths and affirmations; B (Fifth) deals with Scheduled Areas; D (Ninth) deals with land reforms — all common SSC distractors.

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#Schedule 7|Seventh Schedule]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q7 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=3
In a Union List vs State List vs Concurrent List scenario, identify the list that contains a subject on which BOTH the Centre and the State can legislate.

- A) Union List
- B) State List
- C) Concurrent List
- D) Residuary List
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Concurrent List contains subjects on which both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate (e.g., criminal law, marriage, education, electricity). In case of conflict, the Union law prevails. The Union List is exclusively for the Centre, the State List for States, and Residuary subjects go to Parliament.

**Trap:** Many students pick the Union List because Parliament is "supreme" — but on Concurrent subjects, *both* can legislate. The key word in the question is "BOTH."

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#Schedule 7|Three Lists]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q8 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=3
Which of the following subjects was transferred from the State List to the Concurrent List by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976?

- A) Defence
- B) Foreign Affairs
- C) Education
- D) Public Order
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The 42nd Amendment shifted five subjects from State to Concurrent List: Education, Forests, Weights and Measures, Protection of wild animals and birds, and Administration of justice. Defence and Foreign Affairs have always been on the Union List; Public Order is on the State List.

**Trap:** B (Foreign Affairs) is the classic trap because "important" subjects sound like they should belong to the Centre — which is true, but Foreign Affairs was *never* on the State List to begin with, so it could not be "transferred" by the 42nd Amendment.

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#Subjects Transferred through 42nd Amendment|Subjects Transferred]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=3
Consider the following statements about the "Secular" word in the Preamble:
1. It means India has no official state religion.
2. India follows a *positive* concept of secularism (state can involve itself in religious affairs).
3. It was added by the 44th Constitutional Amendment.

Which of the above statements is/are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 1 and 3 only
- C) 2 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- Statement 1 ✓ — India has no official state religion; citizens are free to follow any religion or none.
- Statement 2 ✓ — India follows a *positive* secularism (state involvement in religion to reform/equalise) — distinct from the US *negative* model (strict separation).
- Statement 3 ✗ — "Secular" was added by the **42nd** Amendment (1976), not the 44th.

**Trap:** Statement 3 is the trap — students confuse the 42nd Amendment (which added "Socialist" + "Secular" + "Integrity") with the 44th (which restored some liberties post-Emergency and made the President's advice not binding).

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#SECULAR|Secular]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q10 difficulty=apply subject=polity chapter=3
In which case did the Supreme Court declare that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution?

- A) Berubari Union Case (1960)
- B) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
- C) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967)
- D) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

In *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973), the Supreme Court ruled that the Preamble is part of the Constitution and is therefore subject to the **Basic Structure Doctrine** — Parliament cannot amend the Constitution in a way that destroys its basic structure, of which the Preamble is an integral part. In *Berubari Union* (1960), the Court had earlier held the Preamble was *not* a part of the Constitution; that position was overturned in Kesavananda.

**Trap:** A is the historical trap — the Berubari case is where the Preamble's status was originally discussed, but with the *opposite* conclusion.

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#PREAMBLE|Preamble]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q11 difficulty=recall subject=polity chapter=3
Which schedule of the Indian Constitution deals with the official languages of India, and how many languages does it contain at present?

- A) Sixth Schedule — 14 languages
- B) Seventh Schedule — 22 languages
- C) Eighth Schedule — 22 languages
- D) Ninth Schedule — 24 languages
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Eighth Schedule lists the official languages of India. Originally 14, it now contains 22 (after the 92nd Amendment, 2003 added Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santhali, and the 96th, 2011, renamed Oriya to Odia).

**Trap:** B (Seventh Schedule) is the dominant wrong answer because "7th" feels linked to "list of subjects." But languages are the 8th Schedule — the trick "TEARS OF OLD PM" uses O for Official Languages (8th).

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#Schedule 8|Eighth Schedule]]
</details>

@qpol.preamble.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=polity chapter=3
A scholar describes the Indian Constitution as "Quasi-Federal." Which scholar is most famously associated with this description?

- A) Granville Austin
- B) K. C. Wheare
- C) B. R. Ambedkar
- D) Ivor Jennings
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

K. C. Wheare described the Indian Constitution as **"Quasi-Federal"** — a federation with strong unitary features. Granville Austin called it "Cooperative Federalism"; B. R. Ambedkar described it as "both unitary and federal according to the requirements of time and circumstances"; Ivor Jennings noted that India has a federation "with a bias towards the Centre."

**Trap:** A (Granville Austin) and D (Ivor Jennings) both gave famous descriptions of the Constitution but with different labels. Students often mix them up.

**See also:** [[../../polity/03 - Preamble and Salient Features of the Constitution#Salient Features|Salient Features]]
</details>
