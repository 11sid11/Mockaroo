@qeco.demand.q1 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=2

A country can produce a maximum of 100 tonnes of wheat OR 50 tanks of military hardware with its current resources and technology (no full-employment combination in between is feasible). This description best corresponds to which shape of the Production Possibility Curve (PPC)?

- A) A straight-line (linear) PPC sloping down from left to right

- B) A concave-to-the-origin (bowed-out) PPC

- C) A PPC that is a vertical line on the wheat axis

- D) A PPC that is a horizontal line on the tank axis

@@



<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>



**Answer: A**



When resources are *perfectly substitutable* between the two goods, the opportunity cost per unit is constant, and the PPC is a straight downward-sloping line. Concave PPCs arise when resources are *not* perfectly adaptable (increasing opportunity cost). Vertical/horizontal lines describe extreme specialisation, not the situation described.



**Trap:** Many students pick (B) by default because PPCs are usually drawn concave â€” but the question explicitly states constant trade-offs, which uniquely identifies a linear PPC.



**See also:** [[../../economics/02 - Demand and Supply#Production Possibility Curve]]

</details>

@qeco.demand.q2 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=2
A household’s monthly consumption of wheat is observed over five months as the market price changes, holding income, tastes and the prices of all substitutes/complements fixed. In month 1 the price is ₹20/kg and the household buys 10 kg; in month 2 the price rises to ₹25/kg and they buy 9 kg; in month 3 the price falls back to ₹20/kg and they buy 10 kg again. Which conclusion is most consistent with the standard Law of Demand?
- A) The data refutes the Law of Demand because quantity changed with price
- B) The data is consistent with the Law of Demand: higher price leads to lower quantity demanded, and the return to 10 kg at ₹20 confirms the inverse relationship
- C) The data is consistent with the Law of Supply because quantity rose when price fell
- D) The data cannot be interpreted without knowing the household’s income
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Law of Demand (ceteris paribus) states that as price rises, quantity demanded falls, and vice versa. Here, ₹20 → 10 kg; ₹25 → 9 kg (price up, quantity down); back to ₹20 → 10 kg (price down, quantity up). This two-way movement along the demand curve is exactly what the Law of Demand predicts. The data refute Giffen/Veblen exceptions (where demand would rise with price).

**Trap:** Option (A) confuses the *direction* of movement (downward slope) with a refutation. Option (C) misreads demand as supply. Option (D) over-relies on income data; ceteris paribus already holds income constant.

**See also:** [[../../economics/02 - Demand and Supply#Law of Demand]]
</details>

@qeco.demand.q5 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=2

A market has the following features: many buyers and many sellers, products that are *similar but differentiated* (branding, packaging), some control over price, and free entry and exit. This market structure is called:

- A) Perfect competition

- B) Monopolistic competition

- C) Oligopoly

- D) Monopoly

@@



<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>



**Answer: B**



Monopolistic competition combines features of both: many sellers (like perfect competition) but with differentiated products (like monopoly) and low entry barriers. **Trap:** Option (A) is tempting because "many buyers and sellers" is also true for perfect competition â€” but the *product differentiation* and *price control* keywords uniquely identify monopolistic competition.

@qeco.demand.q7 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=2

The price of petrol rises by 10% and, as a result, the quantity demanded of two-wheeler scooters rises by 8%. The cross-price elasticity between petrol and scooters is:

- A) Negative, indicating the two goods are complements

- B) Positive, indicating the two goods are substitutes

- C) Zero, indicating the two goods are unrelated

- D) Negative, indicating the two goods are substitutes

@@



<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>



**Answer: B**



Cross-price elasticity = %Î”Qx / %Î”Py = 8% / 10% = +0.8 (positive). @qeco.demand.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=2

@qeco.demand.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=2

On a Production Possibility Curve (PPC) graph showing two goods A and B, point P1 lies *on* the curve, point P2 lies *inside* the curve, and point P3 lies *outside* the curve. Which interpretation is correct?

- A) P1 = full utilisation; P2 = unattainable; P3 = under-utilisation

- B) P1 = under-utilisation; P2 = full utilisation; P3 = full utilisation

- C) P1 = full utilisation; P2 = under-utilisation; P3 = unattainable

- D) P1 = unattainable; P2 = full utilisation; P3 = under-utilisation

@@



<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>



**Answer: C**



Points on the PPC represent *efficient* (full) utilisation of resources. Points *inside* the curve mean the economy could produce more of at least one good â€” resources are unemployed or under-utilised. Points *outside* the curve cannot be reached with current resources/technology.



**Trap:** Students mix up "inside" and "outside" because both feel "off the curve" â€” but inside = achievable-but-wasteful, outside = unattainable. Remember: outside is *beyond* your capacity.

@qeco.demand.q11 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=2
A household receives a one-time bonus of ₹1,00,000 and decides to spend ₹75,000 of it and save the remaining ₹25,000. Assuming this is the only change to their income, what are the Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC) and Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS) for this windfall?
- A) MPC = 0.75; MPS = 0.25
- B) MPC = 0.25; MPS = 0.75
- C) MPC = 1.00; MPS = 0.00
- D) MPC = 0.50; MPS = 0.50
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

MPC = ΔC / ΔY = 75,000 / 1,00,000 = **0.75**. MPS = ΔS / ΔY = 25,000 / 1,00,000 = **0.25**. Note that MPC + MPS = 1, by definition (every extra rupee is either consumed or saved). The investment multiplier k = 1 / (1 − MPC) = 1 / 0.25 = 4.

**Trap:** Option (B) swaps MPC and MPS — students who remember “marginal = small change” sometimes confuse which way the saving fraction goes. Option (D) splits 50/50 which would only hold if the household spent exactly half; Option (C) would mean all of the windfall is consumed.

**See also:** [[../../economics/02 - Demand and Supply#Marginal Propensity to Consume]]
</details>
