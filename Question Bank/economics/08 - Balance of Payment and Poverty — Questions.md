@qeco.bop.q1 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=8
Which of the following pairs of poverty estimation committee and its year is correctly matched?
- A) Lakdawala Committee — 2009
- B) Tendulkar Committee — 1993
- C) Rangarajan Committee — 2014
- D) Alagh Committee — 1993
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Rangarajan Committee submitted its report in 2014 and recomputed the poverty line using calorific and protein norms alongside a broader consumption basket. Lakdawala was 1993, Tendulkar was 2009, and Alagh was 1979.

**Trap:** Lakdawala vs Tendulkar years are frequently swapped (1993 ↔ 2009) in SSC traps; Alagh (1979) is also commonly confused with Dandekar & Rath (1971).

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Post-Independence Estimation]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q2 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=8
Who was the first to estimate national income in India and proposed a poverty line ranging from ₹16 to ₹35 per year in his book *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*?
- A) M. Visvesvaraya
- B) Dadabhai Naoroji
- C) R.C. Dutt
- D) V.K.R.V. Rao
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Dadabhai Naoroji, the "Grand Old Man of India," authored *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (1871) and computed the first national income estimate of India, framing the famous "Drain of Wealth" theory.

**Trap:** V.K.R.V. Rao also did early national income estimates (in the 1920s–30s), but Dadabhai came first; M. Visvesvaraya is associated with post-independence planning, not poverty estimation.

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Pre-Independence Estimation]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q3 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=8
Balance of Payments (BoP) is best defined as:
- A) A record of only export–import transactions between countries
- B) A systematic record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world during a given period
- C) The difference between a government's revenue and its expenditure
- D) The total foreign exchange reserves held by a country's central bank
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

BoP is a double-entry bookkeeping record of *all* economic transactions (visible trade, invisibles, capital flows, transfers) between a country's residents and the rest of the world, over a period (usually a year).

**Trap:** Option A is the most common trap — students equate BoP with just the trade account, ignoring invisibles and capital flows. Option C confuses BoP with the fiscal deficit; option D confuses it with FOREX reserves.

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Balance of Payment (BOP)]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q4 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=8
Which of the following transactions is recorded in the **Capital Account** of the Balance of Payments and NOT in the Current Account?
- A) Export of software services to the United States
- B) Remittances sent by Indian workers in Dubai to their families
- C) A Japanese automobile company setting up a manufacturing plant in Chennai (FDI)
- D) Import of crude oil from Russia
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) by the Japanese automaker is recorded in the Capital Account because it represents a long-term capital inflow creating a lasting interest/controlling stake. Software exports, remittances, and crude oil imports are all current account transactions (exports of services, transfers, and visible imports respectively).

**Trap:** FPI (Foreign Portfolio Investment) is *also* a capital account item — students often confuse "investment" with "current" because it involves money flow; remember: only production/trade/transfer income = current account; ownership-style flows = capital account.

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Capital Account]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q5 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=8
A country's annual transactions are: Trade balance = –$80 bn (trade deficit), Net invisibles = +$60 bn (services, IT exports, remittances), Net unilateral transfers = +$10 bn. What is the Current Account Balance?
- A) –$10 billion (Current Account Deficit)
- B) +$10 billion (Current Account Surplus)
- C) –$30 billion
- D) –$80 billion
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Current Account Balance = Trade balance + Net invisibles + Net unilateral transfers
= (–80) + 60 + 10 = **–$10 billion** (Current Account Deficit).

The trade deficit is partially offset by surplus invisibles and transfers, but a small deficit remains — a typical India-style scenario where software services and remittances close most, but not all, of the trade gap.

**Trap:** Many students add only Trade + invisibles and forget transfers; or they ignore the sign of the trade deficit. D) –$80 is just the trade balance, not the CAD.

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Current Account Deficit]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q6 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=8
Which of the following statements best distinguishes Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) from Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)?
- A) FPI involves acquiring substantial management control; FDI is purely financial
- B) FDI is more stable, long-term and typically gives management control; FPI is short-term and liquid
- C) Both are identical in nature and are recorded identically in BoP
- D) FDI flows only into the capital account, while FPI flows into the current account
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

FDI is a long-term investment that creates lasting interest and often management/operational control in a foreign enterprise (typically ≥10% equity). FPI is passive, short-term, and liquid (foreign holdings of stocks, bonds, debt instruments) without management say.

**Trap:** A) reverses the true relationship. D) is wrong — FPI is *also* a capital account item, not current account. C) is wrong because the BoP treatment differs (FDI tends to be more stable and favoured by governments).

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Capital Account]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q7 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=8
A country has a perfectly equal income distribution. Its Lorenz Curve will lie:
- A) Far below the line of equality, indicating high inequality
- B) Exactly along the 45° line of equality, and its Gini coefficient will be 0
- C) Above the line of equality
- D) As an inverted U-shape
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

When income distribution is perfectly equal, the Lorenz Curve coincides with the line of equality (45°). The Gini coefficient, measured as the area between the line of equality and the Lorenz curve divided by the entire triangle, is 0 in that case (it approaches 1 in perfect inequality).

**Trap:** C) is impossible — the Lorenz curve cannot lie *above* the line of equality because that would imply the bottom X% earns *more* than its share, contradicting the definition. D) confuses the Lorenz curve with a Kuznets-style inverted-U relationship.

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Lorenz Curve]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=8
Under a flexible (market-determined) exchange rate regime, a fall in the value of the domestic currency caused by market forces is called:
- A) Devaluation
- B) Depreciation
- C) Appreciation
- D) Revaluation
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Depreciation** = fall in a currency's value due to **market forces** under a flexible/floating exchange rate. **Devaluation** = fall in value deliberately announced by the **government/central bank** under a fixed/pegged exchange rate. Appreciation/revaluation are the upward equivalents.

**Trap:** This is the most common SSC trap — students mix up depreciation (market) vs devaluation (government action). The trigger matters: same direction of price movement, but the *cause* (demand-supply vs policy decision) determines the term.

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Exchange Rate Mechanisms]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=8
A VOSTRO account, in the context of international banking, is best described as:
- A) An account that an Indian bank maintains with a foreign bank abroad, denominated in foreign currency
- B) An account that a foreign bank maintains with an Indian bank in India, denominated in Indian rupees
- C) A foreign currency account maintained by the RBI with the IMF
- D) A special account for Non-Resident Indians to park foreign currency earnings
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**NOSTRO** ("ours") = an account *our* (domestic) bank holds abroad in a foreign currency. **VOSTRO** ("yours") = an account *your* (foreign) bank holds with *us* (domestic), denominated in the domestic currency. From the Indian bank's perspective: Indian bank's account in New York = Nostro; Citibank's account in Mumbai = Vostro.

**Trap:** The most common trap is reversing Nostro and Vostro. Trick: **V**ostro = **V**isit (foreign bank visits us, holds rupees with us). **N**ostro = we are abroad, our money is "there."

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#NOSTRO and VOSTRO]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=8
The "Twin Deficit" problem in an economy refers to the simultaneous occurrence of:
- A) Current account deficit and capital account deficit
- B) Fiscal deficit and current account deficit
- C) Trade deficit and unemployment
- D) Fiscal deficit and revenue deficit
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **Twin Deficit** hypothesis links a country's **fiscal deficit** (government spends more than it earns) with its **current account deficit** (country imports more than it exports). High government borrowing typically raises interest rates, attracting foreign capital but also crowding out private investment, weakening exports and widening CAD. India in 1991 was the classic textbook example.

**Trap:** Students confuse Twin Deficit (fiscal + current account) with **BoP crisis** (current + capital accounts both stressed, forcing emergency IMF assistance). They're related but distinct.

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Twin Deficit]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q11 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=8
During the 1991 Balance of Payments crisis in India, the government had to pledge approximately how much gold to the IMF as collateral against emergency loans?
- A) 5 tonnes
- B) 10 tonnes
- C) 20 tonnes
- D) 67 tonnes
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

India pledged **67 tonnes of gold** (about 20 tonnes shipped to the IMF in 1991) as collateral against emergency loans. FOREX reserves had fallen to barely two weeks of import cover. The crisis precipitated the historic **LPG reforms (Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation)** under Finance Minister Manmohan Singh / PM P.V. Narasimha Rao.

**Trap:** A) 5 and B) 10 are distractors; C) 20 is the amount *shipped* to the IMF — but the *pledged/total* amount was 67 tonnes. The 67-tonne figure is the SSC answer.

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#Balance of Payment Crisis (1991)]]
</details>

@qeco.bop.q12 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=8
The Bretton Woods Conference of 1944 led to the establishment of which two institutions often called the "twin sisters"?
- A) IMF and WTO
- B) IMF and World Bank (IBRD + IDA)
- C) ADB and AIIB
- D) World Bank and Asian Development Bank
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Bretton Woods Conference (July 1944, New Hampshire, USA) established the **IMF** (International Monetary Fund — for exchange-rate stability and short-term balance-of-payments assistance) and the **IBRD** (International Bank for Reconstruction and Development), which along with IDA forms the **World Bank** (for long-term reconstruction and development lending). The WTO was set up later (1995, GATT's successor) and is not a Bretton Woods institution.

**Trap:** WTO is the most common trap — it is *not* a Bretton Woods twin sister. ADB and AIIB are also unrelated to Bretton Woods.

**See also:** [[../../economics/08 - Balance of Payment and Poverty#International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank]]
</details>
