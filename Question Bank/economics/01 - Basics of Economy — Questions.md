@qeco.basics.q1 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=1
Which economist is widely regarded as the "Father of Modern Economics," and what is the fundamental unit of analysis that separates microeconomics from macroeconomics?
- A) John Maynard Keynes; macroeconomics studies individual consumers while microeconomics studies the whole economy
- B) Adam Smith; microeconomics studies individual economic units while macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole
- C) Karl Marx; macroeconomics focuses on labour markets while microeconomics focuses on inflation
- D) Alfred Marshall; microeconomics studies national income while macroeconomics studies firm-level output
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Adam Smith is the Father of Modern Economics. The split is by *unit of analysis*: micro = individual households/firms/markets; macro = aggregate variables like GDP, inflation, and unemployment.

**Trap:** Many students reverse the two (A) because they confuse "individual" with "large-sounding" — Keynes did found macro theory, but he is not the Father of Modern Economics, and micro/macro do not swap units.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#ECONOMICS]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q2 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=1
The LPG reforms of 1991 in India were implemented under which Prime Minister and Finance Minister?
- A) Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Manmohan Singh
- B) Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi and Finance Minister V. P. Singh
- C) Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee and Finance Minister Yashwant Sinha
- D) Prime Minister I. K. Gujral and Finance Minister P. Chidambaram
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

LPG = Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation. The reforms were announced on 24 July 1991 by Finance Minister Manmohan Singh in the budget speech of PM P. V. Narasimha Rao's government, in response to the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis.

**Trap:** Students often credit Rajiv Gandhi (B) because he preceded the reforms, or Vajpayee because LPG deepened later — but the original 1991 package was Rao + Manmohan Singh.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#Licence Raj and LPG Reforms 1991]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q3 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=1
A government has limited tax revenues of ₹100 crore. It must decide how to allocate expenditure across three competing priorities — a primary-health centre upgrade (₹30 cr), a new national highway segment (₹50 cr), and a one-time capital subsidy to a private microchip fabrication plant (₹20 cr). The public-good portion is the health and highway spend; the private-sector support is the subsidy.

Using the standard **Mixed Economy** classification, the public-good spend as a share of total government expenditure is closest to:
- A) 20%
- B) 50%
- C) 80%
- D) 100%
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

In a Mixed Economy (India's post-1991 model), the government both (i) provides public goods itself and (ii) supports private-sector activity. Here, public-good spend = health centre (₹30 cr) + highway (₹50 cr) = ₹80 cr. Share = 80 / 100 = **80%**. The microchip subsidy (₹20 cr) is the private-sector-support share (20%). This composition — heavy direct provisioning plus residual industrial promotion — is the hallmark of the Indian mixed model.

**Trap:** Option (B) 50% tempts those who count only the highway; option (A) 20% is the subsidy share (it's the *private*-support component, not the *public*-good share). The student must read which share the question asks for.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#The economic system can be classified into three types]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q4 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=1
Which of the following is a pure **public good** — i.e., non-rivalrous and non-excludable?
- A) A slice of pizza from a restaurant
- B) A subscription-based streaming service
- C) National defence provided by the government
- D) A seat in a cinema hall during a screening
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Public goods are non-rivalrous (one person's use doesn't reduce another's) and non-excludable (you can't prevent anyone from using them). National defence satisfies both — your protection doesn't reduce mine, and you cannot be excluded.

**Trap:** Streaming (B) is non-rivalrous but excludable — that's a club/toll good, not a public good. Cinema seat (D) is rivalrous (taken = gone). Pizza (A) is private.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#Difference between Public Goods and Private Goods]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q5 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=1
Rakesh works as a software architect designing cloud-based enterprise applications, while his wife Sita is a research scientist at a government laboratory developing new vaccines. According to the sector classification, Rakesh works in the _______ sector and Sita in the _______ sector.
- A) Tertiary; Quaternary
- B) Quaternary; Quinary
- C) Quinary; Secondary
- D) Tertiary; Quinary
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Quaternary = knowledge/information-handling work such as IT services, R&D design, and consulting (Rakesh's software architecture role). Quinary = high-level decision-making, scientific research, and policy work performed largely in public/household sectors (Sita's research-scientist role).

**Trap:** Many students bundle all white-collar work as "tertiary" (D), but quaternary and quinary are sub-categories for higher-order services beyond plain trade/transport.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#Quinary Sector]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q6 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=1
A government press release states: "India should reduce income inequality by raising the top marginal income-tax rate to 50%." Which branch of economics does this statement belong to, and why?
- A) Positive economics — because it states a measurable fact
- B) Normative economics — because it expresses a value judgement about what *ought* to be done
- C) Positive economics — because it is issued by the government
- D) Normative economics — because it concerns taxation, which is a public-finance topic
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Normative statements prescribe what *ought* to happen and embed value judgements ("should", "ought"). The word "should reduce inequality" is the giveaway. Positive economics only describes what *is* and can be tested against data.

**Trap:** Students pick (D) because the topic involves taxation — but the *branch* is determined by whether the statement is descriptive (positive) or prescriptive (normative), not by its subject.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#Normative Vs Positive Economics]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q7 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=1
According to the 2011 Census of India, the share of the tertiary sector in total employment (workforce) is approximately _____, and the share of women workers in the primary sector is approximately _____.
- A) 28%; 55%
- B) 50%; 25%
- C) 70%; 12%
- D) 28%; 33%
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

As per the 2011 Census, the services (tertiary) sector employs roughly 28% of the workforce despite contributing more than 50% of GDP — a classic productivity-and-structural-gap feature. In the primary sector, women constitute about 55% of workers, indicating heavy female participation in agriculture and allied activities.

**Trap:** Option (D) tempts students who confuse all-India female workforce share with the within-primary-sector share — 33% is closer to female LFPR across the whole economy, not within agriculture.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#According to 2011 Census]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=1
A student writes: "The study of how the RBI sets the repo rate is macroeconomics, but the study of how a particular rice mill in Thanjavur sets its output price is microeconomics." This classification is:
- A) Correct on both counts
- B) Wrong on both counts — both are macroeconomic
- C) Correct on the RBI example but wrong on the rice-mill example — both fall under microeconomics since RBI also decides prices
- D) Correct on the rice-mill example but wrong on the RBI example — repo-rate policy is monetary policy and counts as microeconomics
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Repo-rate setting is a nation-wide monetary variable (macro). A single firm's pricing decision is firm-level behaviour (micro). The classification is correct on both counts.

**Trap:** Option (C) traps students who overgeneralise — they think "any price decision = micro," missing that the RBI's rate decision affects the whole economy, not a single transaction.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#ECONOMICS]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=1
Which of the following is the best distinguishing criterion between the quaternary and quinary sectors?
- A) Quaternary is public-sector employment while quinary is private-sector employment
- B) Quaternary deals with information/knowledge services (IT, R&D design); quinary deals with high-level decision-making, scientific research, and household services
- C) Quaternary is urban-based while quinary is rural-based
- D) Quaternary requires a graduate degree while quinary does not
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Quaternary = knowledge work that processes information (e.g., IT services, consultancy, R&D). Quinary = the highest tier — top decision-makers, scientists, policy-makers, and unpaid domestic services. (B) captures the standard textbook split.

**Trap:** Option (A) is the most common error because students confuse "quinary = health/education (often public)" with "quinary = only public." Quinary workers exist in the private sector too, and vice versa.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#Quinary Sector]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=1
"Direct taxes are always progressive while indirect taxes are always regressive." Which of the following statements correctly evaluates this claim?
- A) True — direct taxes are always progressive and indirect taxes are always regressive
- B) False — while direct taxes are usually progressive, indirect taxes like GST can be made progressive via exemptions on essentials; and direct taxes can be regressive if the slab is poorly designed
- C) True — because direct taxes are paid by individuals while indirect taxes are paid by firms
- D) False — only because direct taxes can be regressive; indirect taxes are always regressive
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The claim over-generalises. Direct taxes are *typically* progressive (the more you earn, the higher the rate) but can be regressive in design. Indirect taxes like GST are *typically* regressive but can be designed progressively (lower/zero rate on essentials like food, higher on luxury cars). So neither "always" holds.

**Trap:** Students who learnt only the textbook default (progressive vs regressive) miss that progressivity is a *design* feature, not an automatic property of the tax type.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#Licence Raj and LPG Reforms 1991]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q11 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=1
A rural district has 100 workers. 60 work on farms cultivating paddy, 25 work in a small-scale garment stitching unit, and 15 work as contract teachers in a private school run by a non-government trust. Per Planning Commission conventions, the workers in each activity fall into which sector respectively?
- A) Primary, Secondary, Tertiary
- B) Primary, Tertiary, Secondary
- C) Tertiary, Secondary, Primary
- D) Primary, Secondary, Quaternary
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Paddy cultivation → **Primary** sector (agriculture). Garment stitching → **Secondary** sector (manufacturing/processing). Contract teachers in a school → **Tertiary** sector (services). The classification follows the activity's nature — *what* is being produced (raw materials vs processed goods vs services) — not the income level or formality of the worker.

**Trap:** Option (D) tempts students who label teaching as "knowledge work = quaternary"; the textbook convention places ordinary teaching in tertiary, reserving quaternary for higher-end knowledge services (R&D, IT, consultancy).

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#Tertiary Sector]]
</details>

@qeco.basics.q12 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=1
According to the 2011 Census composition of India's workforce, the primary sector accounts for the largest share (~54.4%), the secondary sector for about 24%, the tertiary sector for about 28%, and the remaining is distributed across higher-tier activities. If India's total workforce is approximately 480 million, the workforce engaged in activities that are NOT agriculture, mining, manufacturing or standard services — i.e., knowledge work, R&D, top-tier decision-making, and high-end domestic services — is closest to which share of total employment?
- A) About 0.5%
- B) About 2.5%
- C) About 7%
- D) About 12%
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The four broad sectors (primary + secondary + tertiary, plus residual household/uncategorised) already account for roughly **93%** of India's workforce per 2011 Census estimates. The **higher-tier activities** — quaternary (IT, R&D, design) and quinary (top decision-makers, high-end research, policy, professional domestic services) — account for the residual roughly **~7%**. This is small but disproportionately important because of its high value-added per worker.

**Trap:** Option (B) 2.5% undercounts by ignoring quinary domestic-services activity. Option (D) 12% overcounts by including manufacturing. Option (A) is unrealistically tiny. The "skill-content" ordering — Primary < Secondary < Tertiary < Quaternary < Quinary — places quaternary and quinary together at the top.

**See also:** [[../../economics/01 - Basics of Economy#SECTORS OF ECONOMY]]
</details>
