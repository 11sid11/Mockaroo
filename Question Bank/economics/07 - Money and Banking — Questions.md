@qeco.banking.q1 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=7
A scheduled commercial bank approaches the RBI when it has exhausted all other avenues of short-term funds. Which role of the RBI is being invoked?
- A) Regulator of the banking system
- B) Monetary authority
- C) Issuer of currency
- D) Lender of last resort
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The RBI acts as the **lender of last resort** to scheduled commercial banks facing acute liquidity crunch after all market sources have been tapped. This role is distinct from routine regulation (A) or monetary policy (B).

**Trap:** Candidates confuse "regulator" with "lender of last resort"; regulator is a permanent supervisory role, while lender of last resort is invoked only in emergencies.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Functions of RBI]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q2 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=7
In which year was the Reserve Bank of India established, and where was its headquarters initially located before being permanently shifted to Mumbai?
- A) 1934 — Kolkata
- B) 1935 — Kolkata
- C) 1935 — New Delhi
- D) 1934 — Mumbai
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The RBI was established on **1 April 1935** under the RBI Act, 1934. It began operations in **Kolkata (Calcutta)** and was permanently shifted to **Mumbai in 1937**.

**Trap:** RBI Act was passed in 1934, but the Bank started functioning in 1935; confusing the two years is the most common error.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Reserve Bank of India (RBI)]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q3 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=7
Which of the following currency notes is signed by the Secretary, Ministry of Finance, rather than the Governor of the RBI?
- A) ₹10 note
- B) ₹50 note
- C) ₹100 note
- D) ₹1 note
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The **₹1 note** is the only denomination issued by the **Ministry of Finance** and bears the signature of the Finance Secretary. All other notes (₹2 onwards in modern series, ₹5, ₹10, etc.) are signed by the RBI Governor.

**Trap:** Many candidates assume all currency is signed by the RBI Governor; the ₹1 note is a statutory exception.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Types of Money]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q4 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=7
A central bank declares that a currency is legal tender purely by government order, without any backing by a physical commodity. This describes which type of money?
- A) Fiduciary money
- B) Commodity money
- C) FIAT money
- D) Plastic money
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**FIAT money** is declared legal tender by government fiat/order and has no intrinsic or commodity backing — its value rests on public trust and state authority. Fiduciary money (A) is backed by trust between parties (e.g., cheques). Plastic money (D) refers to card-based instruments, not a value backing concept.

**Trap:** "Fiduciary" and "FIAT" sound similar but are different — fiduciary depends on trust between two parties, FIAT depends on sovereign decree.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Types of Money in our Economy]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q5 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=7
A loan has remained an NPA for 14 months. Under RBI's income recognition and asset classification norms, it is categorised as:
- A) Substandard asset
- B) Doubtful asset
- C) Loss asset
- D) Standard asset
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

RBI's NPA classification:
- **Substandard:** NPA for **up to 12 months** (90 days–12 months).
- **Doubtful:** NPA for **more than 12 months**.
- **Loss:** Identified as uncollectible by the bank/internal auditor/RBI inspector.

Since the loan has been NPA for **14 months (>12 months)**, it qualifies as a **doubtful asset**.

**Trap:** The cut-off is 12 months, not 6 months or 24 months; candidates often mis-remember the boundary between substandard and doubtful.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Banking Regulations and Security Laws]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q6 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=7
Which money aggregate is the **narrowest** measure of money supply, comprising currency with the public plus demand deposits with the public (commonly denoted as M1)?
- A) M0 (Reserve Money)
- B) M1 (Narrow Money)
- C) M3 (Broad Money)
- D) M4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**M1 = Narrow money** = Currency with public + Demand deposits with public + Other deposits with RBI. M0 (Reserve Money) is held by banks/RBI itself and is **not** held by the public — so M0 is narrower in composition but M1 is the standard **narrow money** measure used by RBI.

**Trap:** M0 is technically narrower (only RBI's view of base money), but M1 is conventionally called "narrow money"; the question tests whether you know which one is termed "narrow money."

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Types of Money Supply]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q7 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=7
Under the RBI Act, 1934, which of the following is classified as a **scheduled commercial bank**?
- A) Regional Rural Bank (RRB)
- B) State Bank of India (SBI)
- C) Local Area Bank
- D) Cooperative bank
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **SBI** is a scheduled commercial bank whose name is included in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934. RRBs (A) and Cooperative banks (D) are scheduled but **not** commercial banks in the strict sense — RRBs are government-sponsored rural banks, and cooperative banks operate under state cooperative acts. Local Area Banks (C) are a separate category with restricted area of operation.

**Trap:** RRBs and cooperatives are "scheduled" but not classified as **scheduled commercial banks** for all regulatory purposes; the distinction matters for CRR/SLR applicability.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Classification of Banking System (According to RBI Act 1934)]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=7
Who was the **first Indian** Governor of the Reserve Bank of India?
- A) Sir Osborne Smith
- B) Sir James Braid Taylor
- C) Sir C.D. Deshmukh
- D) Sir Benegal Rama Rau
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Sir Osborne Smith** (British) was the first Governor (1935–1937). **Sir C.D. Deshmukh** became the **first Indian Governor** in 1943. Sir James Taylor was the second Governor overall; Benegal Rama Rau was a later Indian Governor.

**Trap:** The first **Governor** (Osborne Smith) is British; the first **Indian** Governor is Deshmukh. Candidates confuse "first Governor" with "first Indian Governor."

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Reserve Bank of India (RBI)]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=7
Under **Basel III** norms, what is the minimum total **Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR)** that commercial banks in India must maintain (as commonly tested in SSC)?
- A) 8%
- B) 9%
- C) 11.5%
- D) 12.9%
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Under **Basel III**, the total Capital Adequacy Ratio including the capital conservation buffer is **11.5%** (8% minimum CAR + 2.5% capital conservation buffer + 1% additional buffer). Option (A) 8% is the **Basel I** figure. Option (B) 9% is RBI's earlier phased-in minimum. Option (D) 12.9% is sometimes cited for total loss-absorbing capacity in advanced jurisdictions.

**Trap:** Candidates confuse **Basel I (8%)** with **Basel III (11.5% with full buffers)**; remember Basel I = 8%, Basel III = 11.5% in SSC-tested Indian context.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Basel Norms]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=7
Arrange the following money supply measures in **ascending order of breadth** (narrowest to broadest): M1, M3, M0, M4.
- A) M0, M1, M3, M4
- B) M1, M0, M3, M4
- C) M0, M1, M4, M3
- D) M3, M1, M0, M4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct ascending order is: **M0 (Reserve Money)** → **M1 (Narrow Money)** → **M3 (Broad Money)** → **M4 (Broadest Money)**.

- **M0 (Reserve Money)** = currency with public + bankers’ deposits with RBI. Narrowest.
- **M1 (Narrow Money)** = M0 + demand deposits with banks (current/savings).
- **M3 (Broad Money)** = M1 + time deposits (FD/RD).
- **M4 (Broadest Money)** = M3 + total post office deposits.

**Trap:** A common slip is to put M1 first as “narrowest” because the word “narrow” sounds smallest — but **M0 (Reserve Money)** is the narrowest measure. Option B swaps M0 and M1.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Types of Money Supply]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q11 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=7
The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) was established in 1974 by the Central Bank Governors of which group of countries?
- A) G7
- B) G10
- C) G20
- D) G77
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **BCBS** was set up in **1974** by the Central Bank Governors of the **G10 countries**. It is hosted by the **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)** in **Basel, Switzerland**.

**Trap:** G20 is a broader grouping formed later (1999) and is not the founding group of BCBS; confusing G10 with G7 or G20 is common.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Basel Norms]]

</details>

@qeco.banking.q12 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=7
The **SARFAESI Act** that empowers banks and financial institutions to recover non-performing assets without court intervention was enacted in which year?
- A) 1993
- B) 2000
- C) 2002
- D) 2016
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest (SARFAESI) Act, 2002** allows secured creditors to take possession of collateral and sell it to recover dues, bypassing lengthy court processes. The **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) was enacted in 2016** — this is a related but different legislation.

**Trap:** Candidates confuse SARFAESI (2002) with IBC (2016); both deal with bad loan recovery but operate through different mechanisms.

**See also:** [[../../economics/07 - Money and Banking#Banking Regulations and Security Laws]]

</details>
