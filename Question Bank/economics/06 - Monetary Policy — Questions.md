@qeco.monetary.q1 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=6
A teacher asks students to classify two RBI instruments: (i) the Cash Reserve Ratio, and (ii) moral suasion. The correct classification is:

- A) (i) is qualitative and (ii) is quantitative
- B) (i) is quantitative and (ii) is qualitative
- C) Both are quantitative general tools
- D) Both are qualitative selective tools
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** is a *quantitative* (general) tool — it affects the *aggregate* lendable funds of the entire banking system by mandating a fixed percentage of deposits to be parked with the RBI. **Moral suasion** is a *qualitative* (selective) tool — it works through *persuasion and informal pressure* on banks (no statutory numbers involved). They therefore belong to opposite categories.

**Trap:** Candidates often categorise by "how strict" the tool feels. CRR has a number attached, so it feels technical; moral suasion feels soft. The actual test is whether the tool targets *quantity of credit* (quantitative) or *direction/quality of credit* (qualitative) — not how "strong" it is.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Quantitative Tools]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q2 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=6
Under the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR), the portion of a bank's deposits that must be kept with the Reserve Bank of India:

- A) Earns interest at the repo rate
- B) Earns interest at the bank rate
- C) Earns no interest
- D) Earns interest at the reverse repo rate
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Cash kept by banks with the RBI under the CRR earns **no interest**. This is precisely what makes CRR a powerful monetary tool — it is a *costless funding source* for the RBI and a *non-earning asset* for the bank, so a hike in CRR immediately drains funds from the banking system.

**Trap:** A) and D) confuse CRR with the reverse repo rate (which is what RBI pays *banks* when they voluntarily park excess funds overnight). B) bank rate is unrelated to remuneration on reserves.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Reserves Maintained by Banks]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q3 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=6
Which of the following best distinguishes the **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)** from the **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)**?

- A) SLR is maintained only in cash, while CRR is maintained in gold and government securities
- B) CRR is maintained with the RBI; SLR is maintained by banks themselves in the form of gold or government securities
- C) Both SLR and CRR are maintained entirely with the RBI in cash
- D) SLR applies only to foreign banks, while CRR applies to all commercial banks
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The CRR is a **cash** reserve that every commercial bank must keep **with the RBI** (currently 4–5% of NDTL). The SLR, by contrast, is maintained **by the bank itself** in the form of **gold, government securities, or cash** (currently ~18% of NDTL). A key practical consequence: SLR assets *earn interest* (coupons on G-secs), while CRR balances do not.

**Trap:** A) Reverses the two. C) Ignores the fact that SLR is held *within the bank*, not with RBI. D) Both ratios apply to all scheduled commercial banks in India.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Reserves Maintained by Banks]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q4 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=6
The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) of the Reserve Bank of India, which decides the policy repo rate, was constituted on the recommendation of which committee?

- A) Narasimham Committee
- B) Urjit Patel Committee
- C) Chakravarty Committee
- D) Rangarajan Committee
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **Urjit Patel Committee** (2016) on the monetary policy framework recommended the formation of an empowered six-member **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** with the RBI Governor as Chair, three RBI officials and three external experts nominated by the Central Government. The MPC was formally constituted in September 2016 and given statutory status under the RBI Act (amended in 2016).

**Trap:** A) Narasimham Committee is famous for *financial-sector reforms* (1991, 1998), not the MPC. C) Chakravarty Committee (1985) was about monetary policy *review* but predates the formal MPC structure. D) Rangarajan Committee worked on the *operational* conduct of monetary policy, not its committee structure.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q5 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=6
Inflation has risen sharply above the RBI's target. Which of the following monetary policy actions is **contractionary** and therefore appropriate to cool demand?

- A) RBI purchases government securities in the open market
- B) RBI cuts the repo rate
- C) RBI raises the Cash Reserve Ratio
- D) RBI reduces the Statutory Liquidity Ratio
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

A **contractionary** (tight) monetary policy *reduces* the money supply. **Raising the CRR** forces banks to lock up a larger share of their deposits with the RBI, leaving them with less to lend — this drains liquidity and cools demand, which is exactly the goal during high inflation. The other three options are *expansionary* (they all *increase* liquidity).

**Trap:** A) OMO purchase adds reserves (expansionary). B) Repo-rate cut makes borrowing cheaper (expansionary). D) Cutting SLR frees up bank funds for lending (expansionary). All three would *worsen* inflation, not cool it.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Quantitative Tools]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q6 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=6
The **repo rate** is the rate at which:

- A) Banks lend to each other in the inter-bank call money market
- B) The Reserve Bank of India lends short-term funds to commercial banks against government securities
- C) Commercial banks park their excess funds overnight with the RBI
- D) The government of India borrows from the RBI to finance the fiscal deficit
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **repo rate** is the rate at which the **RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks** against the collateral of government securities (the transaction itself is a "repo" — repurchase agreement). It is the **single most important policy rate** in the LAF corridor and decisively affects the cost of credit across the economy. The **reverse repo rate** (option C) is the mirror — the rate at which banks park excess funds with the RBI.

**Trap:** A) Call money rate is *between banks*, not the RBI. C) Reverse repo is the right answer to that distractor — the question asks about repo. D) Government borrowing from RBI is governed by Ways & Means Advances, not the repo rate.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Monetary Policy Tools used by RBI]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q7 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=6
When the RBI wants to **inject durable liquidity** into the banking system on a permanent basis, the appropriate Open Market Operation is:

- A) A repo auction
- B) A reverse repo auction
- C) An outright purchase of government securities
- D) An MSF borrowing operation
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

An **outright, open-market purchase** of government securities by the RBI — i.e., the RBI buys G-secs from banks with no promise of resale — adds reserves to the banking system **permanently** (sterilised or not, the liquidity does not reverse). A **repo** (option A) is collateralised and *reverses automatically* after the term; it injects liquidity only temporarily. Reverse repo (B) and MSF (D) are *absorption* tools, not infusion tools.

**Trap:** A) is the most common distractor — candidates remember that OMO involves G-secs but forget that repo operations are self-reversing, so they are *temporary*, not *durable* liquidity.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Monetary Policy Tools used by RBI]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=6
Which of the following correctly distinguishes the **bank rate** from the **repo rate**?

- A) Bank rate is the short-term policy rate; repo rate is the long-term rate
- B) Bank rate is used for long-term loans by RBI to banks without collateral; repo rate is for short-term collateralised loans
- C) Both are identical and interchangeable
- D) Repo rate is set by the government; bank rate is set by the RBI
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **bank rate** is the rate at which the RBI lends **long-term** funds to banks, traditionally **without collateral** (eligible for refinance of industrial advances, etc.). The **repo rate** is the rate at which the RBI lends **short-term** funds to banks *against* the collateral of government securities. As BPRMD (2021) emphasised, the repo rate is now the principal policy rate; the bank rate is aligned to the repo rate but conceptually different.

**Trap:** A) Reverses the durations. C) Was once approximately true for decades but is no longer correct — the two are conceptually distinct even if numerically equal. D) Both rates are set by the RBI, not the government.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Monetary Policy Tools used by RBI]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=6
If the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) is 4%, the maximum possible expansion of deposits (the money multiplier) in the banking system is closest to:

- A) 4
- B) 25
- C) 0.04
- D) 100
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **money multiplier** under a simple fractional-reserve system equals **1 / CRR** (i.e., the reciprocal of the reserve ratio). With CRR = 4% = 0.04, the multiplier = 1 / 0.04 = **25**. So every ₹1 of fresh base money can, in theory, support up to ₹25 of total deposits. The direction is *inverse*: *higher* CRR → *smaller* multiplier; *lower* CRR → *larger* multiplier.

**Trap:** A) Candidates mistakenly take the CRR percentage itself as the multiplier. C) Confuses multiplier with the ratio itself. D) Likely from dividing by 0.04 incorrectly. The trap here is twofold: (a) the formula is **1 / CRR**, not 1 / SLR, and (b) it is the *inverse* of the percentage.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Reserves Maintained by Banks]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=6
The key **institutional** distinction between fiscal policy and monetary policy in India is:

- A) Fiscal policy is set by the RBI; monetary policy is set by the Finance Ministry
- B) Both are set by the RBI
- C) Fiscal policy is formulated by the Government of India (Finance Ministry); monetary policy is conducted by the RBI
- D) Both are set by Parliament
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Fiscal policy** — decisions on taxation, government expenditure and borrowing — is formulated by the **Government of India** (the Finance Ministry) and is subject to Parliament's approval. **Monetary policy** — decisions on interest rates, reserves and liquidity — is conducted by the **Reserve Bank of India**, in modern practice through the **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**. Their tools are different too: fiscal uses *taxes and spending*; monetary uses *policy rates, CRR/SLR, OMO*.

**Trap:** A) Reverses the two (a common confusion). B) and D) wrongly attribute both policies to a single authority.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#FISCAL POLICY]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q11 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=6
The **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) rate** under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) is:

- A) The lowest rate in the LAF corridor
- B) Equal to the reverse repo rate
- C) The highest rate in the LAF corridor; banks can borrow overnight up to 2% of their NDTL
- D) The rate at which the RBI lends long-term funds to banks
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **MSF** is a *penal* overnight window at which banks can borrow from the RBI by dipping into a small slice of their **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)** portfolio — up to **2% of their NDTL**. The MSF rate is set **above** the repo rate and is therefore the **highest rate in the LAF corridor** (corridor: reverse repo at the floor → repo in the middle → MSF at the ceiling). It penalises banks for overnight-funding stress and pushes the upper bound of short-term rates.

**Trap:** A) and B) confuse MSF with the *floor* (reverse repo). D) describes the bank rate, not the MSF.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Monetary Policy Tools used by RBI]]
</details>

@qeco.monetary.q12 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=6
Which of the following lists contains **only qualitative (selective) monetary policy tools** of the RBI?

- A) CRR, SLR, repo rate, reverse repo rate
- B) Margin requirements, consumer credit regulation, moral suasion, credit rationing
- C) Open market operations, bank rate, MSF, LAF
- D) Bank rate, repo rate, reverse repo rate, MSF
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Qualitative (selective) tools** target the *direction, terms or quality* of credit rather than its total quantity, and work by regulation or persuasion rather than by reserve ratios. The classic list is: **margin requirements** (loan-to-value ratios), **consumer credit regulation** (down payment, EMI caps), **moral suasion** (informal RBI pressure), and **credit rationing** (selective credit controls). All four in option B are qualitative. The other options are populated with **quantitative (general)** tools — CRR, SLR, repo, reverse repo, bank rate, MSF, OMO — which act on the *aggregate* money supply.

**Trap:** A) and D) are pure quantitative tools. C) is a mix of OMO, bank rate, MSF and LAF — every one of them is *quantitative*. The trap is that "selective credit controls" sound technical, but they are precisely the qualitative family.

**See also:** [[../../economics/06 - Monetary Policy#Qualitative Monetary Policy Tools]]
</details>
