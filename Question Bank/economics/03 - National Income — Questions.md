@qeco.national.q1 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=3
A resident Indian engineer works at a software firm located in Bengaluru and earns a salary there. Separately, an Indian national works at an Amazon office in the United States and sends a portion of her income back home. Under standard national-income accounting, which statement correctly classifies the two wages for inclusion in GDP and GNP of India?
- A) Both wages are counted in India's GDP because both engineers are Indian nationals
- B) Both wages are counted in India's GNP; neither is in India's GDP
- C) The Bengaluru salary is in GDP (and hence in GNP); the US salary is in GNP only (via NFIA), not in GDP
- D) The Bengaluru salary is in GNP only; the US salary is in GDP only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

GDP measures output produced **within the geographic territory** of a country regardless of the producer's nationality — so the Bengaluru salary (produced on Indian soil) is in GDP. GNP measures output produced by **residents/nationals** wherever located — so both salaries are in GNP, with the foreign salary entering via Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). The reverse (D) wrongly strips domestic output out of GDP.

**Trap:** Option (A) wrongly assigns GDP by nationality, not by territory. Option (D) flips the two — confusing "domestic" with "produced by nationals."

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Gross Domestic Product (GDP)]] and [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Gross National Product (GNP)]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q2 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=3
Which of the following formulas correctly converts a Market-Price (MP) aggregate into its Factor-Cost (FC) equivalent?
- A) FC = MP + Net Indirect Tax
- B) FC = MP − Net Indirect Tax
- C) FC = MP × Net Indirect Tax
- D) FC = MP − Depreciation + Net Indirect Tax
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

FC = MP − Net Indirect Tax, where Net Indirect Tax = Indirect taxes − Subsidies. Indirect taxes inflate the market price (so we strip them out), while subsidies deflate it (so we add them back). Option (A) goes the wrong way; (D) wrongly mixes depreciation into the MP↔FC bridge (depreciation is the Gross↔Net bridge).

**Trap:** Option (A) is the most common wrong answer — students remember "MP is higher" and add instead of subtract, forgetting that **Net** Indirect Tax can also be negative (if subsidies exceed indirect taxes).

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Factor Cost vs Market Price]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q3 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=3
Who is credited with first applying the concept of National Income to India, and which body did Professor P.C. Mahalanobis head to estimate it?
- A) M. Visvesvaraya; Planning Commission (1951)
- B) Dadabhai Naoroji; National Income Committee (1949)
- C) P.C. Mahalanobis; Central Statistical Office (CSO)
- D) V.K.R.V. Rao; Reserve Bank of India
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Dadabhai Naoroji was the first to apply the concept of National Income to India (in *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*). Professor P.C. Mahalanobis later headed the official **National Income Committee (1949)** to estimate India's national income on a scientific basis.

**Trap:** Option (D) confuses V.K.R.V. Rao (who used a scientific approach and the two-sector classification) with the National Income Committee leadership; that committee was chaired by Mahalanobis, not Rao.

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#NATIONAL INCOME]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q4 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=3
Match each transaction with whether it represents an **Injection** (money entering the circular flow) or a **Leakage** (money leaving the circular flow):  (i) Households park part of their income in fixed deposits, (ii) A foreign buyer purchases garments from an Indian exporter, (iii) The government collects excise duty from a firm, (iv) A firm imports raw materials from China.
- A) (i) Injection, (ii) Injection, (iii) Leakage, (iv) Leakage
- B) (i) Leakage, (ii) Injection, (iii) Leakage, (iv) Leakage
- C) (i) Injection, (ii) Leakage, (iii) Leakage, (iv) Injection
- D) (i) Leakage, (ii) Leakage, (iii) Injection, (iv) Injection
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

(i) Saving money into fixed deposits is a **leakage** (money exits the circular flow into financial assets). (ii) Exports are an **injection** (foreign money enters the domestic flow). (iii) Taxes are a **leakage** (money leaves households/firms to the government). (iv) Imports are a **leakage** (domestic money flows abroad). So pattern is Leakage, Injection, Leakage, Leakage.

**Trap:** Imports/Exports trip up many students — remember the mnemonic: Exports are money coming **in** (injection), Imports are money going **out** (leakage).

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Injection (Money Entering the Economy)]] and [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Leakage (Money Leaving the Economy)]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q5 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=3
A textile chain reports the following values at each stage of production:

| Stage | Cotton | Yarn | Cloth | Shirt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Output value (₹) | 100 | 200 | 400 | 800 |

Using the **Value Added Method**, what is the total value added by the four stages combined (excluding any double counting)?
- A) ₹1,500
- B) ₹800
- C) ₹600
- D) ₹300
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Value Added = Output − Intermediate Input. At each stage: Cotton 100 − 0 = 100; Yarn 200 − 100 = 100; Cloth 400 − 200 = 200; Shirt 800 − 400 = 400. Total = 100 + 100 + 200 + 400 = **₹600**. The full-chain Output (₹1,500) would double-count intermediate inputs; the final Shirt price (₹800) only counts the last stage.

**Trap:** Option (B) ₹800 = final good value only (it equals total value added *if* there were a single producer). Option (A) ₹1,500 = sum of gross outputs without netting intermediates — i.e., double counting.

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#1. Value Added Method/Production Method/Output Method]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q6 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=3
A country has a National Income (NNP at Factor Cost) of ₹280 lakh crore and a population of 140 crore. What is its **Per Capita Income** (rounded to the nearest rupee)?
- A) ₹2,00,000
- B) ₹20,000
- C) ₹2,000
- D) ₹28,000
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Per Capita Income = National Income / Population = ₹280,00,000 crore ÷ 140 crore = **₹2,00,000**. (₹280,00,000 ÷ 140 = ₹2,000 per crore; per-person = ₹2,00,000.)

**Trap:** Off-by-factor-of-10 errors are common — many students place the decimal differently or divide by 140 instead of 140,00,00,000. Always keep units consistent: ₹ + "crore" cancels out cleanly.

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Per Capita Income]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q7 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=3
Suppose Nominal GDP in a year is ₹250 lakh crore and Real GDP (at constant base-year prices) for the same year is ₹200 lakh crore. What is the **GDP Deflator** for that year, and what does it imply about the price level relative to the base year?
- A) 80; prices have fallen by 20%
- B) 125; prices have risen by 25% above the base year
- C) 150; prices have risen by 50%
- D) 200; prices have doubled
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100 = (250 / 200) × 100 = **125**. A deflator of 125 (above 100) means the overall price level is 25% higher than in the base year — i.e., inflation since the base year. Deflator < 100 would imply deflation; = 100 means nominal = real.

**Trap:** Options (A) and (C) tempt students who invert the ratio or forget the ×100. The deflator is ratio-of-GDPs — *not* growth rate.

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Real GDP vs Nominal GDP]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=3
An Indian software company opens a branch in Canada and the staff there produce output worth ₹5,000 crore during the financial year. Under standard Indian national-income accounting rules, which option correctly treats this ₹5,000 crore?
- A) It is added to India's GDP at market price
- B) It is added to India's GNP via Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA), but excluded from India's GDP
- C) It is added to both India's GDP and Canada's GNP, but to neither's GNP uniquely
- D) It is excluded from both India and Canada because the company is a multinational
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The output is produced on **Canadian territory**, so it goes into **Canada's GDP** — not India's GDP (which is geographically delimited). The staff are, however, **residents of India working abroad on Indian capital**, so the resulting factor income (profit/wages flowing back to India) is part of India's **GNP** via Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). GNP = GDP + NFIA.

**Trap:** Option (A) wrongly treats GDP as if it followed the company, not the territory. Option (C) double-counts this output into India's GDP. The "GNP-only" pattern is exactly the trap SSC loves to test.

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Gross National Product (GNP)]] and [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Domestic Income vs National Income]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=3
A country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) at Market Price is ₹300 lakh crore. The Depreciation (consumption of fixed capital) during the year is ₹40 lakh crore, and Net Indirect Taxes are ₹20 lakh crore. Compute **NNP at Factor Cost** (i.e., National Income).
- A) ₹280 lakh crore
- B) ₹260 lakh crore
- C) ₹240 lakh crore
- D) ₹320 lakh crore
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Step 1: Convert Market Price → Factor Cost by subtracting Net Indirect Tax: NDP_FC = (GDP_MP − Depreciation) − NIT = (₹300 − ₹40) − ₹20 = ₹260 − ₹20 = ₹240 lakh crore. Equivalently, NNP_FC = GDP_MP − Depreciation − NIT. (Note: this implicitly assumes GDP is taken at MP, NFIA = 0.)

**Trap:** Many students subtract depreciation **and** NIT but forget the order, ending up at ₹260 (B). Others compute NDP_FC correctly as ₹260 but then stop one step short, reporting NDP-at-MP. Always end on **NNP at FC** = "National Income."

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Net National Product (NNP) / National Income]] and [[../../economics/03 - National Income#To Summarise]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=3
In a year, indirect taxes collected by the government total ₹80 lakh crore, while subsidies paid by the government total ₹50 lakh crore. Which of the following statements is **correct** about the relationship between an aggregate measured at Market Price and the same aggregate measured at Factor Cost in this economy?
- A) Aggregate at MP is *higher* than at FC (since indirect taxes exceed subsidies)
- B) Aggregate at MP is *lower* than at FC (since subsidies exceed indirect taxes)
- C) MP and FC must be exactly equal in this case
- D) MP and FC differ only by exactly ₹30 lakh crore, with no further steps needed
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Net Indirect Tax = ₹80 − ₹50 = +₹30 lakh crore. Since NIT is positive (indirect taxes > subsidies), the Market Price aggregate *embeds* a positive tax wedge and is therefore **higher** than the Factor-Cost aggregate by exactly ₹30 lakh crore. Formula: FC = MP − NIT.

**Trap:** Option (B) is the inverse trap — when subsidies exceed taxes, MP *would* be lower than FC, but not in this case. Option (D) tempts a "do nothing further" mental shortcut; always re-express FC using the full MP − NIT formula once you have NIT.

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Factor Cost vs Market Price]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q11 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=3
Under the current Indian national accounts, the formula for the **GDP Deflator** is:
- A) (Real GDP ÷ Nominal GDP) × 100; base year = 1999-2000
- B) (Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100; base year = 2011-12
- C) (Nominal GDP + Real GDP) ÷ 2; base year = 2004-05
- D) (Nominal GDP − Real GDP); base year = 2022-23
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100. It is also called the "Implicit Price Deflator" or "Price Index." The current base year in India is **2011-12** (though a committee has been set up to revise it to 2022-23). A deflator > 100 = inflation since the base year; < 100 = deflation.

**Trap:** Option (A) inverts the ratio (giving 100/deflator instead of deflator). Option (D) substitutes a *difference* for a *ratio* — that gives growth-rate-ish numbers, not a price index.

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Real GDP vs Nominal GDP]]
</details>

@qeco.national.q12 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=3
Which formula correctly expresses the relationship between **Personal Income (PI)** and **Personal Disposable Income (PDI)**?
- A) PDI = PI + Direct Tax
- B) PDI = PI − Direct Tax
- C) PDI = PI × Direct Tax
- D) PDI = PI − Indirect Tax
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Personal Income = National Income + Transfer Payments − Undistributed Corporate Profit.** After PI is computed, the household still has to pay direct (income) tax, so **Personal Disposable Income = PI − Direct Tax**. PDI is what households can actually spend or save.

**Trap:** Option (D) wrongly subtracts indirect tax — indirect taxes are not paid directly by individuals out of personal income (they're embedded in market prices of goods). Option (A) just reverses the sign.

**See also:** [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Personal Income]] and [[../../economics/03 - National Income#Personal Disposable Income]]
</details>
