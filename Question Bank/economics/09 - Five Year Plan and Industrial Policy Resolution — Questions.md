@qeco.planning.q1 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=9
Match the Five-Year Plan with its dominant theme, and identify the correct pair:

- A) First FYP — Industrialisation based on the Mahalanobis model
- B) Second FYP — Agriculture and irrigation (Harrod–Domar model)
- C) Third FYP — Self-reliance and "Target Tripling" via the Gadgil formula
- D) Second FYP — Industrialisation based on the Mahalanobis model
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The **Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61)** was built on **P.C. Mahalanobis's four-sector industrialisation model** and emphasised heavy industries (steel, capital goods). The **First FYP (1951–56)** focused on agriculture and irrigation using the **Harrod–Domar** growth model. The Third Plan focused on self-reliance but did not use the "Target Tripling" phrase — that is an SSC trap from later plans.

**Trap:** Options A and B swap the two famous plan models (Harrod–Domar → 1st; Mahalanobis → 2nd). Many aspirants reverse them under time pressure.

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#Second Five-Year Plan (Then PM: J L Nehru)]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q2 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=9
India'"'"'s First Five-Year Plan (1951–1956) primarily focused on which sector, and which growth model did it adopt?

- A) Heavy industry; Mahalanobis model
- B) Agriculture and irrigation; Harrod–Domar model
- C) Self-reliance and removal of poverty; Nehru–Mahalanobis model
- D) Rolling plan framework; Rolling Plan model
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The First FYP (1951–56), authored largely under K.N. Raj and V.K.R.V. Rao with P.C. Mahalanobis on the Planning Commission, used the **Harrod–Domar growth model** and channelled ~44% of plan outlay into **agriculture, irrigation and rural development**, responding to the food crisis following Partition and the Bengal famine (1943).

**Trap:** Many aspirants conflate the First Plan with the Second Plan'"'"'s Mahalanobis industrialisation. The First Plan was the *agricultural* plan; the *Mahalanobis model* came only with the Second Plan.

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#First Five-Year Plan (1st April 1951)]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q3 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=9
The "Bombay Plan" (1944) was drafted by a group of leading industrialists. Who is widely credited as its principal architect?

- A) M.N. Roy
- B) J.R.D. Tata
- C) S.N. Agarwal
- D) P.C. Mahalanobis
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **Bombay Plan (A Brief Memorandum Outlining a Plan of Economic Development for India)**, published in January 1944, was drafted by eight leading industrialists led by **J.R.D. Tata**, with contributions from Ardeshir Dalal, John Mathai, G.D. Birla, Shroff, and others. P.C. Mahalanobis served only as the Plan'"'"'s statistical advisor.

**Trap:** P.C. Mahalanobis (option D) is the **statistical advisor** of the Bombay Plan, not its principal architect — and later designed the **Second FYP** model. M.N. Roy drafted the People'"'"'s Plan (1934), not the Bombay Plan.

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#Bombay Plan (1944)]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q4 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=9
The Second Industrial Policy Resolution (1956) classified industries into Schedule A, Schedule B and Schedule C. The correct interpretation is:

- A) Schedule A — exclusive state monopoly; Schedule B — joint/private; Schedule C — left to private sector
- B) Schedule A — exclusively private sector; Schedule B — state monopoly; Schedule C — reserved for foreign capital
- C) All three schedules allowed private sector dominance with state as facilitator
- D) Schedule A — only foreign investment; Schedule B — only public sector; Schedule C — only SMEs
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The 2nd IPR (1956) classified industries into:
- **Schedule A** (17 industries): exclusive state monopoly (arms, atomic energy, railways, etc.)
- **Schedule B** (12 industries): state-led but private participation allowed (with state holding majority)
- **Schedule C**: the rest, open to private sector with state regulation

Often called the **"Economic Constitution of India"**.

**Trap:** Option B is a complete reversal of A and B schedules. Option C misrepresents the 1956 socialist-mixed-economy tilt. Option D is fabricated (the 1956 IPR did not earmark sectors for foreign capital).

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#2nd IPR, 1956]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q5 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=9
The 1991 New Industrial Policy (LPG reforms) was announced by which Prime Minister, with which Finance Minister, and primarily ended which pre-reform regime?

- A) PM Rajiv Gandhi; FM V.P. Singh; ended the Industrial Policy Statement regime
- B) PM P.V. Narasimha Rao; FM Manmohan Singh; ended the Licence Raj / Inspector Raj
- C) PM Atal Bihari Vajpayee; FM Yashwant Sinha; ended the Hindu Rate of Growth
- D) PM Indira Gandhi; FM Pranab Mukherjee; ended the FERA controls
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **5th IPR / New Industrial Policy (24 July 1991)** was announced under PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao** with FM **Dr. Manmohan Singh**. It dismantled the **Licence Raj (industrial licensing)** and **Inspector Raj**, opened the economy via **L**iberalisation, **P**rivatisation and **G**lobalisation (LPG), devalued the rupee, and dismantled the MRTP Act'"'"'s restrictive provisions on monopolies.

**Trap:** V.P. Singh was Rajiv Gandhi'"'"'s FM (1985–87), not 1991. Yashwant Sinha was Vajpayee'"'"'s FM (1998–2004) — by then reforms were already in motion. Pranab Mukherjee was never FM during Indira Gandhi'"'"'s tenure.

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#5th IPR (New Industrial Policy, 1991 → Economic Liberalisation)]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q6 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=9
A "Plan Holiday" in India refers to the period when no Five-Year Plan was in operation. When were the Plan Holidays, and between which two consecutive FYPs did they fall?

- A) 1966–1969, between the Third and Fourth FYPs (due to wars and drought)
- B) 1978–1980, between the Fifth and Sixth FYPs (Janata Party government)
- C) 1990–1992, between the Seventh and Eighth FYPs (BoP crisis)
- D) Both A and B — India had two distinct Plan Holidays
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

India had **two Plan Holidays**:
1. **1966–1969** — between 3rd and 4th FYPs, due to the Indo-Pak Wars (1965, 1971 preparations), Sino-Indian War (1962 after-effects) and severe drought.
2. **1978–1980** — between 5th and 6th FYPs, when the **Janata Party government** under Morarji Desai scrapped the 6th FYP and experimented with the **Rolling Plan**.

Option C is wrong because 1990–92 were **Annual Plans** (a separate mechanism, not a holiday).

**Trap:** Many aspirants remember only the 1966–69 holiday and forget the 1978–80 one. Option C wrongly conflates Annual Plans with Plan Holidays.

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#Plan Holiday]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q7 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=9
Which institution replaced the Planning Commission of India, and in which year was the last regular Five-Year Plan concluded?

- A) NITI Aayog (2015); Twelfth FYP (2012–2017) was the last plan
- B) Finance Commission (2014); Tenth FYP (2002–2007) was the last plan
- C) NITI Aayog (2014); Tenth FYP (2002–2007) was the last plan
- D) PM'"'"'s Economic Advisory Council (2015); Eleventh FYP (2007–2012) was the last plan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The **NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India)** replaced the **Planning Commission** on **1 January 2015**. The **Twelfth Five-Year Plan (2012–2017)** under PM **Manmohan Singh** was the **last regular Five-Year Plan**; thereafter, India moved to a 3-year **Action Agenda / Vision documents** framework under NITI Aayog.

**Trap:** Year 2014 (option C) is wrong — NITI Aayog was set up on 1 Jan 2015 (after a Cabinet resolution on that date). Option D gets both year and last plan wrong.

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#NITI AAYOG]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=9
Three pre-Independence economic plans are often confused in SSC questions. Identify the correct attribution among these pairs:

- A) Bombay Plan — M.N. Roy, 1934, Marxist / decentralised
- B) People'"'"'s Plan — J.R.D. Tata, 1944, Industrialist-led
- C) Gandhian Plan — Shriman Narayan Agarwal, 1944, Village-based / Panchayati Raj
- D) Bombay Plan — J.R.D. Tata, 1944, Industrialist-led
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

Correct attribution:
- **Bombay Plan (1944)** — drafted by industrialists led by **J.R.D. Tata**.
- **People'"'"'s Plan (1934)** — drafted by **M.N. Roy** (communist/socialist perspective).
- **Gandhian Plan (1944)** — by **Shriman Narayan Agarwal**, focusing on village self-sufficiency.

**Trap:** Options A and B deliberately swap authors between the Bombay Plan and People'"'"'s Plan. Aspirants confuse the *year* (1944 vs 1934) and the *ideology* (industrialist vs Marxist). Option C is also historically correct for the Gandhian Plan, but the question'"'"'s canonical Bombay-Plan pairing is **D**.

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#Pre-Independence]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=9
The growth models used across India'"'"'s Five-Year Plans are an SSC favourite. Identify the correct pairing of plan and growth model:

- A) First FYP — Mahalanobis model; Second FYP — Harrod–Domar model
- B) First FYP — Harrod–Domar model; Second FYP — Mahalanobis model
- C) Second FYP — Gadgil formula; Third FYP — Mahalanobis model
- D) First FYP — Solow residual; Second FYP — Lewis dual-sector model
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Correct pairings:
- **First FYP (1951–56)** → **Harrod–Domar** growth model (agriculture focus).
- **Second FYP (1956–61)** → **Mahalanobis** four-sector industrialisation model (heavy industry focus).
- The **Gadgil formula / formula-based approach** was introduced during the **Fourth and Fifth FYPs** for resource allocation across states — not the Second Plan.

**Trap:** Options A and B are direct reversals. Option C is a complete mis-attribution. Option D invents foreign models (Solow, Lewis) that were not the basis of any Indian FYP.

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#First Five-Year Plan (1st April 1951)]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=9
The 4th Industrial Policy (1980) and the 5th Industrial Policy / New Industrial Policy (1991) are frequently confused. The correct distinguishing facts are:

- A) 4th IPR (1980) was by Rajiv Gandhi; 5th IPR (1991) was by Indira Gandhi
- B) 4th IPR (1980) was by Indira Gandhi (with FERA); 5th IPR (1991) was by Narasimha Rao (LPG reforms)
- C) 4th IPR (1980) was by Morarji Desai; 5th IPR (1991) was by Vajpayee
- D) 4th IPR (1980) abolished licensing; 5th IPR (1991) re-imposed licensing
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- **4th IPR (July 1980)** — announced under PM **Indira Gandhi**. It *eased* licensing for large units and investment limits, but retained the MRTP framework; accompanied by **FERA** (Foreign Exchange Regulation Act) tightening on foreign capital.
- **5th IPR / New Industrial Policy (July 1991)** — announced under PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**. It *abolished* industrial licensing for most sectors (except 5 reserved) and dismantled MRTP/Inspector Raj — initiating **LPG reforms**.

**Trap:** Option A reverses the PMs. Option C uses PMs from the wrong era (Morarji Desai was PM 1977–79; Vajpayee 1998–2004). Option D reverses the licensing direction — the 1980 IPR *reduced* licensing scope, while 1991 *largely abolished* it.

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#4th IPR, 1980]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q11 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=9
The Second Industrial Policy Resolution (1956) was sometimes called the "Economic Constitution of India." How many industries were placed under **Schedule A** (exclusive state monopoly) and **Schedule B** (state-led with private participation) respectively?

- A) Schedule A — 12 industries; Schedule B — 17 industries
- B) Schedule A — 17 industries; Schedule B — 12 industries
- C) Schedule A — 18 industries; Schedule B — 12 industries
- D) Schedule A — 17 industries; Schedule B — 13 industries
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **2nd IPR (1956)** placed **17 industries in Schedule A** (exclusive state monopoly — defence, atomic energy, railways, mining of key minerals, etc.) and **12 industries in Schedule B** (state-led but private participation permitted — coal, iron & steel, shipbuilding, etc.). The remaining industries fell under Schedule C (private sector-led). The 1st IPR (1948) had only 3 categories without precise Schedule A/B/C enumeration as later refined in 1956.

**Trap:** Option A swaps the two counts (12 and 17). Option C uses 18 (off by one — easy slip). Option D uses 13 for Schedule B (off by one in the other direction).

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#2nd IPR, 1956]]
</details>

@qeco.planning.q12 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=9
The last regular Five-Year Plan of India was the Twelfth Plan. Which Prime Minister oversaw it, during which years did it run, and what was its theme?

- A) PM Manmohan Singh; 2012–2017; theme — "Faster, More Inclusive and Sustainable Growth"
- B) PM Narendra Modi; 2012–2017; theme — "India 2030"
- C) PM Atal Bihari Vajpayee; 2007–2012; theme — "Towards a New India"
- D) PM Manmohan Singh; 2007–2012; theme — "Faster, More Inclusive and Sustainable Growth"
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The **Twelfth Five-Year Plan (2012–2017)** ran under PM **Dr. Manmohan Singh** with the theme **"Faster, More Inclusive and Sustainable Growth."** It targeted 8% GDP growth, with sub-targets on health, education and gender. After its conclusion, NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission and India abandoned Five-Year Plans in favour of 3-year Action Agendas (2017–2020, 2020–2025, etc.).

**Trap:** Option D swaps the years (the theme matches but the period is wrong — that theme/period combo is for the 12th, not the 11th). Option B misattributes the PM (Modi took office only in 2014). Option C uses the wrong PM and wrong years (Vajpayee'"'"'s last plan was the 10th).

**See also:** [[../../economics/09 - Five Year Plan and Industrial Policy Resolution#Twelfth Five-Year Plan (Then PM: Manmohan Singh)]]
</details>
