@qeco.budget.q1 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=5
The government sells a 10% stake in a public-sector oil company to private investors and credits the proceeds to the Consolidated Fund of India. On the receipts side, this transaction is correctly classified as:

- A) Tax revenue
- B) Non-tax revenue
- C) Revenue receipt
- D) Capital receipt
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

Proceeds from disinvestment reduce a government *asset* (its ownership stake in the PSU). Because the inflow either creates a liability or reduces an asset, it falls under **capital receipts**, not revenue receipts. Revenue receipts are non-redeemable and neither create liabilities nor reduce assets.

**Trap:** Candidates often classify disinvestment proceeds as "revenue from sale of an asset" and pick C. The rule is: receipts that reduce an asset are always *capital*, even if the underlying asset is a financial one rather than a building.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Capital]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q2 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=5
The Annual Financial Statement (Union Budget of India) is a constitutional requirement under which Article of the Constitution?

- A) Article 110
- B) Article 112
- C) Article 114
- D) Article 116
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Article 112** of the Constitution mandates that the government lay the Annual Financial Statement — a statement of estimated receipts and expenditure — before both Houses of Parliament for every financial year. Article 110 deals with Money Bills, Article 114 with the Appropriation Bill, and Article 116 with votes on account/exceptional grants.

**Trap:** A) Article 110 is the common distractor — it actually defines what constitutes a Money Bill, not the budget itself.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Budget]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q3 difficulty=recall subject=economics chapter=5
Who presented the first Union Budget of independent India?

- A) C.D. Deshmukh
- B) John Mathai
- C) R.K. Shanmukham Chetty
- D) Liaquat Ali Khan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**R.K. Shanmukham Chetty** presented the first budget of independent India on 26 November 1947. John Mathai presented the second. C.D. Deshmukh was a later Finance Minister, not the first.

**Trap:** Many candidates guess John Mathai (the second budget) or C.D. Deshmukh (a famous early RBI Governor-turned-FM), but the historical first was Shanmukham Chetty.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Budget]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q4 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=5
A quiz contestant is told that one Finance Minister presented the Union Budget 10 times in total, including a record 6 consecutive years. The Finance Minister being described is:

- A) P. Chidambaram
- B) Pranab Mukherjee
- C) Morarji Desai
- D) Nirmala Sitharaman
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Morarji Desai** presented the Union Budget 10 times in total — including a record **6 budgets in a row** — making him the most prolific presenter. P. Chidambaram follows with 9, Pranab Mukherjee with 8, and Nirmala Sitharaman with 8 (with her own consecutive-budget record).

**Trap:** Nirmala Sitharaman's recent streak makes her a tempting distractor, but the "10 budgets" overall record still belongs to Morarji Desai.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Most Number of Budget]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q5 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=5
The Indian government decided in 2016, on the basis of a committee's recommendations, to merge the Railway Budget with the General Budget, and the merger took effect from the 2017-18 financial year. Which committee recommended this merger?

- A) Acworth Committee
- B) Kothari Commission
- C) Bibek Debroy Committee
- D) NITI Aayog
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **Bibek Debroy Committee** recommended merging the Railway Budget into the General Budget; the merger was implemented from **2017-18**. The earlier **Acworth Committee** (1921) had in fact recommended the *opposite* — the *separation* of Railway and General Budgets.

**Trap:** A) Acworth Committee is the trick — it's the right committee for Railway Budget history, but in the opposite direction (separation, 1921).

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Railway and General Budget]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q6 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=5
During a recession, the government announces a large package of increased spending on rural employment guarantee works and cuts excise duties on consumer durables. The primary function of the budget being exercised here is:

- A) Allocative function
- B) Redistributive function
- C) Stabilisation function
- D) Developmental function
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **stabilisation function** of the budget refers to the use of government spending and taxation to smooth out the business cycle — counteracting recession (by raising spending or cutting taxes) or inflation (by doing the opposite). Both legs of the action described here — higher spending *and* tax cuts — are textbook stabilisation tools.

**Trap:** A) Allocative is about *which* goods the government funds (public vs private); B) Redistributive is about *who* ends up with the income (progressive taxation, welfare transfers). Neither matches the anti-recession scenario.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Functions of Budget]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q7 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=5
Among the following items in the Union Budget, which one is correctly classified as **non-tax revenue** of the government?

- A) Corporation tax
- B) Goods and Services Tax (GST)
- C) Dividend received by the government from the Reserve Bank of India
- D) Customs duty
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The RBI's surplus dividend paid to the central government is a classic example of **non-tax revenue** — earnings from sources other than taxation (PSU earnings, interest receipts, fees, fines, dividends). Corporation tax and customs duty are *tax* revenue, and GST (central share) is also a tax.

**Trap:** Dividends and profits from PSUs/RBI *look* like earnings from government business and many candidates tag them as "tax". They are not — they are explicitly non-tax revenue receipts.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Revenue Receipts]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=5
The government spends ₹5,000 crore on salaries and pensions of its employees and another ₹3,000 crore on interest payments on its past borrowings. These two items are, by definition, examples of:

- A) Capital expenditure — because salaries create human capital
- B) Capital expenditure — because interest payments reduce future liabilities
- C) Revenue expenditure — they neither create assets nor reduce liabilities
- D) Revenue expenditure — only because they are recurring in nature
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

By definition, **revenue expenditure** is expenditure that *does not create any asset* and *does not reduce any liability*. Salaries, pensions and interest payments fall into this category irrespective of their recurring nature. They are part of the day-to-day cost of running the government.

**Trap:** A) Human-capital arguments are tempting but not the legal classification; D) "Recurring" is *correlated* with revenue expenditure but is not the defining criterion — the real test is whether the spending creates an asset or reduces a liability.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Revenue Expenditure]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=5
Which of the following is **correctly** described as a capital receipt of the government?

- A) Income tax collected from salaried employees
- B) Disinvestment proceeds from sale of a PSU stake
- C) Dividends received from the RBI
- D) Interest received on loans given by the central government to states
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A **capital receipt** is one that either *creates a liability* or *reduces an asset* of the government. Disinvestment reduces the government's asset (its equity stake in the PSU), so the proceeds are a capital receipt. Income tax, RBI dividends, and interest on loans to states are all examples of **revenue receipts** — they neither create liabilities nor reduce assets.

**Trap:** C) is the most common wrong pick — students confuse RBI dividends (a non-tax *revenue* receipt) with disinvestment (a *capital* receipt). Both come from financial assets, but only disinvestment reduces the government's existing holding.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Capital]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=economics chapter=5
In the standard preparation and presentation chain of the Union Budget, which of the following correctly sequences the authorities involved?
- A) Department of Economic Affairs prepares; Finance Minister presents; President lays the Annual Financial Statement before Parliament
- B) Finance Minister prepares; Department of Economic Affairs presents; Prime Minister lays the statement before Parliament
- C) President prepares; Finance Minister presents; Department of Economic Affairs lays the statement before Parliament
- D) Reserve Bank prepares; Finance Minister presents; Prime Minister lays the statement before Parliament
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The **Department of Economic Affairs** (under the Ministry of Finance) **prepares** the Union Budget. The **Finance Minister** then **presents** it to the Lok Sabha. Finally, as required by **Article 112**, the **President** causes the Annual Financial Statement to be **laid before Parliament**. This three-stage chain is the constitutional and administrative sequence every year.

**Trap:** Option (B) swaps prepare/present (the FM is the *presenter*, not the *preparer*). Option (C) wrongly puts the President at the start of preparation. Option (D) wrongly attributes preparation to the RBI, which is the monetary — not fiscal — authority.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Budget]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q11 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=5
A Union Budget shows the following committed outflows for the upcoming year: ₹4,80,000 crore in salaries and pensions of central-government employees, ₹6,30,000 crore in interest payments on past government borrowings, ₹2,20,000 crore in subsidies on food and fertiliser, and ₹1,10,000 crore in defence pensions. According to standard categorisation, which of these items is the largest component of the non-plan revenue expenditure?
- A) Defence pensions
- B) Interest payments on government debt
- C) Salaries of central government employees
- D) Subsidies on food and fertiliser
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Of the four items, **interest payments (₹6,30,000 crore)** are the largest. Historically, interest obligations on accumulated central-government borrowing have been the single biggest component of India’s non-plan revenue expenditure. Salaries + pensions (₹4,80,000 cr + ₹1,10,000 cr in defence pensions = ₹5,90,000 cr) are large but trail interest payments, as do subsidies (₹2,20,000 cr).

**Trap:** Option (C) is the second-largest line, but the question asks for the *largest*; many candidates pick salaries because they are visible and salient. Option (D) subsidies are large but a distant third. Option (A) defence pensions look big but are only ₹1,10,000 cr.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Revenue Expenditure]]
</details>

@qeco.budget.q12 difficulty=apply subject=economics chapter=5
A Group of Ministers is reviewing the fiscal marks of recent Union Budgets to understand the trajectory of social-sector accountability reporting. They find that in FY 2004–05 the government first published a *Statement on Gender Budgeting* alongside the regular budget documents, but it was not until the next FY that a fully separate **Gender Budget** charter disaggregating budget allocations by gender impact was introduced as a stand-alone section. In which financial year was this fully separate Gender Sensitive Budget first introduced?
- A) 2001–02
- B) 2004–05
- C) 2005–06
- D) 2007–08
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **Gender Sensitive Budget** was introduced in the Union Budget for **2005–06**. The earlier FY 2004–05 saw the first *Statement* on gender budgeting, but the fully separate Gender Budget charter (which disaggregates allocations by their gender impact and was published as a stand-alone section) was introduced the next year. After 2005–06, the Ministry of Finance published the annual Gender Budget Statement alongside the regular budget documents.

**Trap:** Option (B) 2004–05 is the off-by-one distractor — many aspirants pick it because it was the year of the first gender-budgeting *statement*, not the year of the fully separate *Gender Budget* charter. Options (A) and (D) are clearly earlier and later distractors.

**See also:** [[../../economics/05 - Budget and Taxation#Most Number of Budget]]
</details>
