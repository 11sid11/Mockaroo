# Boundary Lines of India — Questions

> Source: [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India|Boundary Lines of India]]

SSC CGL Tier-I MCQs on India's neighbours, boundary lines, passes, and classic line-pair traps.

@qgk.boundary_lines.q1 difficulty=recall subject=static-gk chapter=17
The Radcliffe Line was drawn in 1947 principally to demarcate which boundary?
- A) India and China
- B) India and Pakistan
- C) Afghanistan and Pakistan
- D) North Korea and South Korea
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Sir Cyril Radcliffe drew the India–Pakistan boundary in 1947; its eastern section later became the India–Bangladesh boundary.

**Trap:** McMahon is India–China; Durand is Afghanistan–Pakistan.

**See also:** [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India#Internal boundaries (between Indian states)]]
</details>

@qgk.boundary_lines.q2 difficulty=recall subject=static-gk chapter=17
Which country shares India's longest land border?
- A) China
- B) Pakistan
- C) Bangladesh
- D) Nepal
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — The India–Bangladesh border is approximately 4,096 km long, India's longest land border.

**Trap:** China's border is approximately 3,488 km and is second longest.

**See also:** [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India#India's land borders — overview]]
</details>

@qgk.boundary_lines.q3 difficulty=recall subject=static-gk chapter=17
The Durand Line separates:
- A) India and Pakistan
- B) India and China
- C) Afghanistan and Pakistan
- D) Afghanistan and India
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — The Durand Line was drawn in 1893 by Sir Mortimer Durand between Afghanistan and Pakistan.

**Trap:** It is not an India–Afghanistan line, despite India's inherited border claim through the Wakhan corridor.

**See also:** [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India#Historical Boundary Lines]]
</details>

@qgk.boundary_lines.q4 difficulty=recall subject=static-gk chapter=17
Sir Creek is a disputed estuary between:
- A) Gujarat and Sindh
- B) Punjab and Punjab
- C) Rajasthan and Balochistan
- D) Ladakh and Xinjiang
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — Sir Creek lies between Gujarat's Kutch region and Pakistan's Sindh province.

**Trap:** Attari–Wagah, not Sir Creek, is the famous Punjab border crossing.

**See also:** [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India#Internal boundaries (between Indian states)]]
</details>

@qgk.boundary_lines.q5 difficulty=apply subject=static-gk chapter=17
A boundary was drawn at the 1914 Simla Convention and is disputed by China. Which line is it?
- A) Radcliffe Line
- B) McMahon Line
- C) Durand Line
- D) Maginot Line
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Sir Henry McMahon drew the McMahon Line at the 1914 Simla Convention in the eastern India–China sector.

**Trap:** Radcliffe is tied to 1947 and India–Pakistan.

**See also:** [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India#Historical Boundary Lines]]
</details>

@qgk.boundary_lines.q6 difficulty=apply subject=static-gk chapter=17
The de facto India–China boundary, discussed in Western, Middle and Eastern sectors, is called:
- A) Line of Control
- B) Line of Actual Control
- C) McMahon Award
- D) Zero Line
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The Line of Actual Control (LAC) is the de facto India–China boundary.

**Trap:** The LoC concerns India and Pakistan in Jammu and Kashmir/Ladakh.

**See also:** [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India#Internal boundaries (between Indian states)]]
</details>


@qgk.boundary_lines.q7 difficulty=apply subject=static-gk chapter=17

@qgk.boundary_lines.q8 difficulty=apply subject=static-gk chapter=17
Which Indian state shares international borders with Nepal, Bhutan and China?
- A) Assam
- B) West Bengal
- C) Sikkim
- D) Tripura
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — Sikkim borders Nepal, Bhutan and China.

@qgk.boundary_lines.q9 difficulty=apply subject=static-gk chapter=17
A traveller crosses from Sikkim into Tibet through a pass reopened for trade in 2006. Which pass is this?

@qgk.boundary_lines.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=static-gk chapter=17
Which option correctly distinguishes the LoC from the LAC?
- A) LoC: India–China; LAC: India–Pakistan
- B) LoC: India–Pakistan; LAC: India–China
- C) Both are officially demarcated India–Pakistan borders
- D) Both refer exclusively to India–Myanmar
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The LoC separates Indian- and Pakistani-controlled areas; the LAC is the de facto India–China boundary.

**Trap:** The similar abbreviations are frequently reversed.

**See also:** [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India#Key concepts]]
</details>

@qgk.boundary_lines.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=static-gk chapter=17
Which pairs are correctly matched?
1. Johnson Line — Aksai Chin claim
2. McMahon Line — eastern India–China sector
3. Radcliffe Line — Afghanistan–Pakistan
- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — Johnson and McMahon are correctly paired; Afghanistan–Pakistan is the Durand Line, not Radcliffe.

**Trap:** Radcliffe divided British India into India and Pakistan.

**See also:** [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India#Quick recall for exam]]
</details>

@qgk.boundary_lines.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=static-gk chapter=17
Which pair is incorrectly matched?
- A) 38th Parallel — North Korea and South Korea
- B) 49th Parallel — USA and Canada
- C) Oder–Neisse Line — Germany and Poland
- D) Maginot Line — India and Myanmar
@@
<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D** — The Maginot Line was a French fortification facing Germany, not an India–Myanmar boundary.

**Trap:** The 17th Parallel may be associated with Vietnam and is also used in the source's India–Myanmar discussion; neither is the Maginot Line.

**See also:** [[../../static-gk/17 - Boundary Lines of India#International boundaries outside India (frequently asked)]]
</details>
