@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=16
Indias vegetation is broadly classified into how many groups?

- A) 4
- B) 5
- C) 6
- D) 7
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Indias vegetation is broadly divided into 5 groups." The five are: Evergreen, Deciduous, Thorn, Coniferous, and Montane/Mangrove (the chapter also lists Mangrove separately).

**Trap:** Candidates confuse the count with the number of soil types (8 in chapter 17) or the number of wildlife schedules (6 in chapter 20). Each chapter has its own numbered lists — be careful.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#VEGETATION/FORESTS]]
</details>

@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=16
Tropical Evergreen Forests are found in India in which of the following regions?

- A) Rajasthan and Gujarat
- B) Western side of the Western Ghats, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, North-East, Tamil Nadu coast
- C) Indo-Gangetic plain and Punjab
- D) Upper Himalayas and Ladakh
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "In India, they are seen in western side of Western Ghats, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, North-East, Tamil Nadu coast (in heavy rainfall regions)." Evergreen forests need rainfall of 200 cm and temperature above 22 degrees C.

**Trap:** A is a region with thorn forests (< 70 cm rainfall); D is coniferous (high snowfall); C is alluvial/deciduous.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#Evergreen Forests/Tropical Rainforests]]
</details>

@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=16
The Chipko Movement began in 1973 in:

- A) Tehri Garhwal, Uttarakhand
- B) Chamoli district of Uttarakhand (then Uttar Pradesh)
- C) Kumaon hills, Uttarakhand
- D) Garhwal region of Himachal Pradesh
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "The Chipko Movement was a forest conservation movement in India. It began in 1973 in the Chamoli district of Uttarakhand (then Uttar Pradesh) — Led by Sundarlal Bahuguna along with local villagers to protect trees from being cut down by government-backed commercial loggers."

**Trap:** A and C are nearby districts in Uttarakhand. D is the wrong state (Himachal, not Uttarakhand). The chapter pinpoints Chamoli.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#Chipko Movement]]
</details>

@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=16
A region in India receives rainfall of 90 cm per year. Which vegetation type is most likely to be found there?

- A) Tropical Evergreen Forest
- B) Moist Deciduous Forest
- C) Dry Deciduous Forest
- D) Thorn Forest
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter classifies: Tropical Evergreen > 200 cm; Moist Deciduous 100-200 cm; Dry Deciduous 70-100 cm; Thorn Forest < 70 cm. 90 cm falls in the Dry Deciduous range.

**Trap:** B is tempting because 90 cm is close to 100 cm. But the *boundaries* in the chapter are strict: 100-200 cm = moist; 70-100 cm = dry. 90 < 100, so dry deciduous.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#Deciduous Forest]]
</details>

@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=16
Match each shifting-cultivation name to the correct Indian region:

- Region 1: North-East India
- Region 2: Odisha
- Region 3: Rajasthan
- Region 4: Chhattisgarh (Bastar district) and A&N Islands

Which option correctly maps all four to their local shifting-cultivation names (Jhum / Pama Dabi or Bringa / Waltre / Dipa)?

- A) 1-Jhum, 2-Pama Dabi, 3-Waltre, 4-Dipa
- B) 1-Pama Dabi, 2-Jhum, 3-Waltre, 4-Dipa
- C) 1-Jhum, 2-Waltre, 3-Pama Dabi, 4-Dipa
- D) 1-Dipa, 2-Waltre, 3-Pama Dabi, 4-Jhum
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter lists: "North-East: Jhum. Western Ghats: Kumari. Odisha: Pama Dabi/Bringa. Andhra Pradesh: Penda/Podu. Chhattisgarh (Bastar district) and Andaman and Nicobar Islands: Dipa. Rajasthan: Waltre."

**Trap:** D reverses Odisha and Chhattisgarh. Candidates mix up Waltre (Rajasthan) with Waltre being in central India.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#Shifting Cultivation]]
</details>

@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=16
A grassland region receives 500-1500 mm of rainfall per year and lies between 35-50 degrees N/S latitude. Which biome is this?

- A) Tropical Grassland (Savannah)
- B) Temperate Grassland (Prairies)
- C) Taiga / Boreal Biome
- D) Tundra
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter describes Temperate Grassland as "short and nutritious" grasses, found in regions with 500-1500 mm rainfall at 35-50 degrees N/S. Tropical Grasslands (savannah) are at lower latitudes near the equator.

**Trap:** A is in the tropical latitudes (10-20 degrees N/S typically). C is the Taiga at 50-70 degrees. D is Tundra beyond 70 degrees.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#World Biomes]]
</details>

@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=16
According to the 2023 Indian State of Forest Report, which state has the largest forest cover (in absolute terms)?

- A) Arunachal Pradesh
- B) Chhattisgarh
- C) Madhya Pradesh
- D) Mizoram
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Forest cover (area-wise): Madhya Pradesh > Arunachal Pradesh > Chhattisgarh." Mizoram has the highest *percentage* forest cover, not the largest area.

**Trap:** D is the trap because Mizoram ranks #1 in *percentage* (forest cover %). The chapter is clear: Madhya Pradesh is #1 in absolute area.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#Indian State of Forest Reports]]
</details>

@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=16
Consider the following statements about mangrove forests in India:
(1) Mangroves are found in the Sundarbans, the Godavari delta, the Mahanadi delta, and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
(2) The Sundarbans gets its name from the "sundari" mangrove tree and is the largest mangrove forest in the world.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both statements are true
- B) Only statement 1 is true
- C) Only statement 2 is true; statement 1 is false (Sundarbans is in Bangladesh, not India)
- D) Both statements are false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter lists mangrove locations: "In India, mangrove forests are found in the Sundarbans (largest), Godavari delta, Mahanadi delta, and the Andaman and Nicobar islands." Both statements are true.

**Trap:** C is a tempting wrong answer because the *bulk* of the Sundarbans is in Bangladesh (the Indian portion is the southern tip of the South 24 Parganas). But the Indian Sundarbans exists and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The chapter explicitly lists Sundarbans as an Indian mangrove.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#Mangrove Forest]]
</details>

@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=16
Consider the following pair of similar-sounding grasslands:
(1) Campos — a tropical grassland in Brazil.
(2) Llanos — a temperate grassland in Venezuela.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both statements are true
- B) Only statement 1 is true; statement 2 is false (Llanos is in Venezuela but it is a TROPICAL grassland, not temperate)
- C) Only statement 2 is true; statement 1 is false
- D) Both statements are false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Statement 1 is true: Campos is a tropical grassland in Brazil (per the chapter table). Statement 2 is false: Llanos is in Venezuela, but the chapter lists it under *Tropical* Grasslands, not Temperate. Pampas (Argentina) is the temperate grassland in South America.

**Trap:** Candidates confuse "tropical vs temperate" because both South American grasslands are commonly taught. Llanos is tropical, Pampas is temperate.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#GRASSLANDS]]
</details>

@qgeo.forest_and_grassland.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=16
Assertion (A): The Indian Forest Policy (NFP) 1952 (last revised in 1988) targets 33 percent of the geographical area to be under forest or tree cover.
Reason (R): According to the 2023 Indian State of Forest Report, the combined forest and tree cover of India is 25.17 percent.
Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Both A and R are factually true per the chapter: NFP 1952/1988 targets 33 percent; 2023 report shows 25.17 percent forest + trees cover (forest 21.7 percent + tree 3.41 percent). However, R does not *explain* A — it merely states the *current status* against the target. R describes the *gap* between target (33 percent) and current (25.17 percent), but does not causally explain why the target was set at 33 percent.

**Trap:** A is the obvious "looks-right" pick because A and R are both factually correct. But R describes a measurement, not a reason for the policy — so R does not explain A.

**See also:** [[../../geography/16 - Forest and Grassland#Indian State of Forest Reports]]
</details>
