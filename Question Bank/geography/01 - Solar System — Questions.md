@qgeo.solar_system.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=1
Which scientist proposed the Big Bang Theory of the origin of the universe, and in which year?

- A) Edwin Hubble, 1929
- B) Fred Hoyle, 1948
- C) George Lemaître, 1931
- D) Albert Einstein, 1905
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

George Lemaître proposed the Big Bang Theory in 1931, suggesting that the universe began with the explosion of an infinitely hot and dense single point around 13.8 billion years ago, leading to the formation of galaxies and other heavenly bodies.

**Trap:** Option A is the name often confused with cosmology (Edwin Hubble observed galaxy recession); Fred Hoyle proposed the rival Steady State Theory, not the Big Bang.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Big Bang Theory]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=1
Which galaxy is the nearest to the Milky Way?

- A) Triangulum
- B) Andromeda
- C) Magellanic Cloud
- D) Cygnus A
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Andromeda is the nearest galaxy to the Milky Way. Our Solar System lies in the Milky Way galaxy (also known as Akash Ganga), specifically in the Orion arm / Orion spur.

**Trap:** The Magellanic Clouds are satellite galaxies of the Milky Way, not the nearest independent galaxy. Triangulum is the third largest member of the Local Group after Andromeda and Milky Way.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Galaxy]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=1
Halley's Comet, which returns periodically, has an orbital period of approximately:

- A) 56 years
- B) 76 years
- C) 88 years
- D) 100 years
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Halley's Comet is seen every 76 years; it was last seen in 1986 and is expected to return in 2061. Comets are large, icy objects orbiting the Sun made of dust, rock, and frozen gases like water, carbon dioxide, ammonia, and methane.

**Trap:** 88 years is Mercury's revolution period, not Halley's. Students often confuse planetary orbital periods with comet periods.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Comets]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=1
Which is the hottest planet in the Solar System?

- A) Mercury
- B) Venus
- C) Mars
- D) Jupiter
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Venus is the hottest planet in the Solar System. It is also known as the Morning Star, Evening Star, or Lucifer, and is the brightest planet seen from Earth.

**Trap:** Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun, so proximity makes it a tempting choice; however, the chapter identifies Venus as the hottest planet.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Venus]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=1
A meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere and burns up, creating a streak of light. By what name is this phenomenon commonly known?

- A) Comet
- B) Meteor (Shooting Star)
- C) Meteorite
- D) Asteroid
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

When a meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere, it burns up and creates a streak of light called a meteor (commonly known as a Shooting Star). If it survives the trip and hits the ground, it is then called a meteorite.

**Trap:** Many students confuse meteor (burning up in atmosphere) with meteorite (hits ground). The key is: meteoroid = in space; meteor = burning up; meteorite = landed.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Meteor]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=1
The Sun accounts for approximately what percentage of the total mass of the Solar System?

- A) 90.86%
- B) 95.86%
- C) 99.86%
- D) 99.99%
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Sun makes up about 99.86% of the total mass of the Solar System. It is the only star in our solar system, composed of Hydrogen (73%) and Helium (25–28%), and is about 149.6 million km (≈15 crore km) from Earth, with light reaching us in 8 minutes 20 seconds.

**Trap:** Option D (99.99%) is a tempting distractor for "most of the mass"; the precise value stated in the source is 99.86%.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#THE SUN]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=1
Arrange the following planets in decreasing order of size: Earth, Jupiter, Mercury, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Venus, Mars.

- A) Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Earth, Venus, Mars, Mercury
- B) Jupiter, Saturn, Neptune, Uranus, Earth, Venus, Mars, Mercury
- C) Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Venus, Earth, Mars, Mercury
- D) Saturn, Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune, Earth, Venus, Mars, Mercury
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The planet size order (decreasing) is: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Earth, Venus, Mars, Mercury. This is easy to remember with the mnemonic "J S U N E V M M".

**Trap:** Options C and D swap Earth and Venus, or Saturn and Jupiter. Earth is slightly larger than Venus, and Jupiter is the largest planet.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Planets from Increasing to Decreasing Order]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=1
A planet has the following characteristics: it is the farthest from the Sun, has a revolution period of 165 years, and appears bluish due to methane. The planet is:

- A) Uranus
- B) Neptune
- C) Saturn
- D) Jupiter
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun, has the longest revolution of 165 years, is bluish due to methane in its atmosphere, and is the windiest planet. It was discovered by Johann Galle and Urbain Le Verrier in 1846 via mathematical prediction.

**Trap:** Uranus is also bluish-green due to methane — students confuse the two. Only Neptune is the farthest with the 165-year revolution.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Neptune]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=1
The Moon always shows the same face to the Earth (about 60% visible). The main reason for this is:

- A) The Moon does not rotate on its axis
- B) The Moon's rotation period equals its revolution period (27.3 days)
- C) The Moon is tidally locked due to Earth's gravity
- D) The Moon's orbit is perfectly circular
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Moon's rotation time equals its revolution time (27.3 days), so the same side (the near side) is always visible from Earth. The far side (~40%) is never visible to the naked eye.

**Trap:** Option A is the wrong reason — the Moon does rotate, just at the same rate it orbits. Option C is the conceptual outcome but the source explicitly states the reason as rotation = revolution.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Moon]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=1
Which of the following statements about the Sun's layers is correctly ordered from innermost (visible surface) to outermost?

- A) Photosphere → Chromosphere → Corona
- B) Chromosphere → Photosphere → Corona
- C) Corona → Chromosphere → Photosphere
- D) Photosphere → Corona → Chromosphere
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Sun's layers from inside (visible surface) out are: Photosphere (the only visible surface) → Chromosphere (visible as a dim red ring) → Corona (the outermost layer, extremely hot, visible during solar eclipses).

**Trap:** Option C reverses the order completely. Students confuse "corona" with the innermost because it sounds grand/important; in fact, corona is the outermost atmospheric layer.

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Layers of Sun]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=1
Match the following planets with their distinguishing characteristics: (1) Jupiter, (2) Saturn, (3) Uranus, (4) Neptune.

- A) (1) Largest; (2) Windiest; (3) Blue-green; (4) Least dense
- B) (1) Largest; (2) Least dense; (3) Rolling/Lopsided; (4) Windiest
- C) (1) Shortest day; (2) Blue-green; (3) Windiest; (4) Least dense
- D) (1) Most moons; (2) Largest; (3) Rolling/Lopsided; (4) Bluish
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Jupiter is the largest planet (with shortest rotation of 9 h 55 min). Saturn has the least density (0.69 g/cm³, can float in water). Uranus is tilted at 98° and is called the "Rolling/Lopsided Planet". Neptune is the windiest planet with the longest revolution (165 years).

**Trap:** Option A swaps Saturn and Neptune characteristics. Option D misattributes "most moons" — while Jupiter has 95 moons, the question's distinctive feature being asked is "largest", not "most moons".

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#PLANETS]]
</details>

@qgeo.solar_system.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=1
Which of the following is NOT a characteristic that a body must satisfy to be classified as a dwarf planet by the IAU?

- A) It must orbit the Sun
- B) It must have a nearly spherical shape
- C) It must have cleared its orbit of debris
- D) It must not have cleared its orbit of debris
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The three IAU criteria for a dwarf planet are: (1) orbit the Sun, (2) have a nearly spherical shape, and (3) NOT have cleared its orbit of debris. Option C states the opposite of the actual criterion and is therefore NOT a requirement.

**Trap:** The trap is the "double negative" wording. Option D states the actual criterion correctly. Option C uses the negation of D and is the wrong answer, even though it sounds like a "real" criterion because it uses the word "cleared".

**See also:** [[../../geography/01 - Solar System#Three Characteristics of Dwarf Planets]]
</details>
