@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=3
Which layer of the Earth is composed of Silica and Aluminium (SiAl)?

- A) Mantle
- B) Core
- C) Crust
- D) Asthenosphere
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Crust is the outermost layer composed mainly of Silica and Aluminium, hence called SiAl. The Mantle is SiMa (Silica + Magnesium) and the Core is NiFe (Nickel + Iron).

**Trap:** Students confuse SiAl (Crust) with SiMa (Mantle) — remember 'Sial' = 'crust like salt on top'.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Crust]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=3
The discontinuity between the lower crust and the upper mantle is known as:

- A) Conrad discontinuity
- B) Mohorovicic discontinuity
- C) Guttenberg discontinuity
- D) Lehmann discontinuity
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Mohorovicic (Moho) discontinuity separates the lower crust from the upper mantle. It was discovered by Andrija Mohorovicic in 1909.

**Trap:** Conrad is between upper and lower crust; Guttenberg is between lower mantle and outer core; Lehmann is between outer and inner core.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Earth's Discontinuity]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=3
Who proposed the Convection Current Theory to explain the movement of tectonic plates?

- A) Alfred Wegener
- B) Arthur Holmes
- C) McKenzie and Parker
- D) Harry Hess
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Arthur Holmes proposed the Convection Current Theory, theorising that convection currents move through the mantle the same way heated air circulates through a room, and these currents reshape the Earth's surface.

**Trap:** McKenzie and Parker gave the Theory of Plate Tectonics (1967); Wegener gave the Continental Drift Theory (1912); Hess proposed seafloor spreading. Don't confuse the names.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Force of Drifting]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=3
Which seismic wave cannot pass through the Earth's outer core?

- A) P-waves (Primary waves)
- B) S-waves (Secondary waves)
- C) Rayleigh waves
- D) Love waves
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

S-waves (Secondary waves) are transverse — they can only travel through solids. The outer core is liquid, so S-waves cannot pass through it, creating the large S-wave shadow zone. P-waves (longitudinal) can pass through both solids and liquids.

**Trap:** Students often pick P-waves because they are more famous; in fact, P-waves travel through all media, while S-waves travel only through solids.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Body Waves]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
A geologist measures the volume percentages of Earth's layers as 1%, 84%, and 15%. The layer with 84% volume and the layer with 15% volume are respectively:

- A) Crust and Mantle
- B) Crust and Core
- C) Mantle and Core
- D) Mantle and Crust
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

By volume, the distribution is Crust 1%, Mantle 84%, Core 15%. The Mantle has the largest volume (84%) and the Core has the second largest (15%).

**Trap:** Mass-wise, the Core is 31% and Mantle is 68%. Students confuse mass% with volume%. Volume and mass distributions of Earth's layers are different!

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Core]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
A divergent plate boundary is one where:

- A) Plates collide and one subducts beneath the other, destroying crust
- B) Plates move apart, lava erupts, and new crust is formed
- C) Plates slide past each other horizontally without creating or destroying crust
- D) Plates converge and form mountains
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

At a divergent plate boundary, plates move away from each other, causing lava to explode and leading to new crust formation. Convergent boundaries destroy crust via subduction; transform boundaries slide past each other without creating or destroying crust.

**Trap:** Option A describes a convergent boundary (subduction), and Option C describes a transform boundary. The three boundary types are easy to mix up.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Three Types of Plate Boundary]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
The point on the Earth's surface directly above the underground point where an earthquake originates is called:

- A) Hypocentre
- B) Focus
- C) Epicentre
- D) Seismograph
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Epicentre is the point on the Earth's surface directly above the earthquake's focus (hypocentre) where the earthquake originates underground. The Hypocentre (focus) is the point within the Earth where the earthquake actually begins.

**Trap:** Hypocentre and Focus are the same thing — the underground origin point. Some students pick B thinking Focus is the surface point, but the surface point is the Epicentre.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Earthquake]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
The "Ring of Fire" is a horse-shoe-shaped region primarily around which ocean?

- A) Atlantic Ocean
- B) Indian Ocean
- C) Pacific Ocean
- D) Arctic Ocean
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Ring of Fire is a horse-shoe-shaped region around the Pacific Ocean, a result of plate tectonics. It is highly earthquake-prone (90% of earthquakes) and volcano-prone (75% of volcanoes).

**Trap:** Option A (Atlantic Ocean) has the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, but the "Ring of Fire" specifically refers to the Pacific basin's borders.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Ring of Fire]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
Match the earthquake type with its cause: (1) Collapse earthquake, (2) Explosion earthquake, (3) Reservoir-induced earthquake, (4) Volcanic earthquake.

- A) (1) Roof of mine/cavern; (2) Detonation of chemical/nuclear device; (3) Construction of large reservoirs; (4) Volcanic eruption
- B) (1) Plate movement; (2) Volcanic eruption; (3) Roof collapse; (4) Reservoir construction
- C) (1) Volcanic eruption; (2) Plate movement; (3) Detonation; (4) Roof collapse
- D) (1) Plate movement; (2) Mining; (3) Reservoir construction; (4) Volcanic eruption
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

(1) Collapse earthquake: roof of underground mine/cavern collapses. (2) Explosion earthquake: detonation of chemical or nuclear device. (3) Reservoir-induced earthquake: caused by construction of large reservoirs (human-induced). (4) Volcanic earthquake: result of a volcanic eruption.

**Trap:** Option B swaps volcanic and reservoir, and Option D mixes tectonic earthquake (plate movement) with the others. The five types are tectonic, volcanic, collapse, explosion, and reservoir-induced.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Types of Earthquake]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=3
The Richter Scale measures magnitude 0–10, while the Mercalli Scale measures:

- A) Magnitude 0–10
- B) Magnitude 1–12
- C) Intensity 1–12
- D) Depth in km
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Mercalli Scale measures the intensity of an earthquake on a scale of 1 to 12. The Richter Scale measures magnitude (energy released) on a scale of 0 to 10 and is described as a "limitless" scale.

**Trap:** Students often swap the scales. The key is: Richter = magnitude; Mercalli = intensity. Mercalli is the older, qualitative 12-point scale based on observed damage.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Scales to Measure Earthquake]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=3
Arrange the following theories in chronological order of their proposal: (1) Theory of Plate Tectonics, (2) Convection Current Theory, (3) Continental Drift Theory.

- A) (3) Continental Drift; (2) Convection Current; (1) Plate Tectonics
- B) (1) Plate Tectonics; (2) Convection Current; (3) Continental Drift
- C) (2) Convection Current; (3) Continental Drift; (1) Plate Tectonics
- D) (1) Plate Tectonics; (3) Continental Drift; (2) Convection Current
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

In chronological order: Alfred Wegener's Continental Drift Theory (1912) → Arthur Holmes' Convection Current Theory (1930s) → McKenzie and Parker's Theory of Plate Tectonics (1967).

**Trap:** The order is easy to confuse — students who remember two names but not the third often swap them. Memorise: W-H-M-P (Wegener, Holmes, McKenzie-Parker).

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Force of Drifting]]
</details>

@qgeo.earths_interior_and_plate_tectonics.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=3
Which statement correctly pairs each discontinuity with its boundary location?

- A) Conrad: lower crust/upper mantle; Mohorovicic: upper/lower crust; Repetti: lower mantle/outer core; Guttenberg: outer/inner core
- B) Conrad: upper/lower crust; Mohorovicic: lower crust/upper mantle; Repetti: upper/lower mantle; Guttenberg: lower mantle/outer core; Lehmann: outer/inner core
- C) Conrad: crust/mantle; Mohorovicic: upper/lower mantle
- D) Lehmann: crust/mantle; Guttenberg: outer/inner core
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Reading top to bottom: Conrad (upper/lower crust), Mohorovicic (lower crust/upper mantle), Repetti (upper/lower mantle), Guttenberg (lower mantle/outer core), Lehmann (outer/inner core). The trick to remember is "Koi Mujhe Red Gulab Lado".

**Trap:** Options C and D reverse the locations of these discontinuities. The trick is to remember the order from top to bottom.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Earth's Discontinuity]]
</details>
