@qgeo.earth_interior.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=3
Which layer of the Earth is composed of Silica and Aluminium (SiAl)?

- A) Mantle
- B) Core
- C) Crust
- D) Asthenosphere
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Crust is the outermost layer composed mainly of Silica and Aluminium, hence called SiAl. The Mantle is SiMa (Silica + Magnesium) and the Core is NiFe (Nickel + Iron).

**Trap:** Students confuse SiAl (Crust) with SiMa (Mantle) — remember 'Sial' = 'crust like salt on top'.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Layers of Earth]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=3
The discontinuity between the lower crust and the upper mantle is known as:

- A) Conrad discontinuity
- B) Mohorovicic discontinuity
- C) Guttenberg discontinuity
- D) Lehmann discontinuity
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Mohorovicic (Moho) discontinuity separates the crust from the upper mantle. It was discovered by Andrija Mohorovicic in 1909.

**Trap:** Conrad is between upper and lower crust; Guttenberg is between mantle and outer core; Lehmann is between outer and inner core.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Earth's Discontinuity]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=3
Who proposed the Convection Current Theory to explain the movement of tectonic plates?

- A) Alfred Wegener
- B) Arthur Holmes
- C) McKenzie and Parker
- D) Harry Hess
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Arthur Holmes proposed the Convection Current Theory in the 1930s, suggesting that radioactive decay generates heat in the mantle that drives convection currents that move the tectonic plates.

**Trap:** McKenzie and Parker gave the Theory of Plate Tectonics (1967); Wegener gave the Continental Drift Theory (1912). Don't confuse the names.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Force of Drifting]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=3
Which seismic wave cannot pass through the Earth's outer core?

- A) P-waves (Primary waves)
- B) S-waves (Secondary waves)
- C) Rayleigh waves
- D) Love waves
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

S-waves (Secondary waves) are transverse — they can only travel through solids. The outer core is liquid, so S-waves cannot pass through it, creating the large S-wave shadow zone. P-waves (longitudinal) can pass through both solids and liquids.

**Trap:** Students often pick P-waves because they are more famous; in fact, P-waves travel through all media, while S-waves travel only through solids.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Earthquake]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
An earthquake of magnitude 7.5 is reported on the Richter scale. What does the Richter scale actually measure?

- A) Intensity of damage at the surface
- B) Energy released at the focus
- C) Depth of the hypocentre
- D) Distance from the epicentre
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Richter scale measures magnitude — the energy released at the focus (hypocentre) of the earthquake, on a logarithmic scale from 0 to 10 (theoretically open). The Mercalli scale (1–12) measures the observed intensity of damage at the surface.

**Trap:** Many students confuse magnitude (Richter, energy at source) with intensity (Mercalli, damage at surface). The question asks what Richter measures.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Earthquake]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
At a divergent plate boundary, what geological process is most likely to occur?

- A) Subduction and deep ocean trenches
- B) New crust formation via rising magma
- C) Horizontal sliding without crust creation
- D) Mountain building by collision
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

At a divergent boundary, plates move apart; magma rises from the mantle to fill the gap, solidifying to form new crust (e.g., mid-ocean ridges like the Mid-Atlantic Ridge).

**Trap:** Option A describes a convergent boundary; option C describes a transform boundary; option D describes a convergent (collision) boundary.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Plate boundaries]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
The Ring of Fire is best described as:

- A) A belt of active volcanoes encircling the Pacific Ocean
- B) A ring-shaped crater on the Moon
- C) A circular sea in the Atlantic Ocean
- D) A belt of cyclones around the Indian Ocean
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Ring of Fire is a horseshoe-shaped belt around the Pacific Ocean, characterised by intense tectonic activity — about 90% of the world's earthquakes and 75% of its volcanoes occur here, due to subduction at ocean-continent convergent boundaries.

**Trap:** Options C and D are decoys — students confuse 'Ring of Fire' (Pacific tectonic) with Atlantic features or Indian Ocean cyclone systems.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Ring of Fire]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
The Asthenosphere is best described as:

- A) A rigid layer of the crust
- B) A semi-solid layer of the upper mantle that flows slowly
- C) The solid inner core of Earth
- D) A layer of liquid iron in the outer core
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Asthenosphere is a semi-solid, ductile layer of the upper mantle lying beneath the lithosphere. It is the source of magma/lava and is mechanically weak, allowing the lithospheric plates to move over it.

**Trap:** Option A is wrong — the asthenosphere is NOT rigid and NOT part of the lithosphere. The lithosphere includes the crust and the upper solid mantle; the asthenosphere is below the lithosphere.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Layers of Earth]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=3
Why is the outer core of the Earth believed to be in liquid form?

- A) Because volcanoes erupt molten rock from the outer core
- B) Because P-waves slow down sharply at the mantle-core boundary
- C) Because S-waves do not pass through it, creating the S-wave shadow zone
- D) Because the outer core is composed of granite
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

S-waves (transverse) cannot propagate through liquids. Their absence in the S-wave shadow zone (covering about 40% of Earth's surface) provides direct evidence that the outer core is liquid. The outer core is composed of molten nickel-iron (NiFe), not granite.

**Trap:** Volcanoes erupt from the asthenosphere/upper mantle, not the outer core. The outer core is NiFe, not granite (granite is in the continental crust).

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Earthquake]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=3
Match the scientists: (i) McKenzie and Parker, (ii) Arthur Holmes, (iii) Alfred Wegener.

- A) Plate Tectonics; Convection Current; Continental Drift
- B) Continental Drift; Plate Tectonics; Convection Current
- C) Convection Current; Continental Drift; Plate Tectonics
- D) Plate Tectonics; Continental Drift; Convection Current
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

McKenzie and Parker (1967) gave the Theory of Plate Tectonics. Arthur Holmes (1930s) gave the Convection Current Theory. Alfred Wegener (1912) gave the Continental Drift Theory with the supercontinent Pangaea.

**Trap:** The order is easy to confuse — students who remember two names but not the third often swap them. Memorise: W-H-M-P (Wegener, Holmes, McKenzie-Parker).

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Force of Drifting]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=3
Which statement correctly pairs each discontinuity with its boundary location?

- A) Conrad: lower crust/upper mantle; Mohorovicic: upper/lower crust; Repetti: lower mantle/outer core; Guttenberg: outer/inner core
- B) Conrad: upper/lower crust; Mohorovicic: lower crust/upper mantle; Repetti: upper/lower mantle; Guttenberg: lower mantle/outer core; Lehmann: outer/inner core
- C) Conrad: crust/mantle; Mohorovicic: upper/lower mantle
- D) Lehmann: crust/mantle; Guttenberg: outer/inner core
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Reading top to bottom: Conrad (upper/lower crust), Mohorovicic (lower crust/upper mantle), Repetti (upper/lower mantle), Guttenberg (lower mantle/outer core), Lehmann (outer/inner core).

**Trap:** Options C and D reverse the locations of these discontinuities. The trick is to remember the order from top to bottom.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Earth's Discontinuity]]
</details>

@qgeo.earth_interior.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=3
An earthquake with focus at a shallow depth is generally more intense at the surface than one with a deep focus. Which statement correctly explains this?

- A) Deep-focus earthquakes occur only in oceans, so they cause less damage on land
- B) Seismic waves lose energy as they travel, so waves from a deep focus lose more energy before reaching the surface
- C) Shallow-focus earthquakes are tectonic in origin, while deep-focus ones are volcanic
- D) Deep-focus earthquakes release less total energy than shallow ones
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Seismic waves lose energy as they propagate through Earth. A deep-focus earthquake's waves travel further before reaching the surface, dissipating more energy, resulting in less surface intensity than a shallow-focus earthquake of the same magnitude.

**Trap:** Option D is wrong — deep and shallow earthquakes can release the same magnitude of energy; the difference is the surface intensity, not the released energy.

**See also:** [[../../geography/03 - Earth's Interior and Plate Tectonics#Earthquake]]
</details>
