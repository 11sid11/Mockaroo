# Soil and Agriculture — Questions

A 12-question MCQ set on Indian soil types (alluvial, black/regur, red, laterite, arid, peaty), soil classification systems, cropping seasons (Kharif/Rabi/Zaid), Green/White/Yellow revolutions, and agricultural revolutions in India. Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=17
The study of soil is called:

- A) Geology
- B) Pedology
- C) Edaphology
- D) Pomology
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "The study of soil is called Pedology." Edaphology refers to the influence of soil on living things (close but not the chapter answer).

**Trap:** C is the most plausible wrong answer — both words start with "P" / "E" and are about soil. Pomology is the study of fruits.

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#SOIL]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=17
ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research) has classified Indian soils into how many types?

- A) 6
- B) 7
- C) 8
- D) 9
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "ICAR… This institute has categorized Indian soils into 8 types." The 8 types are: Alluvial, Red, Black, Laterite, Arid, Montane, Marshy/Peaty, and Saline/Alkaline.

**Trap:** B is the trap if you confuse it with the number of soil-forming factors (5) or the number of vegetation types (also 5 in Chapter 16). The ICAR classification has 8.

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research)]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=17
Black soil (Regur) is also known as:

- A) Chernozem
- B) Regur
- C) Loess
- D) Podzol
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Black soil is also known as Regur." It is ideal for cotton cultivation, is clayey, has self-ploughing characteristics, and is found in the Deccan Traps (Maharashtra, Gujarat).

**Trap:** A (Chernozem) is a Russian black soil; C (Loess) is wind-blown silt; D (Podzol) is a cold-climate acidic soil. None of these apply to India.

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#Black Soil]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=17
The Green Revolution in India is associated with which crop and which states in its FIRST phase (mid 1960s-1970)?

- A) Rice and wheat in Punjab, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu
- B) Pulses in Rajasthan and Maharashtra
- C) Sugarcane in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar
- D) Millets in Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "The first phase was when the use of High-yielding variety (HYV) seeds was limited to wealthier states like Punjab, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu and it was also limited to rice and wheat."

**Trap:** Candidates confuse the Green Revolution (rice/wheat) with the later Yellow Revolution (oilseeds) or with crops like sugarcane and millets, which were not the GR focus.

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#First Phase]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=17
A farmer wants to grow cotton in a region with black, clayey, self-ploughing soil that develops cracks when dry. Which soil is this?

- A) Alluvial soil
- B) Laterite soil
- C) Black soil (Regur)
- D) Red soil
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Black soil is also known as Regur. It is ideal for cotton cultivation (Cotton requires 210 frost free days). Black soil is formed due to eruption of Lava… This soil has self-ploughing characteristics (It develops cracks when dry and becomes sticky when moist)."

**Trap:** A is alluvial (Northern Plains + deltas, not the Deccan Traps); B is laterite (high-rainfall areas, suited to tea/coffee/cashew); D is red soil (low rainfall areas, used for millets and groundnut).

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#Black Soil]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=17
Match each agricultural revolution to the produce it is associated with:

- Revolution 1: Green Revolution
- Revolution 2: White Revolution
- Revolution 3: Yellow Revolution
- Revolution 4: Blue Revolution

Produces: (a) Food grains, (b) Milk and Dairy, (c) Edible oilseeds, (d) Fish.

Which option correctly pairs all four?

- A) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c, 4-d
- B) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c, 4-d
- C) 1-a, 2-c, 3-b, 4-d
- D) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a, 4-d
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter table lists: Green — Food Grains; White — Milk/Dairy (Operation Flood, Verghese Kurien); Yellow — Oil Seeds (Sam Pitroda); Blue — Fish (Hiralal Chaudhuri and Arun Krishnan).

**Trap:** B and C swap the White and Yellow (and possibly Green) associations. White = Milk is the most-tested association, but candidates confuse it with Pink (Onion) or Silver (Egg/Poultry).

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#Important Agriculture Revolution in India]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=17
A region receives rainfall over 200 cm per year, has high temperatures (above 22 degrees C), and has laterite-derived soil. Which crop is most suitable for this region?

- A) Wheat
- B) Cotton
- C) Cashew nut
- D) Bajra (pearl millet)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Red laterite soil is found in: Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala — these regions are suitable for growing cashew nut crops." Laterite soil is also suitable for tea and coffee.

**Trap:** A (wheat) needs cool climate + alluvial/loamy soil. B (cotton) needs black soil + 210 frost-free days. D (bajra) is a dry-land millet. Laterite soil + heavy rainfall = cashew, tea, coffee.

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#Laterite Soil]] · [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#Coffee]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=17
Which of the following cropping-season and crop pairings is correct?

- A) Kharif — sown in October-November, harvested in March-April (rabi crops like wheat)
- B) Rabi — sown in June-July with the arrival of the monsoon (rice, maize, jute)
- C) Kharif — sown with the Southwest Monsoon in June-July (rice, maize, jute)
- D) Zaid — the main monsoon cropping season
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter describes: Kharif crops are sown with the arrival of the Southwest Monsoon (June-July) and include rice, maize, jowar, bajra, etc. Rabi crops are sown in October-November and harvested in March-April (wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard). Zaid is the short summer season.

**Trap:** A reverses Kharif and Rabi timings. B is also a reversal. D is wrong — Zaid is a short summer season, not the main monsoon.

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#Cropping Seasons in India]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=17
Alluvial soil is described in the chapter as:

- A) Rich in nitrogen and phosphorus, poor in potash
- B) Rich in potash and poor in phosphorus
- C) Rich in iron and magnesium, poor in calcium
- D) Rich in humus, poor in nitrogen
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "It is rich in potash and poor in phosphorus." Alluvial soil is the most widely spread soil in India (40 percent coverage), found in the Northern Plains and the deltas of Peninsular rivers.

**Trap:** A reverses the two nutrients. The chapter is consistent: alluvial is rich in potash, poor in phosphorus.

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#Alluvial Soil]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=17
Match each type of cultivation with its description:

- Cultivation 1: Sericulture
- Cultivation 2: Pisciculture
- Cultivation 3: Apiculture
- Cultivation 4: Viticulture

Descriptions: (a) Rearing of fish, (b) Cultivation of grapevines, (c) Silk rearing, (d) Bee keeping.

Which option correctly pairs all four?

- A) 1-c, 2-a, 3-d, 4-b
- B) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a, 4-d
- C) 1-a, 2-c, 3-d, 4-b
- D) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c, 4-d
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter lists: "Vegetable farming — Horticulture; Silk rearing — Sericulture; Bee keeping — Apiculture; Fish farming — Pisciculture; Flower framing — Floriculture; Cultivation of grapevines — Viticulture / Viniculture." So sericulture = silk, pisciculture = fish, apiculture = bees, viticulture = grapevines.

**Trap:** B swaps Pisciculture and Viticulture. Both involve "growing something" but fish is animal farming, grapes are plants.

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#Types of Culture]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=17
Consider the following:
(1) Acidic soil is treated with gypsum.
(2) Saline soil is treated with lime.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both statements are true
- B) Only statement 1 is true
- C) Only statement 2 is true; statement 1 is false
- D) Both statements are false; the chapter says saline soil is treated with gypsum, and acidic soil is treated with lime (the reverse)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The chapter states explicitly: "Saline soil is treated with Gypsum. Acidic soil is treated with Lime." Both statements in the question have the treatments *swapped* — so both are false.

**Trap:** A and B are traps for candidates who do not read the small print carefully. The chapter has a clear mnemonic: Gypsum for Saline (G-S), Lime for Acidic (L-A).

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#Arid Soil]]
</details>

@qgeo.soil_and_agriculture.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=17
Assertion (A): The White Revolution (Operation Flood) was launched in 1970 to increase milk production in India and was led by Verghese Kurien through Amul (GCMMF).
Reason (R): Amul is owned by the Gujarat Cooperative Milk Marketing Federation (GCMMF) and Kurien is known as the "Father of the White Revolution" in India.
Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "White Revolution is also known as Operation Flood. It was a dairy development program to increase milk production in India. Launched in: 1970 — led by Amul (a brand owned by Gujarat Cooperative Milk Marketing Federation (GCMMP)). Father: Verghese Kurien." Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains the institutional basis of A — Amul (GCMMF) was the implementing cooperative, and Kurien was the architect.

**Trap:** B is the partial-credit trap. R is true and *does* explain A — it gives the institutional and personal reasons Operation Flood worked.

**See also:** [[../../geography/17 - Soil and Agriculture#White Revolution]]
</details>
