@qgeo.peninsular_plateaus.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=10
The Peninsular Plateau of India is best described as:
- A) The smallest physiographic division of India
- B) The largest physiographic division of India, made of old crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks
- C) A young alluvial plain
- D) A volcanic island arc in the Bay of Bengal
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Peninsular Plateau is the largest physiographic division of India, covering a vast area in the southern part of the country" and "The Peninsular plateau is a tableland made up of old crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks."

**Trap:** Candidates confuse the Peninsular Plateau (largest physiographic division, old rocks) with the Indo-Gangetic Plain (young alluvium).

**See also:** [[../../geography/10 - Peninsular Plateaus#PENINSULAR PLATEAU]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_plateaus.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=10
The Peninsular Plateau of India is divided into two broad sections — the Central Highlands and the:
- A) Chotanagpur Plateau
- B) Malwa Plateau
- C) Deccan Plateau
- D) Meghalaya Plateau
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "This plateau is divided into two sections: the Central Highlands and the Deccan Plateau."

**Trap:** Chotanagpur and Malwa are sub-plateaus of the Central Highlands, not the second main division; Meghalaya Plateau is a far northeast extension.

@qgeo.peninsular_plateaus.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=10
A hiker walks from Mumbai towards Pune across a Western Ghats pass; later she crosses from Mumbai to Nasik via another pass. In correct order these are:
- A) Bhor Ghat (Mumbai–Pune) and Thal Ghat (Mumbai–Nasik)
- B) Thal Ghat (Mumbai–Pune) and Bhor Ghat (Mumbai–Nasik)
- C) Pal Ghat (Mumbai–Pune) and Bhor Ghat (Mumbai–Nasik)
- D) Bhor Ghat (Mumbai–Pune) and Pal Ghat (Mumbai–Nasik)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "Thal Ghat: It connects Mumbai to Nasik. Bhor Ghat: It connects Mumbai to Pune." Pal Ghat connects Coimbatore (Tamil Nadu) to Palakkad (Kerala), not Mumbai.

**Trap:** The three Western Ghats passes (Thal, Bhor, Pal) all appear in the same section; the Mumbai-to-Pune and Mumbai-to-Nasik routes must be matched to Bhor Ghat and Thal Ghat respectively.

**See also:** [[../../geography/10 - Peninsular Plateaus#Passes in Western Ghats]]

@qgeo.peninsular_plateaus.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=10
Which of the following best describes how the Western Ghats differ from the Eastern Ghats?
- A) Western Ghats are discontinuous and average 600 m; Eastern Ghats are continuous and average 1200 m
- B) Western Ghats are continuous and average 1200 m; Eastern Ghats are discontinuous, irregular and dissected by rivers
- C) Both are continuous with the same average height
- D) Western Ghats run from Mahanadi to Nilgiri; Eastern Ghats run from Tapi to south of Nilgiri
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter's comparison table: Western Ghats — continuous, average 1200 m, stretch from Tapi to south of Nilgiri Hills, spread across Gujarat/Maharashtra/Karnataka/Kerala/Tamil Nadu/Goa, highest peaks Anamudi (2695 m) and Doddabetta (2637 m). Eastern Ghats — discontinuous, irregular, dissected by rivers, average 600 m, stretch from Mahanadi Valley to Nilgiri Hills.

**Trap:** Option D reverses the latitudinal spans (it states Western Ghats from Mahanadi and Eastern from Tapi) — actually it is Western from Tapi and Eastern from Mahanadi.

**See also:** [[../../geography/10 - Peninsular Plateaus#WESTERN AND EASTERN GHATS]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_plateaus.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=10
The general slope of the Peninsular Plateau, and the direction of flow of most of its rivers, is:
- A) West to east
- B) East to west
- C) North to south
- D) South to north
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "General elevation: West to East." For the Deccan Plateau specifically: "It is higher in the west and slopes gently eastward (the rivers in this region flow towards east)."

**Trap:** Candidates familiar with the westward-flowing Narmada and Tapi may assume the entire plateau slopes west; those are exceptions, and the general peninsular slope is west to east.

**See also:** [[../../geography/10 - Peninsular Plateaus#PENINSULAR PLATEAU]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_plateaus.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=10
Which among the following is the CORRECT pairing of a Western/Eastern Ghats highest peak with its host range or state?
- A) Anamudi (2695 m) — Nilgiri Hills
- B) Doddabetta (2637 m) — Annamalai Hills
- C) Anamudi (2695 m) — Annamalai Hills, Kerala
- D) Mahendragiri (1501 m) — Western Ghats
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Highest peaks: Anamudi (2695 m → on Annamalai Hills) in Kerala and Doddabetta (2637 m → on Nilgiri Hills) in Tamil Nadu." So Anamudi is on Annamalai Hills, not Nilgiri; Doddabetta is on Nilgiri, not Annamalai. Mahendragiri is in the Eastern Ghats (Odisha), not the Western Ghats.

**Trap:** The Annamalai/Nilgiri association is reversed in options A and B; the careful reading of "on Annamalai Hills" vs "on Nilgiri Hills" is the key.

@qgeo.peninsular_plateaus.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=10
Assertion (A): The Peninsular Plateau is the largest physiographic division of India and was developed from the breakup and drifting of the ancient Gondwana Land.
Reason (R): The plateau is a tableland of old crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks with a general elevation of 600–900 m above sea level.
- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A is true: "Peninsular Plateau is the largest physiographic division of India" and "It was developed as a result of the breakup and drifting of the Gondwana Land." R is also true: the plateau "is a tableland made up of old crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks" and the "General elevation of Peninsular Plateau: 600-900 m above sea level." However, R describes the rock composition and elevation — it does not explain WHY the plateau is the largest physiographic division or WHY it arose from Gondwana fragmentation.

**Trap:** A and R are both factually correct, but R does not causally explain A; it merely adds descriptive detail about the same plateau.

**See also:** [[../../geography/10 - Peninsular Plateaus#PENINSULAR PLATEAU]]
</details>
