@qgeo.national_parks.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=20
The first National Park of India is:

- A) Kaziranga NP
- B) Gir NP
- C) Jim Corbett NP
- D) Keibul-Lamjao NP
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "First National Park of India: Jim Corbett National Park (1936)." Project Tiger was launched from Jim Corbett NP in 1973.

**Trap:** Kaziranga is famous for one-horned rhinos (Assam). Gir is for Asiatic Lions (Gujarat). Keibul-Lamjao is the worlds only floating NP (Manipur). Jim Corbett is both the first NP and the birthplace of Project Tiger.

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#Uttarakhand]]
</details>

@qgeo.national_parks.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=20
The IUCN (International Union for Conservation of Nature) is headquartered in:

- A) New York, USA
- B) Geneva, Switzerland
- C) Gland, Switzerland
- D) Nairobi, Kenya
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) — HQ: Gland, Switzerland. Established in: 1948." IUCN maintains the Red Data Book.

**Trap:** B (Geneva) is the HQ of many UN bodies but not IUCN. D (Nairobi) hosts UNEP. A is the UN HQ. The Gland-Switzerland location is distinctive.

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN)]]
</details>

@qgeo.national_parks.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=20
Which National Park is the ONLY habitat of the Asiatic Lion in the world?

- A) Keibul-Lamjao NP (Manipur)
- B) Jim Corbett NP (Uttarakhand)
- C) Gir NP (Gujarat)
- D) Kaziranga NP (Assam)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Gir NP is the only habitat of the Asiatic Lion in the world." India is the only country with both tigers and lions.

**Trap:** A is famous for Elds deer (Sangai). B for tigers. D for one-horned rhinos.

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#Gujarat]]
</details>

@qgeo.national_parks.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=20
The Worlds only floating National Park is:

- A) Keibul-Lamjao NP (Manipur)
- B) Sundarbans NP (West Bengal)
- C) Loktak Lake (Manipur)
- D) Chilika Lake (Odisha)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "Keibul-Lamjao NP is worlds only floating National Park that is located in Loktak Lake (phumdis is seen here)." It is also famous for the Elds deer species.

**Trap:** C (Loktak Lake) is the floating *lake*; A (Keibul-Lamjao) is the floating *national park* on that lake. Both are in Manipur. Candidates confuse the two related features.

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#Manipur]]
</details>

@qgeo.national_parks.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=20
Match each biosphere reserve with its state:

- BR 1: Nanda Devi
- BR 2: Manas
- BR 3: Nilgiri
- BR 4: Great Rann of Kutch

States: (a) Gujarat, (b) Karnataka (and Tamil Nadu, Kerala), (c) Uttarakhand, (d) Assam.

Which option correctly pairs all four?

- A) 1-c, 2-d, 3-b, 4-a
- B) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a, 4-d
- C) 1-d, 2-c, 3-b, 4-a
- D) 1-a, 2-d, 3-c, 4-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter lists: Nanda Devi — Uttarakhand; Manas, Dibru Saikhowa — Assam; Nilgiri — Karnataka (also Tamil Nadu, Kerala); Great Rann of Kutch — Gujarat (this is the largest BR). Pairing correctly: 1-c, 2-d, 3-b, 4-a.

**Trap:** B reverses Nanda Devi (Uttarakhand) with Nilgiri (Karnataka). Both are famous BRs but in different states.

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#BIOSPHERE RESERVES]]
</details>

@qgeo.national_parks.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=20
The Wildlife Protection Act 1972 has how many Schedules?

- A) 4
- B) 5
- C) 6
- D) 7
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Total: 6 schedules." Schedule I covers endangered species; Schedule V covers vermin (rats, fruit bat, common crow); Schedule VI regulates specified plants (orchids, pitcher plants).

**Trap:** Candidates confuse with the number of vegetation types (5 in chapter 16) or the number of soil types (8 in chapter 17). The WPA 1972 has 6 schedules.

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#Wildlife Protection Act 1972]]
</details>

@qgeo.national_parks.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=20
A biosphere reserve has three zones. Which of the following correctly arranges them from the most-protected (core) to the most human-allowed (transition)?

- A) Transition zone, Buffer zone, Core zone
- B) Buffer zone, Core zone, Transition zone
- C) Core zone, Buffer zone, Transition zone
- D) Core zone, Transition zone, Buffer zone
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Three zones of Biosphere: Core zone, Buffer zone and Transition zone." Core: human intervention strictly prohibited. Buffer: research/education only. Transition: cropping, recreation, forestry, settlements permitted.

**Trap:** A reverses the order. The mnemonic is C-B-T (most-protected to most-permissive). B mixes up buffer and core.

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#Biosphere Reserve Zones]]
</details>

@qgeo.national_parks.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=20
Consider the following similar-sounding parks:
(1) Kaziranga NP (Assam) — famous for one-horned rhinoceros.
(2) Karera NP (Madhya Pradesh) — famous for hard-ground barasingha.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both statements are true
- B) Only statement 1 is true; statement 2 is false (Karera is actually a wildlife sanctuary famous for vultures; the chapter lists vulture-related species elsewhere)
- C) Only statement 2 is true; statement 1 is false
- D) Both statements are false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Statement 1 is true: Kaziranga NP (Assam) has the highest population of one-horned rhinoceros. Statement 2 is dubious/incorrect per the chapter: the chapter does not associate Karera with barasingha in Madhya Pradesh — the chapter associates barasingha with Kanha (the famous "Baghela" — hard-ground swamp deer is the symbol of Kanha Tiger Reserve). Karera is not detailed as a barasingha park.

**Trap:** A is tempting because Kaziranga is so famous. But the Karera/barasingha pairing is a trap — Karera is not mentioned in this chapter as a barasingha park; Kanha is the barasingha park (Madhya Pradesh).

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#Assam]] · [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#Madhya Pradesh]]
</details>

@qgeo.national_parks.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=20
Consider the following statements:
(1) Kaziranga NP — Manas NP — Nameri NP are all located in the state of Assam.
(2) Project Elephant was launched in 1992 in India.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both statements are true
- B) Only statement 1 is true
- C) Only statement 2 is true; statement 1 is false (Nameri NP is in Arunachal Pradesh, not Assam)
- D) Both statements are false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter lists under Assam: Dibru-Saikhowa NP-BR, Dehing Patkai NP, Nameri NP-TR, Kaziranga NP, Orang NP-TR, Manas NP, Raimona NP. All these are in Assam. (Nameri TR spans the Assam-Arunachal border, but the chapter places the NP entry under Assam.) Statement 2: "Project Elephant was launched on 1992" — this is true per the chapter.

**Trap:** C is tempting because Nameri is associated with Arunachal Pradesh in some references. The chapter lists Nameri NP under Assam, so per the chapter, statement 1 is true.

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#Assam]] · [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#Uttarakhand]]
</details>

@qgeo.national_parks.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=20
Assertion (A): India has 18 biosphere reserves, of which 12 are under UNESCOs Man and Biosphere (MAB) Programme, and the Oldest BR is Nilgiri (Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka) while the Largest is Great Rann of Kutch.
Reason (R): Nilgiri was the first BR to be added under MAB; Panna is the latest BR under MAB.
Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Total Biosphere Reserve: 18. Oldest BR: Nilgiri BR (spread across Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka). Largest BR: Great Rann of Kutch… Under UNESCOs MAB Programme (1971) -> 12 Biosphere Reserve in India." And: "1. Nilgiri (1st under MAB)" and "12. Panna (latest BR)." So A and R are both true. R does NOT explain A — R provides supplementary facts (which BR is first/latest under MAB) but does not causally explain *why* India has 18 BRs, *why* 12 are under MAB, or *why* Nilgiri is oldest. R merely adds more true detail.

**Trap:** A is the obvious "looks-right" pick because all facts are true. The test is whether R explains A — it does not causally explain; it just adds more true statements.

**See also:** [[../../geography/20 - National Parks#BIOSPHERE RESERVES]]
</details>
