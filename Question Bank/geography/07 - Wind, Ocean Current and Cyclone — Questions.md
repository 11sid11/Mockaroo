- B) Typhoon — Atlantic Ocean
- C) Willy-Willy — Australia
- D) Cyclone — Bay of Bengal only
@@

The chapter lists: Hurricane → Atlantic Ocean; Typhoon → South China Sea and Western Pacific Ocean; Willy-Willy → Australia; Cyclone → India.

@qgeo.wind_ocean_current_and_cyclone.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=7
A cyclone in the Northern Hemisphere rotates:
- A) Clockwise
- B) Anti-clockwise
- C) Vertically only
- D) Does not rotate
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "A cyclone rotates anti-clockwise in Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in Southern Hemisphere."

**Trap:** Anticyclones rotate the other way (clockwise in NH). Conflating cyclone and anticyclone rotation is a common mistake.

**See also:** [[../../geography/07 - Wind, Ocean Current and Cyclone#Formation of Cyclones]]
</details>

Horse Latitudes lie at approximately:

- C) 30°N and 30°S
- D) 45°N and 45°S
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

The chapter explicitly states "Horse latitudes are regions located about 30°N and 30°S of the equator."

**Trap:** Candidates confuse Horse Latitudes with the Doldrums (10°N–10°S) or with the Tropics (23½°). The 30° latitude band is correct.

@qgeo.wind_ocean_current_and_cyclone.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=7
On a sunny summer afternoon along a tropical coastline, an onshore breeze develops from sea to land. Which local wind is this, and what causes it?
- A) Land breeze; land cools faster than sea at night
- B) Sea breeze; land heats up faster than the sea during the day
- C) Harmattan; dry dust-laden wind from Sahara
- D) Chinook; downslope warming on the lee side of mountains
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter describes the Sea Breeze: "It occurs during the day when land heats up faster than the sea, creating low pressure over land and high pressure over the sea, causing air to move from sea to land; more common in spring and summer."

**Trap:** Candidates who recall "land vs sea" but forget the timing may mark A (land breeze) for an afternoon case; land breeze is a night-time phenomenon.

**See also:** [[../../geography/07 - Wind, Ocean Current and Cyclone#Sea Breeze]]
</details>

@qgeo.wind_ocean_current_and_cyclone.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=7
Identify which of the following is NOT a favourable condition for tropical cyclone formation:
- A) Large sea surface temperature
- B) Atmospheric instability
- C) Coriolis force
- D) A strong high-pressure area dominating the region
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The chapter lists favourable conditions as Large sea surface temperature, Atmospheric Instability, Coriolis Force and Pre-existing weak low pressure area. A strong high-pressure area would suppress cyclone formation, not favour it.

**Trap:** Candidates see "pressure" and assume pressure systems always aid storms; tropical cyclones specifically require a *weak low pressure* area to start, never a strong high.

**See also:** [[../../geography/07 - Wind, Ocean Current and Cyclone#Favorable Conditions for Formation of Cyclone]]
</details>

@qgeo.wind_ocean_current_and_cyclone.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=7
Cold ocean currents flowing along the western coasts of tropical and sub-tropical continents largely contribute to the formation of:
- A) Mangrove forests
- B) Coastal deserts
- C) Coral reefs
- D) Fjords
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Cold ocean currents: They largely contribute to the formation of coastal deserts in west coast regions of tropical and sub-tropical continents. This is because the cold water makes the air above it cooler and less able to hold moisture."

**Trap:** Candidates may recall that warm currents bring rain and pick a vegetation-based answer; the textbook explicitly ties cold currents to coastal deserts (e.g., Atacama, Namib, Sahara west coast).

**See also:** [[../../geography/07 - Wind, Ocean Current and Cyclone#Effects of Ocean Currents]]
</details>

@qgeo.wind_ocean_current_and_cyclone.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=7
A ship's captain reports calm winds, sunny skies, and very little precipitation at roughly 30°N latitude. Which global wind belt is the ship in?
- A) Doldrums
- B) Trade winds
- C) Horse Latitudes
- D) Westerlies
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter describes Horse Latitudes at about 30°N and 30°S as "an area of subtropical high", "a high-pressure area at divergence of trade winds and westerlies", characterised by "Sunny skies, calm winds, and a very little precipitation".

**Trap:** Candidates confuse Doldrums (10°N–10°S) with Horse Latitudes; both are calm but Doldrums are near the equator, Horse Latitudes at ~30°.

**See also:** [[../../geography/07 - Wind, Ocean Current and Cyclone#Horse Latitude]]
</details>

@qgeo.wind_ocean_current_and_cyclone.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=7
Which of the following ocean currents is WARM?
- A) Benguela
- B) Labrador
- C) Oyashio
- D) Kuroshio
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The chapter lists Kuroshio among the warm ocean currents (Brazil, Mozambique, Agulhas, Gulf Stream, Alaska, Norwegian, Kuroshio, E. Australia). Benguela, Labrador and Oyashio are all in the "BP GOLF World Cup California" cold-currents mnemonic.

**Trap:** Candidates know "Gulf Stream" is warm but mix up Japan-side currents; Kuroshio is the Japan Current — a warm current often confused with Oyashio (the cold current it meets near Japan).

**See also:** [[../../geography/07 - Wind, Ocean Current and Cyclone#Warm Ocean Currents]]
</details>

@qgeo.wind_ocean_current_and_cyclone.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=7
An anticyclone over northern India in winter brings clear skies and calm weather. How does it rotate, and what is its pressure characteristic?
- A) Anti-clockwise rotation; high-pressure area
- B) Clockwise rotation; high-pressure area
- C) Anti-clockwise rotation; low-pressure area
- D) Clockwise rotation; low-pressure area
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "An anticyclone rotates clockwise in Northern Hemisphere and anti-clockwise in Southern Hemisphere" and an anticyclone is "formed around an area of high pressure where the air sinks, causing clear skies and calm weather."

**Trap:** Candidates confuse cyclone (anti-clockwise in NH) with anticyclone (clockwise in NH). Also, anticyclone must be a HIGH-pressure system, not low.

**See also:** [[../../geography/07 - Wind, Ocean Current and Cyclone#Anticyclone]]
</details>

@qgeo.wind_ocean_current_and_cyclone.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=7
Consider two storms: Storm X forms over warm tropical oceans, moves from east to west, and is compact but destructive. Storm Y forms where warm and cold air masses meet, covers a large area, but causes less destruction. Storm X and Storm Y are, respectively:
- A) Temperate cyclone; Tropical cyclone
- B) Tropical cyclone; Temperate cyclone
- C) Tropical cyclone; Anticyclone
- D) Anticyclone; Temperate cyclone
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter's comparison table lists for Tropical cyclones: form over sea, from favourable conditions over sea, cover less area but cause major destruction, occur rarely, move east to west. For Temperate cyclones: form due to frontogenesis (warm and cold air mass meet), cover large area but cause less destruction. So X = Tropical, Y = Temperate.

**Trap:** Candidates swap the two because "temperate" sounds broader; the size-vs-intensity trade-off is the giveaway — small + destructive = tropical; large + mild = temperate.

**See also:** [[../../geography/07 - Wind, Ocean Current and Cyclone#Tropical and Temperate Cyclone (Extra-Tropical Cyclone)]]
</details>
