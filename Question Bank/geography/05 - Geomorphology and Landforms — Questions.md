@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=5
Which of the following is the correct description of endogenic forces?

- A) Forces acting on Earth's surface from external sources
- B) Forces originating from within the Earth
- C) Forces driven by solar energy
- D) Forces caused by wind and water
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Endogenic forces are internal geological forces that originate from within the Earth. They get their energy from within the Earth (recall the Convection Current Theory). Exogenic forces are external forces driven by solar energy.

**Trap:** Option A describes exogenic forces from outside Earth; Option C is the source of energy for exogenic (not endogenic) forces.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Endogenic Processes]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=5
Which of the following mountain ranges is continuously increasing in height due to the dominance of endogenic forces over exogenic forces?

- A) Aravalli
- B) Himalayas
- C) Western Ghats
- D) Andes
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Himalayas are continuously increasing because endogenic forces are significantly greater than exogenic forces. The Aravalli mountains are continuously decreasing because exogenic forces are significantly greater than endogenic forces.

**Trap:** Option A (Aravalli) is the trap — they are decreasing, not increasing. The Himalayas are the classic example of tectonically active, rising mountains.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Endogenic Processes]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=5
The process of breaking down or dissolving rocks and minerals on the Earth's surface is known as:

- A) Erosion
- B) Weathering
- C) Mass movement
- D) Denudation
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Weathering is the process of rocks and minerals breaking down or dissolving on the Earth's surface. Erosion involves transportation of weathered rock debris; mass movement is the downslope movement under gravity; denudation is the long-term wearing away of Earth's surface.

**Trap:** Erosion and weathering are often confused. Weathering is in-situ (in place); erosion involves transport of material by water, wind, glaciers, etc.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Weathering]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=5
Which landform is created when a stalactite (hanging from the ceiling) joins a stalagmite (rising from the ground) in a limestone cave?

- A) Stalactite
- B) Stalagmite
- C) Pillar
- D) Sink hole
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

When a stalactite (hanging from the ceiling) and a stalagmite (rising from the ground) join together, they form a pillar. These are depositional landforms in Karst topography, found in limestone/dolomite regions.

**Trap:** Students often confuse stalactite (ceiling) with stalagmite (ground). Mnemonic: 'stalactite' has a 'c' for ceiling; 'stalagmite' has a 'g' for ground.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Landforms Created by Groundwater (Karst Topography)]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=5
In which stage of a river's course are V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, waterfalls, and entrenched meanders typically formed?

- A) Youthful stage
- B) Mature stage
- C) Old stage
- D) Delta stage
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Youthful (upper) stage of a river is characterised by fast flow and steep gradients, producing V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, waterfalls, and entrenched meanders. The Mature stage features meanders; the Old stage features ox-bow lakes, deltas, and flood plains.

**Trap:** Option B (Mature) features meanders, not V-shaped valleys. The V-shape comes from the river's erosive power in fast-flowing upper reaches.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Landforms Created by River]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=5
A glacier carves out a bowl-shaped depression at the head of its valley. When the glacier melts and water fills the depression, the resulting lake is called a:

- A) Fjord
- B) Tarn lake
- C) Drumlin
- D) Esker
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A Tarn lake is created when water fills up in a cirque (bowl-shaped depression at the head of a glacial valley). A Fjord is formed when sea water enters a glacial valley. Drumlins and Eskers are depositional landforms.

**Trap:** Option A (Fjord) is the trap — both are glacial, but a Fjord is seawater entering a glacial valley, not a freshwater lake in a cirque.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Landforms Created by Glaciers]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=5
Which of the following rock types is most conducive to the formation of Karst topography?

- A) Granite
- B) Basalt
- C) Limestone
- D) Sandstone
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Karst topography forms in limestone and dolomite — rocks that are permeable, thinly bedded, highly jointed, and rich in calcium carbonate. These conditions allow for the dissolution and underground drainage that produces caves, sinkholes, and other karst features.

**Trap:** Granite is impermeable and resistant to chemical weathering; basalt is dense. Limestone's solubility in slightly acidic water is the key to karst formation.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Landforms Created by Groundwater (Karst Topography)]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=5
A crescent-shaped sand dune with its wings pointing away from the wind direction, typically found in Jaisalmer (Rajasthan), is known as:

- A) Seif dune
- B) Barchan dune
- C) Loess
- D) Playa
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Barchan dunes are crescent-shaped dunes whose wings point away from the wind direction, found in Jaisalmer (Rajasthan). Seif dunes are similar to barchans but with only one wing. Loess is fine wind-blown sand; Playas are flat areas in deserts.

**Trap:** Option A (Seif) is similar to a Barchan but has only one wing. The crescent shape with two wings pointed away from wind is the unique signature of a Barchan.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Landforms Created by Wind (Aeolian Landforms)]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=5
Match the following landforms with their correct erosional agent: (1) Stalactite, (2) Cirque, (3) Mushroom rock, (4) Cliff.

- A) (1) Groundwater; (2) Glacier; (3) Wind; (4) Sea waves
- B) (1) Wind; (2) Sea waves; (3) Glacier; (4) Groundwater
- C) (1) Sea waves; (2) Wind; (3) Groundwater; (4) Glacier
- D) (1) Glacier; (2) Groundwater; (3) Sea waves; (4) Wind
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct matches are: (1) Stalactite — Groundwater (Karst), (2) Cirque — Glacier, (3) Mushroom rock — Wind (Aeolian), (4) Cliff — Sea waves. Each landform is shaped by its dominant erosional agent.

**Trap:** Stalactites are depositional (in Karst caves), not typically considered "erosional" but groundwater is the agent. The trap is associating the wrong agent with the wrong landform.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#LANDFORMS]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=5
A river in its lower course abandons a looping bend, leaving a U-shaped water body cut off from the main channel. Which option correctly identifies both the landform and its dominant process?

- A) Cirque — glacial erosion
- B) Ox-bow lake — river deposition
- C) Tarn — groundwater deposition
- D) Playa — sea-wave erosion
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

An ox-bow lake is a U-shaped water body cut off from the main river. The chapter places ox-bow lakes among depositional river landforms of the old or lower course.

**Trap:** A tarn may also occupy a hollow, but it is water filling a glacial cirque rather than an abandoned river meander.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Landforms Created by River]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=5
Consider the following pairs:
1. Stalactite — grows from a cave ceiling
2. Stalagmite — grows from the cave floor
3. Fjord — sea water entering a glacial valley

Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- A) 1 only
- B) 1 and 2 only
- C) 2 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three pairs are correct. Stalactites are ceiling deposits and stalagmites rise from the ground in karst caves, while a fjord forms where sea water enters a glacial valley.

**Trap:** Stalactite and stalagmite are deliberately easy to reverse; the fjord is a flooded glacial valley, not a river-cut gorge.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Landforms Created by Groundwater (Karst Topography)]]
</details>

@qgeo.geomorphology_and_landforms.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=5
A geomorphologist observes rock breaking at its original location and later sees the debris carried away by flowing water. The two processes, in order, are:

- A) Erosion followed by weathering
- B) Weathering followed by erosion and transportation
- C) Mass movement followed by diastrophism
- D) Deposition followed by exfoliation
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Weathering breaks or dissolves rock in situ. Once flowing water removes and carries that debris, erosion and transportation are occurring. Both contribute to denudation but are distinct stages.

**Trap:** Erosion is often used loosely for all wearing away, but the chapter distinguishes in-situ weathering from ex-situ erosion and transportation.

**See also:** [[../../geography/05 - Geomorphology and Landforms#Exogenic Processes]]
</details>
