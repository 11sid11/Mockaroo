# Himalayan River System — Questions

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=12
Himalayan rivers are described as:

- A) Ephemeral and dependent on the monsoon for water flow
- B) Perennial rivers originating from glaciers in the Himalayas
- C) Seasonal rivers with shorter courses flowing through the Peninsular Plateau
- D) Rivers that originate from the Western Ghats and flow west into the Arabian Sea
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Himalayan River System — They are perennial rivers (flow throughout the year) and they do not depend on monsoon for their water flow. They originate from glaciers in the Himalayas."

**Trap:** A and C describe the Peninsular River System ("ephemeral", "shorter and shallower course"), not the Himalayan rivers. D is invented — Western Ghats rivers are separate and feed short west-coast streams.

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Himalayan River System]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=12
The Indus river originates from:

- A) Chemayungdung Glacier (Tibet)
- B) Bokhar Chu Glacier near Lake Mansarovar in the Kailash range
- C) Gangotri Glacier
- D) Milam Glacier
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Indus — Origin: Bokhar Chu Glacier (Tibet) in the Kailash mountain range near Lake Mansarovar and it enters India at Demchok (Ladakh)."

**Trap:** Chemayungdung Glacier is the origin of the Brahmaputra (q3 will reinforce), Gangotri is the origin of the Ganga (Bhagirathi), and Milam Glacier is the origin of the Sharda/Kali. Three Himalayan rivers, three glaciers — candidates mix them up.

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Indus River System]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=12
The Brahmaputra is known by different names in different regions. Which of the following is the correct sequence (Tibet to Arunachal Pradesh to Assam to Bangladesh)?

- A) Yarlung Tsangpo to Dihang/Siang to Brahmaputra to Jamuna
- B) Siang to Yarlung Tsangpo to Brahmaputra to Padma
- C) Brahmaputra to Siang to Yarlung Tsangpo to Meghna
- D) Tsangpo to Brahmaputra to Dihang to Jamuna
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter lists: "Tibet: Yarlung Tsangpo; Arunachal Pradesh: Siang / Dihang; Assam: Brahmaputra; Bangladesh: Jamuna."

**Trap:** Padma is the name of the Ganga in Bangladesh, not the Brahmaputra. Meghna is the combined river after the Padma-Jamuna confluence, not the Bangladesh name of the Brahmaputra itself.

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Brahmaputra River System]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=12
Which river is called the "Sorrow of Bihar" because of its frequent devastating floods?

- A) Gandak
- B) Ghaghara
- C) Kosi
- D) Son
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Kosi — Origin: Nepal Himalayas… It is also known as 'Sorrow of Bihar', as it brings frequent floods. It joins Ganga at Katihar district (Bihar)."

**Trap:** Candidates confuse "Sorrow of Bengal" (Damodar) with "Sorrow of Bihar" (Kosi). Both are "sorrow of ___" rivers but for different states.

</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=12
Match each drainage pattern with its example:

- Pattern 1: Dendritic
- Pattern 2: Trellis
- Pattern 3: Radial
- Pattern 4: Centripetal

Examples given: (a) Loktak Lake (Manipur); (b) Rivers of Northern Plains (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra); (c) Rivers in the upper Himalayan region (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra); (d) Rivers from Amarkantak Hills (Narmada, Son, Mahanadi).

Which option correctly pairs all four patterns with their examples?

- A) 1-b, 2-c, 3-d, 4-a
- B) 1-b, 2-d, 3-c, 4-a
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-d, 4-a
- D) 1-d, 2-c, 3-b, 4-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter gives: Dendritic -> tree-like, acute-angle tributaries, e.g. Indus/Ganga/Brahmaputra of the Northern Plains. Trellis -> parallel primary tributaries + right-angle secondaries, e.g. upper Himalayan region rivers. Radial -> streams radiating outward from a central high point, e.g. Narmada/Son/Mahanadi from Amarkantak. Centripetal -> opposite of radial, e.g. Loktak Lake.

**Trap:** B and C swap the dendritic and trellis examples — both involve Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra, but in *different* geographic settings (Northern Plains vs upper Himalayas).

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Drainage Pattern]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=12
A student is told: "This is an antecedent river that follows its original course despite geological changes, and is one of the five tributaries of the Indus." Which river is being described?

- A) Jhelum
- B) Son
- C) Mahanadi
- D) Narmada
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter lists antecedent Himalayan rivers as "Sutlej, Kosi, Indus, Ganga." Indus tributaries include (left bank) Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej. Jhelum is both a left-bank tributary of the Indus *and* part of the antecedent-river family (it preserves its original course through the Pir Panjal uplift). Son, Mahanadi, and Narmada are Peninsular rivers, not Indus tributaries.

**Trap:** Son, Mahanadi, Narmada are Peninsular rivers — they do not flow into the Indus. Many candidates confuse "antecedent" (a property) with "tributary" (a relation).

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Drainage Pattern]] · [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Indus River System]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=12
The Dhola-Sadiya Bridge (also called the Bhupen Hazarika Bridge) connecting Assam and Arunachal Pradesh spans which river?

- A) Brahmaputra
- B) Subansiri
- C) Lohit
- D) Manas
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "The Dhola-Sadiya Bridge also known as Bhupen Hazarika Bridge connecting Assam and Arunachal Pradesh, spans over Lohit river."

**Trap:** The bridge is *named* after the Brahmaputra region geography, but it actually spans the Lohit (a left-bank tributary of the Brahmaputra). Candidates often assume "Brahmaputra bridge" without reading the chapter carefully.

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Brahmaputra River System]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=12
Which city is on the banks of the Gomati (Gomti) river?

- A) Kanpur
- B) Lucknow
- C) Agra
- D) Allahabad
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter "Cities on River" table lists: "Lucknow — Gomati." Kanpur, Allahabad, Varanasi, Patna are on the Ganga; Agra is on the Yamuna.

**Trap:** Allahabad is at the Ganga-Yamuna confluence (Sangam) — candidates often confuse it with Lucknow. Note the chapter actually lists "Lucknow — Gomati" and "Lucknow — Gomti" twice in the table (a duplication), but the answer is unambiguous.

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Cities on River]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=12
Which sequence correctly matches each Himalayan river with its geographic landmark?

- River 1: Indus; River 2: Brahmaputra; River 3: Ganga; River 4: Barak

Landmarks: (a) U-turn at Namcha Barwa; (b) Singi Khamban in Tibet; (c) Sunderbans Delta; (d) Surma to Kushiyara to Meghna.

Which option correctly pairs all four rivers with their landmarks?

- A) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c, 4-d
- B) 1-b, 2-c, 3-a, 4-d
- C) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c, 4-d
- D) 1-d, 2-a, 3-b, 4-c
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: In Tibet, the Indus is known as **Singi Khamban** ("lion mouth"). The Brahmaputra "takes a sharp U-turn at Namcha Barwa (Tibet)." The Ganga/Brahmaputra form the **Sundarbans Delta** (largest in the world). The Barak enters Bangladesh as Surma and Kushiyara, then becomes the Meghna after combining with Padma and Jamuna.

**Trap:** The Sunderbans is jointly fed by the Ganga and Brahmaputra — candidates sometimes assign it solely to the Ganga or solely to the Brahmaputra. Also, "Singi Khamban" is specifically the Indus Tibetan name, not the Brahmaputra one.

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Indus River System]] · [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Brahmaputra River System]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=12
Consider the following about river islands:
(1) Majuli — worlds largest river island, located on the Brahmaputra.
(2) Umananda — worlds smallest river island, located on the Ganga near Varanasi.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both statements are true
- B) Only statement 1 is true; statement 2 is false (Umananda is on the Brahmaputra near Guwahati)
- C) Only statement 2 is true; statement 1 is false
- D) Both statements are false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Majuli (worlds largest river island) and Umananda (worlds smallest river island) is located on the Brahmaputra river." So statement 1 is true; statement 2 is false because it places Umananda on the Ganga near Varanasi — it is on the Brahmaputra near Guwahati.

**Trap:** Umananda is one of the most frequently mis-asked-about river islands because candidates confuse it with Srirangapatna (an island on the Kaveri). Umananda location is the Brahmaputra, not the Ganga.

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Brahmaputra River System]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayan_rivers.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=12
Assertion (A): The Indus Water Treaty allocates the three western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) exclusively to Pakistan and the three eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India.
Reason (R): The treaty was signed in 1960 at Karachi and was mediated by the World Bank, with the rivers divided roughly along the watershed tilt of the Punjab plains.
Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter confirms both: the Treaty allocates the western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan and the eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India; and it was signed in Karachi in 1960, mediated by the World Bank. R correctly explains the *geographic basis* for the allocation — the three eastern rivers flow mostly through Indian Punjab before joining the Indus, while the three western rivers cross into Pakistan-controlled territory. The watershed tilt of the Punjab plains is the natural reason for the split.

**Trap:** Candidates who do not read carefully pick C ("R is false") because they think the World Bank "mediated but did not decide" — the treaty text was indeed brokered by the World Bank, so R is true.

**See also:** [[../../geography/12 - Himalayan River System#Indus Water Treaty (1960)]]
</details>
