@qgeo.monsoon.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=15
Which latitude divides India into tropical and subtropical zones?

- A) Equator
- B) Tropic of Capricorn
- C) Tropic of Cancer
- D) 38th Parallel
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Latitude: The Tropic of Cancer divides India into tropical and subtropical zones." The Tropic of Cancer (23.5 degrees N) passes through the middle of India.

**Trap:** The 38th Parallel divides North and South Korea (Chapter 19), not India. The Equator passes through Africa and South America, not India.

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#Factors Affecting Climate of Any Place]]
</details>

@qgeo.monsoon.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=15
The ITCZ (Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone) shifts northward in July to roughly which latitude range, forming the monsoon trough?

- A) 10-15 degrees N
- B) 20-25 degrees N
- C) 30-35 degrees N
- D) Equator
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "It [ITCZ] is shifted around 20 degrees N-25 degrees N latitudes (over the Gangetic plain) in July month and is sometimes called the monsoon trough."

**Trap:** Candidates confuse the ITCZ July position (20-25 N) with its mean annual position (near the equator). The 30-35 N band is too far north.

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#Summer Season (March–May)]]
</details>

@qgeo.monsoon.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=15
Kal Baisakhi refers to which type of pre-monsoon shower, and where is it most associated?

- A) Blossom showers in Kerala (coffee)
- B) Nor westers in West Bengal (helps jute and tea)
- C) Mango showers in Karnataka (mango)
- D) Cherry blossoms in Assam (rice)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Nor westers: They are known as Kal Baisakhi (calamity in the month of Baisakh) in West Bengal (they help in growth of jute and tea crops) and Bardoli Chheerah/Bordoisila in Assam."

**Trap:** Blossom showers are associated with Kerala/coffee, Mango showers with Karnataka/Kerala, and the Assam version is called Bordoisila. Kal Baisakhi is specifically the West Bengal name.

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#Pre-Monsoon Showers]]
</details>

@qgeo.monsoon.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=15
In the Koeppen climate classification, group "A" (Tropical) is defined by which condition?

- A) Potential evaporation exceeds precipitation
- B) Average temperature of the coldest month is 18 degrees C or higher
- C) Average temperature of the coldest month is -3 degrees C or below
- D) Average temperature for all months is below 10 degrees C
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter lists: "A: Tropical — Average temperature of the coldest month is 18 degrees C or higher." B describes Dry Climate; C describes Cold Snow Forest (D); D describes Polar/Tundra (E).

**Trap:** Candidates mix up the threshold (18 degrees C) with the C category threshold (-3 degrees C for the coldest month). The Koeppen boundaries are precise and frequently tested.

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#Koeppen Climate Classification (1884)]]
</details>

@qgeo.monsoon.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=15
Match each seasonal phenomenon with its description:

- Phenomenon 1: Western Disturbances
- Phenomenon 2: ITCZ shift to 20-25 N
- Phenomenon 3: El Nino
- Phenomenon 4: Continentality effect

Descriptions: (a) Pressure difference between Tahiti and Darwin is negative, causing floods in Australia and India; (b) Causes winter rainfall in northern India, known as Mahawat in Rajasthan; (c) Coastal cities like Mumbai have moderate climate due to sea influence; (d) Creates the monsoon trough over the Gangetic plain in July.

Which option correctly pairs all four?

- A) 1-b, 2-d, 3-a, 4-c
- B) 1-b, 2-a, 3-d, 4-c
- C) 1-c, 2-d, 3-a, 4-b
- D) 1-d, 2-b, 3-a, 4-c
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter maps: (1) Western Disturbances -> winter rainfall, "Mahawat" in Rajasthan (cyclonic storms from Mediterranean and Caspian); (2) ITCZ shift to 20-25 N -> monsoon trough over Gangetic plain in July; (3) El Nino -> pressure difference between Tahiti and Darwin is negative, can cause floods in Australia and India; (4) Continentality -> coastal cities like Mumbai have moderate climate due to sea influence (inland cities like Delhi/Kanpur have extreme climate).

**Trap:** B swaps ITCZ shift and El Nino effects. The Tahitis-Darwin pressure relationship is El Nino (negative SOI). The continentality effect is the *sea-moderating* phenomenon, not the ITCZ.

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#El Niño vs La Niña]] · [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#Continentality Effect]]
</details>

@qgeo.monsoon.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=15
The South-West monsoon in India arrives at which of the following places FIRST?

- A) Kolkata
- B) Mumbai
- C) Thiruvananthapuram (Kerala coast)
- D) Delhi
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Southwest monsoon typically first hits the Kerala coast (Thiruvananthapuram region), around June 1, before progressing up the western coast (Mumbai by ~June 10), to Kolkata, and then onward to Delhi.

**Trap:** Mumbai is sometimes (wrongly) called the "first place" because it is the most economically prominent monsoon city. The chapter confirms the monsoon first strikes the southern tip of the Indian peninsula.

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#Summer Season (March–May)]]
</details>

@qgeo.monsoon.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=15
According to the chapter, during EL NINO conditions in India, the most likely impact is:

- A) Above-average and timely monsoon
- B) Below-average and late monsoon
- C) No change in monsoon pattern
- D) Colder winters across north India
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states (El Nino row): "India experiences below average and late monsoon." During El Nino, the pressure difference between Tahiti and Darwin is negative, suppressing Indian monsoon convection.

**Trap:** La Nina (not El Nino) brings the *positive* impact (above-average rains). Candidates confuse the two because they sound similar.

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#El Niño vs La Niña]]
</details>

@qgeo.monsoon.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=15
Consider the following statements about ENSO:
(1) ENSO stands for El Nino Southern Oscillation and the Tahiti-Darwin pressure difference defines the SOI (Southern Oscillation Index).
(2) During El Nino, the pressure difference between Tahiti and Darwin becomes negative; this *favours* heavy rainfall in India and dry conditions in Australia.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both statements are true
- B) Only statement 1 is true; statement 2 is false (El Nino causes DROUGHTS in India and floods in Australia, the opposite)
- C) Only statement 2 is true; statement 1 is false
- D) Both statements are false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Statement 1 is true — ENSO = El Nino Southern Oscillation, and the pressure difference between Tahiti and Darwin defines SOI. Statement 2 is false: the chapter explicitly says El Nino can cause "intense rainfall in Australia and India along with heavy floods; drier conditions in South America" — but on the India row of the table, "It can cause droughts in Asia and Australia… India experiences below average and late monsoon." The chapter has an internal tension between the introductory description and the row label; per the row, El Nino SUPPRESSES the Indian monsoon. The chapter row description is the authoritative one for SSC.

**Trap:** The wording of statement 2 mirrors the introductory paragraph of the El Nino section in the chapter — candidates who only skim the intro pick A. The table row for India reverses this (drought, late monsoon).

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#El Niño vs La Niña]]
</details>

@qgeo.monsoon.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=15
A SSC aspirant studies a region located just above the Tropic of Cancer that receives rainfall only in winter and is dry in summer. According to the Koeppen classification, this region is most likely coded as:

- A) Af
- B) BSh
- C) Csa / Csb (Mediterranean: dry summer)
- D) Dfa
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter poses the question: "What will be the Koeppen Climatic classification of Mediterranean region located just above the Tropic of Cancer region and having no rainfall in summers?" The Mediterranean climate has dry summers, mild rainy winters — Koeppen code Csa (hot summer) or Csb (warm summer) with s = summer dry. The other codes: Af = tropical rainforest (no dry season); BSh = hot semi-arid (steppe); Dfa = humid continental hot summer.

**Trap:** Af (full rainfall tropical) is the wrong temptation because it is the most common Indian-climate answer. Csa/Csb is specifically Mediterranean — dry summer is the diagnostic feature.

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#Koeppen Climate Classification (1884)]]
</details>

@qgeo.monsoon.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=15
Assertion (A): Mawsynram (Meghalaya) is one of the wettest places on Earth because of orographic rainfall caused by the funnel-shaped hills of the Khasi range that force the monsoon winds upward.
Reason (R): Orographic rainfall occurs when moist air is forced to rise over a mountain barrier, cooling adiabatically and releasing precipitation on the windward side.
Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter lists "Mawsynram" as a key concept and "Relief Features: Mountains cause orographic rainfall" among the factors affecting climate. A is true: Mawsynram is one of the wettest places due to orographic uplift over the Khasi Hills (the funnel-shaped gap concentrates moisture). R is the textbook definition of orographic rainfall and *explains* why Mawsynram gets so much rain — the cool, moist monsoon air is forced up by the Khasi Hills, condenses, and drops rain on the windward (southern) slopes.

**Trap:** B is the partial-credit trap. A and R are both factually correct, but the question tests whether R explains A — yes, the *reason* Mawsynram is so wet is exactly the mechanism R describes.

**See also:** [[../../geography/15 - Monsoon#Factors Affecting Climate of Any Place]]
</details>
