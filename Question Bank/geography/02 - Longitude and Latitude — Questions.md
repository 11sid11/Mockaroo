@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=2
Which of the following latitudes is the largest (longest) parallel of latitude on Earth?

- A) Tropic of Cancer
- B) Equator
- C) Arctic Circle
- D) Antarctic Circle
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Equator (0°) is the longest parallel of latitude and is the only latitude that is also a great circle, measuring about 40,075 km. All other parallels shrink in length as they move toward the poles.

**Trap:** Students often think the Tropic of Cancer is the largest because it is 'important'; in fact, latitude length is maximum at the Equator and decreases with latitude.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#Latitude]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=2
Through which country does the Prime Meridian (0° longitude) officially pass?

- A) Paris, France
- B) Greenwich, United Kingdom
- C) Washington, D.C., USA
- D) Geneva, Switzerland
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Prime Meridian (also known as the International Meridian or Greenwich Meridian) passes through Greenwich, London, UK.

**Trap:** Students confuse the Prime Meridian (a longitude of 0°) with the Equator (a latitude of 0°); the Prime Meridian is named after Greenwich, UK.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#Longitude]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=2
On approximately which date is the Earth at perihelion (closest to the Sun)?

- A) 21 June
- B) 4 July
- C) 3 January
- D) 22 December
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Earth reaches perihelion around 3 January, when it is about 147 million km from the Sun. Aphelion (farthest, about 152 million km) occurs around 4 July.

**Trap:** Many students assume Earth is closest to the Sun in summer (June) because it is hot; in fact, the Northern Hemisphere is tilted away at perihelion.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#Distance of Earth from Sun and Moon]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=2
How many degrees of longitude correspond to a one-hour difference in local time?

- A) 1°
- B) 10°
- C) 15°
- D) 30°
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours, so 360° / 24 = 15° per hour. Therefore, 1° of longitude equals 4 minutes of time difference.

**Trap:** Option A (1°) is the time for 4 minutes, not 1 hour — students often confuse degree-to-hour conversion with degree-to-minute conversion.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#Concept of Time using Longitude]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=2
A ship sails west from 30°E across 90° of longitude. What is the difference in local time between the starting point and the destination?

- A) 3 hours
- B) 6 hours
- C) 9 hours
- D) 12 hours
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

15° of longitude = 1 hour, so 90° = 90 / 15 = 6 hours. Moving west, local time decreases by 6 hours.

**Trap:** Some students halve the answer because they think only half the circumference is involved. The conversion is linear: every 15° is exactly one hour regardless of direction.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#Concept of Time using Longitude]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=2
Which of the following correctly describes the Equator in terms of a great circle?

- A) The Equator is not a great circle
- B) The Equator is the only latitude that is a great circle; all longitudes are great circles
- C) The Equator is a great circle, but no other latitude is
- D) Only the Equator and antimeridian are great circles
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A great circle is the largest circle that can be drawn on a sphere, dividing it into two equal hemispheres. All longitudes are great circles, but the only latitude that is also a great circle is the Equator (0° latitude).

**Trap:** Option C is tempting because it states the Equator is a great circle, but it incorrectly excludes all longitudes from being great circles.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#Concept of Great Circle]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=2
While travelling from Japan to the USA across the International Date Line, what change should a traveller make to the calendar date?

- A) Add one day
- B) Subtract one day
- C) No change in date
- D) Add two days
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The International Date Line is at 180° and zig-zags through the Pacific Ocean. According to the chapter's example, a traveller crossing from Japan to the USA subtracts one day and thereby gains a day.

**Trap:** The change of date is easy to reverse. For this source-based question, remember the stated example: Japan → USA means subtract one day.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#International Date Line (180° E/W)]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=2
Which latitude is the smallest (shortest) parallel of latitude on Earth?

- A) Equator
- B) Tropic of Cancer
- C) Arctic Circle
- D) Poles (90°)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The smallest latitudes are the Poles (90°N and 90°S), which shrink to a single point. Latitude length decreases progressively from the Equator (longest) to the Poles (smallest).

**Trap:** Option C (Arctic Circle, 66½°N) is the highest inhabited parallel but is not the smallest. The poles themselves are the smallest, being mere points.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#Latitude]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=2
In the Northern Hemisphere, on 22 December, the sun's vertical rays fall directly on the:

- A) Equator
- B) Tropic of Cancer
- C) Tropic of Capricorn
- D) Arctic Circle
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

22 December is the Winter Solstice in the Northern Hemisphere. On this date, vertical rays of the Sun are directly overhead on the Tropic of Capricorn (23½°S), making it the shortest day in the Northern Hemisphere and the longest day in the Southern Hemisphere.

**Trap:** Option A (Equator) is associated with the equinoxes (21 March / 23 September), not the December solstice.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#SOLSTICE AND EQUINOX]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=2
A pilot flies due east along the 60°N parallel from London (0°) to a point 90° east. Compared to the same easting along the equator, the actual ground distance covered is:

- A) The same as on the equator
- B) Half that on the equator
- C) Twice that on the equator
- D) One-fourth of that on the equator
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The circumference of a parallel at 60°N is half that of the equator (cos 60° = 0.5). So 90° of longitude at 60°N covers exactly half the ground distance of 90° at the equator.

**Trap:** Students often assume all 90° arcs of longitude are equal; in reality, longitude distance shrinks as cos(latitude) of the parallel.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#Longitude]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=2
On 21 June, the sun's vertical rays fall on the Tropic of Cancer. Which of the following correctly describes the day at the North Pole on this date?

- A) Six months of continuous darkness
- B) Six months of continuous daylight begins
- C) Normal 12-hour day like the equator
- D) Sun rises for the first time in the year
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

21 June (Summer Solstice) marks the start of six months of continuous daylight at the North Pole because the North Pole is tilted maximally toward the Sun. Conversely, 22 December marks the start of six months of continuous daylight at the South Pole.

**Trap:** Option D is tempting because the North Pole is associated with the 'first sunrise' but that occurs at the Arctic Circle, not the pole itself.

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#SOLSTICE AND EQUINOX]]
</details>

@qgeo.longitude_and_latitude.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=2
Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes a Solar Eclipse from a Lunar Eclipse?

- A) Solar Eclipse occurs on a full moon; Lunar Eclipse on a new moon
- B) Solar Eclipse occurs on a new moon; Lunar Eclipse on a full moon
- C) Both occur only on full moons
- D) Both occur only on new moons
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A Solar Eclipse occurs when the Moon comes between the Earth and the Sun, casting a shadow on Earth — this happens at New Moon (Amavasya). A Lunar Eclipse occurs when Earth is between the Sun and Moon — at Full Moon (Purnima).

**Trap:** Students often swap these — the key is: Solar (Sun blocked) is New Moon (Moon between Earth and Sun); Lunar (Moon blocked) is Full Moon (Earth between Sun and Moon).

**See also:** [[../../geography/02 - Longitude and Latitude#Solar Eclipse]]
</details>
