@qgeo.atmosphere.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=6
Which gas is the most abundant in Earth's atmosphere by volume?
- A) Oxygen
- B) Nitrogen
- C) Carbon dioxide
- D) Argon
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

According to the chapter, the composition of dry air in the troposphere is Nitrogen (N₂) ≈ 78%, Oxygen (O₂) ≈ 21%, Argon (Ar) ≈ 0.9% and Carbon dioxide (CO₂) ≈ 0.036%. Hence nitrogen dominates the atmosphere.

**Trap:** Candidates often pick oxygen because it is biologically vital, but by sheer volume nitrogen is roughly four times more abundant than oxygen.

**See also:** [[../../geography/06 - Atmosphere#Troposphere → (Tropos: Region of mixing)]]
</details>

@qgeo.atmosphere.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=6
In correct ascending order (lowest to highest altitude), the layers of Earth's atmosphere are:
- A) Stratosphere → Troposphere → Mesosphere → Thermosphere → Exosphere
- B) Troposphere → Mesosphere → Stratosphere → Thermosphere → Exosphere
- C) Troposphere → Stratosphere → Mesosphere → Thermosphere → Exosphere
- D) Troposphere → Stratosphere → Thermosphere → Mesosphere → Exosphere
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Key concepts list explicitly orders the layers bottom-to-top as Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere, Exosphere.

**Trap:** Candidates confuse the temperature trend (which rises again in the stratosphere and thermosphere) with altitude order; altitude is independent of whether temperature rises or falls.

**See also:** [[../../geography/06 - Atmosphere#DIFFERENT LAYERS OF ATMOSPHERE]]
</details>

@qgeo.atmosphere.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=6
According to the standard lapse rate, temperature in the troposphere falls by 1°C for every:
- A) 100 m of ascent
- B) 165 m of ascent
- C) 1.5 km of ascent
- D) 6.5 km of ascent
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter gives the standard lapse rate as 165 m → 1°C decrease (equivalently, 1 km → 6.5°C decrease). The corresponding figure of 6.5°C per km in option D is the same rate expressed differently.

**Trap:** Option D (6.5°C per km) is numerically correct but in the wrong unit; the 1°C benchmark in the chapter is paired with 165 m, not 6.5 km.

**See also:** [[../../geography/06 - Atmosphere#Temperature Behavior]]
</details>

@qgeo.atmosphere.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=6
A meteorologist climbing from sea level to 3 km altitude expects the air temperature, under standard lapse-rate conditions, to drop by roughly:
- A) 1.5°C
- B) 6.5°C
- C) 19.5°C
- D) 39°C
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Standard lapse rate = 1°C drop per 165 m, equivalently 6.5°C per 1 km. For 3 km: 3 × 6.5°C = 19.5°C.

**Trap:** Many candidates pick 6.5°C, confusing the per-km rate with the total drop; always multiply by the altitude interval.

**See also:** [[../../geography/06 - Atmosphere#Temperature Behavior]]
</details>

@qgeo.atmosphere.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=6
Why does the troposphere become cooler with increasing altitude even though solar (shortwave) radiation is most intense at the top of the atmosphere?
- A) The upper troposphere contains less nitrogen
- B) Air is heated mainly by long-wave terrestrial radiation re-emitted from Earth's surface
- C) Ozone in the troposphere absorbs sunlight and cools the air
- D) Wind mixes cold polar air downward
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter explains: "The atmosphere does not heat up directly from solar radiation (insolation). Instead, the Earth's surface absorbs solar energy and then emits it as long-wave infrared radiation (Terrestrial Radiation), which warms the lower atmosphere. As the altitude increases, less terrestrial radiation is available, leading to drop in temperature."

**Trap:** Candidates instinctively assume "sun heats air", but in the troposphere the surface is the primary heat source and infrared re-radiation drives warming — that is why temperature falls with height.

**See also:** [[../../geography/06 - Atmosphere#Temperature Behavior]]
</details>

@qgeo.atmosphere.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=6
Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?
- A) Carbon dioxide
- B) Methane
- C) Nitrogen dioxide
- D) Water vapour
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter explicitly notes "NO₂ is not a greenhouse gas." The listed key greenhouse gases are Water vapor, CO₂, CH₄ (Methane), N₂O (Nitrous Oxide), O₃ (Ozone), and SO₂ (Sulfur Dioxide).

@qgeo.atmosphere.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=6
Among the following surfaces, which has the HIGHEST albedo (reflects the greatest proportion of incoming shortwave radiation)?
- A) Open ocean
- B) Sand desert
- C) Old snow
- D) Sea ice
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The albedo table gives: Ocean/Lakes 8%, Sand/Desert 27%, Sea ice 35%, Old snow 59%, Fresh snow 80%. Of the four choices, old snow (59%) reflects the most. (Fresh snow at 80% is higher still but is not among the options.)

**Trap:** Candidates remember "ice caps have the highest albedo" and pick sea ice; sea ice (35%) is much lower than old snow (59%).

**See also:** [[../../geography/06 - Atmosphere#Albedo]]
</details>

@qgeo.atmosphere.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=6
Assertion (A): The tropopause temperature at the equator is −80°C, and the stratosphere directly above it is colder than the troposphere just below.
Reason (R): Within the troposphere, temperature decreases with height; in the stratosphere, temperature increases with height due to ozone absorbing UV radiation.
- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Tropopause temperature at the equator is −80°C (true). Temperature falls with height in the troposphere and rises with height in the stratosphere because ozone absorbs UV (true). However, R does not explain the specific value −80°C at the equator tropopause — that value comes from the equator's greater depth of troposphere and intense convection, not directly from the ozone-UV mechanism.

**Trap:** The assertion-reason trap is treating the stratosphere mechanism as the explanation for the exact tropopause value; the two facts are independent observations.

**See also:** [[../../geography/06 - Atmosphere#Temperature at Tropopause]]
</details>

@qgeo.atmosphere.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=6
Of the four statements below, pick the one that is correct:
- A) The mesosphere is the hottest layer; the thermosphere is the coldest
- B) The thermosphere is the hottest layer; the mesosphere is the coldest
- C) The stratosphere is the hottest layer; the exosphere is the coldest
- D) The thermosphere and mesosphere are both colder than the troposphere
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: Mesosphere is the "coldest layer", Thermosphere is the "hottest layer". Hence option B is the only consistent pairing.

**Trap:** Many candidates reverse hot and cold because the thermosphere's name contains "thermo-" (heat) but they forget the mesosphere sits above the stratosphere and continues cooling.

**See also:** [[../../geography/06 - Atmosphere#Mesosphere (upto 85 km)]]
</details>

@qgeo.atmosphere.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=6
A weather observatory records surface air temperature at 5°C, yet a balloon-borne sensor at 500 m altitude reads 9°C. This pattern is most likely due to:
- A) Standard lapse rate cooling
- B) Temperature inversion
- C) Convectional rainfall
- D) Anabatic wind on a windward slope
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter defines Temperature Inversion as "temperature increases with height instead of decreasing", with favourable conditions of long winter night, still air and clear cloudless sky. A 4°C rise over 500 m is exactly such an inversion, since the standard lapse rate would predict a drop of ~3.25°C, not a rise.

**Trap:** Students conditioned by the lapse rate may assume "temperature always falls with height" and mark A; an inversion is the textbook exception.

**See also:** [[../../geography/06 - Atmosphere#Temperature Inversion]]
</details>
