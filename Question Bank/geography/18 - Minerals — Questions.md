@qgeo.minerals.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=18
Minerals are classified into how many broad types?

- A) 2 (Metallic and Non-Metallic)
- B) 3 (Metallic, Non-Metallic and Energy Minerals)
- C) 4 (Metallic, Non-Metallic, Energy and Precious)
- D) 5 (Ferrous, Non-Ferrous, Precious, Non-Metallic and Energy)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Minerals are of three types: Metallic, Non-Metallic, and Energy Minerals." Under Metallic, the chapter further breaks into Ferrous, Non-Ferrous, and Precious sub-categories.

**Trap:** D is the trap — those *are* sub-categories, but they are all under "Metallic." The three top-level types are Metallic, Non-Metallic, Energy.

**See also:** [[../../geography/18 - Minerals#MINERALS]]
</details>

@qgeo.minerals.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=18
The finest iron ore with the highest iron content (up to 70 percent) is:

- A) Hematite
- B) Magnetite
- C) Limonite
- D) Siderite
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Magnetite is the finest iron ore with a very high content of iron up to 70 percent." Hematite (50-60 percent iron) is the most important *industrial* iron ore by quantity used.

**Trap:** A is the most-produced iron ore in India (Hematite). Candidates confuse "most produced" with "highest grade." The chapter explicitly labels Magnetite as "the finest iron ore" with the highest iron content.

**See also:** [[../../geography/18 - Minerals#Iron Ore]]
</details>

@qgeo.minerals.q3 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=18
Which state is the largest producer of Bauxite in India?

- A) Jharkhand
- B) Madhya Pradesh
- C) Odisha
- D) Gujarat
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Odisha is the largest producer of Bauxite. Panchpatmali deposits in Koraput district are the most important bauxite deposits in Odisha." Bauxite is the primary ore of aluminium.

**Trap:** B (MP) is the largest producer of Manganese. A (Jharkhand) is the leading producer of mica and copper (Singhbhum). D (Gujarat) is the leading producer of salt and petroleum.

**See also:** [[../../geography/18 - Minerals#Aluminium]]
</details>

The Koderma-Gaya-Hazaribagh belt of Jharkhand is the leading producer of:

- A) Bauxite
- B) Mica
- C) Iron ore
- D) Manganese
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Trap:** A is from Odisha; C is from Odisha-Jharkhand belt (Singhbhum) and Bellary belt; D is from Madhya Pradesh (top producer).

@qgeo.minerals.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=18
A mining engineer is searching for diamond deposits. According to the chapter, the only state in India that produces diamond is:

- A) Madhya Pradesh (Panna)
- B) Andhra Pradesh (Golconda)
- C) Rajasthan (Kishengarh)
- D) Chhattisgarh (Bastar)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "Madhya Pradesh (Panna), is the only state that produces diamond." Panna is in eastern Madhya Pradesh.

**Trap:** B is the trap — Golconda (Andhra Pradesh / Telangana) was a famous *historical* diamond mining area (Koh-i-Noor, Hope Diamond) but no longer produces diamonds. C and D do not have diamond mines per the chapter.

**See also:** [[../../geography/18 - Minerals#Diamond]]
</details>

@qgeo.minerals.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=18
Match each coal type with its carbon content range:

- Coal 1: Anthracite
- Coal 2: Bituminous
- Coal 3: Lignite

Carbon ranges: (a) 70-80 percent, (b) 50-70 percent, (c) 80-90 percent.

Which option correctly pairs all three?

- A) 1-c, 2-a, 3-b
- B) 1-a, 2-c, 3-b
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- D) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter lists: "Anthracite: 80-90 percent carbon, Highest quality, Hard coal. Bituminous: 70-80 percent carbon… also known metallurgical coal/coking coal. Lignite: 50-70 percent carbon… Neyveli in Tamil Nadu is an important source of Lignite."

**Trap:** B swaps Anthracite and Bituminous ranges (close numerical values, easy to confuse). The chapter is precise: Anthracite > Bituminous > Lignite > Peat in carbon content.

**See also:** [[../../geography/18 - Minerals#Types of Coal]]
</details>

@qgeo.minerals.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=18
The Gondwana coal deposits in India are primarily located in which valleys?

- A) Damodar, Son, Mahanadi and Godavari valleys
- B) Brahmaputra and Barak valleys
- C) Narmada and Tapi valleys
- D) Krishna and Kaveri valleys
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "The major resources of Gondwana coal (metallurgical coal) are located in Damodar valley. Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro are important coalfields. The Godavari, Mahanadi, Son and Wardha valleys also contain coal deposits." Over 97 percent of coal reserves are in these valleys.

**Trap:** C (Narmada/Tapi) is a rift-valley region but not a coal belt. B (NE India) has tertiary coal, not Gondwana. D is wrong.

**See also:** [[../../geography/18 - Minerals#1. Coal]]
</details>

The HVJ (Hazira-Vijaipur-Jagdishpur) cross-country gas pipeline, established in 1986 by GAIL, links which two ends?

- B) Digboi refinery (Assam) with Haldia port (West Bengal)

Consider the following statements about iron ore in India:
(1) Iron ore reserves (topmost in India) are in Odisha.
(2) Iron ore production is led by Odisha, followed by Chhattisgarh and Karnataka.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both statements are true
- B) Only statement 1 is true
- C) Only statement 2 is true; statement 1 is false (reserves are in Jharkhand)
- D) Both statements are false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "Reserve (topmost): Odisha. Production: Odisha > Chhattisgarh > Karnataka." Both statements are true.

**Trap:** C is the trap — Jharkhand has the Singhbhum iron ore belt (Noamudi and Gua), but the chapter ranks *reserves* (not production) as Odisha #1.

</details>

@qgeo.minerals.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=18
Match each renewable energy source with its most suitable Indian location:

- Source 1: Tidal energy
- Source 2: Wind energy (largest farm cluster)
- Source 3: Geothermal energy (Puga Valley)
- Source 4: Solar energy (largest park)

Locations: (a) Bhadla (Rajasthan), (b) Nagercoil to Madurai (Tamil Nadu), (c) Gulf of Khambhat and Gulf of Kutch (Gujarat), (d) Ladakh.

Which option correctly pairs all four?

- A) 1-c, 2-b, 3-d, 4-a
- B) 1-a, 2-c, 3-d, 4-b
- C) 1-c, 2-a, 3-b, 4-d
- D) 1-d, 2-c, 3-b, 4-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "The largest wind farm cluster is located in Tamil Nadu from Nagercoil to Madurai." "Tidal energy… Gulf of Khambhat, the Gulf of Kutch in Gujarat on the western coast and Gangetic delta in Sunderban regions of West Bengal." "Geothermal energy… Puga Valley (Ladakh)." "Bhadla Solar Park in Rajasthan is the largest."

**Trap:** B reverses solar and wind. The two large Rajasthan/Tamil Nadu renewable projects are easy to swap.

**See also:** [[../../geography/18 - Minerals#Renewable Sources]]
</details>

@qgeo.minerals.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=18
Assertion (A): Tarapur (Maharashtra) is Indias first nuclear power plant, commissioned in 1969.
Reason (R): Asias first nuclear reactor, Apsara, was set up in 1956 in Bombay and shut down in 2009. The father of Indias nuclear programme is H. J. Bhabha.
Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Both A and R are factually true per the chapter: Tarapur is Indias first nuclear power plant (1969, Maharashtra), and Apsara was Asias first nuclear reactor (1956, Bombay, shut down 2009). H. J. Bhabha is the father of the Indian nuclear programme. However, R provides *historical context* — the existence of Apsara and Bhabha — but does not explain *why* Tarapur was built first in 1969. The 13-year gap (1956 -> 1969) and the shift from a research reactor to a power plant are not explained by R.

**Trap:** A is the obvious "looks-right" pick because all three facts are true. The test is whether R explains A — it gives background but not a direct causal link.

**See also:** [[../../geography/18 - Minerals#1. Nuclear/Atomic Energy]]
</details>
