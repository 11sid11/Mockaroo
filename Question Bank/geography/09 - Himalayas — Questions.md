@qgeo.himalayas.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=9
The Himalayas were formed by the collision of the Indo-Australian tectonic plate with the:
- A) Pacific Plate
- B) Eurasian (Asian) Plate
- C) African Plate
- D) Arabian Plate
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "They were formed when the Indo-Australian tectonic plate and Eurasian (Asian) tectonic plate collided, this caused the closure of the ancient Tethys Sea, leading to the folding and uplifting of sediments deposited on its seabed."

**Trap:** The Pacific and Arabian plates are real tectonic neighbours but were not the colliding partner that formed the Himalayas; the closure of the Tethys Sea is the diagnostic clue.

**See also:** [[../../geography/09 - Himalayas#Formation of Himalayas]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayas.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=9
Which is the highest peak of the Karakoram Range, also the world's second-highest peak?
- A) Mt. Everest
- B) K2 (Mt. Godwin-Austen)
- C) Nanga Parbat
- D) Kangchenjunga
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Highest peak of this range is K2, also known as Mt. Godwin-Austen (8611 m), which is also world's second highest peak."

**Trap:** Mt. Everest is the world's highest (not Karakoram); Nanga Parbat is the westernmost point of the Greater Himalayas (not Karakoram's highest); Kangchenjunga is the highest in India (Sikkim).

**See also:** [[../../geography/09 - Himalayas#Trans-Himalayas]]
</details>

The world's longest mountain range is the:

A trekker from Srinagar wishes to reach Leh-Ladakh overland through a Himalayan pass on the Greater Himalayas, while another route connects Jammu to Srinagar via the Pir Panjal. The two passes are respectively:

- C) Khardung La and Pir Panjal Pass

The chapter states: "Zoji La: It connects Srinagar in Kashmir to Leh-Ladakh" (on the Greater Himalayas) and "Pir Panjal Pass and Banihal Pass: They connect Jammu to Srinagar."

**Trap:** Several passes (Banihal, Pir Panjal, Rohtang, Bara-Lacha La, Khardung La, Zoji La) all appear in the same section; the Srinagar–Leh and Jammu–Srinagar routes must be matched carefully.

@qgeo.himalayas.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=9
From north to south, the parallel Himalayan ranges run in which order?
- A) Shiwaliks → Lesser Himalayas → Greater Himalayas → Trans-Himalayas
- B) Trans-Himalayas → Greater Himalayas → Lesser Himalayas → Shiwaliks
- C) Greater Himalayas → Lesser Himalayas → Shiwaliks → Trans-Himalayas
- D) Lesser Himalayas → Greater Himalayas → Trans-Himalayas → Shiwaliks
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

From the chapter's ranges and average altitudes: Trans-Himalayas (e.g., Karakoram avg 3000 m, with K2 at 8611 m, but Trans-Himalayas sits north), Greater Himalayas average 6000 m, Lesser Himalayas average 4000 m, Shiwaliks average 1000 m. From north to south the order is Trans-Himalayas → Greater Himalayas → Lesser Himalayas → Shiwaliks.

**Trap:** Candidates frequently put Shiwaliks first because they are "outer"; the Trans-Himalayas lie north of the Greater Himalayas (Kailash, Karakoram, Ladakh, Zanskar), so they head the list.

**See also:** [[../../geography/09 - Himalayas#Different Mountain Ranges in Himalayas]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayas.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=9
Which among the following is correctly paired — a Himalayan peak with its home state?
- A) Nanda Devi — Sikkim
- B) Kangchenjunga — Uttarakhand
- C) Phavongpui (Blue Mountain) — Mizoram
- D) Saramati — Tripura
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states "Phavongpui (also known as Blue Mountain): Highest peak of Mizoram." Nanda Devi is Uttarakhand's highest (7816 m), Kangchenjunga is the highest in India (Sikkim, 8598 m), and Saramati is the highest peak of Nagaland — so A, B, and D are each mis-paired.

**Trap:** Several peak-state combinations get swapped; the chapter's "Some Important Mountain/Peaks" section is the source of truth.

**See also:** [[../../geography/09 - Himalayas#Some Important Mountain/Peaks]]
</details>

@qgeo.himalayas.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=9
Assertion (A): Kashmir Valley is described as being between the Greater Himalayas and the Lesser Himalayas, and Karewa formations there grow the local saffron variety "Zafran".
Reason (R): The Kashmir Valley is drained by the Indus and its tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab), and Karewas are composed of glacial and lacustrine deposits.
- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A is true: the chapter places "Kashmir Valley is between Great Himalayas and Lesser Himalayas" and notes Karewas "are used to grow Zafran, a local variety of saffron." R is also true: the chapter states "The Indus River and its tributaries, such as the Jhelum and Chenab, drain in this region" and Karewas are "composed of glacial and lacustrine deposits." However, R explains the drainage and Karewa composition — it does not explain why saffron is grown there or why the valley lies between those ranges.

**Trap:** Assertion-reason format tempts candidates to mark "A and R true, R explains A"; both statements are factual but R does not explain the valley's geography or its saffron cultivation.

**See also:** [[../../geography/09 - Himalayas#Kashmir Valley]]
</details>
