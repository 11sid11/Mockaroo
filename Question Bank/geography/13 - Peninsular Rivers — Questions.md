@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q1 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=13
Peninsular rivers are typically described as:

- A) Perennial rivers fed by glacial melt, like the Himalayan rivers
- B) Ephemeral/seasonal rivers that depend on the monsoon and have shorter courses
- C) Rivers that originate in the Western Ghats and flow north into the Ganga
- D) Rivers that drain exclusively into the Bay of Bengal
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "The Peninsular Rivers originate in the Deccan Plateau, flowing into the Bay of Bengal or the Arabian Sea. They are seasonal / ephemeral, relying on monsoon rains, with shorter courses and gentler slopes than Himalayan Rivers."

**Trap:** A describes the Himalayan river system. D is wrong because many Peninsular rivers (Narmada, Tapi, Mahi, Sabarmati, Luni) drain into the Arabian Sea, not the Bay of Bengal.

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Peninsular Rivers]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q2 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=13
Which of the following rivers is correctly classified as a WEST-flowing Peninsular river (draining into the Arabian Sea)?

- A) Godavari
- B) Krishna
- C) Narmada
- D) Mahanadi
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter lists west-flowing rivers: "Narmada, Tapi, Mahi, Sabarmati, and Luni rivers." Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna are east-flowing (Bay of Bengal).

**Trap:** Candidates confuse Narmada/Tapi (west-flowing) with Mahanadi/Godavari/Krishna (east-flowing). All four are major Peninsular rivers but flow in opposite directions because of the Deccan tilt.

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Peninsular Rivers]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q4 difficulty=recall subject=geography chapter=13
The Godavari originates from which location?

- A) Trimbakeshwar Plateau (Western Ghats) in Nasik, Maharashtra
- B) Amarkantak Plateau in Madhya Pradesh
- C) Multai in Betul district, Madhya Pradesh
- D) Sihawa hills in Chhattisgarh
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "The Godavari river originates from Trimbakeshwar Plateau (Western Ghats) in Nasik, Maharashtra." Amarkantak is the origin of the Narmada; Multai is the origin of the Tapi; Sihawa is the origin of the Mahanadi.

**Trap:** Three of the four options (Amarkantak, Multai, Sihawa) are correct origins of *other* major rivers. Candidates who confuse Narmada/Tapi/Mahanadi origins pick one of those.

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Godavari]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q5 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=13
Match each east-flowing Peninsular river with the city on its banks:

- City 1: Cuttack
- City 2: Nashik
- City 3: Vijayawada
- City 4: Tiruchirappalli (Srirangam)

Which option correctly maps the cities to the rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri)?

- A) 1-Mahanadi, 2-Godavari, 3-Krishna, 4-Kaveri
- B) 1-Mahanadi, 2-Krishna, 3-Godavari, 4-Kaveri
- C) 1-Godavari, 2-Mahanadi, 3-Kaveri, 4-Krishna
- D) 1-Kaveri, 2-Godavari, 3-Mahanadi, 4-Krishna
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Cuttack is on the Mahanadi (Odisha delta region); Nashik is on the Godavari at its origin in the Western Ghats; Vijayawada is on the Krishna in Andhra Pradesh; Srirangam / Tiruchirappalli is on the Kaveri in Tamil Nadu. (Kolkata is on the Hugli; Hyderabad is on the Musi — both distractor cities from the cities table.)

**Trap:** Candidates confuse Vijayawada (Krishna) with Hyderabad (Musi, a Krishna tributary) and Nashik (Godavari origin) with cities on the Krishna. Nashik/Nasik is the origin of the Godavari, NOT the Krishna.

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#East Flowing Rivers]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q6 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=13
Which of the following is true of Narmada and Tapi?

- A) Both flow through rift valleys between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges and drain into the Bay of Bengal
- B) Both flow through rift valleys between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges and drain into the Arabian Sea (Gulf of Khambhat)
- C) Both originate from Amarkantak; both form deltas at their mouths
- D) Both are tributaries of the Ganga
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "Narmada… flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura mountain ranges… drains itself into the Arabian Sea (flows into Gulf of Khambhat)." And "Tapi / Tapti… flows through a rift valley alongside Narmada (2nd longest westward flowing river)." Narmada originates at Amarkantak, but Tapi at Multai (Satpura Range).

**Trap:** A is wrong because Narmada/Tapi drain west (Arabian Sea), not east. C is wrong because only Narmada originates at Amarkantak; both are west-flowing rivers and form estuaries (not deltas).

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Narmada]] · [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Tapi / Tapti]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q7 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=13
The Subarnarekha river gets its name from which feature?

- A) The presence of silver mines along its banks
- B) The sand of its riverbed, which contains particles of gold
- C) A tributary named Subarna joining it near Ranchi
- D) The reflection of sunrise on its surface
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "The river sand contains gold particle, which is why it is named Subarnarekha (meaning 'Streak of Gold')."

**Trap:** A is the most tempting wrong answer because "silver" sounds like Subarnarekha (which is a transliteration of "streak of gold," not "silver"). Candidates confuse the literal translation of "subarna" with the metal silver.

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Subarnarekha]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q8 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=13
Which of the following rivers cuts/across the Tropic of Cancer TWICE?

- A) Narmada
- B) Tapi
- C) Mahi
- D) Luni
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter states: "Mahi river originates from the Vindhya mountains… It crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice." Mahi is famous for this double-crossing.

**Trap:** Narmada and Tapi flow through rift valleys and run parallel to the Tropic of Cancer; they do not cross it twice. Luni is endorheic (ends in Rann of Kutch) and does not cross the Tropic.

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Mahi]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q9 difficulty=apply subject=geography chapter=13
The Luni river is unique among Indian rivers because:

- A) It is the longest west-flowing river and forms a delta at its mouth
- B) It is an endorheic river (does not reach the sea) and is saline
- C) It flows from east to west and forms an estuary at the Gulf of Khambhat
- D) It is a tributary of the Indus
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter states: "The Luni River is an endorheic river, meaning it drains into an inland basin and does not reach the sea. Instead, it ends in the Rann of Kutch. It is the only major river in India that contains saline water due to its high rate of evaporation."

**Trap:** A confuses Luni with Narmada (longest west-flowing river, but Narmada forms an estuary, not Luni). C is partly correct about west-flow but wrong about the Gulf of Khambhat (that is Narmada/Tapi). D is wrong — Luni is not a tributary of the Indus.

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Luni]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=13
Consider the following statements about Peninsular river tributaries:
(1) Bhitarkanika National Park in Odisha is located in the delta of the Brahmani, Baitarani, and Dhamra rivers.
(2) APJ Abdul Kalam Island (formerly Wheeler Island) is located near the mouth of the Dhamra river, which is the confluence of Baitarani and Brahmani.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both statements are true
- B) Only statement 1 is true; statement 2 is false (APJ Abdul Kalam Island is in the Bay of Bengal off the Andhra coast)
- C) Only statement 2 is true; statement 1 is false
- D) Both statements are false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "APJ Abdul Kalam Islands (formerly known as Wheeler Islands) is located near the mouth of Dhamra river (the confluence of Baitarani and Brahmani)." and "Bhitarkanika National Park in Odisha is located in the delta of the Brahmani, Baitarani, and Dhamra rivers." Both statements are true and consistent.

**Trap:** Candidates who do not read carefully pick B because they confuse Odisha with Andhra Pradesh. Wheeler / Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam Island is off Odisha (Chandipur), not Andhra.

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Some Important Facts]]
</details>

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=13
Which of the following pairings correctly identifies the highest waterfall in India with its river and state?

- A) Kunchikal (highest in India) — Varahi river — Karnataka (Shimoga)
- B) Jog Falls (highest in India) — Sharavati river — Karnataka
- C) Athirappilly (highest in India) — Periyar river — Kerala
- D) Dudh Sagar (highest in India) — Mandovi river — Goa
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "Kunchikal (highest waterfall: 455 m) — Varahi — Karnataka (Shimoga)." Jog Falls is the *second* highest, Athirappilly and Dudh Sagar are famous but not the highest.

**Trap:** Jog Falls is the most famous and is sometimes misremembered as the highest. The chapter clearly says Kunchikal is the highest and Jog is second highest.

@qgeo.peninsular_rivers.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=geography chapter=13
Assertion (A): The Narmada is the longest west-flowing Peninsular river and drains into the Gulf of Khambhat (Arabian Sea).
Reason (R): Narmada flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges, which is why it flows westward instead of eastward like most Peninsular rivers.
Which of the following is correct?

- A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
- C) A is true but R is false
- D) A is false but R is false
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter states: "The length of Narmada river is 1312 km. It is the longest west flowing river that drains itself into the Arabian Sea (flows into Gulf of Khambhat)… flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura mountain ranges." R correctly explains the *cause* of Narmada west-flow direction: the rift valley between Vindhya and Satpura forced it westward, opposite to the Deccan tilt. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation.

**Trap:** B is a partial-credit trap — A and R are both factually correct, but the question is whether R explains A. R does explain the westward flow, which is the unusual direction for a Peninsular river.

**See also:** [[../../geography/13 - Peninsular Rivers#Narmada]]
</details>
