A student dips a strip of blue litmus paper into a clear, sour-tasting liquid. The strip immediately turns red. The chapter's named property of the liquid is:

- A) Acidic
- B) Basic
- C) Neutral

@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

An acid turns blue litmus paper **red**; a base turns red litmus paper blue. The sour taste and the litmus change together identify the liquid as an acid.

**Trap:** B (basic) is what red litmus turns blue in â€” easy to invert when distracted. The "sour taste" cue is the chapter's signature for an acid, but a base would never make a blue strip turn red.

@qchem.acid.q6 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=6
Which is the safe method for diluting concentrated acid?

- A) Add water rapidly to acid
- B) Add acid slowly to water with constant stirring
- C) Mix equal volumes without stirring
- D) Heat the acid before adding water
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Acid should be added slowly to water with constant stirring. Adding water to acid can produce intense exothermic heating, splashing, and burns.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/06 - Acid, Base and Salt#Dilution of Acids]]
</details>
