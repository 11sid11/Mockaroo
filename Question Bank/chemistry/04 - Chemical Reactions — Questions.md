@qchem.chemical_reactions.q1 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=4
How does the chapter distinguish exothermic and endothermic reactions?

- A) Exothermic reactions absorb heat; endothermic reactions release heat
- B) Exothermic reactions release heat; endothermic reactions absorb heat
- C) Both types only release light, not heat
- D) Both types absorb heat from their surroundings
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

An **exothermic** reaction releases energy from the system as heat, whereas an **endothermic** reaction absorbs heat from its surroundings. The chapter reinforces this distinction with “exo” meaning “out” and “endo” meaning “in.”

**Trap:** A reverses the two definitions. C and D erase the release-versus-absorption distinction given in the source.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Exothermic Reactions and Endothermic Reactions]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q2 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=4
A combination reaction is one in which:

- A) Two or more reactants form a single product
- B) One compound breaks into two or more simpler products
- C) Two compounds exchange ions to form two new compounds
- D) A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

In a **combination reaction**, a **single product** forms from **two or more reactants**. The chapter notes that most combination reactions are exothermic.

**Trap:** B defines decomposition, C defines double displacement, and D defines displacement.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Types of Chemical Reaction]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q3 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=4
Which statement defines a combustion reaction as presented in the chapter?

- A) A compound breaks down only in sunlight
- B) Two compounds exchange their ions without releasing energy
- C) A substance loses oxygen and gains hydrogen
- D) A substance reacts with oxygen gas and releases light and heat
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

A **combustion reaction** occurs when a substance reacts with **oxygen gas**, releasing energy as **light and heat**. The chapter states that combustion always takes place in the presence of oxygen.

**Trap:** A is limited to photolysis, B resembles double displacement, and C gives the source's definition of reduction.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Types of Chemical Reaction]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q4 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=4
Which pairing of processes is correct?

- A) Oxidation: removal of oxygen; Reduction: addition of oxygen
- B) Oxidation: addition of hydrogen; Reduction: removal of hydrogen
- C) Oxidation: addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen; Reduction: removal of oxygen or addition of hydrogen
- D) Oxidation: exchange of ions; Reduction: formation of a precipitate
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter defines **oxidation** as addition of oxygen and removal of hydrogen, and **reduction** as removal of oxygen and addition of hydrogen.

**Trap:** A and B reverse the definitions. D describes ideas associated with double displacement rather than oxidation and reduction.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#6. Oxidation Reaction]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q5 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=4
Rust is technically identified in the chapter as:

- A) Anhydrous iron(II) oxide, FeO
- B) Hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃ · xH₂O
- C) Iron sulphate, FeSO₄
- D) A mixture of CuCO₃ and Cu(OH)₂
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Rust is the reddish-brown, flaky product of iron corrosion. The chapter identifies it technically as **hydrated iron(III) oxide**, with the formula **Fe₂O₃ · xH₂O**.

**Trap:** D is the green corrosion mixture formed on copper, not rust. A and C are not the formula assigned to rust in the source.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Corrosion]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q6 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=4
A magnesium ribbon is cleaned before it is burned in air. Why is this done?

- A) To remove an MgO layer that prevents further reaction with atmospheric oxygen
- B) To coat it with MgO so that it burns more slowly
- C) To remove magnesium metal and expose an inert core
- D) To supply the ribbon with carbon dioxide for combustion
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Magnesium reacts with atmospheric oxygen to form an **MgO layer**, which prevents further reaction with oxygen. Cleaning the ribbon removes this layer before burning.

**Trap:** B turns the obstructing coating into the goal. C and D introduce processes not stated in the chapter.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Types of Chemical Reaction]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q7 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=4
During electrolysis of water, 30 mL of oxygen is collected. What amount of hydrogen should be collected according to the chapter?

- A) 15 mL
- B) 30 mL
- C) 60 mL
- D) 90 mL
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The electrolysis equation is `2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂`, and the chapter states that the amount of hydrogen collected is **double** the amount of oxygen. Therefore, 2 × 30 mL = **60 mL**.

**Trap:** A reverses the ratio, while B ignores it. D incorrectly triples the oxygen amount.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Types of Chemical Reaction]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q8 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=4
An iron nail is dipped into blue CuSO₄ solution. Which observation matches the reaction described in the chapter?

- A) The nail turns green and the solution becomes brown
- B) The nail becomes brownish and the solution changes from blue to green
- C) A yellow precipitate forms and the solution remains blue
- D) Brown NO₂ fumes evolve and the nail becomes white
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Iron displaces copper in `Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu`. The chapter states that the iron nail becomes **brownish**, while the **blue** CuSO₄ solution turns **green**.

**Trap:** C borrows the yellow precipitate associated with PbI₂. D borrows the brown fumes from heated lead nitrate.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Reactivity Series]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q9 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=4
Which reaction should produce a yellow precipitate according to the chapter?

- A) Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ + NaCl
- B) Pb(NO₃)₂ + KI → PbI₂ + KNO₃
- C) Ba(OH)₂ + NH₄Cl → BaCl₂ + NH₄OH
- D) Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The double displacement of lead nitrate and potassium iodide forms **PbI₂**, which the chapter marks as a **yellow precipitate**. By contrast, BaSO₄ is listed as a white precipitate.

**Trap:** A is also a precipitation reaction, but its precipitate is white. C is identified as endothermic, and D is a displacement reaction.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#5. Double Displacement Reaction]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q10 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=4
In `PbO + C → 2Pb + CO₂`, how are PbO and C identified in the chapter?

- A) PbO is the reducing agent; C is the oxidising agent
- B) Both PbO and C are oxidising agents
- C) PbO is the oxidising agent; C is the reducing agent
- D) Both PbO and C are reducing agents
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**PbO** is the oxidising agent because it gives oxygen to carbon. **C** is the reducing agent because it removes oxygen from PbO.

**Trap:** A swaps the agents. The source assigns complementary roles, so B and D cannot be correct.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#7. Reduction Reaction]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q11 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=4
Why is nitrogen gas used in chips packets according to the chapter?

- A) To speed up oxidation of fats and oils
- B) To prevent oxidation that can make the chips stale and soggy
- C) To turn the oils into a yellow precipitate
- D) To promote corrosion of the packet
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Rancidity is an **oxidation** process in which fats and oils break down, producing unpleasant smell, taste, or texture. Nitrogen is used in chips packets to **prevent oxidation**, which may otherwise make the chips stale and soggy.

**Trap:** A states the opposite purpose. C and D confuse rancidity with unrelated reactions.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Rancidity]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=chemistry chapter=4
Consider these statements about decomposition reactions:

1. Heating lead nitrate gives brown fumes of NO₂.
2. In electrolysis of water, the hydrogen collected is double the oxygen collected.
3. AgCl and AgBr are kept in dark bottles and used in black-and-white photography.

Which option is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2
- B) Only statements 2 and 3
- C) Only statements 1 and 3
- D) Statements 1, 2, and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements come directly from the decomposition examples: heated lead nitrate releases **brown NO₂ fumes**; water electrolysis produces hydrogen in double the amount of oxygen; and light-sensitive **AgCl and AgBr** are kept in dark bottles and used in black-and-white photography.

**Trap:** The three statements refer to different decomposition modes—thermal decomposition, electrolysis, and photolysis—so overlooking one mode makes the partial combinations tempting.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Types of Chemical Reaction]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q13 difficulty=tricky subject=chemistry chapter=4
A metal M displaces zinc from ZnSO₄, while zinc displaces copper from CuSO₄. Based on the chapter's reactivity principle and series, which order is correct?

- A) Cu > Zn > M
- B) Zn > M > Cu
- C) M > Zn > Cu
- D) M > Cu > Zn
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive one. Since M displaces Zn, **M > Zn**; since Zn displaces Cu, **Zn > Cu**. Combining the two relationships gives **M > Zn > Cu**.

**Trap:** B reverses the first displacement relationship, while D reverses the order of zinc and copper given by the series.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Reactivity Series]]
</details>

@qchem.chemical_reactions.q14 difficulty=tricky subject=chemistry chapter=4
Consider the following changes in the redox reaction shown in the chapter:

- Chromium changes from +6 in Cr₂O₇²⁻ to +3 in Cr³⁺.
- Sulphur changes from +4 in SO₃²⁻ to +6 in SO₄²⁻.

Which interpretation is correct?

- A) Chromium is oxidised and sulphur is reduced
- B) Both chromium and sulphur are oxidised
- C) Chromium is reduced and sulphur is oxidised
- D) Both chromium and sulphur are reduced
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Chromium's oxidation state falls from **+6 to +3**, so chromium is **reduced**. Sulphur's oxidation state rises from **+4 to +6**, so sulphur is **oxidised**.

**Trap:** A swaps the two changes. B and D ignore that one oxidation state rises while the other falls in this redox process.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/04 - Chemical Reactions#Redox Reaction]]
</details>
