@qchem.carbon.q1 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=7
Which property of carbon is described as the "unique ability to form long chains of C-C bond"?

- A) Tetravalency
- B) Catenation
- C) Hybridisation
- D) Isomerism
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter's "Basic Features of Carbon" table defines **Catenation** as the "unique ability to form long chains of C-C bond" and **Tetravalency** as the "capability to form 4 bonds with other atom of carbon or other monovalent elements."

**Trap:** A (tetravalency) is the other feature in the table, but it describes bonding number, not chain formation. C and D are related properties but are not what the table labels as catenation.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Catenation]]
</details>

@qchem.carbon.q2 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=7
How many other carbon atoms is each carbon atom bonded to in the structure of diamond?

- A) 1
- B) 2
- C) 3
- D) 4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The chapter states that "each carbon atom is bonded to 4 other carbon atoms" in diamond, with a 3D-tetrahedral structure and sp³ hybridisation. Diamond is the hardest naturally occurring substance.

**Trap:** C (each carbon bonded to 3 others) describes **graphite**, not diamond. B is trap for graphite's layered bonding.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Diamond]]
</details>

According to the IUPAC prefix table in the chapter, a straight-chain hydrocarbon with 8 carbon atoms is named using the prefix:

- A) hept-
- B) hex-
- C) oct-
- D) non-
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The "Naming of Hydrocarbons" table maps 8 carbons to the prefix **oct-**. The same table gives 6 → hex-, 7 → hept-, 9 → non-.

**Trap:** A is for 7 carbons, B is for 6, D is for 9. Each prefix is one carbon off from the next.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Naming of Hydrocarbons]]
</details>

@qchem.carbon.q4 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=7
The functional group **-COOH** belongs to which class of compound, and what is its IUPAC suffix?

- A) Alcohols; -ol
- B) Aldehydes; -al
- C) Carboxylic acid; -oic
- D) Ketones; -one
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The "Functional Groups" table lists -COOH (common formula R-COOH, e.g. acetic acid CH₃COOH) as **Carboxylic acid** with the IUPAC suffix **-oic** (ethanoic acid).

**Trap:** A is the alcohol -OH group. B is the aldehyde -CHO group. D is the ketone -CO- group.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Functional Groups]]
</details>

@qchem.carbon.q5 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=7
Members of a homologous series differ by what repeating unit, and by how many mass units per step?

- A) -CH₃ unit, mass 15
- B) -CH₂ unit, mass 14
- C) -CH₂ unit, mass 12
- D) -C₂H₄ unit, mass 28
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The "Homologous Series" section states that a homologous series "differs by -CH₂ unit and mass by 14 units." The same section notes that chemical properties remain the same across the series while melting and boiling points increase with molecular mass.

**Trap:** A swaps CH₃ for CH₂ and uses methyl's mass. C uses the right unit but the carbon-only mass. D doubles both — but the standard increment per step is one CH₂.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Homologous Series]]
</details>

Balance the combustion reaction of methane in oxygen. What are the products according to the chapter?

- A) CO + H₂
- B) CO₂ + 2 H₂O + Heat + Light
- C) C + 2 H₂O + Heat
- D) CH₃OH + O₂ + Light
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter gives: **CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + Heat + Light** under "Chemical Properties of Carbon Compounds → Combustion."

**Trap:** A is incomplete combustion (CO, not CO₂). C is charring without oxygen. D is no reaction at all — methanol is not a combustion product here.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Chemical Properties of Carbon Compounds]]
</details>

@qchem.carbon.q7 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=7
Methane is treated with chlorine in sunlight. What is the immediate product formed in the first substitution step?

- A) CH₂Cl₂ (dichloromethane)
- B) CHCl₃ (chloroform)
- C) CH₃Cl (chloromethane) + HCl
- D) CCl₄ + HCl
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The "Substitution Reaction" chain listed in the chapter is:

- CH₄ + Cl₂ (sunlight) → **CH₃Cl + HCl** (Methane → Chloromethane)
- CH₃Cl + Cl₂ → CH₂Cl₂ + HCl
- CH₂Cl₂ + Cl₂ → CHCl₃ + HCl (Chloroform)
- CHCl₃ + Cl₂ → CCl₄ + HCl

Only the **first** step yields chloromethane (CH₃Cl).

**Trap:** A, B, and D are downstream products — they form only after the corresponding number of additional chlorinations.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Chemical Properties of Carbon Compounds]]
</details>

For the structure CH₃-C≡C-CH₂-CH₃ (positions 1, 2, 3, 4, 5), what is the IUPAC name given in the chapter?

- A) Pentyne
- B) Pent-2-yne
- C) 2-Methylpentyne
- D) Pent-1-yne
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter's IUPAC examples list **CH₃-C≡C-CH₂-CH₃ (positions 1,2,3,4,5) → Pent-2-yne** with the note "Nearest position for double/triple/functional group."

**Trap:** A "Pentyne" is shown only for CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-C≡CH (terminal alkyne, position 1). C is a distractor based on the alkyl-substitution example. D misreads the nearest-position rule.

</details>

@qchem.carbon.q9 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=7
In saponification, an ester is treated with a base such as NaOH. Which pair of products does the chapter give for CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOH?

- A) CH₃COONa + C₂H₅OH
- B) CH₃OH + C₂H₅COONa
- C) CH₃COOH + C₂H₅ONa
- D) CH₃ONa + C₂H₅COOH
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The "Saponification Reaction" section gives the equation:

**CH₃COOC₂H₅ (NaOH) → CH₃COONa + C₂H₅OH**

The chapter explicitly states that "an ester is treated with a base (like sodium hydroxide) that breaks it down into an **alcohol and a sodium salt of a carboxylic acid**."

**Trap:** B swaps the alkyl fragments by pretending the ester is "reversed." C keeps the ester bonds intact (no reaction). D likewise leaves the molecule intact with Na⁺ swapped in.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Saponification Reaction]]
</details>

@qchem.carbon.q10 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=7
Which of the following correctly matches reagents to the oxidation of ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) to ethanoic acid in the chapter?

- A) H⁺/KMnO₄ or H⁺/K₂Cr₂O₇
- B) Conc. H₂SO₄ at 443 K (dehydration)
- C) Sodium metal (Na)
- D) Nickel or palladium catalyst with H₂
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter shows the oxidation pathway:

**CH₃-CH₂-OH → (H⁺/KMnO₄ OR H⁺/K₂Cr₂O₇) → CH₃-CO-OH (Ethanoic acid)**

Both oxidising agents are listed together.

**Trap:** B is the **dehydration** step (Conc. H₂SO₄ at 443 K → ethene), not oxidation. C is the sodium-reaction step (→ sodium ethoxide + H₂). D is the addition reaction used on unsaturated hydrocarbons.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Oxidation of Alcohol to Ethanoic Acid]]
</details>

@qchem.carbon.q11 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=7
According to the chapter, why does soap fail to clean effectively in **hard water**?

- A) Hard water neutralises the soap completely into salt and water
- B) Soap reacts to form insoluble calcium and magnesium salts called **scum**
- C) Hard water dissolves the soap's micelles and releases trapped dirt back
- D) The Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions convert soap into detergent molecules
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The "Soap does not work in" section states: "**Hard water:** Because in hard water it reacts to form Calcium & Magnesium salts, which is an insoluble substance called **Scum**."

**Trap:** A uses neutralisation language that fits acid–base reactions, not hard-water chemistry. C inverts the mechanism (soap works because micelles trap dirt; releasing dirt would actually make cleaning effective). D is a fabricated transformation — Ca/Mg don't convert soap into detergent.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Soap does not work in]]
</details>

@qchem.carbon.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=chemistry chapter=7
Consider the following statements about diamond and graphite:
1. Diamond is the hardest naturally occurring substance.
2. Graphite is a good conductor of heat and electricity.
3. Graphite is the most stable form of carbon.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2
- B) Only statements 1 and 3
- C) Only statements 2 and 3
- D) Statements 1, 2, and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are stated explicitly:
- Diamond entry: "Diamond is the **hardest naturally occurring substance**."
- Graphite entry: "Graphite is a **good conductor of heat and electricity**."
- Graphite entry: "It is the **most stable form of carbon**."

**Trap:** A common misconception is that diamonds and graphite both conduct electricity — but the chapter says diamond is a "bad conductor." The "stability" claim is also an easy trap (students associate stability with hardness, which the source ties to diamond, not graphite).

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Diamond]] · [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Graphite]]
</details>

@qchem.carbon.q13 difficulty=tricky subject=chemistry chapter=7
Using the chapter's trick formula `2^(n−4) + 1`, how many isomers does the chapter list for **octane** (n = 8)?

- A) 9
- B) 16
- C) 18
- D) 35
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter's worked examples list:

- Hexane (n=6): 2^(6−4) + 1 = 2² + 1 = **5**
- Heptane (n=7): 2^(7−4) + 1 = 2³ + 1 = **9**
- Octane (n=8): 2^(8−4) + 1 = 2⁴ + 1 = 16 + 1 + 1 = **18**
- Nonane (n=9): 2^(9−4) + 1 = 2⁵ + 1 = 32 + 1 + 2 = **35**

So octane is given as **18** (the chapter's own number, which equals 2⁴ + 1 + 1 in its format).

**Trap:** B (16) uses 2⁴ without the + 1 + 1 the chapter adds. A (9) is heptane's value. D (35) is nonane's value.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Isomers In]]
</details>

@qchem.carbon.q14 difficulty=tricky subject=chemistry chapter=7
Which combination correctly distinguishes **denatured spirit** from **ethanol** and explains how one recognises it, according to the chapter?

- A) Denatured spirit is pure ethanol with extra water; recognised by its clear colour
- B) Denatured spirit is ethanol made unfit for drinking by adding methanol and a **blue dye** for easy recognition
- C) Denatured spirit is methanol with ethanol; recognised by a foul smell only
- D) Denatured spirit is ethanol with sodium ethoxide; recognised by effervescence
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The "Important Carbon Compounds → Ethanol" section defines denatured spirit as: "ethanol which is made unfit for drinking by adding poisonous substances like **methanol** that imparts a bad taste, foul smell, or colour. A **blue dye** is also added to it, so that it is easily recognized."

**Trap:** A misstates denatured spirit as ethanol + water — water alone wouldn't make it unfit for drinking. C reverses the proportions and drops the blue dye. D fabricates a "sodium ethoxide" ingredient (sodium ethoxide is the product of ethanol + Na, not of denaturation).

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/07 - Carbon and Its Compounds#Ethanol]]
</details>
