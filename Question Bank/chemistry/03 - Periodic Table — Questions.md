@qchem.periodic_table.q1 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=3
Döbereiner noticed that three chemically similar elements (a "triad") — say, with atomic masses 7, 23, and 39 — had a striking property: the middle element's atomic mass was approximately the average of the other two. Which of the following statements is the chapter's formal description of this **Law of Triads** (1817)?

- A) In a three-element group arranged by increasing atomic mass, the middle element's atomic mass is approximately the average of the first and third
- B) Every first and eighth element have similar properties when elements are arranged by increasing atomic mass
- C) The properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers
- D) The atomic volume of an element must equal its atomic weight
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Döbereiner arranged elements with similar properties into groups of three called **triads**. In increasing atomic-mass order, the middle element's mass was approximately the average of the first and third elements' masses. With the numbers above, the average of 7 and 39 is 23, exactly the middle element's mass.

**Trap:** B describes **Newland's Law of Octaves**. C substitutes the modern table's atomic-number basis. D distorts Lothar Meyer's atomic-volume plot. The 7/23/39 example is the chapter's own lithium–sodium–potassium triad.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Döbereiner's Law of Triads (1817)]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q2 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=3
Which set of facts about Newland's arrangement is given in the chapter?

- A) Eight elements per row; 63 elements; Uranium last
- B) Seven elements per row; 63 elements; Thorium last
- C) Seven elements per row; 56 elements; Technetium last
- D) Seven elements per row; 56 elements; Thorium last
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

Newland arranged elements in horizontal rows containing **seven elements** and compared the recurring pattern to musical octaves. His arrangement contained **56 elements**, with **Thorium** as the last element.

**Trap:** The figure 63 belongs to the number of known elements arranged by Mendeleev, while Technetium is identified elsewhere in the chapter as the first man-made element.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Newland's Law of Octaves (1865)]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q3 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=3
Mendeleev's Periodic Table of 1869 arranged the 63 known elements primarily in increasing order of their:

- A) Atomic numbers
- B) Relative atomic masses
- C) Atomic volumes
- D) Numbers of valence electrons
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the **63 known elements** in increasing order of their **relative atomic masses**. His table had eight groups and seven periods, and his periodic law treated element properties as periodic functions of atomic mass.

**Trap:** Atomic number is the basis associated with Moseley's modern table. Lothar Meyer plotted atomic volume against atomic weight, but that was not Mendeleev's stated ordering rule.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Mendeleev's Periodic Table (1869)]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q4 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=3
Which description of the modern periodic table matches the chapter?

- A) Given by Mendeleev in 1869; based on atomic number; 8 groups and 7 periods
- B) Given by Lothar Meyer in 1862; based on atomic volume; 18 groups and 8 periods
- C) Given by Henry Moseley in 1913; based on atomic number; 18 groups and 7 periods
- D) Given by Newland in 1865; based on atomic mass; 7 groups and 18 periods
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The modern periodic table was given by **Henry Moseley in 1913**. Elements are characterized by **atomic number**, and the table has **18 groups** and **7 periods**.

**Trap:** A mixes Mendeleev's year and group count with Moseley's ordering basis. The other choices combine facts from earlier classification attempts in ways not stated by the source.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#MODERN PERIODIC TABLE]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q5 difficulty=recall subject=chemistry chapter=3
In a chemistry class, the teacher writes two facts on the board:

- Fact 1: "The element with the highest electronegativity is in group 17, period 2."
- Fact 2: "The element with the highest electron affinity is in group 17, period 3."

Which pair of element names matches these facts?

- A) Chlorine and fluorine
- B) Fluorine and chlorine
- C) Oxygen and chlorine
- D) Fluorine and bromine
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter identifies **fluorine** (group 17, period 2) as the element with the **highest electronegativity** and **chlorine** (group 17, period 3) as the element with the highest electron affinity. The two superlatives are awarded to two different halogens.

**Trap:** A swaps the two — and is the most common SSC trap because both elements are in the same group. C puts electronegativity on oxygen, which is wrong (oxygen is not the highest). D substitutes bromine for chlorine in the electron-affinity slot.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Electronegativity]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q6 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=3
An element has six valence electrons. Using the rule stated in the chapter, what is its valency?

- A) 2
- B) 4
- C) 6
- D) 8
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

For an element with five, six, seven, or eight valence electrons, the chapter calculates valency as **8 − number of valence electrons**. Thus, 8 − 6 = **2**.

**Trap:** C simply repeats the valence-electron count instead of applying the 8 − n rule. B would result from four valence electrons.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Valency Based on Valence Electrons]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q7 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=3
An electron is to be removed from each of two neutral gaseous atoms, one lying above the other in the same group. According to the general trend in the chapter, from which atom should removal require less ionisation energy?

- A) The upper atom, because ionisation energy decreases upward
- B) The lower atom, because its outermost electrons are farther from the nucleus
- C) The upper atom, because its atomic radius is necessarily larger
- D) Both atoms, because ionisation energy is constant within a group
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Ionisation energy **decreases down a group**. In the lower atom, the outermost electrons are farther from the nucleus and therefore more easily removed, so less energy is required.

**Trap:** A reverses the trend. D ignores the source's explicit down-group change, while C gives the wrong atom for the stated distance rationale.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Ionisation Energy]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q8 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=3
An element's valence electron enters a d subshell. In which part of the modern periodic table should it be placed?

- A) Groups 1–2, among s-block elements
- B) Groups 3–12, among transition metals
- C) Groups 13–18, among p-block elements
- D) The f-block, among inner transition metals
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The chapter assigns elements whose valence electron enters a **d subshell** to the **d-block**, covering **Groups 3–12** and called the transition metals.

**Trap:** A, C, and D correctly name other block regions, but none matches a d-subshell valence electron.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#MODERN PERIODIC TABLE]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q9 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=3
Which set consists entirely of species shown in the chapter as isoelectronic with 10 electrons each?

- A) O²⁻, F⁻, Mg²⁺
- B) O, F, Mg
- C) O²⁻, F, Mg²⁺
- D) O, F⁻, Mg
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The chapter's isoelectronic example gives **O²⁻, F⁻, and Mg²⁺** as having **10 electrons each**. Isoelectronic species share the same electron count and electronic structure even though their nuclear charges differ.

**Trap:** Removing the indicated ionic charge from any member changes the electron count represented in the source's example.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Isoelectronic Species]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q10 difficulty=apply subject=chemistry chapter=3
A molecule contains one carbon atom and four chlorine atoms. How should it be classified by atomicity, and which formula is given for it?

- A) Diatomic; CCl
- B) Triatomic; CCl₂
- C) Polyatomic; CCl₄
- D) Monoatomic; Cl₄
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter gives **carbon tetrachloride as CCl₄**. That molecule contains five atoms in total, and molecules made of four or more atoms are classified as **polyatomic**.

**Trap:** “Tetra-” refers to four chlorine atoms, not four total atoms. The carbon atom raises the total atomicity to five.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Writing Chemical Formula of a Compound]] · [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Polyatomic Molecules]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=chemistry chapter=3
Consider the following statements about early periodic classification:

1. Newland's Law of Octaves was not valid beyond calcium.
2. Newland included noble gases in his table.
3. Lothar Meyer plotted atomic volume against atomic weight to show periodic trends.

Which option is correct?

- A) Only statement 1
- B) Only statements 1 and 3
- C) Only statements 2 and 3
- D) Statements 1, 2, and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Statements **1 and 3** are supported. Newland's law failed beyond calcium, and Meyer plotted atomic volume against atomic weight. Statement 2 is false because noble gases had not yet been discovered and were therefore absent from Newland's table.

**Trap:** Statement 2 can sound like a completeness claim, but the chapter explicitly lists the absence of noble gases as a limitation.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Limitations of Newland's Model]] · [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#Lothar Meyer]]
</details>

@qchem.periodic_table.q13 difficulty=tricky subject=chemistry chapter=3
An element lies farther to the right than another element in the same period. Applying the chapter's general trends, which combination is expected for the right-hand element?

- A) Lower ionisation energy, lower electron affinity, and lower electronegativity
- B) Higher ionisation energy, generally greater electron affinity, and generally higher electronegativity
- C) Higher ionisation energy, lower electron affinity, and lower electronegativity
- D) Lower ionisation energy, generally greater electron affinity, and higher electronegativity
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Across a period from left to right, the chapter states that **ionisation energy increases**, electron affinity **generally increases (becomes more negative)**, and electronegativity **generally increases**.

**Trap:** Each other option reverses at least one of these three same-direction trends. The qualifiers “generally” preserve the wording used for electron affinity and electronegativity.

**See also:** [[../../chemistry/03 - Periodic Table#TRENDS IN PERIODIC TABLE]]
</details>
