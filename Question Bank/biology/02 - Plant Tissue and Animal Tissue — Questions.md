# Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue — Questions

@qbio.plant_tissue.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=2
Plant tissues are broadly classified into which two main categories?

- A) Simple and complex permanent tissues
- B) Meristematic and permanent tissues
- C) Parenchyma and sclerenchyma tissues
- D) Xylem and phloem tissues
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Plant tissues split first into **Meristematic** (actively dividing cells found at growing regions) and **Permanent** (non-dividing mature cells that perform specific functions). Permanent tissues are then subdivided into simple, complex, and secretory types.

**Trap:** A is tempting because permanent tissue itself has "simple" and "complex" sub-classes — but the *broad* classification is meristematic vs permanent.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Plant Tissue]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=2
The meristem located at the base of leaves or internodes (such as in grasses) and responsible for the increase in length of leaves and internodes is called:

- A) Apical meristem
- B) Lateral meristem
- C) Intercalary meristem
- D) Protoderm
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Intercalary meristem** sits at the base of leaves or internodes and pushes those internodes/leaf blades longer (the reason grasses keep growing after being cut). Apical meristem increases length of root/shoot; lateral meristem (cambium) increases girth.

**Trap:** A and B are common because both are well-known meristems — but apical is at the *tip* and lateral is *beneath the bark*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Types of Meristematic Tissue]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q3 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=2
A botanist wants a tissue that can **store food** and — in some cases — carry out **photosynthesis** when loosely packed with chlorophyll. Which simple permanent tissue fits?

- A) Collenchyma
- B) Sclerenchyma
- C) Parenchyma
- D) Xylem parenchyma only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Parenchyma** is the simple permanent tissue responsible for food storage, and when its cells contain chlorophyll it is called **chlorenchyma** (photosynthetic). When loosely packed it is called **aerenchyma** (provides buoyancy). Collenchyma gives mechanical support to growing parts; sclerenchyma provides hard, dead, lignified support.

**Trap:** D sounds more specific but "xylem parenchyma" is a sub-type of parenchyma that stores food — the *general* tissue that does both jobs is parenchyma.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Parenchyma Tissue]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=2
Match the plant tissue to its primary role:

| Tissue | Primary role |
|---|---|
| (i) Xylem | (a) Transports food (sugars) |
| (ii) Phloem | (b) Transports water and minerals |
| (iii) Collenchyma | (c) Hard, dead, lignified support |
| (iv) Sclerenchyma | (d) Flexible support to growing parts |

- A) i-b, ii-a, iii-d, iv-c
- B) i-a, ii-b, iii-c, iv-d
- C) i-b, ii-a, iii-c, iv-d
- D) i-a, ii-b, iii-d, iv-c
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — Xylem carries water + minerals; phloem carries sugars; collenchyma is flexible support for growing parts (young stems, leaf petioles); sclerenchyma is the hard, dead, lignified support.

**Trap:** C swaps collenchyma and sclerenchyma roles — a frequent slip because both give mechanical support.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Complex Permanent Tissue]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q5 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=2
The cambium — a thin formative layer between xylem and phloem — is responsible for:

- A) Primary growth (increase in length)
- B) Transport of sugars
- C) Secondary growth (increase in girth/thickness)
- D) Protection of the root tip
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Cambium** is a lateral meristem; it produces new xylem and phloem cells on either side, which increases the girth of roots and stems — this is **secondary growth**. Apical meristems do primary (length-wise) growth.

**Trap:** A is the typical role of apical meristem, not cambium.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Lateral Meristem]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=2
A pathologist examines a smear and finds cells that are **striated, attached to bones, and working under voluntary control**. The cells belong to which tissue?

- A) Smooth muscle
- B) Cardiac muscle
- C) Skeletal muscle
- D) Connective tissue proper
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Skeletal muscle** cells are long, cylindrical, multinucleate, striated, and attached to bones; they work under voluntary control. Cardiac muscle is also striated but is *branched*, *uninucleate*, and *involuntary*, found in the heart wall — not bones. Smooth muscle is non-striated and involuntary.

**Trap:** B (cardiac) is the strongest distractor because it is also striated, but the "attached to bones + voluntary" clue pins it to skeletal.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Muscular Tissue]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=2
Consider the following statements:
1. Mature human RBCs lack a nucleus.
2. The normal RBC count is roughly 5,000–8,000 per cubic millimetre of blood.
3. Platelets are essential for blood clotting.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- Statement 1 ✓ — Mature mammalian RBCs **lack a nucleus** (anuclear) and most organelles.
- Statement 2 ✗ — 5,000–8,000 per mm³ is the normal range for **WBCs**, not RBCs. The RBC range is ~4.5–5.5 million per mm³.
- Statement 3 ✓ — Platelets (thrombocytes) are essential for coagulation; their deficiency causes thrombocytopenia (seen in dengue).

**Trap:** Statement 2 swaps WBC and RBC counts — a very common SSC trap because both are 4-digit ranges in "per mm³".

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#RBC (Red Blood Cells)/Erythrocytes]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=2
A student matches:
- (i) Albumin → maintains osmotic balance
- (ii) Fibrinogen → defence
- (iii) Globulin → anticoagulant

How many of these pairings are correct?

- A) Only (i) is correct
- B) (i) and (ii) are correct
- C) All three are correct
- D) (ii) and (iii) are correct
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- (i) Albumin → maintains osmotic balance ✓
- (ii) Fibrinogen → *blood clotting* (NOT defence). Defence is **globulin's** job.
- (iii) Globulin → *defence* (NOT anticoagulant). Anticoagulants in plasma are fibrinogen/heparin.

So only (i) is correctly paired. (ii) and (iii) are swapped.

**Trap:** Students confuse "fibrinogen" with "immune" because of "fibrin" sounding like "fighting". Defence is globulin's job.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Plasma]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q9 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=2
A nerve signal travels from one neuron to the next across a synapse. Which part of the *first* neuron releases the **neurotransmitters** that cross the synapse?

- A) Dendrites
- B) Cell body (soma)
- C) Axon terminals (synaptic knobs)
- D) Myelin sheath
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Axon terminals** (also called synaptic knobs) contain neurotransmitter vesicles. When the impulse reaches them, they release the neurotransmitter into the synaptic cleft, which then binds receptors on the next neuron's dendrites/cell body.

- Dendrites *receive* signals (carry impulses *toward* the cell body).
- Myelin sheath *insulates* and speeds up conduction.
- Cell body contains the nucleus and integrates signals.

**Trap:** A is the lure for "where signals come in" — but the question asks what *releases* neurotransmitters at the *output* end, which is the axon terminal.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Axon Terminals (Synaptic knobs)]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=2
Arrange the following animal-tissue categories in their textbook sequence from **simplest covering tissue to most specialised coordinating tissue**:

1. Epithelial tissue (covers surfaces)
2. Connective tissue (binds/supports other tissues)
3. Muscular tissue (contracts for movement)
4. Nervous tissue (transmits impulses)

Which order is correct?

- A) 1 → 2 → 3 → 4
- B) 4 → 3 → 2 → 1
- C) 2 → 4 → 3 → 1
- D) 1 → 4 → 2 → 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

A useful organising trick: **E**pithelial → **C**onnective → **M**uscular → **N**ervous (E C M N — "Every Cell Makes Nerves"). In SSC textbook ordering the four major animal tissues are listed in this E-C-M-N sequence, ending with nervous tissue as the most specialised.

**Trap:** C and D shuffle nervous tissue to a non-terminal position — the SSC convention puts nervous tissue last as the most specialised of the four.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Animal Tissues]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q11 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=2
The longest cell in the human body is:

- A) Red blood cell
- B) Neuron (nerve cell)
- C) Skeletal muscle fibre
- D) Columnar epithelial cell
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A **neuron** is the longest cell in the human body — its axon can extend over a metre (e.g. from spinal cord to toes). It is specialised to transmit nerve impulses.

**Trap:** Skeletal muscle fibres can also be very long, but neurons (especially motor neurons) win as the longest cell overall.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Anatomy of a Neuron]]
</details>

@qbio.plant_tissue.q12 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=2
A doctor notes that a patient's blood smear shows **low platelet count** and the patient has a recent history of fever with severe joint pain following a mosquito bite. The condition is most likely:

- A) Thrombocytosis
- B) Leukaemia
- C) Thrombocytopenia
- D) Haemophilia
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Thrombocytopenia** = abnormally low platelet count. It is classically associated with **dengue**, which is a mosquito-borne viral illness with fever and severe joint pain. Thrombocytosis is the opposite (high platelet count). Haemophilia is a clotting-factor deficiency (genetic) and leukaemia is a WBC/blood-cancer condition — neither directly fits the mosquito + low-platelet presentation.

**Trap:** A is the *opposite* condition (high platelets) and exists only to lure students who remember "platelet abnormality" but not the direction.

**See also:** [[../../biology/02 - Plant Tissue and Animal Tissue#Platelets/Thrombocytes]]
</details>
