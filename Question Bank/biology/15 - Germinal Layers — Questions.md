# Germinal Layers — Questions

> Source: [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers|Germinal Layers]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - biology]]

A 10-question MCQ set on diploblastic vs triploblastic animals and the three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm). Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

Scoring: SSC CGL rules — **+2 correct, -0.50 wrong, 0 unattempted**.

@qbio.germinal.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=15
Animals with **two germ layers** (ectoderm and endoderm) are called:

- A) Diploblastic
- B) Triploblastic
- C) Acoelomate
- D) Coelomate
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

**Diploblastic** animals develop from only **two germ layers**: outer **ectoderm** and inner **endoderm** (with a non-cellular jelly-like mesoglea between). Examples: Porifera, Cnidaria (Coelenterata), Ctenophora.

**Trap:** B (triploblastic) is the opposite — three layers (ecto + meso + endo).

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#Diploblastic]]
</details>

---

@qbio.germinal.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=15
**Triploblastic** animals have which three germ layers?

- A) Ectoderm, endoderm, mesoglea
- B) Ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm
- C) Epidermis, dermis, hypodermis
- D) Blastoderm, endoderm, ectoderm
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Triploblastic** animals develop from three germ layers: **Ectoderm** (outer), **Mesoderm** (middle), **Endoderm** (inner). Examples: Platyhelminthes onwards (Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Chordata, etc.).

**Trap:** A swaps "mesoglea" (a jelly-like substance in diploblastic cnidarians) for "mesoderm". C is a body-tissue layering, not a germ layer.

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#Triploblastic]]
</details>

@qbio.germinal.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=15
The **ectoderm** primarily gives rise to:

- A) Muscles, bones, circulatory system
- B) Lining of digestive tract, lungs, liver
- C) Skin, nervous system, sense organs
- D) Gonads only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Ectoderm** (outer layer) → **skin** (epidermis), **nervous system** (brain, spinal cord, nerves), and **sense organs** (eyes, ears, nose). **Mesoderm** → muscles, bones, circulatory system, gonads. **Endoderm** → lining of digestive tract, lungs, liver, pancreas.

**Trap:** A is *mesoderm*. B is *endoderm*. D is too narrow (gonads are mesodermal but not the *only* derivative).

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#What Each Germ Layer Gives Rise To]]
</details>

@qbio.germinal.q4 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=15
The **mesoderm** gives rise to all of the following EXCEPT:

- A) Muscles
- B) Bones
- C) Lining of digestive tract
- D) Circulatory system
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **mesoderm** forms muscles, bones, circulatory system, and gonads. The **lining of the digestive tract** is derived from **endoderm**, not mesoderm.

**Trap:** A, B, D are all mesoderm derivatives — easy to confuse with the *endoderm*'s role.

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#What Each Germ Layer Gives Rise To]]
</details>

@qbio.germinal.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=15
A surgeon examining the early embryo notes that the **nervous system and skin epidermis** are forming from one specific germ layer, while **muscles and bones** are forming from a different layer. Which pair of germ layers is responsible?

- A) Ectoderm (nervous system/skin) and Mesoderm (muscles/bones)
- B) Ectoderm (muscles/bones) and Endoderm (nervous system/skin)
- C) Mesoderm (nervous system/skin) and Endoderm (muscles/bones)
- D) Endoderm (nervous system/skin) and Ectoderm (muscles/bones)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — **Ectoderm** gives rise to the **nervous system** (brain, spinal cord, nerves) and the **epidermis** of skin. **Mesoderm** gives rise to **muscles, bones**, the circulatory system, and gonads. The third layer, **endoderm**, forms the gut lining and its outgrowths (liver, pancreas, lungs).

- B ✗ — reverses ectoderm and mesoderm roles.
- C ✗ — places the nervous system in mesoderm (incorrect) and muscles in endoderm (also incorrect).
- D ✗ — reverses two layers.

**Trap:** B, C, and D each assign the *nervous-system/skin* derivative to a layer other than ectoderm. The "nervous system = ectoderm" fact is well-known, but pairing it with the *muscles/bones* clue tests whether the student can also correctly identify mesoderm simultaneously.

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#What Each Germ Layer Gives Rise To]]
</details>

- B) i-a, ii-b, iii-c
- C) i-b, ii-a, iii-c

@qbio.germinal.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=15
Which of the following phyla are **triploblastic**?

- A) Porifera and Cnidaria
- B) Platyhelminthes, Annelida, Chordata
- C) Only Chordata
- D) Only Porifera
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Triploblastic** phyla include Platyhelminthes (and all phyla from Platyhelminthes onwards): Aschelminthes/Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Chordata.

**Trap:** A (Porifera + Cnidaria) are **diploblastic**, not triploblastic.

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#Triploblastic]]
</details>

@qbio.germinal.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=15
A student claims: "All animals are triploblastic because they all have three body layers." Is this correct?

- A) Yes — every animal has ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm
- B) No — Porifera and Cnidaria are diploblastic (only two layers)
- C) Yes — mesoglea counts as the third layer
- D) No — only vertebrates are triploblastic
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Diploblastic** animals (Porifera, Cnidaria, Ctenophora) have only two true germ layers (ectoderm + endoderm). The **mesoglea** in cnidarians is a *non-cellular* jelly-like substance — NOT a true third germ layer. So they are diploblastic.

**Trap:** C is wrong because mesoglea is not a true germ layer (no cells derived from it). D is wrong — many invertebrates are triploblastic, not just vertebrates.

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#Diploblastic]]
</details>

@qbio.germinal.q8 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=15
The human **liver** is derived from which germ layer?

- A) Ectoderm
- B) Mesoderm
- C) Endoderm
- D) All three layers
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **liver** (along with the lining of the digestive tract, lungs, pancreas) is derived from the **endoderm** — the innermost germ layer. The endoderm forms the gut and its associated organs.

**Trap:** B (mesoderm) is the trap because the liver is a major organ — students assume "important organ = mesoderm". But endoderm gives rise to the gut lining and its outgrowths.

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#What Each Germ Layer Gives Rise To]]
</details>

@qbio.germinal.q9 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=15
Which phylum is **diploblastic** AND radially symmetrical as an adult?

- A) Porifera
- B) Cnidaria (Coelenterata)
- C) Platyhelminthes
- D) Annelida
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Cnidaria** (jellyfish, *Hydra*, sea anemones, corals) are both **diploblastic** (only two germ layers) and **radially symmetrical**. (Porifera is also diploblastic but is *asymmetrical*; Platyhelminthes and Annelida are bilateral and triploblastic.)

**Trap:** A (Porifera) is diploblastic but *asymmetrical*, not radial.

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#Diploblastic]]
</details>

@qbio.germinal.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=15
Consider the following statements about germ layers:
1. The number of germ layers is a key classification axis for animals.
2. All chordates are triploblastic.
3. The middle germ layer is called mesoglea in diploblastic animals.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- Statement 1 ✓ — The chapter explicitly lists germ layers as a key classification axis (diploblastic vs triploblastic).
- Statement 2 ✓ — All chordates are triploblastic (three germ layers).
- Statement 3 ✗ — In diploblastic animals, the *middle* non-cellular layer is **mesoglea**, but the *germ* layer is not called that. The germ layers in diploblastic animals are *ectoderm* and *endoderm* — there is *no* true middle germ layer. Mesoglea is just jelly.

**Trap:** Statement 3 sounds plausible but conflates "mesoglea" (jelly layer in cnidarians) with "mesoderm" (the middle *germ layer* of triploblastic animals).

**See also:** [[../../biology/15 - Germinal Layers#What Each Germ Layer Gives Rise To]]
</details>
