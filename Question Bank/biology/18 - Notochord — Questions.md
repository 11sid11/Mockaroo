@qbio.notochord.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=18
The notochord of a chordate embryo is best described as a:

- A) Ventral, ectoderm-derived hollow tube
- B) Dorsal, mesoderm-derived rod-like structure
- C) Dorsal, endoderm-derived chain of bones
- D) Ventral, mesoderm-derived fluid cavity
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The notochord is a rod-like dorsal structure derived from the mesoderm. It develops as a rudimentary supporting backbone in early chordate embryos.

**Trap:** A mixes the notochord with the dorsal hollow nerve cord and assigns the wrong germ-layer origin.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#NOTOCHORD]]
</details>

@qbio.notochord.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=18
Which feature must occur at some stage in the life cycle of every member of phylum Chordata?

- A) A vertebral column
- B) Jointed appendages
- C) A notochord
- D) An external skeleton
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

A notochord at some stage of life is a defining feature of Chordata. A vertebral column is characteristic of vertebrates, not of every chordate.

**Trap:** A is tempting because vertebrates are the most familiar chordates, but protochordates do not form a vertebral column.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#Key Features]]
</details>

@qbio.notochord.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=18
In vertebrates, the embryonic notochord is largely replaced by the:

- A) Vertebral column
- B) Ventral nerve cord
- C) Exoskeleton
- D) Pharyngeal gill slits
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

In subphylum Vertebrata, the vertebral column replaces the notochord as the principal supporting structure.

**Trap:** D is another chordate feature, but it neither supports the body in this way nor replaces the notochord.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#Key Features]]
</details>

@qbio.notochord.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=18
An embryologist finds a dorsal supporting rod in an early embryo. The rod is not yet a chain of vertebrae. Which conclusion is most appropriate?

- A) It is a notochord and indicates chordate affinity
- B) It is an exoskeleton and indicates arthropod affinity
- C) It is a ventral nerve cord and indicates annelid affinity
- D) It is a coelom and indicates diploblastic organisation
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

A dorsal rod-like embryonic support is the notochord. Its presence at this stage identifies the organism as a chordate even if vertebrae have not formed.

**Trap:** B treats any support as a skeleton, but an arthropod exoskeleton is external rather than a dorsal embryonic rod.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#Why It Matters]]
</details>

@qbio.notochord.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=18
A specimen has pharyngeal gill slits, a dorsal hollow nerve cord, and a post-anal tail. Which observation would complete the four-feature chordate set?

- A) A ventral solid nerve cord
- B) A dorsal notochord
- C) Jointed legs
- D) A calcareous shell
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The four listed chordate features are the notochord, pharyngeal gill slits, dorsal hollow nerve cord, and post-anal tail. The missing feature is a notochord.

**Trap:** A reverses the form and position of the chordate nerve cord and does not supply the missing support structure.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#Why It Matters]]
</details>

@qbio.notochord.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=18
An adult animal lacks a vertebral column, but its larva possessed a poorly developed notochord. It is most consistent with:

- A) Protochordata
- B) Vertebrata
- C) Arthropoda
- D) Annelida
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

In Protochordata, the notochord may be present only in the larval stage or remain poorly developed, and no vertebral column forms.

**Trap:** B is tempting because both groups are chordates, but vertebrates characteristically develop a vertebral column.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#Key Features]]
</details>

@qbio.notochord.q7 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=18
A mutation prevents normal development of the mesoderm in a chordate embryo. Which structure from this chapter would be most directly affected?

- A) Notochord
- B) Pharyngeal cavity
- C) Epidermal surface
- D) Gut lining
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The notochord is mesoderm-derived. Impaired mesoderm development would therefore directly disturb its formation.

**Trap:** The other structures may occur in a chordate, but the chapter specifically identifies the notochord as arising from mesoderm.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#NOTOCHORD]]
</details>

@qbio.notochord.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=18
Consider the following statements:
1. Every chordate has a vertebral column at some stage of life.
2. Every chordate has a notochord at some stage of life.

Which option is correct?

- A) Both 1 and 2 are correct
- B) Only 1 is correct
- C) Only 2 is correct
- D) Neither 1 nor 2 is correct
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Statement 2 is true because a notochord is diagnostic of Chordata. Statement 1 is false because protochordates do not form a vertebral column.

**Trap:** A incorrectly treats “chordate” and “vertebrate” as equivalent categories.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#Key Features]]
</details>

@qbio.notochord.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=18
Which row correctly compares the notochord in Vertebrata and Protochordata?

- A) Vertebrata: absent in embryo; Protochordata: replaced by vertebral column
- B) Vertebrata: replaced by vertebral column; Protochordata: no vertebral column forms
- C) Vertebrata: ventral; Protochordata: dorsal
- D) Vertebrata: ectodermal; Protochordata: endodermal
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Vertebrates possess the notochord embryonically and later replace it with a vertebral column. Protochordates do not form vertebrae.

**Trap:** A reverses the comparison while exploiting the fact that the adult vertebrate notochord is no longer the principal backbone.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#Key Features]]
</details>

@qbio.notochord.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=18
Assertion (A): Absence of a notochord in an adult does not by itself exclude the animal from Chordata.
Reason (R): In chordates, the notochord need only be present at some life stage and may later be replaced.

- A) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
- C) A is true, but R is false
- D) A is false, but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Chordate identity depends on a notochord at some stage, not necessarily throughout adulthood. In vertebrates it is replaced by the vertebral column, so R explains A.

**Trap:** D assumes a defining character must remain visible in the adult, overlooking embryonic criteria.

**See also:** [[../../biology/18 - Notochord#Why It Matters]]
</details>
