# Plant and Animal Kingdom — Questions

@qbio.kingdom.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=3
Who is known as the "Father of Taxonomy" and proposed the **Two Kingdom Classification** in 1758?

- A) Ernst Haeckel
- B) R. H. Whittaker
- C) Carl Woese
- D) Carolus Linnaeus
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

**Carolus Linnaeus** (1707–1778) published *Systema Naturae* in 1735 and is widely called the *Father of Taxonomy*. In 1758 he proposed the **Two Kingdom Classification** — Plantae + Animalia. Haeckel added Protista (1866), Whittaker proposed the Five Kingdom scheme (1969), and Woese proposed the Three Domain system (1990).

**Trap:** Students often confuse Linnaeus with Whittaker because both deal with kingdoms — but Linnaeus = Two Kingdom (1758), Whittaker = Five Kingdom (1969).

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Two Kingdom Classification]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=3
The correct descending order of taxonomic hierarchy (highest to lowest rank) is:

- A) Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum → Kingdom
- B) Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species
- C) Kingdom → Class → Phylum → Order → Genus → Family → Species
- D) Phylum → Kingdom → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Standard hierarchy: **Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**. Mnemonic (top-down): *King Philip Came Over For Good Spaghetti* — or for the chapter's example (top-down): *KABADDI PLAY OUR CLASS OF GIRLS SOME*.

**Trap:** C and D scramble the order (especially confusing Class and Phylum, or Kingdom and Phylum). Remember: "King Philip Came Over..." for *K*, *P*, *C*, *O*, *F*, *G*, *S*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Order of Classification]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=3
According to the **Five Kingdom Classification** (Whittaker, 1969), which kingdom includes *prokaryotic* organisms such as bacteria and blue-green algae?

- A) Protista
- B) Monera
- C) Fungi
- D) Plantae
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Monera** = prokaryotic, unicellular (true bacteria, blue-green algae / cyanobacteria). Protista = eukaryotic unicellular; Fungi = eukaryotic heterotrophic spore-producers; Plantae = autotrophic multicellular; Animalia = heterotrophic multicellular.

**Trap:** A (Protista) is the distractor — Protista is also unicellular but it is *eukaryotic* (has a true nucleus), unlike Monera.

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Five Kingdom Classification]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=3
A laboratory specimen is found to be a single-celled eukaryote with a true nucleus, living in pond water. It cannot photosynthesise and feeds by engulfing food particles. The organism most likely belongs to:

- A) Monera
- B) Protista
- C) Fungi
- D) Plantae
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Protista** = eukaryotic unicellular organisms (*Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena*). The clues rule out other kingdoms: not Monera (which is prokaryotic, no true nucleus), not Fungi (which are multicellular, with a few unicellular exceptions like yeast but they don't engulf food), and not Plantae (autotrophic).

**Trap:** A (Monera) is the trap for students who only remember "single-celled" but forget the *prokaryotic vs eukaryotic* distinction.

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Three Kingdom Classification]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=3
Which of the following correctly **distinguishes dicots from monocots**?

- A) Dicots have a single cotyledon; monocots have two
- B) Dicots have parallel leaf venation; monocots have reticulate
- C) Dicots have fibrous roots and floral parts in multiples of three; monocots have tap roots and floral parts in multiples of four or five
- D) Dicots have vascular bundles arranged in a ring; monocots have scattered vascular bundles in the stem
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D** — In a **dicot** stem, vascular bundles are arranged in a **ring** around the central pith; in a **monocot** stem, vascular bundles are **scattered** throughout the ground tissue.

- A ✗ — reverses the actual relationship (dicots = 2 cotyledons; monocots = 1).
- B ✗ — reverses leaf venation (dicots = reticulate; monocots = parallel).
- C ✗ — reverses roots and floral-part counts (dicots = tap root + parts in 4s/5s; monocots = fibrous + parts in 3s).

**Trap:** A, B, and C each reverse a single characteristic that students commonly get backwards. SSC questions frequently pair all four "dicot vs monocot" axes in a single question to trap rote learners who only remember *one* feature.

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#KINGDOM PLANTAE]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q6 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=3
Bryophytes (mosses) are commonly called the "**amphibians of the plant kingdom**" because:

- A) They live only in water and have gills
- B) They need water for sexual reproduction but live on land
- C) They have four limbs like amphibians
- D) They are the first plants to develop vascular tissue
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Bryophytes (mosses, liverworts, hornworts) live on land but need water for their sperm to swim to the egg — so they straddle land and water reproduction, earning the nickname *amphibians of the plant kingdom*. They lack true vascular tissue and true roots.

**Trap:** A is wrong because bryophytes do not have gills. D is wrong — pteridophytes (ferns) were the *first vascular plants*, not bryophytes.

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Bryophyta]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q7 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=3
A marine animal has a soft, spiny-skinned body with **radial symmetry** and a **water vascular system** with tube feet. It most likely belongs to which phylum?

- A) Mollusca
- B) Arthropoda
- C) Echinodermata
- D) Cnidaria (Coelenterata)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Echinodermata** = spiny-skinned, radial symmetry (as adults), tube feet via a water vascular system. Examples: starfish, sea urchin, sea cucumber (*Holothuria*). Cnidaria are also radial but lack the water vascular system and tube feet. Molluscs are soft-bodied, unsegmented, and mostly bilateral; arthropods have jointed appendages and an exoskeleton.

**Trap:** D (Cnidaria) is the trap — both Cnidaria and Echinodermata are radially symmetrical, but only echinoderms have a *water vascular system*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Phylum Echinodermata]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q8 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=3
A teacher asks the class to arrange the following phyla in increasing order of **body organisation level** (cellular → tissue → organ → organ-system):

- (i) Annelida
- (ii) Porifera
- (iii) Platyhelminthes
- (iv) Coelenterata (Cnidaria)

The correct sequence is:

- A) (ii) → (iv) → (iii) → (i)
- B) (iv) → (ii) → (iii) → (i)
- C) (ii) → (iii) → (iv) → (i)
- D) (i) → (iii) → (iv) → (ii)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Body organisation levels:
- **Porifera** → cellular level (no true tissues)
- **Coelenterata (Cnidaria)** → tissue level
- **Platyhelminthes** → organ level
- **Annelida** (and Arthropoda, Mollusca, Chordata) → organ-system level

So: Porifera → Coelenterata → Platyhelminthes → Annelida = (ii) → (iv) → (iii) → (i).

**Trap:** D reverses the entire sequence. C swaps Platyhelminthes and Coelenterata — easy slip because both are still "early" phyla.

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Body Organisation]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=3
Consider the following statements about Porifera (sponges):
1. They have a cellular level of body organisation.
2. They show radial symmetry.
3. They are mostly asymmetrical as adults.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- Statement 1 ✓ — Porifera show **cellular** level of organisation (no true tissues).
- Statement 2 ✗ — Porifera are *not* radially symmetrical; many are *asymmetrical* (no plane divides the body into equal halves).
- Statement 3 ✓ — Porifera are mostly **asymmetrical** as adults.

**Trap:** Statement 2 is the wrong-direction lure — students remember "sponges are simple" and assume "simple = radial" (which is actually the rule for Cnidaria/Echinodermata, not Porifera).

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Phylum Porifera]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q10 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=3
Which class of vertebrates has a **two-chambered heart**, is **cold-blooded**, and breathes through gills? (e.g. dogfish, shark, tuna)

- A) Amphibia
- B) Pisces
- C) Reptilia
- D) Aves
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Pisces** (fishes) have a two-chambered heart (one atrium + one ventricle), are cold-blooded (ectothermic), and breathe through gills. Amphibians have a *three*-chambered heart, reptiles mostly three (crocodile four), and Aves have a four-chambered heart.

**Trap:** A (Amphibia) is the trap — amphibians do have gills *in larval stage* but as adults use lungs/skin; their heart is three-chambered.

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Classes of Vertebrates]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=3
A student asserts: "Birds (Aves) and mammals both have a four-chambered heart, are warm-blooded, and are viviparous." Which part of this statement is **incorrect**?

- A) Birds and mammals both have a four-chambered heart
- B) Birds and mammals are both warm-blooded
- C) Birds are viviparous
- D) All parts are correct
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Birds (Aves) are **oviparous** — they *lay eggs* (e.g. hens). Only mammals (mostly) are viviparous (give birth to live young). Exceptions among mammals are platypus and echidna, which lay eggs. So the *viviparous* claim applies to mammals but not birds.

- A ✓ — both have 4-chambered hearts.
- B ✓ — both are warm-blooded (endothermic).
- C ✗ — birds lay eggs.

**Trap:** A and B are true (both are endothermic with 4 chambers), so the obvious answers "all correct" (D) is tempting — but "viviparous" is the killer detail.

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Aves (Birds)]]
</details>

@qbio.kingdom.q12 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=3
A snake, a turtle, a lizard, a chameleon, and a crocodile are kept in a zoo. A student notes that one of them is **exceptional among reptiles** in having a four-chambered heart instead of the typical three-chambered one. Which animal is this exception?

- A) Snake
- B) Turtle
- C) Lizard
- D) Crocodile
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

Most reptiles have a three-chambered heart (2 atria + 1 ventricle with incomplete partition). **Crocodile** is the famous exception — it has a fully four-chambered heart. (Crocodiles are also closer to birds evolutionarily than to other "reptiles".)

**Trap:** All four animals listed are reptiles, so students may not realise which one breaks the rule. Remember: *crocodile* = four-chambered heart (a frequent SSC direct question).

**See also:** [[../../biology/03 - Plant and Animal Kingdom#Reptiles]]
</details>
