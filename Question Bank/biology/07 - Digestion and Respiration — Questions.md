@qbio.digestion.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=7
Digestion begins in the mouth with the enzyme **salivary amylase**, which breaks down:

- A) Proteins
- B) Fats
- C) Starch (complex carbohydrates)
- D) Nucleic acids
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Salivary amylase** (also called ptyalin) is secreted by salivary glands and starts breaking down **starch** (a complex carbohydrate) into simpler sugars in the mouth. Mechanical digestion (chewing) also begins here.

**Trap:** A (proteins) — proteins are first digested by *pepsin* in the stomach, not in the mouth.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Mouth (Oral Cavity/Buccal Cavity)]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=7
Which enzyme is secreted by the stomach and digests proteins in an acidic medium?

- A) Trypsin
- B) Pepsin
- C) Lipase
- D) Amylase
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Pepsin** is secreted by the stomach lining and digests proteins into smaller peptides. It works *only* in the acidic medium created by HCl (pH ~1.6) in gastric juice. Trypsin (also protein-digesting) is secreted by the pancreas into the small intestine and works in *basic* medium.

**Trap:** A (trypsin) is the distractor — also a protein-digesting enzyme but in the wrong location (pancreas / small intestine).

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Stomach]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q3 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=7
**Bile** is stored in the **gall bladder** and released into the small intestine. Which of the following correctly describes bile?

- A) Bile is an enzyme that digests proteins
- B) Bile is alkaline and emulsifies fats (breaks large fat globules into smaller ones)
- C) Bile is acidic and stored in the pancreas
- D) Bile is secreted by the small intestine
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Bile** is produced by the **liver** and stored in the **gall bladder**. It is *alkaline* (basic) in nature and neutralises the acidic food (chyme) entering the small intestine from the stomach. Critically, bile is **not an enzyme** — it emulsifies fats, breaking large fat globules into smaller ones, increasing surface area for lipase to act.

**Trap:** A is the trap (bile is NOT an enzyme). C reverses the location and pH. D reverses the source.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Bile (bile juice)]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q4 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=7
The **largest gland** in the human body is the:

- A) Pancreas
- B) Liver
- C) Thyroid
- D) Salivary gland
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **liver** is the largest gland in the human body. It is dark red-brown, located on the right side of the abdomen beneath the diaphragm. It stores glycogen, produces bile, metabolises carbs/proteins/fats, and detoxifies blood.

**Trap:** A (pancreas) is the **second** largest gland. D (salivary glands) are smaller and located in/around the mouth.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Liver]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=7
A doctor measures the patient's breathing and notes that during **inhalation**, the diaphragm:

- A) Relaxes and moves upward
- B) Contracts and moves downward; rib cage moves up
- C) Contracts and moves upward; rib cage moves down
- D) Does not change position
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

During **inhalation**: the diaphragm **contracts and moves downward**, and the rib cage moves **up and out**. This expands the chest cavity, lowering internal pressure, so air rushes into the lungs. The opposite happens during exhalation.

**Trap:** A describes exhalation, not inhalation. C reverses both movements. D is wrong — the diaphragm is the primary breathing muscle.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Respiratory System - Inhalation & Exhalation]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=7
A patient has inhaled a peanut that lodges in the airway. The peanut is most likely to get stuck at which of the following points, where the airway is **narrowest** and most prone to obstruction?

- A) Pharynx
- B) Larynx (at the vocal cords)
- C) Trachea
- D) Bronchi
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The **larynx** (voice box) is the narrowest part of the adult airway. The vocal cords form a slit-like opening, and any foreign body that passes the larynx will usually go on into the trachea or bronchi. Choking first-aid (the Heimlich manoeuvre) works by forcing air *up* from the lungs to dislodge the obstruction at this level.

- A ✗ — the pharynx is wider and shared with the digestive tract.
- C ✗ — the trachea has C-shaped cartilage rings keeping it open, so it does not collapse easily around an obstruction.
- D ✗ — the bronchi are wider and the peanut usually enters *one* of them (often the right, because it is more vertical).

**Trap:** C and D are tempting because the student thinks of "where foreign bodies go" — but the *narrowest* choke point is the larynx.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Respiratory System - Trachea]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=7
Consider the following statements about cellular respiration:
1. Aerobic respiration occurs in the cytoplasm and mitochondria and produces ~38 ATP per glucose.
2. Anaerobic respiration in yeast produces ethanol and CO₂.
3. Accumulation of lactic acid in muscle cells during heavy exercise causes cramps.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are true:
- Statement 1 ✓ — Aerobic respiration (with O₂) yields ~38 ATP/glucose and occurs in cytoplasm (glycolysis) and mitochondria (Krebs cycle + ETC).
- Statement 2 ✓ — Anaerobic respiration in **yeast** (fermentation) produces ethanol + CO₂ + 2 ATP.
- Statement 3 ✓ — In **muscle cells** under O₂ deficit (heavy exercise), anaerobic respiration produces **lactic acid**, which accumulates and causes cramps.

**Trap:** Easy to drop statement 3 thinking lactic acid only occurs in industry — but it also occurs in human muscle cells during strenuous exercise.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Cellular Respiration]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=7
In the overall reaction for aerobic respiration:

**C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + Energy (ATP)**

Which molecule is being **broken down** to release energy?

- A) Carbon dioxide
- B) Water
- C) Glucose
- D) ATP
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Glucose** (C₆H₁₂O₆) is the substrate that is oxidised (broken down) by aerobic respiration, with O₂ as the final electron acceptor, releasing CO₂, H₂O, and energy stored as **ATP**. ATP is the *product*, not the substrate.

**Trap:** D (ATP) is the trap — ATP is what *carries the energy*, not what is broken down.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Cellular Respiration]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q9 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=7
The **small intestine** is the longest part of the alimentary canal. Which of the following is the **main site** of digestion and absorption?

- A) Stomach
- B) Small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum)
- C) Large intestine
- D) Oesophagus
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **small intestine** (~6.5–7.5 m long) is the main site of digestion and absorption. It is divided into duodenum, jejunum, and ileum. Finger-like projections called **villi** on its inner wall greatly increase the surface area for absorption.

**Trap:** A (stomach) does some protein digestion but is not the *main* site. C (large intestine) mainly absorbs water and forms faeces.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Small Intestine]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=7
A student claims: "Yeast cells use anaerobic respiration, so they don't have mitochondria." Is this correct?

- A) Yes — yeast is anaerobic so it has no mitochondria
- B) No — yeast has mitochondria but prefers fermentation in low-oxygen conditions
- C) Yes — yeast cells are prokaryotes without organelles
- D) No — yeast performs only aerobic respiration
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Yeast** is a eukaryotic organism and **does have mitochondria**. It is a *facultative anaerobe* — in the presence of oxygen it performs aerobic respiration (mitochondria); in low-oxygen conditions it switches to fermentation (ethanol + CO₂). This is why yeast is used in baking (CO₂ makes dough rise) and brewing (ethanol).

**Trap:** A is wrong — being capable of anaerobic respiration doesn't mean lacking mitochondria. C is wrong — yeast is eukaryotic, not prokaryotic.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Anaerobic respiration]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q11 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=7
The **epiglottis** is a small flap of tissue that prevents food from entering the:

- A) Oesophagus
- B) Windpipe (trachea)
- C) Stomach
- D) Pharynx
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **epiglottis** is a flap of cartilage at the root of the tongue that closes over the opening of the **trachea (windpipe)** during swallowing, preventing food and liquid from entering the airway and lungs.

**Trap:** A (oesophagus) is the food pipe — food *should* go down it. The epiglottis protects the *wrong* pipe (the air one).

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Alveoli (Air Sacs)]]
</details>

@qbio.digestion.q12 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=7
A patient has yellowing of the skin and eyes (jaundice). The doctor suspects an issue with bile pigment processing. Which organ is most likely affected?

- A) Stomach
- B) Liver
- C) Pancreas
- D) Small intestine
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Jaundice** is caused by an excess of **bilirubin** (a yellow pigment) in the blood, which is normally processed and excreted by the **liver** via bile. Liver damage or obstruction of bile ducts leads to bilirubin buildup, causing yellowing of skin and eyes.

**Trap:** A and C are digestive organs but not primarily responsible for bilirubin processing. D receives bile but doesn't process bilirubin.

**See also:** [[../../biology/07 - Digestion and Respiration#Liver]]
</details>
