# Coelom — Questions

> Source: [[../../biology/16 - Coelom|Coilom]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - biology]]

A 10-question MCQ set on the three coelom types (acoelomate, pseudocoelomate, eucoelomate) and their phyla-level distribution. Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

Scoring: SSC CGL rules — **+2 correct, -0.50 wrong, 0 unattempted**.

@qbio.coelom.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=16
A **coelom** is best described as:

- A) A solid mass of cells in the body
- B) A fluid-filled body cavity between the body wall and gut wall, lined by mesoderm
- C) The outer covering of an animal
- D) A digestive cavity
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A **coelom** is a *true* body cavity — fluid-filled space between the body wall and the gut wall, *fully lined by mesoderm*. It allows organ differentiation, independent movement of gut, and acts as a hydrostatic skeleton in many animals.

**Trap:** A is wrong (coelom is a cavity, not a solid mass). C confuses with body wall. D confuses with the gut lumen.

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Three Types of Coelom Organisation]]
</details>

@qbio.coelom.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=16
Animals without a body cavity (the space between gut and body wall is filled with mesoderm) are called:

- A) Pseudocoelomate
- B) Acoelomate
- C) Eucoelomate
- D) Coelomate
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Acoelomate** animals have *no* coelom — the area between the gut and body wall is filled with mesoderm. Examples: Porifera, Cnidaria, Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes.

**Trap:** A (pseudocoelomate) is a *partial* cavity, not absent. C and D are synonyms for true coelom.

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Acoelomate]]
</details>

@qbio.coelom.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=16
Which phylum has a **pseudocoelom** (a body cavity *not fully lined* by mesoderm)?

- A) Platyhelminthes
- B) Nematoda / Aschelminthes
- C) Annelida
- D) Chordata
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Pseudocoelomate** = body cavity present but **only partially lined** by mesoderm. Example phylum: **Nematoda / Aschelminthes** (roundworms like *Ascaris*, *Wuchereria*).

**Trap:** A (Platyhelminthes) is *acoelomate* (no cavity). C (Annelida) and D (Chordata) are *eucoelomate* (true coelom).

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Pseudocoelomate]]
</details>

@qbio.coelom.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=16
A biologist dissects an organism and observes the body cavity between the gut and body wall. The cavity is **fluid-filled**, and **mesoderm completely lines** both the outer (body-wall side) and inner (gut-wall side) surfaces of the cavity. The organism's coelom is best classified as:

- A) Acoelomate
- B) Pseudocoelomate
- C) Eucoelomate (true coelom)
- D) Haemocoel
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — A **true coelom (eucoelom)** is *completely* lined by mesoderm on both the body-wall side (parietal peritoneum) and the gut-wall side (visceral peritoneum). This is what allows organs to hang from mesenteries and move independently of the body wall, and it is the hallmark of phyla like **Annelida, Mollusca, Echinodermata, and Chordata**.

- A ✗ — acoelomates have *no* body cavity at all (the space is filled with mesoderm).
- B ✗ — pseudocoelomates have a cavity, but mesoderm lines it only on the *outer* (body-wall) side; the gut wall is not covered by mesoderm.
- D ✗ — haemocoel is the open body cavity of an *open* circulatory system (e.g. arthropods), not a coelom type.

**Trap:** B is tempting because pseudocoelomate *also* has a cavity — but the key distinction is the **complete vs partial** mesoderm lining.

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Quick Reference]]
</details>

@qbio.coelom.q5 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=16
**Eucoelomate** (true coelom) animals have their body cavity:

- A) Filled with mesoderm
- B) Lined by mesoderm (true lining)
- C) Only partially lined by mesoderm
- D) Absent
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Eucoelomate** animals have a *true coelom* — a body cavity **fully lined by mesoderm**. Examples: Annelida, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Chordata.

**Trap:** A (filled with mesoderm) describes *acoelomate*. C (partially lined) describes *pseudocoelomate*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Eucoelomate (True Coelomate)]]
</details>

@qbio.coelom.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=16
*Ascaris* (a roundworm) and *Wuchereria* (the filarial worm) belong to which coelom category?

- A) Acoelomate
- B) Pseudocoelomate
- C) Eucoelomate
- D) No coelom at all (solid body)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

*Ascaris* and *Wuchereria* are roundworms belonging to **Nematoda / Aschelminthes** — the **pseudocoelomate** phylum. They have a body cavity, but it is only *partially* lined with mesoderm.

**Trap:** A (acoelomate) is wrong — *Ascaris* does have a body cavity (visible as a pseudocoelom). C (eucoelomate) would require full mesodermal lining.

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Pseudocoelomate]]
</details>

@qbio.coelom.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=16
A student says: "Earthworms have a true coelom, but roundworms don't." Is this correct?

- A) Yes — earthworms (Annelida) are eucoelomate; roundworms (Nematoda) are pseudocoelomate
- B) No — both have true coeloms
- C) No — neither has a coelom
- D) Yes — but the reason is wrong; both are eucoelomate
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

**Earthworms** (Phylum Annelida) are **eucoelomate** — they have a *true* coelom fully lined by mesoderm (and use it as a hydrostatic skeleton for burrowing). **Roundworms** (Phylum Nematoda) are **pseudocoelomate** — they have a body cavity but it is only partially lined by mesoderm.

**Trap:** B is wrong because the two phyla have *different* coelom types. D is wrong because the coelom distinction is real and important for classification.

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Eucoelomate (True Coelomate)]]
</details>

@qbio.coelom.q8 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=16
Which of the following phyla are **acoelomate**?

- A) Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes
- B) Nematoda only
- C) Annelida and Chordata
- D) Only Chordata
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

**Acoelomate** phyla: **Porifera, Cnidaria (Coelenterata), Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes** — the first three because they lack organised body cavities anyway, and Platyhelminthes because its mesoderm fills the space between gut and body wall.

**Trap:** B (Nematoda) is pseudocoelomate, not acoelomate. C and D are eucoelomate.

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Acoelomate]]
</details>

@qbio.coelom.q9 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=16
Which phyla are **eucoelomate** (have a true coelom)?

- A) Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes
- B) Nematoda
- C) Annelida, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Chordata
- D) Only Chordata
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Eucoelomate** phyla (with true coelom) include **Annelida, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Chordata** — the higher invertebrate and vertebrate phyla.

**Trap:** A is the *acoelomate* list. B is the *pseudocoelomate* phylum. D is too narrow.

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Eucoelomate (True Coelomate)]]
</details>

@qbio.coelom.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=16
Consider the following statements:
1. A coelom is always fully lined by mesoderm.
2. Platyhelminthes is acoelomate.
3. The pseudocoelom of Nematoda is partially lined by mesoderm.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

- Statement 1 ✗ — A coelom is *only* fully lined by mesoderm in **eucoelomates**. In pseudocoelomates it is *partially* lined. So the word "always" is wrong.
- Statement 2 ✓ — Platyhelminthes are acoelomate (no body cavity).
- Statement 3 ✓ — Nematoda have a pseudocoelom that is partially lined by mesoderm (this is exactly what "pseudo" means).

**Trap:** Statement 1 is the trick — "always fully lined" is only true for the *true coelom*, not for all coeloms.

**See also:** [[../../biology/16 - Coelom#Three Types of Coelom Organisation]]
</details>
