@qbio.segmentation.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=19
In zoology, segmentation means the division of an animal's body into:

- A) Repeating, similar units
- B) Two mirror-image halves only
- C) Three germinal layers
- D) Separate digestive cavities
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Segmentation is the division of the body into repeating, similar units called segments.

**Trap:** B defines bilateral symmetry, which concerns body arrangement around a plane rather than serial repetition along the body.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#SEGMENTATION]]
</details>

@qbio.segmentation.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=19
Metameric segmentation into many similar body units is characteristic of:

- A) Arthropoda
- B) Annelida
- C) Chordata
- D) Mollusca
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Annelids such as earthworms show metameric segmentation: the body consists of many similar, externally evident repeats.

**Trap:** Arthropods are segmented too, but their segments are regionalised into specialised body regions through tagmosis.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#Phyla with Segmentation]]
</details>

@qbio.segmentation.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=19
The regional grouping of arthropod segments into head, thorax, and abdomen is called:

- A) Metamerism
- B) Cephalisation
- C) Tagmosis
- D) Ossification
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Arthropod segmentation is regionalised into distinct regions such as head, thorax, and abdomen; this organisation is called tagmosis.

**Trap:** A refers to the series of similar repeating segments emphasised in Annelida, not their grouping into specialised regions.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#Phyla with Segmentation]]
</details>

@qbio.segmentation.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=19
An animal's body consists of a long series of externally visible, broadly similar repeating units. This pattern is best identified as:

- A) Annelid-type metameric segmentation
- B) Arthropod tagmosis
- C) Chordate internal segmentation
- D) Radial symmetry
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

External repetition of many similar units is the metameric pattern typical of annelids such as earthworms.

**Trap:** B involves regional specialisation of segments, not a long sequence of broadly similar repeats.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#Note]]
</details>

@qbio.segmentation.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=19
A zoologist observes jointed appendages attached to specialised head, thorax, and abdomen regions. The segmentation is:

- A) External metamerism without regionalisation
- B) Tagmosis typical of Arthropoda
- C) Internal segmentation typical of Chordata
- D) Absent because the segments are dissimilar
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Arthropod segments are organised into specialised regions, commonly head, thorax, and abdomen, and bear jointed appendages. This is tagmosis.

**Trap:** D wrongly assumes segments must remain identical; regionalised, specialised segments still constitute segmentation.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#Note]]
</details>

@qbio.segmentation.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=19
An animal shows no chain of external rings, yet imaging reveals a serial arrangement of vertebrae and ribs. Which pattern does this indicate?

- A) Annelida—external metamerism
- B) Arthropoda—tagmosis
- C) Chordata—internal segmentation
- D) Mollusca—unsegmented body
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Chordates show internal segmentation represented by serial vertebrae and ribs. Obvious external body rings are not required.

**Trap:** A focuses on serial repetition but ignores that annelid segmentation is externally metameric rather than vertebral.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#Phyla with Segmentation]]
</details>

@qbio.segmentation.q7 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=19
A segmented body is damaged in one local region, but other repeated units continue functioning. Which advantage of segmentation is illustrated most directly?

- A) Redundancy
- B) Radial symmetry
- C) Absence of locomotion
- D) Loss of flexibility
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Repeated units provide redundancy: damage to one region need not disable every comparable unit. Segmentation also improves flexibility and locomotion.

**Trap:** D states the opposite of an advantage identified in the chapter.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#SEGMENTATION]]
</details>

@qbio.segmentation.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=19
Consider the following pairs:
1. Annelida — external metameric repeats
2. Arthropoda — regionalised segments
3. Chordata — serial vertebrae and ribs

How many pairs are correctly matched?

- A) One only
- B) Two only
- C) All three
- D) None
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

All three are correct: annelids show metamerism, arthropods tagmosis, and chordates internal segmentation through vertebrae and ribs.

**Trap:** Equating segmentation only with external rings can lead to wrongly rejecting the chordate pair.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#Phyla with Segmentation]]
</details>

@qbio.segmentation.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=19
Which sequence correctly orders examples from similar external repeats, to regionalised external segments, to internal skeletal repeats?

- A) Earthworm → arthropod → chordate
- B) Arthropod → earthworm → chordate
- C) Chordate → arthropod → earthworm
- D) Earthworm → chordate → arthropod
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

An earthworm represents annelid metamerism, an arthropod represents tagmosis, and a chordate represents internal vertebral repetition.

**Trap:** D gets the first category right but swaps regionalised external segmentation with internal segmentation.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#Phyla with Segmentation]]
</details>

@qbio.segmentation.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=19
Assertion (A): An arthropod may be segmented even though its body units are not all alike.
Reason (R): Arthropod segments are regionalised into specialised regions such as head, thorax, and abdomen.

- A) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
- C) A is true, but R is false
- D) A is false, but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Tagmosis groups and specialises arthropod segments. Thus a segmented plan does not require every unit to look alike, and R explains A.

**Trap:** D applies the annelid model of similar repeats too rigidly to all segmented animals.

**See also:** [[../../biology/19 - Segmentation#Note]]
</details>
