# Body Level of Organisation — Questions

> Source: [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation|Body Level of Organisation]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - biology]]

A 10-question MCQ set on the five hierarchical levels of body organisation (cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism) and their phyla-level examples. Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

Scoring: SSC CGL rules — **+2 correct, -0.50 wrong, 0 unattempted**.

---

@qbio.body_level.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=13
The five levels of body organisation, in correct order from simplest to most complex, are:

- A) Tissue → Cell → Organ → Organ system → Organism
- B) Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism
- C) Cell → Organ → Tissue → Organ system → Organism
- D) Organ → Tissue → Cell → Organ system → Organism
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The hierarchy is: **Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism**. Cells are the basic unit; similar cells form tissues; different tissues combine into organs; organs working together form organ systems; all systems together form an organism.

**Trap:** A starts with tissue (skipping cell). C puts organ before tissue (illogical). D is fully reversed.

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#The 5 Levels]]
</details>

@qbio.body_level.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=13
**Sponges (Porifera)** exhibit which level of body organisation?

- A) Cellular level (no true tissues)
- B) Tissue level
- C) Organ level
- D) Organ-system level
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

**Porifera (sponges)** show only the **cellular level** of organisation — their cells are loosely associated but do *not* form true tissues. This is the simplest level in the animal kingdom.

**Trap:** B (tissue level) is the next step up — exhibited by **Cnidaria/Coelenterata** (jellyfish, *Hydra*).

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#Per-Phylum Examples (Animalia)]]
</details>

@qbio.body_level.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=13
Which phylum shows the **organ level** of body organisation?

- A) Porifera
- B) Cnidaria (Coelenterata)
- C) Platyhelminthes
- D) Annelida
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Platyhelminthes** (flatworms — e.g. *Planaria*, liver fluke, tapeworm) shows the **organ level** of body organisation. Below them: Porifera (cellular), Cnidaria (tissue). Above them: Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Chordata (all organ-system level).

**Trap:** D (Annelida) is one level *above* — organ-system level, not organ level.

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#Per-Phylum Examples (Animalia)]]
</details>

@qbio.body_level.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=13
A biologist classifies the following phyla by increasing body-organisation level: Porifera → Cnidaria → Platyhelminthes → Annelida. Which combination is correct?

- A) Cellular → Tissue → Organ-system → Organ
- B) Cellular → Tissue → Organ → Organ-system
- C) Tissue → Cellular → Organ-system → Organ
- D) Cellular → Organ → Tissue → Organ-system
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The correct ascending levels:
- **Porifera** → **Cellular** (no true tissues)
- **Cnidaria** → **Tissue**
- **Platyhelminthes** → **Organ**
- **Annelida** → **Organ-system**

So: **Cellular → Tissue → Organ → Organ-system**.

**Trap:** A ends in "Organ-system → Organ" (reversed). C scrambles the order. D puts organ before tissue.

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#Per-Phylum Examples (Animalia)]]
</details>

@qbio.body_level.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=13
The **stomach** is an example of which level of body organisation?

- A) Cell
- B) Tissue
- C) Organ
- D) Organ system
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **stomach** is an **organ** — a structure composed of different tissues (epithelial, muscular, connective, nervous) working together to perform a specific function (digestion of food). Different organs together (stomach, intestine, liver, pancreas, etc.) form the *digestive system* (an organ system).

**Trap:** B (tissue) is too simple — a tissue is one cell type, while the stomach has multiple. D (organ system) is too broad — the stomach is a single organ within the digestive system.

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#The 5 Levels]]
</details>

@qbio.body_level.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=13
Which of the following phyla shows the **organ-system level** of organisation?

- A) Porifera
- B) Cnidaria
- C) Platyhelminthes
- D) Annelida
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

**Annelida** (and Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Chordata) show the **organ-system level** — the highest level of body organisation. Their bodies have multiple organs working in coordinated systems.

**Trap:** A (Porifera) = cellular; B (Cnidaria) = tissue; C (Platyhelminthes) = organ. Only D and the more complex phyla reach organ-system level.

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#Per-Phylum Examples (Animalia)]]
</details>

@qbio.body_level.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=13
A new phylum is discovered whose members have:
- Cells that aggregate but do *not* form true tissues,
- A *single opening* that serves as both mouth and anus,
- No separate organs or organ systems.

Which level of body organisation does this phylum exhibit?

- A) Cellular
- B) Tissue
- C) Organ
- D) Organ-system
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — **Cellular** level: cells grouped but no true tissues, no organs, no systems. This matches the textbook description of **Porifera** (sponges), whose body is essentially a loose aggregation of cells. The single-opening gastrovascular cavity is also a Porifera-like primitive feature.

- B ✗ — tissue level requires *true* tissues (e.g. Cnidaria).
- C ✗ — organ level requires multiple tissues forming a discrete structure (e.g. Platyhelminthes).
- D ✗ — organ-system level requires multiple organs cooperating (e.g. Annelida onwards).

**Trap:** B (tissue) is the lure because students associate "aggregation of cells" with "tissue". True tissues, however, require cells to be *integrated* by junctions into a functional layer — not merely clustered.

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#Per-Phylum Examples (Animalia)]]
</details>

@qbio.body_level.q8 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=13
A group of similar cells performing a common function is called a:

- A) Organ
- B) Tissue
- C) Organ system
- D) Organism
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells performing a common function (e.g. muscle tissue, nerve tissue, epithelial tissue). Different tissues combine to form organs.

**Trap:** A (organ) is the next level up — different tissues working together. C (organ system) is two levels above tissue.

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#The 5 Levels]]
</details>

@qbio.body_level.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=13
Consider the following statements:
1. The hierarchy of body organisation follows the order: cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.
2. The complexity of body organisation increases as we move from cellular to organ-system level.
3. In the animal kingdom, the progression of body organisation tracks increasing evolutionary complexity.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three are true:
- Statement 1 ✓ — Standard hierarchy cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.
- Statement 2 ✓ — Each step up introduces more structural and functional complexity.
- Statement 3 ✓ — Evolutionarily, simpler phyla (Porifera) have cellular organisation; complex phyla (Chordata) have organ-system level. The progression mirrors evolutionary advancement.

**Trap:** Statement 3 is sometimes considered "too philosophical" — but in modern biology, increasing body-organisation level correlates with increasing evolutionary complexity.

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#The 5 Levels]]
</details>

@qbio.body_level.q10 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=13
The **digestive system** (mouth → oesophagus → stomach → intestine → anus) is an example of:

- A) Cell
- B) Tissue
- C) Organ
- D) Organ system
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The **digestive system** is an **organ system** — a coordinated group of organs (mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, liver, pancreas, anus) that work together to digest food and absorb nutrients.

**Trap:** A and B are too simple. C (organ) is wrong because the digestive system is *multiple organs* working together — that definition matches "organ system", not organ.

**See also:** [[../../biology/13 - Body Level of Organisation#The 5 Levels]]
</details>
