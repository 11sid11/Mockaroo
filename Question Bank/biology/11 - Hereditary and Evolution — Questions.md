@qbio.heredity.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=11
Who is known as the **"Father of Genetics"** for his experiments on pea plants?

- A) Charles Darwin
- B) Gregor Johann Mendel
- C) Hugo de Vries
- D) Lamarck
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Gregor Johann Mendel** (1822–1884) is the *Father of Genetics*. Working with pea plants (*Pisum sativum*), he discovered the laws of inheritance (dominance, segregation, independent assortment).

**Trap:** A (Darwin) is the Father of *Evolution*, not Genetics — easy to mix up.

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#GREGOR MENDEL'S EXPERIMENT]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=11
Alternate forms of a gene at the same locus on a chromosome are called:

- A) Chromatids
- B) Alleles
- C) Genomes
- D) Loci
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Alleles** are different versions of the same gene occupying the same locus on homologous chromosomes. For example, for the gene controlling pea plant height, the *T* (tall) and *t* (dwarf) alleles are two variants of the same gene.

**Trap:** A (chromatids) are identical copies of a chromosome formed during DNA replication. C (genome) is the entire set of an organism's DNA. D (loci) is the *position* of a gene, not its variant form.

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Alleles]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q3 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=11
In a monohybrid cross between a **tall pea plant (TT)** and a **dwarf pea plant (tt)**, what is the phenotype ratio in the **F2 generation** (after self-pollination of F1)?

- A) All tall
- B) 1 tall : 1 dwarf
- C) 3 tall : 1 dwarf
- D) 1 tall : 3 dwarf
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

In the F2 generation of a monohybrid cross (Tt × Tt), the phenotype ratio is **3 tall : 1 dwarf** (genotype ratio 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt). Since T (tall) is dominant over t (dwarf), the heterozygous Tt plants also appear tall — giving 3 tall phenotypes for every 1 dwarf.

**Trap:** A is the F1 ratio (all tall). B is wrong because the F2 *genotype* is 1:2:1, not 1:1.

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Monohybrid Cross]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=11
In a **dihybrid cross** between pea plants differing in two traits (round-yellow × wrinkled-green), the F2 phenotypic ratio is:

- A) 3:1
- B) 1:2:1
- C) 9:3:3:1
- D) 1:1
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The classic **dihybrid cross** F2 phenotypic ratio is **9:3:3:1** — nine showing both dominant traits (round & yellow), three showing one dominant (round, green), three showing the other dominant (wrinkled, yellow), and one showing both recessive (wrinkled & green). This was the empirical foundation for Mendel's **Law of Independent Assortment**.

**Trap:** A (3:1) is the *monohybrid* ratio. B (1:2:1) is the monohybrid *genotype* ratio.

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Dihybrid Cross]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q5 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=11
Which of Mendel's laws states that during gamete formation, the two alleles for a trait separate so that each gamete receives only one allele?

- A) Law of Dominance
- B) Law of Segregation
- C) Law of Independent Assortment
- D) Law of Inheritance
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **Law of Segregation** (Mendel's 2nd law) states that during gamete formation, the two alleles for each gene segregate from each other so that each gamete carries only one allele for each gene.

- Law of Dominance (1st) → one allele masks the other in heterozygotes.
- Law of Independent Assortment (3rd) → alleles of different genes sort into gametes independently.

**Trap:** A (dominance) is also Mendel's but deals with *which* trait is expressed. C is about *different genes* (not the same gene).

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Three Laws of Inheritance]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=11
The wing of a **bird** and the wing of a **butterfly** both serve the same function (flight). However, they have different anatomical structures (the bird wing has bones; the butterfly wing does not). These are examples of:

- A) Homologous organs
- B) Analogous organs
- C) Vestigial organs
- D) Atavistic organs
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Analogous organs** have *similar function* but *different anatomical structure* and evolutionary origin (e.g. wings of birds, butterflies, bats — different structures, same job). In contrast, **homologous organs** have *similar structure* but may have *different functions* and are inherited from a common ancestor (e.g. human arm, whale flipper, bat wing, horse foreleg — same basic bone structure, different uses).

**Trap:** A is the trap — wings of birds and bats *would* be homologous (same bone structure), but bird wing vs butterfly wing is *analogous* (no common bone plan).

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Homologous Organ and Analogous Organ]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=11
Consider the following statements:
1. **Acquired traits** (e.g. learning to ride a bike) are passed from parent to offspring through DNA.
2. **Inherited traits** (e.g. eye colour, blood group) are passed through genes.
3. Variation is more common in sexually-reproducing organisms than asexually-reproducing ones.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

- Statement 1 ✗ — Acquired traits develop during an organism's *lifetime* due to environmental factors; they are **NOT** passed through DNA. (This was Lamarck's mistaken idea, disproved by Weismann.)
- Statement 2 ✓ — Inherited traits (eye colour, blood group) are passed from parent to offspring via genes/DNA.
- Statement 3 ✓ — Sexual reproduction combines genes from two parents, generating greater variation; asexual reproduction produces genetically identical clones (no variation unless mutation).

**Trap:** Statement 1 is the classic Lamarckian misconception (e.g. "a bodybuilder's children will be born muscular"). Modern genetics rejects this.

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Types of Traits]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q8 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=11
Human beings have **23 pairs of chromosomes**. Of these, how many pairs are **autosomes** (non-sex chromosomes)?

- A) 1 pair
- B) 22 pairs
- C) 23 pairs
- D) 46 pairs
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Of the **23 pairs** of human chromosomes, **22 pairs are autosomes** and **1 pair is the sex chromosomes** (XX in females, XY in males). Total: 46 chromosomes (23 × 2).

**Trap:** C (23 pairs) confuses the *total* pair count with autosomes. D (46) confuses total *chromosomes* with pairs.

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Sex Determination]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q9 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=11
A woman has **albinism** (an autosomal recessive disorder). Her husband is a carrier (heterozygous, Aa). What is the probability that their child will be **albino (aa)**?

- A) 0%
- B) 25%
- C) 50%
- D) 75%
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The cross is **aa × Aa**. The mother (aa) can only contribute *a*. The father (Aa) contributes A or a with 50% chance each. So offspring:
- ½ chance = **Aa** (carrier, phenotypically normal)
- ½ chance = **aa** (albino)

So **50%** of children will be albino.

**Trap:** B (25%) is the trap for two-carrier parents (Aa × Aa), not for an aa × Aa cross.

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Monohybrid Cross]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=11
Consider the following statements:
1. Males have one X and one Y chromosome (XY); females have two X chromosomes (XX).
2. The mother always contributes an X chromosome.
3. The father's sperm determines the sex of the offspring.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are true:
- Statement 1 ✓ — Males are XY, females are XX.
- Statement 2 ✓ — Mother's ovum always carries an X.
- Statement 3 ✓ — Father's sperm can carry either X or Y (50/50), so the *father's* sperm decides the offspring's sex.

**Trap:** Some people incorrectly believe the mother determines the sex — but she always contributes X. The decision is the father's.

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Sex Determination]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q11 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=11
The DNA fingerprinting technique was developed by **Sir Alec Jeffreys** and is widely used in forensic science. DNA fingerprinting relies on the fact that:

- A) All humans have identical DNA sequences
- B) Each individual has unique repeated DNA sequences (except identical twins)
- C) All cells in the body have different DNA
- D) DNA is only found in blood
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

DNA fingerprinting identifies individuals based on **unique repeated DNA sequences** (VNTRs — Variable Number Tandem Repeats) that differ between unrelated people. Identical twins share the same DNA fingerprint because they came from the same fertilised egg. Every cell of an individual (except red blood cells, which lack nuclei) contains the same DNA.

**Trap:** A is the opposite — human DNA is mostly shared (~99.9%) but the *repeat* regions vary. C is wrong — all nucleated cells in an individual have *identical* DNA. D is wrong — DNA is found in any nucleated cell (hair roots, saliva, skin).

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Sex Determination]]
</details>

@qbio.heredity.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=11
A biology student writes: "Evolution is driven by random **mutations** followed by **natural selection** acting on existing variation." Which component is *not* random?

- A) Mutation
- B) Natural selection
- C) Both mutation and natural selection
- D) Neither — both are random
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Mutations** are random changes in DNA (the raw material for evolution). **Natural selection** is *not* random — it is the *non-random* differential survival and reproduction of individuals with advantageous traits (e.g. better camouflage, resistance to disease). Together, random mutation + non-random selection drives evolution by adaptation.

**Trap:** A is wrong because mutation IS random. C is wrong because selection is non-random. D is wrong because the whole point is that selection is *biased* (non-random).

**See also:** [[../../biology/11 - Hereditary and Evolution#Heredity and Variation]]
</details>
