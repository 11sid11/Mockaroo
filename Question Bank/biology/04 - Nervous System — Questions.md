@qbio.nervous.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=4
The fluid portion of blood (about 55% of blood volume) is called plasma. Plasma is approximately what fraction water?

- A) 55% water
- B) 75% water
- C) 92% water
- D) 100% water
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Plasma is ~92% water and ~8% proteins (albumin, globulin, fibrinogen), salts, and hormones. Plasma makes up about 55% of whole blood; the remaining 45% is solid (RBC, WBC, platelets).

**Trap:** A (55%) is the percentage of plasma *in whole blood*, not the water percentage *within* plasma.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Plasma]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=4
Normal **red blood cell (RBC)** count in a healthy adult is closest to:

- A) 5,000–8,000 per cubic mm
- B) 1.5–3.5 lakh per cubic mm
- C) 4.5–5.5 million per cubic mm
- D) 25–30 million per cubic mm
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Normal RBC count ≈ 4.5–5.5 million per cubic mm. WBC range is 5,000–8,000 per cubic mm; platelet range is 1.5–3.5 lakh (150,000–350,000) per cubic mm.

**Trap:** A is the WBC range; B is the platelet range. SSC questions commonly swap these to test whether students read the unit.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#RBC (Red Blood Cells)/Erythrocytes]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q3 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=4
A patient presents with **low platelet count** following a recent bout of fever with severe joint pain and a mosquito-bite history. The most likely diagnosis is:

- A) Thrombocytosis
- B) Thrombocytopenia (dengue complication)
- C) Leukaemia
- D) Sickle cell anaemia
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Thrombocytopenia** = low platelet count, classically associated with **dengue** (mosquito-borne, fever + severe joint pain). Thrombocytosis is the *opposite* (high platelets). Leukaemia is a WBC cancer; sickle cell is a genetic RBC disorder.

**Trap:** A is the same-name-but-opposite-direction distractor. C and D affect *other* blood components.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Platelets/Thrombocytes]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q4 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=4
A neuron's **dendrites** carry nerve impulses:

- A) Away from the cell body
- B) Toward the cell body
- C) Both directions
- D) Only between Schwann cells
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Dendrites** are short branched extensions that *receive* signals from other neurons/sensory cells and carry impulses **toward** the cell body (afferent direction). The **axon** carries impulses *away* from the cell body.

**Trap:** A describes the axon, not the dendrite — easy to mix up because both are "wires" sticking out of the cell body.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Dendrites]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=4
A nerve impulse reaches the end of a neuron's axon. In what order do the following events occur?
1. Neurotransmitter is released into the synaptic cleft.
2. The impulse travels along the axon.
3. Neurotransmitter binds receptors on the next neuron's dendrites/cell body.
4. The signal is received by the next neuron.

- A) 1 → 2 → 3 → 4
- B) 2 → 1 → 3 → 4
- C) 2 → 1 → 4 → 3
- D) 4 → 2 → 1 → 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The correct sequence: the impulse travels along the axon (2), triggers release of neurotransmitter into the cleft (1), the neurotransmitter binds receptors on the next neuron (3), and the next neuron receives the signal (4). So: **2 → 1 → 3 → 4**.

**Trap:** A starts with release before the impulse has even reached the terminal — physically impossible. C and D shuffle the receptor-binding step.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Transmission of Neuron Impulses]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=4
Which of these glial cells forms the **myelin sheath** around axons outside the brain and spinal cord?

- A) Oligodendrocytes
- B) Astrocytes
- C) Schwann cells
- D) Microglia
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Schwann cells** form the myelin sheath around peripheral axons (outside the brain and spinal cord). Inside the CNS, **oligodendrocytes** form myelin. Astrocytes support neurons; microglia are the CNS immune cells.

**Trap:** A is the distractor — oligodendrocytes *do* form myelin, but only in the CNS. The "outside the brain and spinal cord" keyword locks the answer to Schwann cells.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Schwann Cells]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q7 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=4
A patient has lost voluntary control of body movements but the eyes can still track a moving object. Imaging suggests damage localised to one specific brain region. Which region is most likely affected?

- A) Cerebrum
- B) Cerebellum
- C) Medulla oblongata
- D) Pons
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The **cerebrum** controls voluntary movement, speech, intelligence, memory and thought. Damage to motor areas of the cerebrum causes loss of voluntary motor control. The cerebellum coordinates balance and posture; medulla controls involuntary vital functions (breathing, heartbeat); pons acts as a bridge between brain regions.

**Trap:** B (cerebellum) is the trap because the cerebellum also affects movement — but it coordinates *balance and posture*, not *voluntary movement* per se.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Functions of the Brain]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q8 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=4
A child touches a hot utensil and instantly pulls their hand back — *before* consciously feeling pain. Which part of the nervous system mediates this rapid, involuntary response?

- A) Cerebrum (conscious thought)
- B) Reflex arc via spinal cord
- C) Cerebellum
- D) Medulla oblongata
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A **reflex arc** is a neural pathway that controls reflex actions — sudden, involuntary responses to stimuli without involving the brain (conscious thought). The signal goes: receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord → motor neuron → effector (muscle). The brain only registers the pain *after* the reflex has already pulled the hand away.

**Trap:** A (cerebrum) is wrong because conscious cerebrum processing would *delay* the response; reflexes are evolutionarily faster precisely because they bypass the cerebrum.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Reflex Action and Reflex Arc]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q9 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=4
Which of the following is a function of the **autonomic nervous system (ANS)**?

- A) Planning tomorrow's exam schedule
- B) Regulating heartbeat, digestion, and breathing without conscious control
- C) Reading comprehension and language
- D) Skeletal muscle voluntary movement
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **autonomic nervous system (ANS)** controls involuntary functions: heartbeat, digestion, respiration, pupil dilation, etc. It is split into the sympathetic ("fight-or-flight") and parasympathetic ("rest-and-digest") branches.

**Trap:** A and C describe **cerebral/cognitive** functions; D describes **somatic** voluntary motor control. The ANS by definition is involuntary.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=4
Consider the following statements:
1. The brain is protected by three membranes called meninges.
2. CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) cushions the brain against shocks.
3. Alzheimer's disease is caused by loss of myelin sheath on peripheral nerves.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- Statement 1 ✓ — The brain and spinal cord are protected by three meninges (dura mater, arachnoid mater, pia mater).
- Statement 2 ✓ — CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) acts as a cushion and shock-absorber, and also helps exchange nutrients/waste.
- Statement 3 ✗ — Alzheimer's is a neurodegenerative disease (progressive loss of memory/cognition), NOT a demyelinating disease of peripheral nerves. Demyelination of peripheral nerves describes Guillain-Barré syndrome or multiple sclerosis (CNS).

**Trap:** Statement 3 sounds plausible because "loss of myelin" is a real disease mechanism — but it belongs to different diseases, not Alzheimer's.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Meninges]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=4
A doctor suspects a patient's symptoms — gradual memory loss, confusion, and difficulty recognising family members — point to a specific CNS disorder. The disease is most likely:

- A) Parkinson's disease
- B) Alzheimer's disease
- C) Epilepsy
- D) Amnesia
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Alzheimer's disease** is a progressive neurodegenerative disease marked by memory loss, confusion, and difficulty with recognition. It is associated with amyloid plaques and tau tangles in the brain. Parkinson's disease primarily affects movement (tremors, rigidity). Epilepsy is a seizure disorder. Amnesia is *loss of memory* (often from injury) but not the same progressive pattern as Alzheimer's.

**Trap:** D (amnesia) is tempting because of the memory-loss keyword — but amnesia is a *symptom*, not the chronic neurodegenerative disease described.

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Alzheimer's Disease]]
</details>

@qbio.nervous.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=4
A teacher sketches a reflex arc and labels it: **Receptor → Sensory neuron → Spinal cord → Motor neuron → Effector (muscle)**. A student argues the order is wrong because impulses should travel from the brain first. Which of the following best addresses the student's confusion?

- A) The student is correct — all reflexes require the brain
- B) Reflexes bypass the brain to be faster; the spinal cord integrates the signal and sends it back via the motor neuron
- C) The order should start from the effector
- D) Reflexes travel through the cerebellum, not the spinal cord
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Reflexes are evolutionarily fast because they are integrated in the **spinal cord**, not the brain. Sensory neuron enters the spinal cord → synapses with interneuron → motor neuron leaves the spinal cord → muscle contracts. The brain only learns about the event *afterwards* (and the pain is felt after the hand is already pulled away). This is why a headless frog can still perform reflex withdrawal.

**Trap:** A reverses the entire point of a reflex (speed via spinal integration). D confuses the cerebellum (which coordinates voluntary movement) with the reflex integrator (spinal cord).

**See also:** [[../../biology/04 - Nervous System#Reflex Action and Reflex Arc]]
</details>
