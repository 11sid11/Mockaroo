@qbio.diseases.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=9
Dengue fever is transmitted by the bite of which mosquito?

- A) Anopheles mosquito
- B) Culex mosquito
- C) Aedes aegypti mosquito
- D) Aedes albopictus only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Dengue** is caused by the **Flavivirus** (DENV 1–4) and is transmitted primarily by the bite of the **Aedes aegypti** mosquito (which also transmits chikungunya and Zika). Culex transmits filariasis/Japanese encephalitis; Anopheles transmits malaria.

**Trap:** A (Anopheles) is the malaria mosquito — easy to confuse because both are mosquito-borne fevers.

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#Dengue]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=9
AIDS is caused by **HIV**, which is classified as a:

- A) Bacterium
- B) Retrovirus (RNA virus)
- C) Fungus
- D) Protozoan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus)** is a **retrovirus** — an RNA virus that uses reverse transcriptase to make DNA from its RNA genome, which then integrates into the host genome. It attacks CD4+ T-cells, weakening the immune system. AIDS Day is observed on 1st December. ELISA is the standard screening test.

**Trap:** A (bacterium) is wrong because HIV is a virus — antibiotics don't work against it. D (protozoan) is wrong because protozoa are eukaryotic cells, not viruses.

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome)]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=9
**BCG** vaccine is given to protect against:

- A) Polio
- B) Tuberculosis (TB)
- C) Diphtheria
- D) Measles
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**BCG (Bacillus Calmette-Guérin)** vaccine protects against **tuberculosis (TB)**, caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. Polio vaccine is by Salk/Sabin; DPT covers Diphtheria, Pertussis, Tetanus; MMR covers Measles, Mumps, Rubella.

**Trap:** A (polio) is the trap because polio vaccine is more famous in India's Pulse Polio Programme — but it's a *different* vaccine from BCG.

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#TB (Tuberculosis)]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=9
A patient presents with high fever, severe headache, joint pain, and a recent history of mosquito bite. Lab tests show **thrombocytopenia** (low platelet count). The most likely diagnosis is:

- A) Malaria
- B) Dengue
- C) Cholera
- D) Typhoid
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Dengue** classically presents with fever, severe joint/bone pain ("breakbone fever"), and **thrombocytopenia** (low platelet count) — caused by the Flavivirus and transmitted by *Aedes aegypti*. Malaria causes fever with chills but does *not* typically cause thrombocytopenia as the dominant feature. Cholera causes severe watery diarrhoea; typhoid has stepwise fever and abdominal symptoms.

**Trap:** A (malaria) is the most common mosquito-borne fever students think of — but the thrombocytopenia clue specifically points to dengue.

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#Dengue]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=9
Which of the following diseases is **non-communicable** (does NOT spread from person to person)?

- A) Tuberculosis
- B) Malaria
- C) Diabetes
- D) Common cold
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Diabetes** (along with arthritis, asthma, cancer) is **non-communicable** — caused by metabolic issues, deficiency, or proliferation of abnormal cells, not by pathogens. TB (bacterial), malaria (protozoal via mosquito), and common cold (viral) are communicable.

**Trap:** A and D are both pathogens (bacterial/viral) — communicable. B is also transmissible via mosquito bite, though not person-to-person. The chapter's definition of communicable requires person-to-person spread OR pathogen-based.

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#Communicable and Non-Communicable Diseases]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q6 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=9
Match each disease to its causative organism:

| Disease | Causative organism |
|---|---|
| (i) Cholera | (a) *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* |
| (ii) TB | (b) *Variola* virus |
| (iii) Smallpox | (c) *Vibrio cholerae* |
| (iv) Malaria | (d) *Plasmodium* (protozoan) |

- A) i-c, ii-a, iii-b, iv-d
- B) i-a, ii-c, iii-b, iv-d
- C) i-c, ii-b, iii-a, iv-d
- D) i-d, ii-a, iii-b, iv-c
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- (i) **Cholera** → *Vibrio cholerae* (c) — water-borne, "blue death"
- (ii) **TB** → *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (a)
- (iii) **Smallpox** → *Variola* virus (b) — eradicated globally in 1980 (last case in India 1977)
- (iv) **Malaria** → *Plasmodium* (d) — protozoan, transmitted by *Anopheles* mosquito

**Trap:** B swaps cholera and TB. C swaps TB and smallpox.

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#DISEASES CAUSED BY BACTERIA]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=9
Consider the following statements:
1. Rabies is caused by a virus that attacks the central nervous system, causing hydrophobia.
2. Leprosy (Hansen's disease) is caused by a bacterium that primarily affects the skin and nerves.
3. Cervical cancer is caused by HPV and is detected by the PAP smear test.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are true:
- Statement 1 ✓ — Rabies is caused by *Lyssavirus* and affects the CNS, leading to hydrophobia (fear of water).
- Statement 2 ✓ — Leprosy is caused by *Mycobacterium leprae* and affects skin and peripheral nerves; also called Hansen's disease.
- Statement 3 ✓ — Cervical cancer is caused by HPV (Human Papillomavirus) and is detected by the **PAP smear test**.

**Trap:** Statement 1 is sometimes confused with tetanus (which also affects muscles/jaw) — but rabies specifically causes hydrophobia, not lockjaw.

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#Rabies]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q8 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=9
**Down's syndrome** is a genetic disorder caused by:

- A) A missing X chromosome (XO)
- B) An extra copy of chromosome 21 (Trisomy 21)
- C) An extra Y chromosome in females
- D) Mutation of a single gene (Mendelian disorder)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Down's syndrome** is an **autosomal chromosomal disorder** caused by an extra copy (or partial copy) of **chromosome 21** — known as **Trisomy 21** (47 chromosomes instead of 46). Turner's syndrome (XO) is the missing-X condition; Klinefelter's (XXY) is the extra-X-in-males condition. Mendelian disorders (e.g. sickle cell, haemophilia) are single-gene disorders, not whole-chromosome ones.

**Trap:** A (Turner's XO) is the trap because both Down's and Turner's are chromosomal disorders — but the *chromosome affected* differs (21 vs X).

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#Autosomal Disorders]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q9 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=9
**Minamata disease** is caused by chronic poisoning with which heavy metal?

- A) Lead
- B) Mercury
- C) Cadmium
- D) Copper
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Minamata disease** = chronic **mercury** poisoning (also called *Mad Hatter disease* because hat-makers used mercury). Itai-itai disease is caused by **cadmium**; Wilson's disease by **copper** accumulation; blue baby syndrome by **nitrate**; siderosis by **iron**.

**Trap:** C (cadmium → Itai-itai) and D (copper → Wilson's) are traps — both are heavy-metal diseases but each has its specific named condition.

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#Some Other Diseases]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=9
Consider the following statements:
1. **Pandemics** spread across several countries/continents.
2. **Epidemics** are sudden outbreaks in a large population/region.
3. **Endemic** diseases are constantly present in a region at low levels.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are true:
- Statement 1 ✓ — **Pandemic** = spread across countries/continents/world (e.g. COVID-19, 1918 flu).
- Statement 2 ✓ — **Epidemic** = sudden outbreak through a large population (e.g. Ebola outbreak in West Africa, Nipah in Kerala).
- Statement 3 ✓ — **Endemic** = constantly present in a region at relatively low levels (e.g. malaria in parts of Africa, dengue in parts of India).

**Trap:** Students mix up *epidemic* and *pandemic* — the difference is geographic scope (regional vs worldwide).

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#Pandemic, Epidemic and Endemic]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q11 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=9
A wheat farmer notices orange-brown rust-like pustules on his crop leaves, leading to significant yield loss. The most likely cause is:

- A) Citrus canker (bacteria)
- B) Tobacco mosaic virus
- C) Rust of wheat (fungus)
- D) Yellow vein mosaic virus (okra)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Rust of wheat** is caused by a **fungus** (*Puccinia* spp.) and produces characteristic orange-brown pustules on leaves and stems, reducing yield. Citrus canker affects citrus fruits; tobacco mosaic virus affects tobacco plants; yellow vein mosaic virus affects okra (lady's finger).

**Trap:** B (TMV) and D (yellow vein mosaic) are *viral* plant diseases and affect different crops. The "wheat + rust + orange-brown pustules" combination uniquely fits fungal rust.

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#DISEASES IN PLANTS]]
</details>

@qbio.diseases.q12 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=9
A patient is found to have a disease caused by a **deficiency of nutrients**. Which of the following is most likely a deficiency disease?

- A) Scurvy (vitamin C deficiency)
- B) Malaria
- C) Tuberculosis
- D) Influenza
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

**Scurvy** is a classic **deficiency disease** caused by lack of **vitamin C** (ascorbic acid), leading to bleeding gums, weakness, and poor wound healing. Malaria (protozoan via mosquito), TB (bacterial), and influenza (viral) are all infectious, not deficiency-related.

**Trap:** B, C, D are all pathogen-driven — communicable diseases. Only A is a *deficiency* disease (non-communicable, caused by lack of a nutrient).

**See also:** [[../../biology/09 - Diseases#Communicable and Non-Communicable Diseases]]
</details>
