@qbio.joints.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=22
A point at which two or more bones meet is called a joint or:

- A) Articulation
- B) Ossicle
- C) Tendon
- D) Foramen
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

A joint is the meeting point of two or more bones and is also called an articulation.

**Trap:** An ossicle is a small bone; the word does not mean the meeting point between bones.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#JOINTS]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=22
Which type of joint has a fluid-filled cavity and permits a wide range of movement?

- A) Fibrous
- B) Cartilaginous
- C) Synovial
- D) Fixed suture
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

A synovial joint has a fluid-filled cavity between the bones and is the most mobile major type of joint.

**Trap:** Cartilaginous joints allow some movement, but they lack the defining fluid-filled synovial cavity and are less mobile.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#1. Synovial Joint]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=22
Bones joined by dense connective tissue with little or no movement form a:

- A) Synovial joint
- B) Fibrous joint
- C) Cartilaginous joint
- D) Fluid joint
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Fibrous joints use dense connective tissue and permit little to no movement. Skull sutures are standard examples.

**Trap:** Cartilaginous joints also have restricted movement, but the joining material is cartilage and slight movement is possible.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#2. Fibrous Joint]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q4 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=22
Which combination describes a cartilaginous joint?

- A) Bones connected by cartilage; slight movement
- B) Fluid-filled cavity; wide movement
- C) Dense connective tissue; no movement
- D) No material between bones; unrestricted movement
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

In cartilaginous joints, cartilage connects the bones and permits slight movement, as between vertebrae.

**Trap:** B describes a synovial joint, while C describes a fibrous joint.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#3. Cartilaginous Joint]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=22
A scan shows a fluid-filled cavity between the articulating bones of a freely movable shoulder. This is a:

- A) Fibrous joint
- B) Cartilaginous joint
- C) Synovial joint
- D) Skull suture
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The shoulder is a synovial joint. Its fluid-filled cavity is the key structural clue, and this type permits wide movement.

**Trap:** Cartilaginous joints permit slight movement but fit neither the shoulder example nor the fluid-cavity clue.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#1. Synovial Joint]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=22
A seam between two skull bones normally permits virtually no movement. How should it be classified?

- A) Synovial
- B) Fibrous
- C) Cartilaginous
- D) Appendicular
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Skull sutures are fibrous joints: dense connective tissue holds the bones together and permits little to no movement.

**Trap:** Cartilaginous joints are also restricted, but allow slight movement and use cartilage rather than dense fibrous tissue.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#2. Fibrous Joint]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q7 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=22
Connections between adjacent vertebrae allow limited flexibility rather than the wide motion of the shoulder. They are classified here as:

- A) Fibrous joints
- B) Synovial joints
- C) Cartilaginous joints
- D) Fixed sutures
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The chapter lists joints between vertebrae as cartilaginous: bones are linked by cartilage and slight movement is possible.

**Trap:** B overgeneralises from the spine's flexibility; the listed intervertebral connections are not assigned the wide-motion category here.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#3. Cartilaginous Joint]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q8 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=22
Bones joined by cartilage retain slight movement. Which example belongs to this category?

- A) Skull suture
- B) Tooth in jawbone
- C) Ribs connected to sternum
- D) Shoulder
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The rib–sternum connection is listed as cartilaginous, like joints between vertebrae, and allows slight movement.

**Trap:** A and B are fibrous examples, while D is synovial; their connecting material and mobility differ.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#Quick Reference]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q9 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=22
A student ranks three examples from least movable to most movable. Which order is correct?

- A) Shoulder → vertebrae → skull suture
- B) Skull suture → vertebrae → shoulder
- C) Vertebrae → skull suture → shoulder
- D) Skull suture → shoulder → vertebrae
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A skull suture permits little or no movement; vertebral cartilaginous joints permit slight movement; the synovial shoulder permits wide motion.

**Trap:** C places the slightly movable vertebral joint below the virtually immovable skull suture.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#Quick Reference]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=22
Consider the following pairs:
1. Knee — synovial
2. Tooth attached to jaw — fibrous
3. Ribs attached to sternum — cartilaginous

How many pairs are correctly matched?

- A) One only
- B) Two only
- C) All three
- D) None
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

All three match the chapter: knee is synovial, tooth–jaw attachment is fibrous, and rib–sternum connection is cartilaginous.

**Trap:** Tooth–jaw attachment may not look like a conventional movable joint, but joints can allow little or no movement.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#Quick Reference]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=22
Which row correctly matches joint type, connecting feature, and mobility?

- A) Synovial — dense connective tissue — no movement
- B) Fibrous — fluid-filled cavity — wide movement
- C) Cartilaginous — cartilage — slight movement
- D) Cartilaginous — dense connective tissue — no movement
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Cartilaginous joints connect bones by cartilage and allow slight movement. Synovial joints have fluid-filled cavities; fibrous joints use dense connective tissue.

**Trap:** D assigns the structure and mobility of a fibrous joint to the cartilaginous category.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#TYPES OF JOINTS]]
</details>

@qbio.joints.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=22
Assertion (A): A joint need not permit a wide range of movement.
Reason (R): Fibrous joints such as skull sutures permit little to no movement.

- A) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
- C) A is true, but R is false
- D) A is false, but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

A joint is fundamentally a meeting point of bones. Fibrous skull sutures are joints despite being nearly immovable, so R establishes A.

**Trap:** D confuses “joint” with “freely movable synovial joint,” which is only one category.

**See also:** [[../../biology/22 - Joints#JOINTS]] · [[../../biology/22 - Joints#2. Fibrous Joint]]
</details>
