@qbio.circulation.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=8
Normal human heartbeat rate is approximately:

- A) 50 beats per minute
- B) 72 beats per minute
- C) 100 beats per minute
- D) 120 beats per minute
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

A healthy adult's heart beats about **72 times per minute** (one full cardiac cycle per beat = systole + diastole). Pulse rate (60–100 bpm) is the *acceptable range* — but the chapter's textbook "normal" is 72 bpm.

**Trap:** C (100 bpm) is the *upper limit of the normal range*, often confused with the typical value.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Heart Beat]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=8
The **pulmonary artery** carries:

- A) Oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart
- B) Deoxygenated blood from the heart to the lungs
- C) Oxygenated blood from the heart to the body
- D) Deoxygenated blood from the body to the heart
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **pulmonary artery** is one of the famous exceptions to the "artery = oxygenated" rule — it carries **deoxygenated blood** from the **right ventricle** to the **lungs** for oxygenation. The pulmonary *vein* carries oxygenated blood from the lungs back to the heart (the opposite exception).

**Trap:** A reverses the direction AND the oxygenation. The pulmonary artery is the trap because students remember "arteries carry oxygenated blood" — which is *almost always* true except here.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Right Ventricle]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=8
The **graveyard of red blood cells (RBCs)** is the:

- A) Liver
- B) Spleen
- C) Bone marrow
- D) Kidney
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Old/damaged **RBCs** are destroyed (and recycled) in the **spleen** — hence the textbook label "*graveyard of RBCs*". The spleen is a large bean-shaped lymphoid organ. Bone marrow is where new RBCs are *born*, not destroyed.

**Trap:** A (liver) is wrong — the liver breaks down haemoglobin *after* the spleen has disposed of the cell, but the spleen is the primary graveyard. C (bone marrow) is the *birthplace*, not graveyard.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#RBC (Red Blood Cells)/Erythrocytes]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=8
A patient needs an emergency blood transfusion. Which blood group is the **universal donor**?

- A) AB+
- B) O+
- C) O−
- D) B−
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**O−** (O-negative) is the **universal donor** — it can donate to all four blood groups (A, B, AB, O) and both Rh types (+/−), because O has no A/B antigens on the RBC surface, and Rh− means no Rh antigen. **AB+** is the *universal recipient* (can receive from anyone).

**Trap:** B (O+) is the trap — O+ is the most common universal-ish donor in practice (can give to all positive types), but O− is the textbook *universal donor* because it lacks both A/B and Rh antigens.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Blood Group]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=8
**Karl Landsteiner** is credited with discovering blood groups. Which blood group system did his work establish?

- A) AB system
- B) ABO system
- C) MN system
- D) HLA system
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Karl Landsteiner discovered the **ABO blood group system** in 1900. He identified the O, A, and B blood types initially, with AB identified shortly after. The Rh factor was later discovered using rhesus monkeys. The Bombay Blood Group was discovered in 1952 by Y. M. Bhende.

**Trap:** A (AB system) is too narrow — AB is just one of the four groups, not the system name.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Blood Group]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=8
In a cardiac cycle, the **lub** sound of the heart is caused by:

- A) Closure of the aortic and pulmonary valves
- B) Closure of the mitral and tricuspid valves
- C) Blood flowing into the coronary arteries
- D) The contraction of the diaphragm
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The first heart sound (**"lub"**) is caused by the closure of the **mitral (bicuspid) and tricuspid valves** at the start of ventricular systole (contraction). The second sound (**"dub"**) is caused by the closure of the **aortic and pulmonary valves** at the start of ventricular diastole.

**Trap:** A is the *opposite* — those valves make the "dub" sound. Easy to confuse because both are closure events.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Heart Beat]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q7 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=8
A doctor records a patient's blood pressure as **120/80 mm Hg**. What do the two numbers represent?

- A) Both are diastolic pressures
- B) 120 = diastolic, 80 = systolic
- C) 120 = systolic (contraction), 80 = diastolic (relaxation)
- D) 120 = pulse rate, 80 = cardiac output
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Blood pressure is reported as **systolic/diastolic** (contraction/relaxation). **120 mm Hg** = systolic pressure (peak when ventricles contract and pump blood out); **80 mm Hg** = diastolic pressure (lowest when ventricles relax and fill). Measured with a **sphygmomanometer**.

**Trap:** B reverses the order. D confuses blood pressure units (mm Hg) with bpm or mL/min.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Sphygmomanometer]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q8 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=8
The functional unit of the **kidney** that filters blood to form urine is the:

- A) Neuron
- B) Nephron
- C) Glomerulus alone
- D) Ureter
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **nephron** is the basic structural and functional unit of the kidney. Each kidney has about **1 million nephrons**. A nephron consists of the renal corpuscle (Bowman's capsule + glomerulus) and a renal tubule (PCT, loop of Henle, DCT, collecting duct).

**Trap:** C (glomerulus alone) is wrong because the nephron includes the glomerulus *plus* the tubule — the glomerulus is only part of the nephron.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Nephrons]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q9 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=8
Animals that excrete **urea** as their main nitrogenous waste are called:

- A) Ammonotelic
- B) Ureotelic
- C) Uricotelic
- D) Aminotelic
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Ureotelic** animals excrete **urea** (e.g. mammals, many terrestrial amphibians, marine fishes, sting rays). **Ammonotelic** animals excrete ammonia (e.g. *Amoeba*, bony fishes, *Prawn*). **Uricotelic** animals excrete uric acid (e.g. most insects, land snails, lizards, birds).

**Trap:** A (ammonotelic) is the trap because both "urea" and "ammonia" sound similar and both contain nitrogen — but the excretion *product* differs (urea vs ammonia).

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Excretion in Animals]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=8
Consider the following statements:
1. The liver converts toxic ammonia into urea.
2. Urine is yellow due to a pigment called urochrome (urobilin) derived from haemoglobin breakdown.
3. A dialysis machine replicates kidney function by filtering blood externally.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three are true:
- Statement 1 ✓ — Liver converts toxic ammonia (NH₃) into urea via the urea cycle.
- Statement 2 ✓ — Urine's yellow colour comes from **urochrome / urobilin**, a breakdown product of haemoglobin.
- Statement 3 ✓ — **Dialysis** filters blood externally when kidneys fail, removing waste and excess fluid via a dialyzer.

**Trap:** Statement 3 sometimes trips students who associate "filtering blood" only with the kidney — but dialysis literally substitutes for it.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Dialysis]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=8
Which excretory organ is primarily responsible for **removing CO₂ and water vapour** from the body?

- A) Kidneys
- B) Skin (sweat glands)
- C) Lungs
- D) Liver
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **lungs** remove **CO₂ (~18 L/day)** and water vapour from the body through expiration. The kidneys excrete urea, salts, water (urine). The skin (sweat) removes small amounts of urea, NaCl, lactic acid. The liver excretes bile pigments (bilirubin, biliverdin).

**Trap:** B (skin) is the trap because the skin *does* have a minor excretory role — but CO₂ removal at the scale of ~18 L/day is uniquely the lungs.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Role of Other Organs in Excretion]]
</details>

@qbio.circulation.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=8
A student notes: "**Double circulation** means blood passes through the heart twice in one complete circuit." Which sequence correctly traces the pulmonary part of double circulation?

- A) Body → Right atrium → Right ventricle → Lungs → Left atrium → Left ventricle → Body
- B) Right ventricle → Lungs → Left atrium
- C) Lungs → Right atrium → Right ventricle → Body
- D) Right atrium → Lungs → Left ventricle → Body
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

In double circulation, blood passes through the heart *twice*. The pulmonary loop:
- Right ventricle → (via pulmonary artery) → Lungs (CO₂ is exchanged for O₂) → (via pulmonary vein) → Left atrium

So the pulmonary leg is: **Right ventricle → Lungs → Left atrium**.

**Trap:** A traces *both* loops together (the full circuit), not just the pulmonary leg. C starts from the lungs (wrong direction). D skips the right ventricle, making the oxygenated blood lose its place.

**See also:** [[../../biology/08 - Circulatory System and Excretory System#Double Circulation]]
</details>
