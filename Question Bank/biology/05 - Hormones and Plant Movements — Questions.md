@qbio.hormones.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=5
Which hormone is secreted by the **beta (β) cells of the Islets of Langerhans** in the pancreas?

- A) Glucagon
- B) Insulin
- C) Adrenaline
- D) Thyroxine
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Beta (β) cells** of the Islets of Langerhans produce **insulin**, which converts glucose to glycogen (glycogenesis). Alpha (α) cells produce glucagon, which does the opposite (glycogenolysis — glycogen back to glucose). Insulin deficiency causes diabetes mellitus.

**Trap:** A (glucagon) is the alpha-cell product — easy to swap because the two hormones regulate blood sugar in opposite directions.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Pancreas]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=5
The **"master gland"** of the human endocrine system, located at the base of the brain, is the:

- A) Thyroid
- B) Adrenal
- C) Pituitary
- D) Pineal
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The **pituitary gland** (pea-sized, at the base of the brain, controlled by the hypothalamus) is called the "master gland" because its hormones regulate many other endocrine glands (growth, prolactin, oxytocin, TSH, ACTH, etc.).

**Trap:** D (pineal) and A (thyroid) are both brain-region/neck glands — but neither is the "master." The pineal secretes melatonin; the thyroid secretes thyroxine.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Pituitary Gland]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=5
**Adrenaline** is often called the **"flight-or-fight"** hormone. Where is it secreted from?

- A) Thyroid gland
- B) Adrenal gland (above the kidney)
- C) Pancreas
- D) Pituitary gland
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Adrenaline** (epinephrine) is secreted by the **adrenal glands**, which are cap-like structures sitting on top of each kidney. It increases heart rate, blood pressure, and metabolism in response to stress — preparing the body to fight or flee.

**Trap:** D (pituitary) is the "master gland" but does not produce adrenaline directly — it releases ACTH that stimulates the adrenal cortex. The actual *flight-or-fight* hormone comes from the adrenal *medulla*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Adrenal Gland]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=5
A patient has **excess production of growth hormone (GH)** *after* puberty. Which condition results?

- A) Dwarfism
- B) Gigantism
- C) Acromegaly
- D) Cretinism
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Excess GH after puberty** causes **acromegaly** — abnormal growth of certain body parts (hands, feet, jaw, brow) because the long bones can no longer grow lengthwise (their growth plates have fused). **Gigantism** is excess GH *before* puberty (excessive overall skeletal growth). **Dwarfism** is GH deficiency. **Cretinism** is hypothyroidism in childhood.

**Trap:** B (gigantism) is the trap because it also involves *excess* GH — but timing matters: pre-puberty → gigantism, post-puberty → acromegaly.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Pituitary Regions]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=5
A patient has been producing excessive dilute urine and cannot retain water. The deficiency is most likely of which hormone?

- A) Insulin
- B) Adrenaline
- C) Antidiuretic hormone (ADH/Vasopressin)
- D) Thyroxine
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**ADH (antidiuretic hormone / vasopressin)** is produced by the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary. It increases water reabsorption in the kidneys. Deficiency of ADH causes **diabetes insipidus** — the body produces too much dilute urine and cannot retain water (different from diabetes mellitus, which is insulin-related).

**Trap:** A (insulin) is the trap because both "diabetes" names share the word — but diabetes *insipidus* = ADH deficiency, diabetes *mellitus* = insulin deficiency.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Posterior Pituitary (Neurohypophysis)]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=5
A laboratory technician measures hormone levels in a patient's blood. The results show **low thyroxine (T3/T4)**, **elevated TSH**, and a visibly **swollen neck (goitre)**. The most likely site of the primary problem is:

- A) Anterior pituitary (failing to stimulate the thyroid)
- B) Hypothalamus (failing to release TRH)
- C) Thyroid gland itself (failing to produce T3/T4 despite TSH stimulation)
- D) Adrenal cortex
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — Low T3/T4 + elevated TSH + goitre points to **primary hypothyroidism** localised to the **thyroid gland**. The anterior pituitary responds to low T3/T4 by secreting *more* TSH (loss of negative feedback), which overstimulates the thyroid and causes it to enlarge (goitre). The most common cause worldwide is **iodine deficiency** (because iodine is required to synthesise T3/T4).

- A ✗ — pituitary failure would give *low* TSH *and* low T3/T4.
- B ✗ — hypothalamic failure would also give low TSH.
- D ✗ — the adrenal cortex produces cortisol/aldosterone, not thyroid hormones.

**Trap:** A and B are tempting because any link in the HPT (hypothalamus–pituitary–thyroid) axis can fail — but only the *low T3/T4 with high TSH* signature unambiguously locates the fault at the thyroid itself.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Thyroid Gland]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=5
Consider the following statements:
1. The pineal gland secretes melatonin in response to darkness.
2. The thymus gland matures T-lymphocytes.
3. Oxytocin is produced by the anterior pituitary.

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- Statement 1 ✓ — Pineal gland secretes **melatonin** (the "hormone of darkness"), helping regulate the sleep-wake cycle via signals from the retina.
- Statement 2 ✓ — **Thymus** (upper chest, behind sternum) matures **T-lymphocytes** (T-cells) critical for immune defence, especially in childhood.
- Statement 3 ✗ — **Oxytocin** is produced by the *hypothalamus* and *stored/released* by the **posterior** pituitary (not anterior). Anterior pituitary releases GH, prolactin, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH.

**Trap:** Statement 3 is the trap because oxytocin is *released from* the posterior pituitary — but it is *made in* the hypothalamus. The "anterior" keyword is wrong.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Pineal Gland]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q8 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=5
Which plant hormone is responsible for **cell elongation** and **phototropism** (bending of plants toward light)?

- A) Gibberellin
- B) Cytokinin
- C) Auxin
- D) Abscisic acid (ABA)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Auxin** (the most famous being IAA, indole-3-acetic acid) is produced at shoot tips and promotes cell elongation. It migrates *away* from the lit side, so the shaded side elongates more, bending the plant toward the light (phototropism). Auxin was discovered by F. W. Went.

**Trap:** A (Gibberellin) is the trap — gibberellins *also* promote elongation (and stem growth), but the phototropism key effect is auxin's.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Plant Hormones]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q9 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=5
A plant hormone is responsible for **closing stomata** during drought stress and promoting seed dormancy. Which hormone is this?

- A) Auxin
- B) Gibberellin
- C) Cytokinin
- D) Abscisic acid (ABA)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

**Abscisic acid (ABA)** is the plant's "stress hormone." It promotes seed dormancy, helps seeds survive desiccation, and triggers stomatal closure during drought to reduce water loss. Auxin = elongation; gibberellin = stem elongation and seed germination; cytokinin = cell division.

**Trap:** A (auxin) is the most famous plant hormone, but it doesn't close stomata. B (gibberellin) actually *opposes* ABA by breaking dormancy and promoting germination.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Plant Hormones]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=5
Match each plant hormone to its primary action:

| Hormone | Primary action |
|---|---|
| (i) Cytokinin | (a) Promotes stem elongation, breaks seed dormancy |
| (ii) Gibberellin | (b) Cell division, delay of leaf senescence |
| (iii) Auxin | (c) Stomatal closure, seed dormancy |
| (iv) Abscisic acid | (d) Cell elongation, phototropism |

- A) i-b, ii-a, iii-d, iv-c
- B) i-a, ii-b, iii-c, iv-d
- C) i-b, ii-a, iii-c, iv-d
- D) i-d, ii-c, iii-b, iv-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- (i) **Cytokinin** → cell division, delay of leaf senescence = (b)
- (ii) **Gibberellin** → stem elongation, breaks seed dormancy = (a)
- (iii) **Auxin** → cell elongation, phototropism = (d)
- (iv) **Abscisic acid** → stomatal closure, seed dormancy = (c)

**Trap:** C swaps auxin and ABA. D is fully scrambled. The auxin ↔ gibberellin distinction is the most commonly confused pair (both promote growth but by different mechanisms).

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Plant Hormones]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q11 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=5
A potted plant kept near a window has its shoot bending toward the window light. This response is an example of:

- A) Geotropism
- B) Phototropism
- C) Hydrotropism
- D) Thigmotropism
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Phototropism** = directional growth movement in response to *light* (positive phototropism in shoots = bending *toward* light). Auxin redistributes to the shaded side, causing it to elongate more, bending the shoot toward the light.

- Geotropism = response to gravity (roots grow downward = positive geotropism).
- Hydrotropism = response to water.
- Thigmotropism = response to touch (e.g. tendrils wrapping a support).

**Trap:** A (geotropism) is the trap because *roots* also bend — but downward, in response to gravity, not light.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Tropisms]]
</details>

@qbio.hormones.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=5
Consider the following statements:
1. Tropic movements are *directional* and depend on the direction of the stimulus.
2. Nastic movements are *non-directional* and depend on the *intensity* of the stimulus.
3. The Venus flytrap closing on touch is a classic example of thigmonastic movement.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are true:
- Statement 1 ✓ — **Tropic** movements (tropisms) are directional — they depend on the *direction* of the stimulus (light, gravity, water, touch).
- Statement 2 ✓ — **Nastic** movements are *non-directional* — they depend on the *intensity* of the stimulus (e.g. photonasty depends on light intensity, not direction).
- Statement 3 ✓ — The Venus flytrap's leaves snapping shut on touch is **thigmonasty** (non-directional touch response).

**Trap:** B and C drop one statement, but here all three are genuinely true. The classic trap would be to drop 3 if students confuse nastic with tropic — but the Venus flytrap is famously a *nastic* (not tropic) movement.

**See also:** [[../../biology/05 - Hormones and Plant Movements#Nastic Movements]]
</details>
