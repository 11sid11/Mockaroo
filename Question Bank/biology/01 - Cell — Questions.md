@qbio.cell.q1 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=1
A student places a human RBC in three beakers labelled X, Y and Z. In X the cell stays the same size, in Y it swells and bursts, in Z it shrinks and crenates. The most likely tonicity of X, Y, Z respectively is:

- A) Hypertonic, isotonic, hypotonic
- B) Isotonic, hypotonic, hypertonic
- C) Hypotonic, isotonic, hypertonic
- D) Isotonic, hypertonic, hypotonic
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

- X (no change) → **isotonic** (solute = solvent inside and outside).
- Y (swells/lyses) → **hypotonic** (more water outside than in, water enters → turgidity, and RBCs lack a wall so they burst).
- Z (shrinks/crenates) → **hypertonic** (water leaves the cell → plasmolysis).

**Trap:** Many students reverse X and Z because they picture "shrinking" as a loss of something the cell "needs to keep" and assume that environment is "low concentration." It is the opposite — a hypertonic medium draws water *out*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#What happens to a cell when placed in a solution]]
</details>

@qbio.cell.q2 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=1
Which of the following correctly pairs a scientist with their contribution to cell theory?

- A) Robert Hooke — observed living cells in pond water for the first time
- B) Antonie van Leeuwenhoek — coined the term "cell" from cork slices
- C) Rudolf Virchow — *omnis cellula e cellula* (cells arise from pre-existing cells)
- D) Matthias Schleiden — stated that all cells arise from a "cell fluid"
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

- **Hooke** saw *dead* cells (cork, 1665) and coined the word "cell."
- **Leeuwenhoek** saw *living* cells (pond water, 1674).
- **Schleiden & Schwann** (1838) proposed that all organisms are made of cells.
- **Virchow** (1855) added *omnis cellula e cellula* — new cells come only from existing cells.

**Trap:** A and B are swapped to lure students who remember the names but not which discovery goes with which.

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Discovery of Cell]] · [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Cell Theory]]
</details>

@qbio.cell.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=1
Match the cell organelle with its primary function:

| Organelle | Function |
|---|---|
| (i) Ribosome | (a) Photosynthesis |
| (ii) Mitochondrion | (b) Protein synthesis |
| (iii) Chloroplast | (c) ATP production / powerhouse |
| (iv) Golgi apparatus | (d) Packaging & dispatch of proteins |

- A) i-b, ii-c, iii-a, iv-d
- B) i-c, ii-b, iii-a, iv-d
- C) i-b, ii-a, iii-c, iv-d
- D) i-d, ii-c, iii-a, iv-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — Ribosomes do protein synthesis, mitochondria produce ATP, chloroplasts (only in plant cells) carry out photosynthesis, Golgi packages and ships proteins.

**Trap:** C swaps mitochondria and chloroplast — a common slip because both sound "energy-related."

@qbio.cell.q4 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=1
The double membrane of the mitochondrion has a heavily-folded inner membrane. These folds are called:

- A) Thylakoids
- B) Cristae
- C) Lamellae
- D) Stroma
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane are **cristae**, which increase surface area for the electron transport chain.

**Trap:** *Thylakoids* are the analogous folds in **chloroplasts**; *stroma* is the fluid matrix inside a chloroplast. Students who confuse chloroplast and mitochondria structure pick (a) or (d).

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Mitochondria]]
</details>

@qbio.cell.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=1
A cell is placed in a solution and water begins to enter it. To prevent the cell from bursting, plant cells rely on a feature animal cells lack. This feature is:

- A) A selectively permeable plasma membrane
- B) A cell wall made of cellulose
- C) A large central vacuole that expels excess water
- D) Tight junctions between adjacent cells
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The **cell wall** (cellulose in plants; chitin in fungi; peptidoglycan in bacteria) provides a rigid counter-pressure (turgor) that prevents lysis. Animal cells lack a wall, so in a hypotonic medium they swell and burst.

**Trap:** A is true but irrelevant — the plasma membrane is present in *both* cell types and cannot on its own stop lysis.

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Cell Wall]] · [[../../biology/01 - Cell#PLANT VS ANIMAL CELL]]
</details>

@qbio.cell.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=1
Which of the following transport processes across the cell membrane requires **cellular energy (ATP)**?

- A) Diffusion of oxygen across the alveolar membrane
- B) Osmosis of water into a parched plant root
- C) Active transport of K⁺ ions into a cell against their concentration gradient
- D) Facilitated diffusion of glucose through GLUT transporters
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — *Active transport* moves solutes **against** the concentration gradient (low → high) using ATP. All the other listed processes are *passive* (down the gradient, no ATP).

**Trap:** D uses a *carrier protein*, which tricks students into assuming "protein = energy needed." Facilitated diffusion is still passive — it just uses a protein to bypass the lipid bilayer; no ATP is consumed.

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Active Transport/Reverse Osmosis]] · [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Movement across Cell Membrane]]
</details>

@qbio.cell.q7 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=1
The correct sequence of enzymes acting at a eukaryotic DNA replication fork, in the order they act, is:

- A) Primase → Helicase → Polymerase → Ligase
- B) Helicase → Primase → Polymerase → Ligase
- C) Helicase → Ligase → Polymerase → Primase
- D) Polymerase → Helicase → Primase → Ligase
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

1. **Helicase** unwinds the double helix.
2. **Primase** lays down an RNA primer (replication cannot start de novo).
3. **DNA polymerase** extends the new strand by adding nucleotides.
4. **DNA ligase** joins the Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand.

**Trap:** Many students write "polymerase first" because polymerase is "the main enzyme." But polymerase cannot start without a primer, and the primer needs an unwound template.

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Enzymes Essential for DNA Replication in Animal Cell]]
</details>

@qbio.cell.q8 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=1
After one round of semi-conservative DNA replication starting from one fully-labelled (heavy, ¹⁵N) double helix in a ¹⁴N medium, the resulting two daughter double helices are best described as:

- A) Both fully heavy
- B) Both fully light
- C) One fully heavy and one fully light
- D) Each molecule has one heavy strand and one light strand
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D** — **Semi-conservative** replication: each daughter DNA molecule retains **one parental (heavy) strand** and contains **one newly synthesized (light) strand**. This is the Meselson–Stahl result.

**Trap:** C is the classic wrong answer — students remember that "old + new" strands end up in daughters but forget that *each* daughter gets *one* of each.

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Semi Conservative DNA Replication]]
</details>

@qbio.cell.q9 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=1
A biology textbook lists the following as "present in plant cells but absent in animal cells": cell wall, chloroplast, large central vacuole, plastids. Which option lists **only** items that satisfy this rule?

- A) Cell wall and chloroplast only
- B) Cell wall, chloroplast and large central vacuole only
- C) Cell wall, chloroplast, large central vacuole and plastids
- D) Plastids only, because vacuoles exist in animal cells too
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — All four (cell wall, chloroplast, large central vacuole, plastids) are plant-only structures per the chapter table. Animal cells *do* have small vacuoles, but not the **large** central one.

**Trap:** D is a tempting "gotcha" answer. The trick is the word "large" — animal cells do have small vesicles/vacuoles (food vacuoles, lysosomes), but the **large central** vacuole that occupies most of the plant cell volume is absent.

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#PLANT VS ANIMAL CELL]]
</details>

During mitosis, the stage at which chromosomes line up on the equatorial plate (metaphase plate) of the cell is called:

@qbio.cell.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=1
Consider the following: (Statement) Lysosomes are called "suicide bags" of the cell. (Assertion 1) They contain hydrolytic enzymes. (Assertion 2) They can digest the cell's own components when the cell is damaged.

- A) Both assertions are true and together explain the statement
- B) Both assertions are true but Assertion 2 alone explains the statement
- C) Assertion 1 is true, Assertion 2 is false
- D) Assertion 1 is false, Assertion 2 is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — Lysosomes carry hydrolytic enzymes (Assertion 1 ✓) that can break down cellular debris — and, in cell injury/apoptosis, the cell's *own* components (Assertion 2 ✓). Together these justify the "suicide bag" label.

**Trap:** B is a partial-credit trap. Assertion 1 alone only tells you *what's inside*; the "suicide" connotation specifically requires that the enzymes can act on the cell itself.

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Lysosomes]]
</details>

@qbio.cell.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=1
Consider the following statements about microtubule arrangements:
1. Centrioles have a 9+0 arrangement (nine peripheral triplets, no central pair).
2. The axoneme of cilia and flagella has a 9+2 arrangement (nine outer doublets + two central singlets).

Which of the following is correct?

- A) Statement 1 is false; centrioles have 9+2 like axonemes.
- B) Statement 2 is false; axonemes have 9+0 like centrioles.
- C) Both statements are true; they describe different structures.
- D) Both statements are true; they describe the same structure.
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — Statement 1 correctly describes **centrioles** (9+0). Statement 2 correctly describes the **ciliary/flagellar axoneme** (9+2). They are different structures with different microtubule arrangements — that's why SSC questions test whether you can keep them separate.

**Trap:** D is the lure for students who remember "9-something" but not which structure has which pattern. A and B invert the facts.

**See also:** [[../../biology/01 - Cell#Centrosome]]
</details>
