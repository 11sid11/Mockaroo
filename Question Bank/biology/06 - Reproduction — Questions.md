@qbio.reproduction.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=6
In a flower, the **male reproductive organ** is collectively called the:

- A) Pistil
- B) Stamen
- C) Stigma
- D) Ovary
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **stamen** is the collective male reproductive part of a flower, consisting of the **anther** (produces pollen) and **filament** (supports the anther). The **pistil** is the collective female reproductive part, consisting of stigma, style, and ovary.

**Trap:** A (pistil) is the female counterpart — easy to confuse with the male stamen because both names sound "specific". D (ovary) is part of the pistil, not the stamen.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Anatomy of a Flower]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=6
Which asexual reproduction method is found in **yeast** (unicellular) and **Hydra** (multicellular)?

- A) Binary fission
- B) Multiple fission
- C) Budding
- D) Fragmentation
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Budding** = a small outgrowth (bud) forms on the parent body due to cell division, grows, and detaches to form a new individual. It is seen in **yeast** (unicellular) and **Hydra** (multicellular). Binary fission is seen only in unicellular organisms like *Amoeba*; multiple fission in *Plasmodium*; fragmentation in *Spirogyra*.

**Trap:** A (binary fission) is the trap because both budding and binary fission are asexual — but binary fission is *only* in unicellulars, not multicellulars like Hydra.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Budding]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=6
Transfer of pollen from the anther of one flower to the stigma of **another flower on a different plant of the same species** is called:

- A) Self-pollination (autogamy)
- B) Cross-pollination (allogamy)
- C) Geitonogamy
- D) Fertilisation
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Cross-pollination (allogamy)** is the transfer of pollen from one flower to the stigma of another flower on a *different plant* of the same species. It promotes genetic diversity. Self-pollination (autogamy) occurs within the same flower. Geitonogamy is pollination between flowers of the *same* plant (genetically similar to autogamy in effect). Fertilisation is the *fusion* of gametes — not the transfer of pollen.

**Trap:** C (geitonogamy) is the trap — it sounds similar to allogamy but refers to *same-plant* pollination, not different plants.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Pollination]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=6
Consider the following statements about pollination:

1. **Entomophily** (insect pollination) is typical of brightly coloured, scented flowers with sticky pollen.
2. **Anemophily** (wind pollination) is typical of small, dull-coloured flowers that produce large amounts of light, dry pollen.
3. **Hydrophily** (water pollination) is seen in plants like *Vallisneria*, where pollen often floats on or below the water surface.
4. Pollen of insect-pollinated flowers is typically **non-sticky and powdery** to ride air currents.

Which of the following is INCORRECT?

- A) Statement 1
- B) Statement 2
- C) Statement 3
- D) Statement 4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D** — Insect-pollinated flowers produce **sticky** (or spiky/hook-shaped) pollen that adheres to the bodies of visiting insects — not non-sticky powdery pollen. Non-sticky, powdery pollen is the hallmark of **wind-pollinated** plants (anemophily), where it must be light enough to be carried by air currents.

- Statement 1 ✓ — entomophilous flowers use colour, scent, and nectar to attract insects, and their pollen is sticky enough to attach to insect bodies.
- Statement 2 ✓ — anemophilous flowers (e.g. grasses, maize) produce abundant light, dry pollen.
- Statement 3 ✓ — *Vallisneria* shows true hydrophily; pollen is released underwater and floats to female flowers.

**Trap:** Statement 4 swaps the *insect* and *wind* pollen characteristics. Students often generalise "pollen is light and powdery" without remembering that insect-pollinated pollen is the *opposite*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Pollination]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q5 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=6
A fruit that develops **without fertilisation** of an ovule — and is therefore seedless — is called a:

- A) Drupe
- B) False fruit
- C) Parthenocarpic fruit
- D) Legume
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Parthenocarpic fruits** develop *without fertilisation* of the ovule, so they are seedless (e.g. banana, grape, cucumber). A **drupe** is a fruit with a hard shell and one seed (e.g. coconut). A **false fruit** develops from other floral parts *in addition* to the ovary (e.g. apple, cashew).

**Trap:** B (false fruit) is the distractor — false fruits *do* involve the ovary and usually have seeds. The seedless + no-fertilisation combo uniquely defines parthenocarpy.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Parthenocarpic Fruits]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q6 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=6
Arrange the steps of human fertilisation and early development in the **correct chronological order**:

1. Insemination
2. Implantation
3. Gametogenesis
4. Fertilisation
5. Gestation
6. Parturition

- A) 3 → 1 → 4 → 2 → 5 → 6
- B) 1 → 3 → 4 → 2 → 5 → 6
- C) 3 → 1 → 4 → 5 → 2 → 6
- D) 3 → 4 → 1 → 2 → 5 → 6
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct order is:
- **Gametogenesis** (3) — sperm and eggs form.
- **Insemination** (1) — sperm is transferred into the female reproductive tract.
- **Fertilisation** (4) — sperm meets egg, zygote forms.
- **Implantation** (2) — blastocyst attaches to uterine wall.
- **Gestation** (5) — embryo develops into foetus inside the uterus.
- **Parturition** (6) — baby is delivered.

So: **3 → 1 → 4 → 2 → 5 → 6**.

**Trap:** D skips insemination (which is required before sperm can meet the egg). C moves gestation before implantation, which is biologically impossible.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Process of Fertilization]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q7 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=6
A species reproduces by laying eggs that **develop and hatch inside the mother's body**, so the offspring emerge as live young. This reproductive strategy is called:

- A) Oviparous
- B) Viviparous
- C) Ovoviviparous
- D) Parthenogenetic
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Ovoviviparous** animals lay eggs that *develop inside the mother's body* and hatch *inside* her (e.g. sharks, snakes, some fishes like guppies). **Oviparous** lay eggs that develop *outside* the mother's body (e.g. birds, most reptiles, frogs). **Viviparous** give birth to live young developed inside the body, nourished via a placenta (e.g. most mammals). Parthenogenesis is asexual reproduction from an unfertilised egg (e.g. honey bees).

**Trap:** B (viviparous) is the trap because both involve live birth — but viviparous young are nourished *via placenta* during internal development, while ovoviviparous young are nourished by *yolk* inside an egg that happens to be retained.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Types of Fertilisation]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q8 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=6
The female reproductive structure in which **fertilisation** typically occurs in human females is the:

- A) Ovary
- B) Uterus
- C) Fallopian tube (oviduct)
- D) Vagina
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

In humans, fertilisation occurs in the **fallopian tube (oviduct)**, not in the uterus. The ovary releases an egg, which is swept into the fallopian tube; if sperm are present, fertilisation happens there. The resulting zygote then travels to the uterus and implants in the uterine wall.

**Trap:** B (uterus) is the trap — the uterus is where the embryo *implants and grows*, not where fertilisation happens.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Parts of Female Reproductive System]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=6
Consider the following statements:
1. The father determines the sex of the human baby.
2. The mother always contributes an X chromosome to the offspring.
3. A human male has one X and one Y chromosome (XY); female has two X (XX).

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are true:
- Statement 1 ✓ — Father's sperm determines sex (carries either X or Y); mother always contributes X.
- Statement 2 ✓ — Mother's ovum always carries an X chromosome.
- Statement 3 ✓ — Males are XY, females are XX.

X + X = girl; X + Y = boy. So the father's sperm is the deciding factor.

**Trap:** The classic wrong answer is "the mother determines sex" — but the mother only ever gives X, so the *father's* X vs Y call is what matters.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Sex Determination]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q10 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=6
A 45-year-old man has a low sperm count, small testes, and is found to have an extra X chromosome (XXY karyotype). The most likely diagnosis is:

- A) Turner's syndrome
- B) Klinefelter's syndrome
- C) Down's syndrome
- D) Androgen insensitivity syndrome
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — **Klinefelter's syndrome** (47, XXY) is a chromosomal aneuploidy in which a male has an *extra* X chromosome. Features include small testes, low sperm count (often infertility), reduced body hair, tall stature, and gynaecomastia. It is caused by non-disjunction during meiosis in either parent.

- A ✗ — Turner's syndrome is **45, X0** — it affects *females* (one missing X), leading to underdeveloped ovaries and short stature.
- C ✗ — Down's syndrome is **trisomy 21** (extra chromosome 21) — features intellectual disability, flat face, single palmar crease; not sex-chromosome related.
- D ✗ — androgen insensitivity is genetic (X-linked, 46, XY) but the karyotype is *not* XXY.

**Trap:** A and D both involve abnormal sex-chromosome situations, but only XXY is Klinefelter's. Students mix up Turner's (XO, female) and Klinefelter's (XXY, male).

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Sex Determination]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=6
A biology textbook claims: "**Parthenogenesis** is a form of *sexual* reproduction because it produces offspring." Is this claim correct?

- A) Yes — any reproduction that creates offspring is sexual
- B) No — parthenogenesis is *asexual* because the egg develops *without fertilisation* (no sperm involved)
- C) Yes — because honey bees reproduce this way
- D) Cannot be determined
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Parthenogenesis** is **asexual reproduction** because the offspring develops from an *unfertilised* egg — no sperm is involved. (The fact that honey bees reproduce this way doesn't change its classification; in bees, the queen's unfertilised eggs become drones, while fertilised eggs become workers.) The defining feature of *sexual* reproduction is the *fusion of male and female gametes* — parthenogenesis lacks this entirely.

**Trap:** A is wrong because the definition of sexual reproduction requires *gamete fusion*. C is wrong because the species that does it (honey bees) doesn't redefine the category.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Parthenogenesis]]
</details>

@qbio.reproduction.q12 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=6
In **vegetative propagation** of plants, new individuals are produced from which parts of the parent?

- A) Seeds and fruits only
- B) Vegetative parts — roots, stems, or leaves (no seeds involved)
- C) Pollen grains only
- D) Only grafting of stems
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Vegetative propagation** is a form of asexual reproduction in which a new plant grows from the *vegetative parts* of the parent — root, stem, or leaf — *without* seeds. Examples: cutting (rose, sugarcane), layering (lemon, strawberry), grafting (rose). The new plant is genetically identical to the parent (a clone).

**Trap:** A (seeds/fruits) is sexual reproduction by definition. D (only grafting) is too narrow — vegetative propagation includes cutting, layering, grafting, and tissue culture.

**See also:** [[../../biology/06 - Reproduction#Vegetative Propagation]]
</details>
