@qbio.circulatory_types.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=17
In an **open circulatory system**, blood:

- A) Is always contained within vessels
- B) Flows freely through body cavities (haemocoel) without being contained in vessels
- C) Travels only in one direction
- D) Contains no haemoglobin
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

In an **open circulatory system**, blood is *not* always confined to vessels — it flows freely through body cavities (called a **haemocoel**), bathing the organs directly. Found in **Arthropoda** and most **Mollusca**.

**Trap:** A describes the *closed* circulatory system. C is unrelated. D is wrong — open-system animals often have haemocyanin or other pigments.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Two Types]]
</details>

@qbio.circulatory_types.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=17
Which phylum has a **closed circulatory system** (blood always within vessels)?

- A) Arthropoda
- B) Mollusca
- C) Annelida
- D) Platyhelminthes
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Annelida** (earthworms, leeches) and **Chordata** (vertebrates) have a **closed circulatory system** — blood is always contained within vessels as it circulates.

**Trap:** A (Arthropoda) and B (Mollusca) are mostly *open* circulatory systems. D (Platyhelminthes) has no specialised circulatory system at all.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Two Types]]
</details>

@qbio.circulatory_types.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=17
Which of the following phyla have an **open** circulatory system?

- A) Arthropoda and Mollusca
- B) Annelida and Chordata
- C) Only Arthropoda
- D) Only Chordata
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

**Arthropoda** (insects, spiders, crustaceans) and most **Mollusca** (snails, clams — except cephalopods like octopus/squid which have closed systems) have open circulatory systems.

**Trap:** B is the *closed* system list. C is too narrow.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Quick Reference]]
</details>

@qbio.circulatory_types.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=17
An insect (e.g. a grasshopper) is found to have blood flowing freely through its body cavity rather than being contained in vessels. This describes:

- A) A closed circulatory system
- B) An open circulatory system (haemocoel)
- C) No circulatory system
- D) A double circulatory system
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Insects (Arthropoda) have an **open circulatory system** — blood (haemolymph) flows freely through the **haemocoel** (body cavity) and bathes the organs directly. This is less efficient than a closed system but adequate for small animals with low metabolic rates.

**Trap:** A is wrong — closed means blood stays in vessels. D (double circulation) is a *human* feature (separate pulmonary and systemic circuits), not a *type* of open/closed.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Two Types]]
</details>

@qbio.circulatory_types.q5 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=17
In a closed circulatory system, blood flows through a continuous network of:

- A) Body sinuses (haemocoel)
- B) Vessels (arteries, capillaries, veins)
- C) Open spaces between tissues
- D) The coelom
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — A **closed** circulatory system routes blood through a continuous series of **vessels**: arteries (carry blood away from heart), capillaries (site of exchange), and veins (return to heart). Annelida and Chordata are the main phyla with this design.

**Trap:** A and C describe the *open* system (haemocoel — arthropods, most molluscs). D confuses coelom (body cavity lined by mesoderm) with blood vessel lumen.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Two Types]]
</details>

@qbio.circulatory_types.q6 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=17
The oxygen-carrying pigment dissolved in the haemolymph of most **molluscs** and some **arthropods** is:

- A) Haemoglobin
- B) Haemocyanin
- C) Chlorocruorin
- D) Myoglobin
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — **Haemocyanin** is a copper-based respiratory pigment that is *colourless when deoxygenated* and **blue when oxygenated**. It is found in the haemolymph of most arthropods (e.g. crustaceans, horseshoe crabs) and many molluscs (snails, octopuses). Haemoglobin (iron-based, red) is found in vertebrate RBCs and some annelids; chlorocruorin is a green pigment found in some annelids (e.g. *Spirographis*); myoglobin is an intracellular oxygen-storage protein in muscle, not a circulating blood pigment.

**Trap:** A (haemoglobin) is the trap because it's the most familiar blood pigment — but it is *not* the typical pigment in arthropod/mollusc haemolymph.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Pigments and Plasma]]
</details>

@qbio.circulatory_types.q7 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=17
A student argues: "Open circulatory systems are *less efficient* than closed systems, so they must be *primitive* and *inferior*." Which of the following best addresses this argument?

- A) Yes — open systems are always inferior and represent evolutionary failure
- B) No — open systems are *adequate* for the metabolic needs of small animals like insects; efficiency is *relative* to body size and oxygen demand
- C) Yes — all invertebrates should evolve closed systems over time
- D) No — open systems are *more* efficient than closed systems in every case
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — Open circulatory systems are not "primitive" or "inferior" — they are *evolutionarily appropriate* for animals with low metabolic rates, small body size, and high surface-area-to-volume ratio (e.g. insects, most molluscs). The haemolymph delivers nutrients and removes wastes adequately for these animals. Closed systems become advantageous as body size and oxygen demand increase (e.g. vertebrates). "Efficiency" is always relative to the animal's physiology, not an absolute ranking.

**Trap:** A and C are teleological fallacies — they assume evolution has a "goal" toward closed systems. D is the opposite extreme — open systems are *not* universally more efficient.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Why Two Types Exist]]
</details>

@qbio.circulatory_types.q8 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=17
Which of the following phyla has **no specialised circulatory system** at all (no heart, no vessels)?

- A) Annelida
- B) Arthropoda
- C) Platyhelminthes
- D) Chordata
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C** — **Platyhelminthes** (flatworms) are flat enough that their body cells can exchange gases and nutrients by *diffusion* directly with the environment — they have **no circulatory system**. Similarly, Porifera and Cnidaria lack a circulatory system. Annelida (closed), Arthropoda (open), and Chordata (closed) all have specialised systems.

**Trap:** A and D are closed-system phyla (have hearts and vessels). B is open-system (has a tubular heart, but blood spills into haemocoel). The "no circulatory system" answer is the one phylum where nutrients are simply *diffused*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Quick Reference]]
</details>

@qbio.circulatory_types.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=17
Consider the following statements about cephalopods (octopus, squid):

1. Cephalopods are molluscs.
2. Unlike most molluscs, cephalopods have a **closed** circulatory system.
3. They also have a more developed brain and complex behaviours (camouflage, problem-solving).

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D** — All three are true:
- Statement 1 ✓ — octopuses and squids are in Phylum **Mollusca** (Class Cephalopoda).
- Statement 2 ✓ — unlike most molluscs (which are open), cephalopods have a **closed** circulatory system with multiple hearts (a systemic heart + two branchial hearts), supporting their high metabolic rate.
- Statement 3 ✓ — cephalopods have the largest brains of any invertebrates and exhibit remarkable intelligence (camouflage via chromatophores, tool use, problem-solving in lab tests).

**Trap:** B drops statement 2 because students generalise "all molluscs have open systems" — but cephalopods are the famous exception, and the closed system is part of what enables their active predatory lifestyle.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Quick Reference]]
</details>

@qbio.circulatory_types.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=17
Assertion (A): An earthworm and a fish both have closed circulatory systems.
Reason (R): Both belong to phyla in which blood is always confined within vessels as it circulates.

- A) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A
- B) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
- C) A is true, but R is false
- D) A is false, but R is true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — An earthworm (Annelida) and a fish (Chordata) both have **closed circulatory systems** — blood is always inside vessels. The reason (R) correctly explains this: in both phyla, the defining anatomical feature is that blood vessels form a complete, sealed loop. (In contrast, arthropods like insects have *open* systems where blood leaves the vessels and bathes the tissues.)

**Trap:** D assumes "different phyla = different system types" — but Annelida and Chordata independently evolved closed systems, and R explains why *both* are closed.

**See also:** [[../../biology/17 - Circulatory System Types#Two Types]]
</details>
