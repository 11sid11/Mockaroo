# Symmetry — Questions

> Source: [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry|Symmetry]] | Subject: [[../../_MOCs/MOC - biology]]

A 10-question MCQ set on the three types of symmetry (asymmetrical, radial, bilateral) and their phyla-level distribution. Calibrated to SSC CGL Tier-I standard.

Scoring: SSC CGL rules — **+2 correct, -0.50 wrong, 0 unattempted**.

---

@qbio.symmetry.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=14
Which of the following describes **radial symmetry**?

- A) Body can be divided into equal halves by a single plane only
- B) Body can be divided into equal halves by any plane passing through the central axis
- C) No plane divides the body into equal halves
- D) Body has left and right sides only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Radial symmetry** = any plane through the central axis of the body divides it into two equal (mirror-image) halves. Examples: starfish, sea urchin (Echinodermata); jellyfish, *Hydra* (Cnidaria).

**Trap:** A describes **bilateral** symmetry. C describes **asymmetrical** bodies (Porifera).

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Three Types of Symmetry]]
</details>

@qbio.symmetry.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=14
**Porifera (sponges)** typically show which type of symmetry?

- A) Radial
- B) Bilateral
- C) Asymmetrical
- D) Spherical
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Sponges (Porifera) are mostly **asymmetrical** — no plane divides them into equal halves. (Some may appear radially symmetrical but most are irregular.)

**Trap:** A (radial) is the trap because some sponges look like cylinders or vases. The textbook classification is *asymmetrical*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Three Types of Symmetry]]
</details>

@qbio.symmetry.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=14
**Bilateral symmetry** means the body can be divided into two equal halves by:

- A) Any plane through the central axis
- B) A single (sagittal) plane only
- C) No plane
- D) Two perpendicular planes
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Bilateral symmetry** = only *one* plane (the sagittal/median plane) divides the body into left and right mirror-image halves. Examples: Platyhelminthes onwards (humans, insects, fish, birds, etc.).

**Trap:** A is *radial* symmetry (any central-axis plane). C is *asymmetrical*.

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Three Types of Symmetry]]
</details>

@qbio.symmetry.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=14
A jellyfish (*Aurelia*) is found drifting in the sea. Its body can be cut into equal halves by *any* plane passing through the central oral-aboral axis. The type of symmetry is:

- A) Asymmetrical
- B) Radial
- C) Bilateral
- D) Pentamerous
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Jellyfish (Phylum Cnidaria) show **radial symmetry** — any plane through the central oral-aboral axis divides the body into two equal halves. (Starfish is *pentamerous radial* — 5-armed, but still radial because *any* plane through the central axis works.)

**Trap:** C (bilateral) is the trap because humans are bilateral — but jellyfish are not.

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Three Types of Symmetry]]
</details>

@qbio.symmetry.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=14
A textbook lists these three phyla:
- **Porifera** — no plane of symmetry
- **Cnidaria** — any plane through the central axis divides the body into equal halves
- **Platyhelminthes** — only one specific plane (sagittal) divides the body into equal halves

Which sequence correctly identifies the symmetry type for each phylum?

- A) Porifera → Asymmetrical; Cnidaria → Radial; Platyhelminthes → Bilateral
- B) Porifera → Bilateral; Cnidaria → Radial; Platyhelminthes → Asymmetrical
- C) Porifera → Asymmetrical; Cnidaria → Bilateral; Platyhelminthes → Radial
- D) Porifera → Radial; Cnidaria → Bilateral; Platyhelminthes → Asymmetrical
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A** — **Porifera** has no plane of symmetry (asymmetrical). **Cnidaria** shows radial symmetry (any plane through the central oral-aboral axis works). **Platyhelminthes** (and all phyla from here onward) are bilaterally symmetrical — only the *single* sagittal plane gives mirror-image halves.

- B ✗ — swaps Porifera and Platyhelminthes symmetry types.
- C ✗ — swaps Cnidaria and Platyhelminthes symmetry types.
- D ✗ — all three wrong.

**Trap:** B and C each swap exactly two of the three answers — students who haven't memorised which phylum has which type can guess wrong by relying on "higher phyla = bilateral" intuition (which is right for Platyhelminthes but not for assigning Porifera/Cnidaria).

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Quick Reference]]
</details>

@qbio.symmetry.q6 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=14
Which phyla exhibit **bilateral symmetry**?

- A) Porifera and Cnidaria
- B) Only Cnidaria
- C) Platyhelminthes, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Chordata
- D) Only Chordata
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Bilateral symmetry** is found in **Platyhelminthes** (and all phyla *after* it): Aschelminthes/Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Chordata. Most higher animals are bilateral.

**Trap:** A is the wrong way — Porifera = asymmetrical, Cnidaria = radial.

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Quick Reference]]
</details>

@qbio.symmetry.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=14
A student claims: "Humans have bilateral symmetry because we have two arms, two legs, and two eyes." Is this argument correct?

- A) Yes — paired body parts prove bilateral symmetry
- B) No — bilateral symmetry is determined by a single sagittal plane dividing left and right, not by counting paired parts
- C) Yes — symmetry means any two equal halves
- D) No — humans are actually radial
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Bilateral symmetry is defined by the **single sagittal plane** dividing the body into left and right mirror-image halves — the *position* of that plane, not the *count* of paired parts. Humans have one such plane. A starfish with 5 arms has radial symmetry (any plane through the central axis works), not bilateral, even though it has multiple "arms".

**Trap:** A is the classic misconception — symmetry is about *planes of division*, not *counting* features.

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Three Types of Symmetry]]
</details>

@qbio.symmetry.q8 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=14
A starfish has 5 arms arranged around a central disc. Any plane through the central axis divides it into two equal halves. The symmetry is:

- A) Bilateral
- B) Asymmetrical
- C) Radial (specifically pentamerous radial)
- D) Spherical
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Echinodermata** (starfish) show **radial symmetry** — specifically **pentamerous radial symmetry** (5-part). Because *any* plane through the central axis divides the body into equal halves, it's radial, not bilateral.

**Trap:** A (bilateral) is the trap because 5 arms might suggest 5 "sides" — but bilateral requires only *one* specific plane. Spherical (D) is not a recognised biological symmetry type.

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Three Types of Symmetry]]
</details>

@qbio.symmetry.q9 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=14
An organism has no plane that can divide its body into two equal halves. Its symmetry is:

- A) Radial
- B) Bilateral
- C) Asymmetrical
- D) Pentaradial
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Asymmetrical** organisms (e.g. most Porifera) lack any plane of symmetry. The body is irregular.

**Trap:** A and B both require at least one plane of symmetry. D (pentaradial) is a specific sub-type of radial symmetry, not a separate category.

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Three Types of Symmetry]]
</details>

@qbio.symmetry.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=14
Consider the following statements:
1. Cnidarians have radial symmetry as adults but some show bilateral symmetry in larval stages.
2. Echinoderms are radially symmetrical as adults but bilaterally symmetrical as larvae.
3. All vertebrates are bilaterally symmetrical.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

- Statement 1 ✗ — Cnidarians are radially symmetrical (no bilateral larval stage mentioned in this chapter).
- Statement 2 ✓ — Echinoderms (starfish, sea urchin) are **radially symmetrical as adults** but **bilaterally symmetrical as larvae** — a classic evolutionary detail.
- Statement 3 ✓ — All vertebrates (Phylum Chordata) are bilaterally symmetrical — bilateral is a defining feature of higher animals.

**Trap:** Statement 1 is wrong — the larva-bilateral-then-radial story is specifically for *echinoderms*, not cnidarians. Many students mix these up.

**See also:** [[../../biology/14 - Symmetry#Quick Reference]]
</details>
