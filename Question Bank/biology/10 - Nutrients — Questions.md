# Nutrients — Questions

@qbio.nutrients.q1 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=10
Which macronutrient provides **9 calories per gram** when metabolised in the body?

- A) Carbohydrates
- B) Proteins
- C) Fats
- D) Vitamins
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Fats** provide **9 calories per gram** (more than double that of carbs/protein). Carbohydrates and proteins each provide **4 calories per gram**. Vitamins do not provide energy (0 calories).

**Trap:** A (carbohydrates) is the trap — carbs are the body's *primary* quick energy source, but fats are *denser* in energy per gram.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Fats]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q2 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=10
**Scurvy** — a disease characterised by bleeding gums, weakness, and poor wound healing — is caused by the deficiency of which vitamin?

- A) Vitamin A
- B) Vitamin B12
- C) Vitamin C
- D) Vitamin D
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Scurvy** is caused by deficiency of **vitamin C (ascorbic acid)**. It was historically common among sailors on long voyages (no fresh fruit). Sources of vitamin C include citrus fruits (lemon, orange), amla, strawberries, and spinach.

**Trap:** A (vitamin A) deficiency causes night blindness; D (vitamin D) deficiency causes rickets/osteomalacia; B12 deficiency causes pernicious anaemia — none cause scurvy.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Vitamin C]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q3 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=10
**Kwashiorkor** is a deficiency disease caused primarily by lack of:

- A) Calories (energy)
- B) Protein
- C) Vitamin C
- D) Iron
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Kwashiorkor** is caused by **severe protein deficiency** (even with adequate calories). It typically affects young children in developing countries and is characterised by a **swollen, distended abdomen** (due to fluid retention / oedema), skin lesions, and stunted growth. **Marasmus**, in contrast, is caused by deficiency of *both* calories and protein (general starvation) — leading to emaciation and wasting.

**Trap:** A (calories) is the trap for *Marasmus*, not Kwashiorkor.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Kwashiorkor]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q4 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=10
A child has soft, weak, deformed bones and bowed legs. The doctor diagnoses rickets. Which vitamin is deficient?

- A) Vitamin A
- B) Vitamin C
- C) Vitamin D
- D) Vitamin K
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Rickets** (in children) and **osteomalacia** (in adults) are caused by deficiency of **vitamin D** (calciferol), which is essential for calcium absorption and bone mineralisation. Vitamin D is called the "*sunshine vitamin*" because the skin synthesises it from sunlight (UV-B).

**Trap:** A (vitamin A) deficiency → night blindness; B (vitamin C) → scurvy; K → bleeding/clotting issues. None relate to bone mineralisation.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Vitamin D]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q5 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=10
A patient presents with **dermatitis, diarrhoea, and dementia** — the classic "3 D's". The doctor suspects a deficiency of:

- A) Vitamin B1 (thiamine)
- B) Vitamin B3 (niacin)
- C) Vitamin B12 (cyanocobalamin)
- D) Vitamin C (ascorbic acid)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B** — The "**3 D's**" — *D*ermatitis, *D*iarrhoea, *D*ementia — are the textbook triad of **pellagra**, caused by deficiency of **niacin (vitamin B3)**. If untreated, the fourth D (*D*eath) can follow. Pellagra was historically common in populations whose diet was based on maize (which contains bound, unabsorbable niacin unless treated with alkali).

- A ✗ — vitamin **B1 (thiamine)** deficiency causes **beriberi** (wet: cardiac oedema; dry: peripheral neuropathy) and Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome.
- C ✗ — vitamin **B12** deficiency causes **pernicious anaemia** with neurological symptoms (tingling, memory issues).
- D ✗ — vitamin **C** deficiency causes **scurvy** (bleeding gums, poor wound healing).

**Trap:** A and C both involve B-vitamin deficiencies that produce overlapping neurological features — but only niacin produces the full *3-D* dermatological-and-GI signature of pellagra.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Vitamin B Complex]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q6 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=10
Which of the following vitamins is **fat-soluble** and is stored in the body's fatty tissues and liver?

- A) Vitamin B1
- B) Vitamin C
- C) Vitamin B12
- D) Vitamin A
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

**Vitamin A** is one of the four **fat-soluble vitamins** (A, D, E, K) — stored in fatty tissues and liver. B-vitamins and vitamin C are water-soluble — not stored (excess is excreted in urine).

**Trap:** A, B, C are all water-soluble — easy to confuse because students think of "vitamins = one group", but solubility matters for storage and toxicity.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Types of Vitamins]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q7 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=10
Consider the following statements:
1. There are 20 amino acids, of which 9 are essential (must be obtained from diet).
2. The "9 essential amino acids" include histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, and valine.
3. All 20 amino acids can be synthesised by the human body.

Which is correct?

- A) Only statements 1 and 2 are true
- B) Only statements 1 and 3 are true
- C) Only statements 2 and 3 are true
- D) All three statements are true
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

- Statement 1 ✓ — There are 20 standard amino acids, of which **9 are essential** (the body cannot synthesise them and they must come from the diet).
- Statement 2 ✓ — The 9 essential amino acids are exactly those listed (commonly remembered by mnemonic or the table).
- Statement 3 ✗ — The other 11 are **non-essential** amino acids — they *can* be synthesised by the body, so dietary intake is not strictly required.

**Trap:** Statement 3 is wrong because "non-essential" means *the body can make them*, not that they're unimportant.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Amino Acids]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q8 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=10
The overall equation for photosynthesis is:

**6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (in presence of sunlight)**

Which organelle is the primary site of photosynthesis?

- A) Mitochondrion
- B) Ribosome
- C) Chloroplast
- D) Golgi apparatus
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Photosynthesis occurs in the **chloroplast** of plant cells (especially mesophyll cells of leaves). The light-dependent reactions happen in the **thylakoid membranes** (which stack into grana), and the light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle) happen in the **stroma**. Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light and reflects green light, which is why plants look green.

**Trap:** A (mitochondrion) is the trap — mitochondria perform *cellular respiration* (the opposite of photosynthesis). The plant cell does both.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Photosynthesis]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q9 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=10
**Cuscuta** (dodder) is a plant that lacks chlorophyll and wraps around other plants to draw nutrition from them. Cuscuta is therefore a:

- A) Autotroph
- B) Saprophyte
- C) Parasite
- D) Holozoic organism
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Cuscuta** is a **parasitic plant** — it derives nutrition *from another living plant* (the host). Because it lacks chlorophyll, it cannot make its own food (so it's not an autotroph). It draws nutrients from a *living* host, which distinguishes parasitism from saprophytism (feeding on dead organic matter).

**Trap:** B (saprophyte) is the trap — saprophytes feed on *dead* matter, not on *living* hosts.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Types]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=biology chapter=10
A student says: "Animals are heterotrophs, so they must always be parasitic." Is this correct?

- A) Yes — all heterotrophs are parasites
- B) No — heterotrophs include saprophytes (e.g. fungi), parasites (e.g. *Cuscuta*, *Plasmodium*), and holozoic organisms (e.g. humans, dogs)
- C) Yes — because heterotroph means "feeding on others"
- D) No — animals are autotrophs
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

"Heterotroph" simply means an organism that *cannot produce its own food* and obtains nutrients from other sources. Heterotrophs come in three flavours:
- **Saprophytic** (e.g. fungi, yeast) — feed on dead organic matter.
- **Parasitic** (e.g. *Cuscuta*, *Plasmodium*, nematodes) — feed on living hosts.
- **Holozoic** (e.g. humans, dogs, *Amoeba*) — ingest solid/liquid food and break it down internally.

**Trap:** A is wrong because *saprophytic* and *holozoic* heterotrophs are clearly *not* parasites. C confuses the *broad* category (heterotroph) with one of its sub-types (parasite).

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Types]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q11 difficulty=recall subject=biology chapter=10
Which mineral is essential for the formation of **thyroid hormone** and whose deficiency causes goitre?

- A) Iron
- B) Calcium
- C) Iodine
- D) Zinc
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

**Iodine** is a key component of **thyroid hormones** (T3, T4) which regulate metabolism, growth, and development. Iodine deficiency leads to hypothyroidism and can cause the thyroid gland to enlarge (goitre). Iodised salt is a major public-health intervention.

**Trap:** A (iron) deficiency causes anaemia; B (calcium) deficiency affects bones; D (zinc) affects wound healing and immunity. None relate to thyroid hormone.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Minerals]]
</details>

@qbio.nutrients.q12 difficulty=apply subject=biology chapter=10
Which of the following is a **micronutrient** (needed in small amounts) for plant growth?

- A) Nitrogen (N)
- B) Phosphorus (P)
- C) Potassium (K)
- D) Iron (Fe)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

**Micronutrients** (trace elements) for plants include **Fe (iron)**, Mn, Cu, Zn, B, Cl, Mo. They are needed in small amounts. **Macronutrients** include N, P, K, S, Ca, Mg, C, H, O — needed in large amounts.

**Trap:** A, B, C are all plant *macronutrients* (the famous NPK fertiliser trio) — needed in much larger quantities than iron.

**See also:** [[../../biology/10 - Nutrients#Micronutrients and Macronutrients in Plants]]
</details>
