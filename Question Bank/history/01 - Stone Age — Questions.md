# Stone Age — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md|Stone Age]]

@qhis.stone_age.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=1
The Old Stone Age is also known as:

- A) Palaeolithic
- B) Mesolithic
- C) Neolithic
- D) Chalcolithic
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Palaeolithic literally means "Old Stone Age" (palaeo = old, lithos = stone).

**Trap:** B looks tempting because "meso" means middle; students often confuse the prefix meaning. C and D are later phases with agriculture and copper use, respectively.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.stone_age.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=1
In which Stone Age phase did the domestication of animals and the beginnings of settled life first appear?

- A) Lower Palaeolithic
- B) Upper Palaeolithic
- C) Mesolithic
- D) Neolithic
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Mesolithic (Middle Stone Age) is the transitional phase where hunting-herding and early animal domestication began, before full-scale agriculture of the Neolithic.

**Trap:** D is the more famous "agricultural revolution" — students jump there. B is a common trap because microliths appeared in Upper Palaeolithic, not domestication.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.stone_age.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=1
Bhimbetka rock shelters, a famous prehistoric site in Madhya Pradesh, were discovered by:

- A) Mortimer Wheeler
- B) Robert Bruce Foote
- C) V. S. Wakankar
- D) H. D. Sankalia
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

V. S. Wakankar discovered the Bhimbetka rock shelters in 1957—58. They are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

**Trap:** A, B, and D are all real names of famous Indian archaeologists (Mohenjo-daro excavator, first Palaeolithic tool finder in India, and the Deccan College archaeologist respectively) — students confuse personalities.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.stone_age.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=1
Small, geometric stone tools called microliths are the hallmark of which period?

- A) Lower Palaeolithic
- B) Upper Palaeolithic
- C) Mesolithic
- D) Neolithic
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Microliths (tiny flint blades) characterize the Mesolithic phase and were often hafted onto bone or wooden handles to make composite tools.

**Trap:** Lower Palaeolithic is dominated by large hand-axes / cleavers (Acheulean), and Upper Palaeolithic by blade tools — both far larger than microliths.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.stone_age.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=1
A student reads that a prehistoric site yielded hand-axes, cleavers and choppers made of quartzite, with no pottery or polished stone. The site most likely belongs to:

- A) Mesolithic
- B) Neolithic
- C) Lower Palaeolithic
- D) Chalcolithic
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Large unflaked core tools (hand-axes, cleavers) define the Acheulean / Lower Palaeolithic, with no pottery or polished implements — those appear only later.

**Trap:** B and D are easy traps because the student hears "prehistoric tools" and assumes Neolithic/Chalcolithic. A is ruled out by the absence of microliths.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.stone_age.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=1
Which Indian site is the type-site for the Soanian industry of the Lower Palaeolithic?

- A) Bhimbetka
- B) Soan Valley (Pakistan)
- C) Nevasa
- D) Bagor
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Soanian industry is named after the Soan Valley in the Potwar plateau of Pakistan, where these pebble-tool artefacts were first identified.

**Trap:** A, C and D are real Indian prehistoric sites with different industries (Bhimbetka = Mesolithic—historical; Nevasa = Chalcolithic; Bagor = Mesolithic).

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.stone_age.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=1
The painted rock-shelter art at Bhimbetka spans which approximate time range?

- A) Only Upper Palaeolithic
- B) Mesolithic to historic (roughly 8000 BCE to medieval times)
- C) Only Chalcolithic
- D) Only Indus Valley period
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Bhimbetka's painted shelters contain Mesolithic and Chalcolithic art continuing into early historic and even medieval phases, making it one of the longest sequences of rock art in India.

**Trap:** A is tempting because students associate "stone age art" with Upper Palaeolithic cave art (e.g., Lascaux). C and D are far too narrow for this multi-period site.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.stone_age.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=1
The Bagor site in Rajasthan is best known for which of the following?

- A) Lower Palaeolithic hand-axes
- B) Chalcolithic copper tools
- C) Largest Mesolithic habitation site in India with evidence of animal domestication
- D) Indus Valley seals
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Bagor (Bhilwara, Rajasthan) is the largest Mesolithic habitation site in the Indian subcontinent, with evidence of domesticated cattle, sheep and goats, plus chert-tool making and bead-work.

**Trap:** A is a "stone age" default; B and D tempt students by inserting familiar later categories into a Mesolithic site description.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.stone_age.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=1
Which of the following statements is INCORRECT about the Indian Stone Age?

- A) The Palaeolithic sites in India are largely surface finds on river terraces
- B) Indian Lower Palaeolithic tools include hand-axes, cleavers and choppers
- C) The Mesolithic economy was based purely on settled agriculture
- D) Burial practices with grave goods appear at some Mesolithic sites
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Mesolithic economy was based on hunting, fishing and gathering with incipient animal domestication — NOT settled agriculture (which is the hallmark of the Neolithic).

**Trap:** A is true of how Palaeolithic sites are typically found. B is textbook. D is true (e.g., Mahadaha, Damdama). Students often miss the agriculture distinction between Meso and Neolithic.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.stone_age.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=1
The transition from food-gathering to food-producing economy in India is most closely associated with which period?

- A) Upper Palaeolithic
- B) Mesolithic
- C) Neolithic—Chalcolithic
- D) Iron Age
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Neolithic—Chalcolithic phase saw the rise of settled agriculture, domestication of plants (wheat, barley, rice) and animals, and the first permanent villages.

**Trap:** B is a tempting trap because Mesolithic saw incipient domestication of animals, but the full food-producing economy belongs to Neolithic. D post-dates and does not define the transition.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.stone_age.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=1
Match the following periods to their characteristic tool-types:

| Period | Tool-type |
|---|---|
| 1. Lower Palaeolithic | a. Microliths |
| 2. Mesolithic | b. Hand-axes / cleavers |
| 3. Neolithic | c. Polished stone axes |

- A) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- B) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- D) 1-b, 2-c, 3-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

1-b (Lower Palaeolithic = hand-axes / cleavers / Acheulean), 2-a (Mesolithic = microliths), 3-c (Neolithic = ground / polished stone axes).

**Trap:** D reverses Mesolithic and Neolithic; B mixes up Palaeolithic and Mesolithic; C scrambles all three.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.stone_age.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=1
Consider the following statements about Indian prehistory:

1. The Acheulean industry is named after a site in France.
2. The Indian Mesolithic site of Langhnaj in Gujarat has yielded evidence of burials.
3. Adamgarh hill near Hoshangabad has both Palaeolithic tools and Mesolithic paintings.

Which of the above are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

1 is correct (Saint-Acheul, France). 2 is correct (Langhnaj has burial evidence). 3 is correct (Adamgarh has both Palaeolithic artefacts and Mesolithic rock paintings).

**Trap:** A and B try to drop a true statement on the assumption that "only" some are true. C drops statement 2, which students often overlook because Mesolithic burial evidence is a niche fact.

**See also:** [[../../history/01 - Stone Age.md#Content|Stone Age — source chapter]]
</details>
