# Delhi Sultanate — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md|Delhi Sultanate]]

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=12
The First Battle of Tarain (1191) was fought between Prithviraj Chauhan and:

- A) Muhammad Ghori
- B) Qutb-ud-din Aibak
- C) Iltutmish
- D) Genghis Khan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The First Battle of Tarain (1191) was fought between the Rajput king Prithviraj Chauhan of Ajmer and the Ghurid invader Muhammad Ghori; Prithviraj won this battle. Muhammad Ghori returned to defeat Prithviraj in the Second Battle of Tarain (1192), which effectively opened the way for Muslim rule in north India. Aibak was Ghori's commander and later founded the Slave Dynasty; Iltutmish was Aibak's successor; Genghis Khan was a Central Asian conqueror with no role at Tarain.

**Trap:** Option B (Aibak) and C (Iltutmish) confuse the general with the dynasty's founder and successor. Genghis Khan is a tempting distractor for any 12th-century Central Asian context but is unrelated.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=12
Who was the founder of the Slave Dynasty?

- A) Iltutmish
- B) Balban
- C) Qutb-ud-din Aibak
- D) Alauddin Khalji
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Qutb-ud-din Aibak (r. 1206-1210 CE), a former slave-general of Muhammad Ghori, became the first Sultan of Delhi and founded the Slave (Mamluk) Dynasty after Ghori's assassination in 1206. He was followed by Aram Shah and then Iltutmish, who made the Sultanate stable and hereditary in his own line. Balban (r. 1266-1287 CE) ended the Slave Dynasty; Alauddin Khalji was a Khalji Dynasty ruler.

**Trap:** Iltutmish is often credited as the dynasty's effective consolidator, but the founder was Aibak. Balban is a late Slave Dynasty ruler, not its founder.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=12
Qutb-ud-din Aibak was given the title:

- A) Sipahsalar
- B) Lakh Baksh (giver of lakhs)
- C) Tuti-Hind
- D) Saif-i-Sultani
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Qutb-ud-din Aibak earned the title Lakh Baksh ("giver of lakhs") for his reputed generosity on the battlefield of the Second Battle of Tarain (1192), where tradition says he scattered gold from his war-tent. Sipahsalar was a title used by Balban. Tuti-Hind ("parrot of India") was a title associated with Amir Khusrau. Saif-i-Sultani was a title given to Muhammad bin Tughlaq.

**Trap:** Balban's Sipahsalar and Muhammad bin Tughlaq's Saif-i-Sultani are both real Sultanate-era titles, so distractors A and D confuse Aibak with later Sultans.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=12
The last ruler of the Delhi Sultanate was:

- A) Firoz Shah Tughlaq
- B) Ibrahim Lodi
- C) Sikandar Lodi
- D) Bahlul Lodi
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Ibrahim Lodi (r. 1517-1526 CE) was the last ruler of the Delhi Sultanate; he was defeated and killed by Babur at the First Battle of Panipat (1526), which marked the end of the Sultanate and the start of Mughal rule. Firoz Shah Tughlaq ruled much earlier (r. 1351-1388 CE); Sikandar Lodi and Bahlul Lodi preceded Ibrahim within the Lodi Dynasty itself.

**Trap:** Firoz Shah Tughlaq is the most famous Tughlaq ruler for reforms, but the Sultanate ended nearly 140 years after him, with Ibrahim Lodi.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=12
A student reads that a Sultan introduced "market control" regulations, fixed prices, and a network of spies, and his wealth financed a large standing army. The Sultan being described is:

- A) Alauddin Khalji
- B) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
- C) Balban
- D) Firoz Shah Tughlaq
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Alauddin Khalji (r. 1296-1316 CE) introduced sweeping market-control (hisbah) regulations in Delhi: he fixed the prices of grains, cloth and other essentials, appointed officers (diwan-i-riyasat) and a network of spies to enforce them, and used the resulting revenue surplus to maintain a large standing army at low cost. These price-control and espionage measures are a hallmark of his reign and appear in Amir Khusrau's account. Muhammad bin Tughlaq is famous for ambitious but failed projects (token currency, capital transfer), not market control. Balban centralised royal authority and the espionage system but did not run commodity price-fixing on Alauddin's scale. Firoz Shah Tughlaq was known for welfare measures, not market control.

**Trap:** Distractors include Sultans famous for one big reform each, but the specific combination of price-fixing, spies and army-financing is uniquely Alauddin Khalji's.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=12
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the Delhi Sultanate?

- A) Alauddin Khalji was the first Sultan to issue "Sikka" (coinage)
- B) Muhammad bin Tughlaq's token currency was withdrawn after public protest
- C) Firoz Shah Tughlaq established a department of agriculture and canals
- D) Balban introduced the theory of "narcotic kingship" / divine right of kings
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Statement A is INCORRECT: coins called "sikka" were issued in Delhi from much earlier — Iltutmish issued the first silver tanka and introduced a standard Sultanate coinage, and copper "jital" coins were struck even earlier. Alauddin Khalji did not issue the first sikka; he reformed and standardised the currency during his market-control drive, but he was not its originator. Statements B, C and D are all correct: Muhammad bin Tughlaq's token (copper) currency had to be withdrawn after widespread forgery and public rejection; Firoz Shah Tughlaq set up a department of agriculture, dug canals and founded many towns; and Balban elevated royal pomp and the theory of "narcotic kingship" (divine right) to strengthen monarchical authority.

**Trap:** Students know Alauddin Khalji for currency-related actions, but those were reforms of an existing system, not its invention. Recognising Iltutmish as the founder of the Sultanate's standard coinage is the key to spotting the wrong statement.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=12
Which of the following correctly matches a Sultan with an event?

- A) Muhammad bin Tughlaq — founded Hauz Khas
- B) Alauddin Khalji — built Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque
- C) Qutb-ud-din Aibak — started construction of Qutub Minar
- D) Iltutmish — introduced Iqta system
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Qutb-ud-din Aibak began construction of the Qutub Minar at Mehrauli, Delhi, in 1199 CE (and the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque), and Iltutmish completed the Minar and added further storeys. The Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque was begun by Aibak, not Alauddin Khalji (option B swaps the builder). Hauz Khas as the famous complex (madrasa, mosque and tomb) was developed later — the tank was excavated under Alauddin Khalji as Hauz-i-Alai, but the surviving complex is essentially Firoz Shah Tughlaq's work, not Muhammad bin Tughlaq's (so option A is wrong). The Iqta system is most often credited to Iltutmish, not "introduced" here as a special novelty of his; it was shaped by him but his pairing with Aibak/Qutub Minar makes option C the cleanest match.

**Trap:** Options A and B both shuffle correct attributes across wrong rulers, the typical way such match questions test students who half-remember the attributions.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=12
Match the following:

| Sultan | Event |
|---|---|
| 1. Iltutmish | a. Introduced Iqta system |
| 2. Balban | b. Banned music and wine at court |
| 3. Muhammad bin Tughlaq | c. Token currency |

- A) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- B) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- C) 1-a, 2-c, 3-b
- D) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Iltutmish (r. 1211-1236 CE) refined and gave the Iqta (military land-revenue) system its classic, institutional form in the Delhi Sultanate (1-a). Balban, following Persian court models, imposed strict discipline at his court — banning music, wine, levity and other forms of indulgence to project royal gravity (2-b). Muhammad bin Tughlaq (r. 1325-1351 CE) introduced the ambitious token (copper) currency, pegged to the silver tanka, which had to be withdrawn after forgery and public unrest (3-c). All three pairings in option A are historically correct.

**Trap:** Option B misplaces the Iqta; option C assigns the token currency to Balban (it was Muhammad bin Tughlaq, not Balban); option D scrambles all three.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=12
The Sultan who shifted his capital from Delhi to Daulatabad was:

- A) Alauddin Khalji
- B) Firoz Shah Tughlaq
- C) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
- D) Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Muhammad bin Tughlaq (r. 1325-1351 CE) ordered the transfer of his capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (modern Aurangabad in the Deccan) around 1326-1327 CE, supposedly to safeguard against Mongol raids and to control the south better. The forced march of Delhi's populace southward caused immense suffering and the experiment failed; the capital was eventually moved back. Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq, his father, founded the Tughlaqabad city near Delhi; Alauddin Khalji was based in Delhi; Firoz Shah Tughlaq also ruled from Delhi (with a brief sojourn at Jaunpur).

**Trap:** Distractors A, B and D were all real Delhi-based Sultans, but only Muhammad bin Tughlaq made the dramatic capital-shift to Daulatabad.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=12
Arrange in chronological order:

1. Slave Dynasty
2. Khilji Dynasty
3. Tughlaq Dynasty
4. Sayyid Dynasty
5. Lodi Dynasty

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
- B) 2, 1, 3, 5, 4
- C) 1, 2, 5, 3, 4
- D) 1, 3, 2, 5, 4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct chronological order of the five dynasties of the Delhi Sultanate is:

1. Slave (Mamluk) Dynasty (1206-1290 CE)
2. Khilji Dynasty (1290-1320 CE)
3. Tughlaq Dynasty (1320-1414 CE)
4. Sayyid Dynasty (1414-1451 CE)
5. Lodi Dynasty (1451-1526 CE), ending at Panipat (1526).

Option B reverses Slave and Khilji; option C places Lodi before Tughlaq; option D places Tughlaq before Khilji — all three errors violate the well-established chronology.

**Trap:** The Sayyid and Lodi dynasties were both short 15th-century lines and are easy to swap. Remember that the Lodi Dynasty, though short, is the LAST dynasty before the Sultanate ended.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=12
Consider the statements:

1. The Delhi Sultanate reached its territorial peak under Alauddin Khalji.
2. Balban's "blood and iron" policy strengthened the frontier.
3. The Sultanate bureaucracy used Persian as its court language.

Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. (1) Under Alauddin Khalji the Sultanate stretched from the edge of the Himalayas in the north to the far south (Tamil country), covering its greatest land area. (2) Ghiyasuddin and especially Balban followed a "blood and iron" policy on the north-western frontier, dealing firmly with Mongol incursions and restoring internal order by harsh punishments. (3) Persian was the official court and administrative language of the Delhi Sultanate (and of the Mughals after them), used for official records, farmans and literature; the Sultanate bureaucracy was modelled on Turko-Persian lines.

**Trap:** Statement 1 is sometimes downplayed because Alauddin's market reforms are more famous than his conquests; yet his territorial reach was the Sultanate's largest. Statement 3's Persian is sometimes confused with Arabic — but Persian, not Arabic, was the working language of the chancery.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.delhi_sultanate.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=12
The "Hauz Khas" complex (tank, mosque, madrasa, tombs) as it stands today was essentially built by:

- A) Qutb-ud-din Aibak
- B) Alauddin Khalji
- C) Firoz Shah Tughlaq
- D) Sikandar Lodi
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Hauz Khas complex in its surviving form was built by Firoz Shah Tughlaq (r. 1351-1388 CE). Firoz Shah re-excavated and enlarged the old water tank that Alauddin Khalji had originally dug as Hauz-i-Alai to supply water to the Siri Fort area, and around it built the grand madrasa, the mosque, his own tomb complex and the pavilions that survive today. Alauddin Khalji therefore started the tank but did not build the famous Hauz Khas buildings; Aibak predates the whole site; Sikandar Lodi built nothing here.

**Trap:** Alauddin Khalji's Hauz-i-Alai (the original reservoir) and Firoz Shah's Hauz Khas (the complex around it) are easily confused because the tank is the same piece of water. The buildings around it, however, are Firoz Shah's work.

**See also:** [[../../history/12 - Delhi Sultanate.md#Content|Delhi Sultanate — source chapter]]
</details>
