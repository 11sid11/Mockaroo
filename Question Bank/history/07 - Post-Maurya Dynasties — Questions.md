@qhis.post_maurya_dynasties.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=7
The Shunga dynasty was founded by:

- A) Pushyamitra Shunga
- B) Kanishka
- C) Menander
- D) Gautamiputra Satakarni
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Pushyamitra Shunga, the commander-in-chief (senapati) of the last Maurya ruler Brihadratha, assassinated him around 185 BCE and established the Shunga dynasty in Magadha.

**Trap:** B (Kanishka) was a Kushana emperor, C (Menander) was an Indo-Greek king, and D (Gautamiputra Satakarni) was a Satavahana ruler. All four belong to the broader post-Maurya period, but only Pushyamitra founded the Shungas.

**See also:** [[../../history/07 - Post-Maurya Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Maurya Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_maurya_dynasties.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=7
The Indo-Greek king who converted to Buddhism after dialogues with the monk Nagasena was:

- A) Demetrius
- B) Menander (Milinda)
- C) Apollodotus
- D) Eucratides
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Menander (Milinda in Pali) is the Indo-Greek ruler famous for his conversion to Buddhism following philosophical dialogues with the monk Nagasena; the text of those dialogues is recorded in the Milindapanha.

**Trap:** Demetrius and Apollodotus were also Indo-Greek kings but are not associated with Buddhist conversion. Eucratides was a Greek ruler of Bactria.

**See also:** [[../../history/07 - Post-Maurya Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Maurya Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_maurya_dynasties.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=7
The Saka Era was started by:

- A) Vikramaditya
- B) Kanishka
- C) Rudradaman I
- D) Chandragupta II
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Saka Era (beginning in 78 CE) is traditionally associated with the accession of Kanishka and is used widely in official Indian epigraphy and the Indian national calendar.

**Trap:** A (Vikramaditya) is associated with the Vikrama Era of 58 BCE. C (Rudradaman I) was a famous Saka ruler of western India but did not start the era. D (Chandragupta II) belongs to the Gupta period.

**See also:** [[../../history/07 - Post-Maurya Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Maurya Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_maurya_dynasties.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=7
The capital of the Satavahana dynasty was:

- A) Pataliputra
- B) Vidisha
- C) Paithan (Pratisthan)
- D) Nashik
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Satavahana capital was Paithan, also called Pratisthan, in Maharashtra on the banks of the Godavari; it is repeatedly named in Puranic lists and the Ajanta inscriptions.

**Trap:** A is the Mauryan capital. B (Vidisha) and D (Nashik) are important Satavahana sites and findspots of Satavahana inscriptions (e.g. Nanaghat near Nashik), but neither was the capital.

**See also:** [[../../history/07 - Post-Maurya Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Maurya Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_maurya_dynasties.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=7
A student reads that a post-Maurya ruler performed two Ashvamedha sacrifices and was a patron of Sanskrit learning. The ruler being described is:

- A) Pushyamitra Shunga
- B) Gautamiputra Satakarni
- C) Kanishka
- D) Samudragupta
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Pushyamitra Shunga performed two Ashvamedha sacrifices (referenced in the Ayodhya and other inscriptions) and is credited with reviving Brahmanical Sanskrit learning after the Mauryan Buddhist leanings.

**Trap:** B (Gautamiputra Satakarni) performed Vedic sacrifices but his dynasty is most noted for its coinage and Andhra identity. C (Kanishka) was a Buddhist patron (Fourth Council). D (Samudragupta) of the Guptas performed the Ashvamedha but is not post-Maurya.

**See also:** [[../../history/07 - Post-Maurya Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Maurya Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_maurya_dynasties.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=7
The "Charitrasaras" (Scythian chief ministers) and the devapala-shahi/kshatrapa system are associated with which ruling group?

- A) Indo-Greeks
- B) Shakas
- C) Kushanas
- D) Satavahanas
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Shakas (Scythians) are associated with the Charitrasaras (chief ministers), devapala-shahi title, and the kshatrapa (kshatrapa) system of governance in western and north-western India.

**Trap:** The Indo-Greeks used Hellenistic titles (basileus, strategos), the Kushanas used maharaja/raja titles and provincial governors differently, and the Satavahanas used the amti/mahatalavara system — none of those map onto the Saka-specific vocabulary.

**See also:** [[../../history/07 - Post-Maurya Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Maurya Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_maurya_dynasties.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=7
Consider the statements about the Satavahanas:

1. They were the first Indian rulers to issue lead and copper coins.
2. Their official language was Prakrit; the Nanaghat and Nasik inscriptions are in Prakrit.
3. They revived Vedic Brahmanism and persecuted Buddhism.

Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Statements 1 and 2 are correct — the Satavahanas were notable issuers of lead (and potin / copper) coins and their early official language was Prakrit, attested in the Nanaghat and Nasik cave inscriptions. Statement 3 is INCORRECT — the Satavahanas were tolerant of Buddhism; their patronage of Buddhist cave complexes at Nasik, Ajanta and Karla is direct evidence against persecution.

**Trap:** B and D both accept statement 3; many textbook descriptions highlight the Satavahanas as Brahmanical revivalists, but their epigraphic record shows active Buddhist patronage.

**See also:** [[../../history/07 - Post-Maurya Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Maurya Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_maurya_dynasties.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=7
Who among the following is known as the "Second Ashoka" for his Buddhist propagation?

- A) Chandragupta Maurya
- B) Harshavardhana
- C) Kanishka
- D) Samudragupta
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Kanishka is often called the "Second Ashoka" because of his vast patronage of Buddhism across Central Asia, his convening of the Fourth Buddhist Council, and his support for Mahayana Buddhism and the spread of Buddhist art along the Silk Road.

**Trap:** A (Chandragupta Maurya) is the founder of the Mauryan empire itself, not the second Ashoka. B (Harshavardhana) was a Buddhist patron of northern India but is more typically called the last great Hindu emperor. D (Samudragupta) performed the Ashvamedha but is famous as the "Napoleon of India", not as a Buddhist missionary.

**See also:** [[../../history/07 - Post-Maurya Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Maurya Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>
