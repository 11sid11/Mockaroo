@qhis.gandhian_era.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=21
In which year did Gandhi return to India from South Africa?

- A) 9 January 1915
- B) 1916
- C) 1917
- D) 1919
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi returned to India from South Africa on 9 January 1915, arriving at the Apollo Bunder in Bombay. He had spent roughly 21 years in South Africa (1893-1914), where he had developed his techniques of satyagraha and his political philosophy of non-violent resistance while campaigning for the rights of the Indian diaspora in the Transvaal and Natal. On his return, he was received as a hero of the Indian community, settled initially at Santiniketan with Tagore, and shortly afterwards established the Sabarmati Ashram near Ahmedabad. His first major public campaigns in India began with the Champaran and Ahmedabad mill strikes of 1917-18.

**Trap:** B (1916) is one year late. C (1917) is the year of the Champaran Satyagraha, not of his return. D (1919) is the year of the Jallianwala Bagh massacre and the Rowlatt Act agitation, well after his return.

**See also:** [[../../history/21 - Gandhian Era.md#Content|Gandhian Era — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gandhian_era.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=21
The Champaran Satyagraha (1917) was related to:

- A) Indigo planters' Tinkathia system
- B) Salt tax
- C) Rowlatt Act
- D) Textile workers' strike
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Champaran Satyagraha of 1917 was Gandhi's first satyagraha campaign inside India. It was launched in the Champaran district of Bihar against the European indigo planters who forced the peasants to grow indigo on three-twentieths (3/20, or "tinkathia") of their holdings as a condition of tenancy. Gandhi undertook an inquiry, was arrested for refusing to leave, and the case ultimately led to the appointment of a Champaran Agrarian Enquiry Committee (with Gandhi as a member) which abolished the tinkathia system.

**Trap:** B (salt tax) is the issue of the 1930 Salt March / Dandi March. C (Rowlatt Act) is the issue of the 1919 anti-Rowlatt agitation. D (textile workers' strike) refers to the 1918 Ahmedabad mill strike, which was a separate Gandhian campaign of the same early period, but not Champaran.

**See also:** [[../../history/21 - Gandhian Era.md#Content|Gandhian Era — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gandhian_era.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=21
The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place at:

- A) Delhi
- B) Amritsar on 13 April 1919
- C) Lahore
- D) Bombay
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Jallianwala Bagh massacre occurred on 13 April 1919 (the day of the Punjabi New Year festival of Baisakhi) at the walled garden Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar, Punjab. Brigadier-General Reginald Edward Harry Dyer ordered his troops (the Gurkha 1/3rd and the 2/10th Baluchis) to fire without warning on an unarmed crowd that had gathered for a peaceful protest meeting against the Rowlatt Act and the arrest of Satyapal and Saifuddin Kitchlew. Official British figures cited 379 dead; Indian estimates placed the death toll at over 1,000. Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood in protest, and Gandhi devoted himself to non-cooperation.

**Trap:** A (Delhi) is the seat of the imperial capital but not the site of the massacre. C (Lahore) was the capital of Punjab and was a politically active city, but the actual shooting was at Amritsar. D (Bombay) is the city of Gandhi's return from South Africa in 1915, not the location of the massacre.

**See also:** [[../../history/21 - Gandhian Era.md#Content|Gandhian Era — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gandhian_era.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=21
The Chauri Chaura incident (1922) led to the withdrawal of:

- A) The Non-Cooperation Movement
- B) The Civil Disobedience Movement
- C) The Quit India Movement
- D) The Khilafat Movement
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

On 5 February 1922, a large mob of peasants attacked and burned the police station at Chauri Chaura in the Gorakhpur district of the United Provinces, killing 22 policemen. The violent incident horrified Gandhi, who believed that the Indian masses had not yet developed the discipline required for non-violent mass struggle. On 12 February 1922, the Working Committee of the Indian National Congress suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement nationwide, and Gandhi himself was arrested in March 1922 and charged with sedition.

**Trap:** B (Civil Disobedience Movement) was launched only in 1930, years after the Chauri Chaura withdrawal. C (Quit India Movement) was launched in August 1942, two decades later. D (Khilafat Movement) was a separate, primarily Muslim agitation (1919-1924) about the fate of the Ottoman Caliphate; it overlapped with Non-Cooperation but was not the movement withdrawn because of Chauri Chaura.

**See also:** [[../../history/21 - Gandhian Era.md#Content|Gandhian Era — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gandhian_era.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=21
A student reads that Gandhi called for the boycotting of British institutions, courts, schools and titles, and that the movement was withdrawn after a violent incident. The movement being described is:

- A) The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)
- B) The Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-34)
- C) The Quit India Movement (1942)
- D) The Khilafat Movement
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The description matches the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-1922). Gandhi, at the special session of the Congress at Calcutta in September 1920 and the regular session at Nagpur in December 1920, launched a programme that included (a) surrender of British titles and honours, (b) boycott of British courts, schools and colleges, (c) boycott of foreign cloth and promotion of khadi, (d) renunciation of government employment, and (e) the constructive programme of Hindu-Muslim unity and removal of untouchability. The movement was withdrawn in February 1922 after the violent mob attack at the Chauri Chaura police station.

**Trap:** B (Civil Disobedience Movement, 1930-34) was triggered by the Salt March and centred on breaking specific laws (salt, forest, foreign cloth) rather than the broad non-cooperation programme. C (Quit India Movement, 1942) called for immediate British withdrawal (do or die) and was not withdrawn but suppressed. D (Khilafat Movement) was the pan-Islamic agitation over the dismemberment of the Ottoman Empire and was not itself called off because of a violent incident.

**See also:** [[../../history/21 - Gandhian Era.md#Content|Gandhian Era — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gandhian_era.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=21
The Nagpur flag of the Indian National Congress (1931) had:

- A) Saffron, white and green with a spinning wheel (charkha)
- B) Only saffron
- C) Blue and red
- D) White and saffron only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

At the 1931 Karachi session of the Indian National Congress, a flag committee formally adopted the national flag in its tricolour form: saffron (top), white (middle) and green (bottom) horizontal bands, with a spinning wheel (charkha) drawn in dark blue on the white band. The version usually described as the "Nagpur flag" had been in de facto use from 1923 onwards (Pingali Venkayya's design, presented at a Congress gathering at Bezwada and later confirmed at Nagpur), and the 1931 committee action merely formalised what had already become the de facto national flag. The flag committee was chaired by Rajendra Prasad, with members including Jawaharlal Nehru, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad and Sarojini Naidu.

**Trap:** B (only saffron) is wrong: the flag has always been tricolour (saffron-white-green) since the 1921-23 design. C (blue and red) describes neither the 1931 flag nor any prior version. D (white and saffron only) omits the green band and the charkha, which were both essential components of the 1931 design.

**See also:** [[../../history/21 - Gandhian Era.md#Content|Gandhian Era — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gandhian_era.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=21
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the Gandhian Era?

- A) The Khilafat Movement was a pan-Islamic agitation against the dismemberment of the Ottoman Empire
- B) The Simon Commission (1928) was welcomed by Indians as a step toward reform
- C) The Salt March of 1930 began at Sabarmati Ashram and ended at Dandi
- D) The Round Table Conferences were held in London between 1930 and 1932
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Statement B is incorrect. The Simon Commission (chaired by Sir John Simon) was NOT welcomed by Indians. It arrived in India in February 1928 and was immediately and unanimously boycotted by all sections of the Indian political spectrum (including the Congress, the Muslim League, the Hindu Mahasabha and the Liberals) for the simple reason that it had no Indian member. The commission was set up to review the operation of the Government of India Act of 1919, and Indians demanded that an Indian representative sit on the commission that would frame further constitutional reform. The slogan "Simon go back" became a rallying cry of the boycott; Lala Lajpat Rai was lathi-charged in Lahore in October 1928 and died shortly afterwards from injuries related to that police action.

**Trap:** A, C and D are all correct factual statements about the Gandhian Era: A correctly characterises the Khilafat Movement as a pan-Islamic protest against the dismemberment of the Ottoman Empire after the First World War; C correctly identifies Sabarmati Ashram as the starting point and Dandi (a coastal village in Gujarat) as the endpoint of the 1930 Salt March; D correctly states that three Round Table Conferences were held in London (the first in November 1930 - January 1931, the second in September - December 1931, and the third in November - December 1932). Any of A, C or D would be a wrong choice; B is the only incorrect statement.

**See also:** [[../../history/21 - Gandhian Era.md#Content|Gandhian Era — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gandhian_era.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=21
Match the following events with their years:

| Event | Year |
|---|---|
| 1. Champaran Satyagraha | a. 1922 |
| 2. Chauri Chaura incident | b. 1917 |
| 3. Salt March (Dandi March) | c. 1930 |

- A) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- B) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- D) 1-b, 2-c, 3-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct matching is 1-b, 2-a, 3-c. (1) The Champaran Satyagraha was launched in 1917, so event 1 matches year (b). (2) The Chauri Chaura incident (the mob attack on the police station that prompted Gandhi to withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement) occurred on 5 February 1922, so event 2 matches year (a). (3) The Salt March began from Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March 1930 and concluded at Dandi on 6 April 1930, so event 3 matches year (c) 1930.

**Trap:** B swaps Champaran and Chauri Chaura (placing Champaran in 1922 and Chauri Chaura in 1917). C reverses the chronology of Champaran and Chauri Chaura. D places the Salt March in 1917 and Chauri Chaura in 1930 — both clearly wrong, since the Salt March could not have preceded the Champaran Satyagraha and Chauri Chaura is firmly dated to February 1922.

@qhis.gandhian_era.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=21
The Khilafat Movement of 1919 was led jointly by:

- A) Gandhi and Maulana Abul Kalam Azad
- B) Ali brothers (Shaukat and Muhammad) and Gandhi
- C) Sir Muhammad Iqbal and Jinnah
- D) Rabindranath Tagore and Gandhi
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Khilafat Movement (1919-1924) was the pan-Islamic agitation launched by the Ali brothers — Maulana Muhammad Ali Johar and Maulana Shaukat Ali — along with other Muslim leaders, and was given broader political direction and an all-India platform by Mahatma Gandhi's hand-in-hand alliance of the Khilafat cause with the Non-Cooperation Movement (the so-called "Hindu-Muslim entente" of 1920-22). Gandhi associated himself with the movement because he saw it as a way to build Hindu-Muslim unity against the British and as a means of providing a peaceful outlet for Muslim anger over the post-war dismemberment of the Ottoman Empire and the threatened abolition of the Caliphate by the Treaty of Sevres.

**Trap:** A is wrong because Maulana Abul Kalam Azad was a leading Congressman and a prominent supporter of Khilafat, but he was not one of the two principal leaders who launched the movement. C is wrong because Sir Muhammad Iqbal and Muhammad Ali Jinnah were associated with the Muslim League's constitutional politics, not with the Khilafat agitation. D is wrong because Rabindranath Tagore was a vocal critic of political extremism (he returned his knighthood after Jallianwala Bagh but did not join either Khilafat or Non-Cooperation) and never led the Khilafat Movement.

**See also:** [[../../history/21 - Gandhian Era.md#Content|Gandhian Era — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gandhian_era.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=21
Arrange the following events in chronological order:

1. Champaran Satyagraha
2. Rowlatt Act agitation (and Jallianwala Bagh)
3. Chauri Chaura incident
4. Salt March (Dandi March)

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 2, 1, 3, 4
- C) 1, 2, 4, 3
- D) 4, 1, 2, 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct chronological order is 1-2-3-4 (Champaran 1917 -> Rowlatt Act agitation / Jallianwala Bagh 1919 -> Chauri Chaura Feb 1922 -> Salt March 1930). The Champaran Satyagraha was Gandhi's first campaign in India, in 1917. The Rowlatt Act was passed in March 1919, the Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place on 13 April 1919, and the anti-Rowlatt agitation swept the Punjab in 1919. The Chauri Chaura incident occurred on 5 February 1922 and led to the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation. The Salt March began on 12 March 1930 and ended at Dandi on 6 April 1930.

**Trap:** B places the Rowlatt agitation before Champaran, reversing 1919 and 1917. C places the Salt March before Chauri Chaura (i.e. 1930 before 1922). D places the Salt March first of all, which is impossible because it postdates Champaran by thirteen years.

</details>
@qhis.gandhian_era.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=21
Consider the statements:

1. The Indian Councils Act of 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms) introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
2. The Rowlatt Act of 1919 authorized detention without trial.
3. Gandhi's Harijan movement campaigned against untouchability.

Which of the above are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. (1) The Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909 did introduce separate electorates for Muslims (a system whereby Muslim voters in a constituency would elect Muslim representatives only) — this was the price paid by Viceroy Lord Minto and Secretary of State John Morley to win Muslim League support for the constitutional package. The separate electorate principle was a precursor to the later communal award logic. (2) The Rowlatt Act (officially the Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act of 1919) authorised detention without trial and trial in camera, on the recommendation of the Rowlatt Committee; it provoked all-India opposition, the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, and was a catalyst for Gandhi's emergence as a mass leader. (3) Gandhi founded the Harijan Sevak Sangh in 1932 to campaign against untouchability, the weekly journal "Harijan" ran from 1933, and the campaign for temple entry and the right of Harijans to use public wells, roads and schools was a major Gandhian constructive programme in the 1930s.

**Trap:** A drops statement 3, but the anti-untouchability campaign is one of Gandhi's most famous programmes. B drops statement 1, but the 1909 separate-electroates provision is a textbook item. C drops statement 2, but the detention-without-trial provision of the Rowlatt Act is precisely what made the law so controversial. The only choice that captures all three correct statements is D.

**See also:** [[../../history/21 - Gandhian Era.md#Content|Gandhian Era — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gandhian_era.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=21
The famous "Dandi March" began at:

- A) Chittagong
- B) Sabarmati Ashram (12 March 1930)
- C) Wardha
- D) Sabarmati Ashram in 1915
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Salt March (also called the Dandi March or the Salt Satyagraha) began on 12 March 1930 from the Sabarmati Ashram near Ahmedabad in Gujarat, with 78 satyagrahis led by Mahatma Gandhi himself. The march covered approximately 240 miles (about 385 km) over 24 days, ending at the coastal village of Dandi on 6 April 1930, where Gandhi symbolically broke the British salt monopoly by picking up a handful of natural salt from the seashore. The march violated the 1882 Salt Act, which prohibited the manufacture of salt by Indians, and triggered the Civil Disobedience Movement of 1930-34.

**Trap:** A (Chittagong) is the site of the 1930 Chittagong armoury raid by Surya Sen's revolutionary group, an event of the same period but unrelated to the Salt March. C (Wardha) was the location of Gandhi's ashram in Maharashtra from 1936 onwards (after he returned from prison following the Round Table Conferences) and was not the start of the Salt March. D (Sabarmati Ashram in 1915) gets the location right but the year wrong: the Sabarmati Ashram was established in 1917 (Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915), and the Salt March took place in 1930, not 1915.
