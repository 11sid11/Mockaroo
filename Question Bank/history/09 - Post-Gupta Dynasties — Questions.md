@qhis.post_gupta_dynasties.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=9
The capital of the Badami Chalukya dynasty was:

- A) Kanchipuram
- B) Vatapi (Badami)
- C) Manyakheta
- D) Kalyani
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Badami Chalukyas ruled from Vatapi, the ancient name for modern Badami in Karnataka. Manyakheta and Kalyani were capitals of later Chalukya or Rashtrakuta lines, not the original Badami line.

**Trap:** Manyakheta (Rashtrakuta) and Kalyani (later Western Chalukya) are easy distractors for the Badami capital.

**See also:** [[../../history/09 - Post-Gupta Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Gupta Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_gupta_dynasties.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=9
Harshavardhana's capital was:

- A) Prayaga
- B) Kanyakubja (Kannauj)
- C) Ujjayini
- D) Tamralipta
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Harshavardhana made Kanyakubja (Kannauj) his capital after consolidating power in northern India. Prayaga was important for assemblies, and Tamralipta was a port; Ujjayini was the famous ancient city of the Avantis.

**Trap:** Prayaga, Ujjayini and Tamralipta were significant cities in this era but were not Harsha's seat of power.

**See also:** [[../../history/09 - Post-Gupta Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Gupta Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_gupta_dynasties.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=9
The Rashtrakuta dynasty was founded by:

- A) Dantidurga
- B) Amoghavarsha
- C) Krishna I
- D) Indra III
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Dantidurga founded the Rashtrakuta dynasty in the mid-8th century by overthrowing his Chalukya overlords. Amoghavarsha, Krishna I and Indra III were later, prominent Rashtrakuta rulers.

**Trap:** Amoghavarsha and Krishna I are celebrated Rashtrakuta kings, but neither founded the line.

**See also:** [[../../history/09 - Post-Gupta Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Gupta Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_gupta_dynasties.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=9
A student reads that an inscription records how the Pallava king Nandivarman was defeated at Manimangala by the Chalukya ruler Vikramaditya II. The inscription being described is:

- A) Aihole inscription
- B) Sanchi inscription
- C) Mahakuta inscription
- D) Western Chalukya inscription
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Aihole inscription of Pulakeshin II commemorates his victory over the Pallava king and details further successes by later Chalukya rulers such as Vikramaditya II. Sanchi and Mahakuta inscriptions belong to other contexts, and the Western Chalukya line belongs to a later period.

**Trap:** Sanchi is associated with early inscriptions; Mahakuta has Chalukya records but not the Nandivarman campaign; the Western Chalukya reference belongs to a later dynasty.

**See also:** [[../../history/09 - Post-Gupta Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Gupta Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_gupta_dynasties.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=9
The Hoysala dynasty was known for which architectural style?

- A) Nagara
- B) Dravida with star-shaped (stellate) plans (Belur, Halebidu)
- C) Vesara
- D) Kalinga
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Hoysalas developed a distinctive variant of Dravida architecture featuring star-shaped (stellate) plans, with the temples at Belur, Halebidu and Somanathapura as prime examples. Nagara is the northern style; Vesara is a hybrid; and Kalinga belongs to Odisha.

**Trap:** Vesara is often associated with Hoysala-influenced forms, but the canonical Hoysala contribution is the stellate Dravida plan.

**See also:** [[../../history/09 - Post-Gupta Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Gupta Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_gupta_dynasties.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=9
Arrange the following dynasties in chronological order of FOUNDATION:

1. Chalukyas of Badami
2. Rashtrakutas
3. Hoysalas
4. Harshavardhana's Pushyabhuti dynasty

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 4, 1, 2, 3
- C) 1, 4, 2, 3
- D) 4, 2, 1, 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The correct foundation order is Pushyabhuti (6th century), then Chalukyas of Badami (550 CE), then Rashtrakutas (753 CE), and finally Hoysalas (early 11th century, c. 1026 CE).

**Trap:** Option A begins with the Chalukyas without recognising the earlier Pushyabhuti dynasty; option D places the Rashtrakutas before the Chalukyas.

**See also:** [[../../history/09 - Post-Gupta Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Gupta Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_gupta_dynasties.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=9
Consider the statements:

1. Harsha's army included war elephants and a powerful cavalry, but his reign was short.
2. The Hoysalas are known for their star-shaped temples at Belur and Halebidu.
3. Pulakeshin II repulsed Harsha's armies at the Narmada.

Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. Harsha's forces included war elephants and cavalry, and his reign ended abruptly after his death in 647 CE. The Hoysalas are famous for their stellate-plan temples at Belur and Halebidu, and Pulakeshin II famously stopped Harsha at the Narmada.

**Trap:** Statement 1 might seem wrong because Harsha's army was strong, but the point is that even strong armies could not prevent his reign's sudden end without an heir.

**See also:** [[../../history/09 - Post-Gupta Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Gupta Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.post_gupta_dynasties.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=9
Which post-Gupta dynasty is credited with developing the Vesara style that bridges Nagara and Dravida?

- A) Pallavas
- B) Badami Chalukyas
- C) Rashtrakutas
- D) Hoysalas
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Badami Chalukyas are credited with developing the Vesara style, a hybrid that blends features of the Nagara and Dravida traditions. The Rashtrakutas and Hoysalas inherited and refined this hybrid, but the canonical Vesara exponents are the Badami Chalukyas.

**Trap:** The Hoysalas are sometimes loosely associated with Vesara due to their Chalukya inheritance, but they are more specifically known for stellate Dravida forms.

**See also:** [[../../history/09 - Post-Gupta Dynasties.md#Content|Post-Gupta Dynasties — source chapter]]
</details>
