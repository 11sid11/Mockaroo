# Gupta Dynasty — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md|Gupta Dynasty]]

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=8
The founder of the Gupta Dynasty was:

- A) Chandragupta I
- B) Sri Gupta
- C) Samudragupta
- D) Kumaragupta I
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Sri Gupta is regarded as the founder of the Gupta Dynasty. Chandragupta I was the first major imperial ruler of the line, but he was not its founder.

**Trap:** Chandragupta I and Samudragupta greatly expanded Gupta power, while Kumaragupta I ruled later.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=8
The Gupta king known as the "Napoleon of India" is:

- A) Chandragupta I
- B) Samudragupta
- C) Chandragupta II
- D) Kumaragupta I
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Historian V. A. Smith called Samudragupta the "Napoleon of India" because of the extensive military campaigns and conquests described in the Prayag Prasasti.

**Trap:** Chandragupta II is associated with the title Vikramaditya, not this epithet.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=8
Samudragupta's military achievements are recorded in the Prayag Prasasti composed by:

- A) Kalidasa
- B) Harisena
- C) Vasugupta
- D) Fa-Hien
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Harisena, Samudragupta's court poet and minister, composed the Prayag Prasasti, also known as the Allahabad Pillar inscription.

**Trap:** Kalidasa was a celebrated Sanskrit poet, while Fa-Hien was a Chinese pilgrim who visited India during Chandragupta II's reign.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=8
The decimal number system and the use of zero are believed to have been developed in India during which period?

- A) Maurya period
- B) Gupta period
- C) Mughal period
- D) British period
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Gupta period is associated with major advances in Indian mathematics, including the development of the decimal place-value system and the use of zero.

**Trap:** The other periods made important contributions in different fields, but the stated mathematical developments are conventionally linked to the Gupta age.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=8
A student reads that an inscription praises a Gupta king for conquests from the Aryavrata to south India's Pandyas, with a long list of tribes and kings subjugated. The inscription being described is:

- A) Allahabad Pillar inscription of Samudragupta
- B) Mehrauli inscription of Chandragupta II
- C) Aihole inscription of Pulakeshin II
- D) Bhitari inscription of Skandagupta
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Allahabad Pillar inscription, or Prayag Prasasti, presents Harisena's account of Samudragupta's campaigns against rulers of Aryavrata, the south and frontier regions.

**Trap:** The Mehrauli inscription praises a king named Chandra; Aihole concerns Pulakeshin II; Bhitari records Skandagupta's achievements.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=8
Chandragupta II took the title Vikramaditya after defeating:

- A) The Huns
- B) The Sakas (Western Kshatrapas)
- C) The Kushanas
- D) The Pandyas
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Chandragupta II defeated the Sakas, or Western Kshatrapas, and extended Gupta control over western India. His victory is associated with the title Vikramaditya.

**Trap:** Skandagupta, rather than Chandragupta II, is especially associated with resisting the Hunas.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=8
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the Gupta period?

- A) Gold coins (Dinaras) were issued in large numbers
- B) The postal system was improved, with relay stations on main routes
- C) Land revenue was the principal source of state income
- D) The king had absolute power without any council of ministers
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

Gupta kings were assisted by a Mantriparishad, or council of ministers. It is therefore incorrect to claim that the king ruled without any council.

**Trap:** The Guptas issued abundant gold dinaras; land revenue was the chief source of state income; and communication arrangements included relay facilities on major routes.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=8
The Gupta-era astronomer who calculated the value of pi to four decimal places and the length of the solar year was:

- A) Varahamihira
- B) Brahmagupta
- C) Aryabhata
- D) Bhaskara I
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Aryabhata gave a close approximation of pi and calculated the length of the solar year with remarkable accuracy in the Aryabhatiya.

**Trap:** Varahamihira, Brahmagupta and Bhaskara I were also major Indian mathematicians or astronomers, making them close distractors.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=8
Match the following Gupta rulers with their famous works / activities:

| Ruler | Work / Activity |
|---|---|
| 1. Chandragupta II | a. Compiled Prayag Prasasti |
| 2. Samudragupta | b. Vikramaditya legend / Navaratnas |
| 3. Kumaragupta I | c. Founded Nalanda University |

- A) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- B) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a

@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

Chandragupta II is linked with the Vikramaditya tradition and Navaratnas; Samudragupta's achievements were compiled in the Prayag Prasasti; and Kumaragupta I is traditionally credited with founding Nalanda University.

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=8
Arrange in chronological order:

1. Chandragupta I's accession
2. Samudragupta's reign
3. Chandragupta II's war with the Sakas
4. Skandagupta's repulsion of the Hunas

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 2, 1, 3, 4
- C) 1, 3, 2, 4
- D) 2, 1, 4, 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct sequence is Chandragupta I — Samudragupta — Chandragupta II — Skandagupta. Their reigns place the accession, campaigns, Saka victory and Huna resistance in that order.

**Trap:** Each other option reverses at least one ruler's place in the established Gupta succession.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=8
Consider the statements about the Gupta period:

1. The Guptas revived the Vedic religion and the varnashrama system.
2. The Guptas issued abundant gold coins but no silver or copper.
3. Sanskrit literature and science flourished during the Gupta era.

Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Statements 1 and 3 are correct. The period witnessed a Brahmanical revival and major growth in Sanskrit literature and science. Statement 2 is incorrect because the Guptas issued silver and copper coins as well as gold coins.

**Trap:** The phrase "abundant gold coins" is true, but the absolute claim that there were no silver or copper issues makes statement 2 false.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.gupta_dynasty.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=8
The "Mehrauli" iron pillar near the Qutub Minar in Delhi is an example of:

- A) Maurya metalwork
- B) Gupta-era metallurgy (no-rust iron)
- C) Mughal metallurgy
- D) Sultanate metallurgy
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Mehrauli iron pillar is a celebrated example of Gupta-era metallurgy. Its high corrosion resistance, often described as "no-rust iron," demonstrates the advanced iron-working knowledge of the period.

**Trap:** Its present location near Sultanate monuments does not make it Sultanate metalwork; it predates both the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal period.

**See also:** [[../../history/08 - Gupta Dynasty.md#Content|Gupta Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>
