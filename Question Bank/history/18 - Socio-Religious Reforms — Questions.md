# Socio-Religious Reforms — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md|Socio-Religious Reforms]]

@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=18
Who founded the Arya Samaj in 1875?

- A) Swami Vivekananda
- B) Swami Dayanand Saraswati
- C) Ramakrishna Paramahamsa
- D) Annie Besant
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Swami Dayanand Saraswati (1824-1883) founded the Arya Samaj in Bombay on 7 April 1875. Its slogan was "Back to the Vedas" and it opposed idolatry, caste rigidity and child marriage while promoting female education and widow remarriage.

**Trap:** A and C are famous contemporary Hindu reformers (Vivekananda, Ramakrishna) but they led the Ramakrishna Mission, not the Arya Samaj. D was a Theosophist, not an Arya Samaj leader.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=18
The "Satyarth Prakash" (The Light of Truth) was authored by:

- A) Raja Ram Mohan Roy
- B) Swami Dayanand Saraswati
- C) Sri Aurobindo
- D) B. R. Ambedkar
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

"Satyarth Prakash" (1875) is the magnum opus of Swami Dayanand Saraswati and the principal scripture of the Arya Samaj. It asserts the infallibility of the Vedas, condemns idolatry, caste by birth, child marriage and prohibition of foreign travel, and argues for a return to Vedic purities.

**Trap:** A is the founder of the Brahmo Samaj and wrote "Brahmo Samaj" tracts, not "Satyarth Prakash". C and D are 20th-century thinkers with entirely different literary output.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=18
The Brahmo Samaj was founded in 1828 by:

- A) Keshub Chunder Sen
- B) Debendranath Tagore
- C) Raja Ram Mohan Roy
- D) Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Raja Ram Mohan Roy founded the Brahmo Samaj at Calcutta on 20 August 1828. It was the first systematic monotheistic reform movement among Hindus in British India, campaigning against sati, idolatry and caste, and campaigning for women's rights.

**Trap:** A and B are real but later Brahmo leaders: Keshub Chunder Sen led the "Young Bengal" / "Nababidhan" phase after 1858, and Debendranath Tagore was the second president who formally reorganised the Samaj. D led separate reform causes (widow remarriage, women's education) but did not found the Brahmo Samaj.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=18
Which reform organisation was founded by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan?

- A) Aligarh Movement / Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College
- B) Muslim League
- C) Khilafat Movement
- D) Tabligh-i-Jamaat
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817-1898) founded the Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh in 1875, which later grew into Aligarh Muslim University (1920). This is broadly called the "Aligarh Movement" and aimed at modern, English-style education for Indian Muslims without converting them to Christianity.

**Trap:** B (Muslim League, 1906) was founded by Nawab Salimullah and others, much later. C (Khilafat Movement, 1920) was led by the Ali brothers and Gandhi. D (Tabligh-i-Jamaat, 1926) was founded by Muhammad Ilyas. All are real Muslim organisations but not Sir Syed's.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=18
A student reads that a 19th-century reformer opposed child marriage and the purdah system, supported widow remarriage, and campaigned against sati. The reformer being described is:

- A) Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
- B) Jyotirao Phule
- C) B. R. Ambedkar
- D) Dayanand Saraswati
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820-1891) is most closely associated with the widow remarriage movement in 19th-century Bengal. He campaigned vigorously against child marriage and purdah, supported female education (helping found Bethune School), and was a vocal opponent of sati.

**Trap:** B (Jyotirao Phule) campaigned mainly against caste, untouchability and for women's education among lower castes — a related but different agenda. C (Ambedkar) is 20th-century, not 19th-century. D (Dayanand) opposed child marriage and caste but is not specifically known for the widow remarriage campaign that defined Vidyasagar.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=18
The Sati system was officially banned in India by:

- A) Lord Wellesley
- B) Lord William Bentinck (1829)
- C) Lord Dalhousie
- D) Lord Curzon
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Governor-General Lord William Bentinck passed the Bengal Sati Regulation (Regulation XVII, A.D. 1829) on 4 December 1829, declaring the practice of sati illegal and punishable by law. The push for the reform came largely from Hindu reformers like Raja Ram Mohan Roy.

**Trap:** A (Wellesley) is associated with subsidiary alliance and Madras Presidency expansion. C (Dalhousie) is associated with the Doctrine of Lapse, railway, telegraph and postal reforms. D (Curzon) is associated with the Partition of Bengal (1905) and the Archaeological Survey of India. None of them passed the Sati ban.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=18
Which of the following is INCORRECT about 19th-century reform movements?

- A) The Theosophical Society was founded in India by Annie Besant
- B) The Ramakrishna Mission was established by Swami Vivekananda after his guru's death
- C) Jyotirao Phule founded the Satyashodhak Samaj
- D) The Aligarh Movement primarily aimed at creating a pan-Islamic state
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The Aligarh Movement led by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan was NOT aimed at creating a pan-Islamic state. Its real goals were modern Western-style education for Indian Muslims, removing "backwardness" through English learning, and reconciliation between Muslims and the British after the trauma of the 1857 revolt. The "pan-Islamic state" label is a misreading.

**Trap:** A, B and C are all correct statements — Annie Besant did establish the India branch of the Theosophical Society (1907) building on Blavatsky and Olcott's work; Vivekananda founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897 after Ramakrishna Paramahamsa's death in 1886; Phule founded the Satyashodhak Samaj in 1873 to fight caste exploitation. Students often select D as "plausible-sounding" but it is the false option.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=18
Match the following:

| Reformer | Movement / Organisation |
|---|---|
| 1. Swami Vivekananda | a. Ramakrishna Mission |
| 2. Jyotirao Phule | b. Satyashodhak Samaj |
| 3. Annie Besant | c. Theosophical Society in India |

- A) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- B) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- D) 1-a, 2-c, 3-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

1-a (Vivekananda $\rightarrow$ Ramakrishna Mission, 1897), 2-b (Phule $\rightarrow$ Satyashodhak Samaj, 1873), 3-c (Besant $\rightarrow$ Theosophical Society in India, she became president of the worldwide TS in 1907 and led its India branch from Madras).

**Trap:** B swaps 1 and 2. C swaps 1 and 3. D pairs Phule with the Theosophical Society, which is the most common mix-up because Phule's work is sometimes bundled into "social reform" along with Besant's.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=18
The Widow Remarriage Act was passed in India in:

- A) 1829
- B) 1856
- C) 1872
- D) 1891
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Hindu Widow's Remarriage Act (Act XV of 1856), pushed by Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar and supported by the Tagore family and other reformers, legalised the remarriage of Hindu widows. It also declared any such remarriage legal for inheritance purposes.

**Trap:** A (1829) is the Sati ban. C (1872) is the Native Marriages Act. D (1891) is close to Vidyasagar's death year (1891), but the Act was passed in 1856 — students often confuse the dates of Vidyasagar's life with the Act's passage.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=18
Arrange the following in chronological order of founding:

1. Brahmo Samaj
2. Arya Samaj
3. Theosophical Society (India branch, by Olcott and Blavatsky)
4. Ramakrishna Mission

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 1, 2, 4, 3
- C) 2, 1, 3, 4
- D) 1, 3, 2, 4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct chronological order is: Brahmo Samaj (1828, by Ram Mohan Roy) $\rightarrow$ Arya Samaj (1875, by Dayanand Saraswati) $\rightarrow$ Theosophical Society in India (Olcott and Blavatsky arrived in Madras and established the Adyar headquarters in 1882; the international TS was founded in New York in 1875 but its India presence begins 1882) $\rightarrow$ Ramakrishna Mission (1897, by Swami Vivekananda). So 1, 2, 3, 4.

**Trap:** B swaps 3 and 4 (a common error because students forget that the Theosophical Society's India branch predates the Ramakrishna Mission). C swaps 1 and 2. D places the Theosophical Society between Brahmo and Arya, which is chronologically impossible.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=18
Consider the following statements:

1. Ram Mohan Roy is sometimes called "the Father of Modern India" for his reforms.
2. The Aligarh Movement played a key role in the revival of modern Muslim education.
3. Swami Vivekananda represented Hinduism at the Parliament of the World's Religions in Chicago (1893).

Which of the above are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. (1) Ram Mohan Roy is widely described as the "Father of Modern India" / "Father of the Indian Renaissance" for his campaigns against sati, advocacy of civil liberties, promotion of modern education and founding of the Brahmo Samaj. (2) The Aligarh Movement led by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan revived modern, English-language education for Indian Muslims through the MAO College (1875). (3) Swami Vivekananda delivered his famous addresses on Hinduism and Vedanta at the 1893 Parliament of the World's Religions in Chicago.

**Trap:** Some students drop (2) on the grounds that the Aligarh Movement was "political" rather than educational, but its central goal was modern Western-style education for Indian Muslims. Others drop (1) because they reserve "Father of the Nation" for Gandhi — but Roy is "Father of Modern India", a different honorific.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.socio_religious_reforms.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=18
The "Purdah" system and "Zanana" reforms were targeted primarily by:

- A) Arya Samaj
- B) Brahmo Samaj
- C) Ramakrishna Mission
- D) Theosophical Society
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Brahmo Samaj, especially under Ram Mohan Roy and later Keshub Chunder Sen, was the most prominent 19th-century movement attacking the purdah system and the seclusion of women in the zanana. Roy argued against seclusion in his tracts; Keshub Sen organised anti-purdah campaigns and promoted women's education and inter-caste marriages in the 1860s-70s.

**Trap:** A (Arya Samaj) campaigned against child marriage and untouchability but purdah / zanana reform is not its central focus. C (Ramakrishna Mission) is best known for seva and spiritual revival. D (Theosophical Society) promoted universal brotherhood and education but did not run a sustained anti-purdah campaign. Students often pick the more famous Arya Samaj.

**See also:** [[../../history/18 - Socio-Religious Reforms.md#Content|Socio-Religious Reforms — source chapter]]
</details>
