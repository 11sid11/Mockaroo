# Sangam Age — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md|Sangam Age]]

@qhis.sangam_age.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=10
The three crowned dynasties of the Sangam Age are:

- A) Cheras, Cholas, Pandyas
- B) Pallavas, Chalukyas, Pandyas
- C) Cheras, Kadambas, Gangas
- D) Cholas, Pallavas, Cheras
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The three crowned dynasties (Moovendhar) of the Sangam Age were the Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas. The Pallavas, Chalukyas, Kadambas and Gangas belong to the post-Sangam or later periods.

**Trap:** Pallavas and Chalukyas are common Sangam-era distractors but are post-Sangam powers; only the Chera-Chola-Pandya trio was 'crowned' in the Tamil country.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.sangam_age.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=10
The Sangam literature was largely patronized by which dynasty?

- A) Cheras
- B) Cholas
- C) Pandyas
- D) Pallavas
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The three Sangams were held at Madurai under Pandya patronage, and most Sangam poems were composed by poets patronized by the Pandya court.

**Trap:** Cheras and Cholas also patronized poets, but the institutional centre of the Sangam tradition was Pandya Madurai. Pallavas are post-Sangam.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.sangam_age.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=10
The capital of the Chera dynasty was:

- A) Vanji (Karur / Kodungallur)
- B) Madurai
- C) Puhar (Kaveripattinam)
- D) Uraiyur
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Chera capital was Vanji, identified with Karur in the interior and Kodungallur (Cranganore) on the coast. Madurai was the Pandya capital; Puhar was the Chola port; Uraiyur was the Early Chola capital.

**Trap:** Puhar (Kaveripattinam) is the famous Sangam-era port but it belongs to the Cholas, not the Cheras. Uraiyur belongs to the Early Cholas.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.sangam_age.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=10
The Chera emblem was:

- A) Bow and arrow
- B) Fish
- C) Tiger
- D) Garuda
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Cheras adopted the bow and arrow as their royal emblem. The Pandyas used the fish; the Cholas used the tiger; the Pallavas used the lion or Garuda motifs in later periods.

**Trap:** Fish (Pandya) and tiger (Chola) are common Sangam-era dynastic emblems, but the Chera emblem is specifically the bow and arrow.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.sangam_age.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=10
A student reads that an early Chola ruler (Karikala) is associated with the conquest of the Adhirajas, building dams across the Kaveri, and the Sangam poem Pattinappalai. The ruler being described is:

- A) Rajaraja I
- B) Rajendra I
- C) Karikala Chola
- D) Vijayalaya Chola
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Karikala Chola is celebrated in Sangam tradition for defeating the Adhirajas (a federation of minor chieftains), building the Grand Anicut across the Kaveri, and is the hero of the Pattinappalai poem praising Puhar.

**Trap:** Rajaraja I and Rajendra I belong to the Imperial Cholas, several centuries later. Vijayalaya Chola founded the medieval Chola line and is unrelated to the Sangam.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.sangam_age.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=10
The Silappadikaram, an important Sangam-era text, is associated with:

- A) Jainism and Buddhist Jainism
- B) An Epic of Kannagi's chastity (Hindu cult)
- C) A Buddhist Jataka tale
- D) Shaiva bhakti lore
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Silappadikaram ("The Tale of an Anklet") is an epic of Sangam-era Tamil literature that celebrates the chastity of Kannagi and is set within a Hindu devotional framework; it does not belong to Buddhist Jataka literature or to Jainism, though Jain and Buddhist influences are visible in some Sangam poems.

**Trap:** A few Sangam poems show Jain or Buddhist influence, but Silappadikaram specifically centres on Kannagi's chastity, not on Jainism or Buddhism.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.sangam_age.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=10
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the Sangam Age?

- A) The capital of the Pandyas was Madurai
- B) The Pandya emblem was fish
- C) The Chera capital was Uraiyur
- D) The three Sangams were held at Madurai under Pandya patronage
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Chera capital was Vanji (Karur / Kodungallur), not Uraiyur. Uraiyur was the capital of the Early Cholas. The Pandya capital was indeed Madurai, their emblem was the fish, and the Sangams met at Madurai under Pandya patronage.

**Trap:** Uraiyur is a famous Sangam-era capital but belongs to the Cholas, not the Cheras. Students often misattribute Uraiyur to the Cheras.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.sangam_age.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=10
Match the following dynasties with their capitals:

| Dynasty | Capital |
|---|---|
| 1. Pandyas | a. Vanji |
| 2. Cheras | b. Madurai |
| 3. Early Cholas | c. Uraiyur |

- A) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- B) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- C) 1-b, 2-c, 3-a
- D) 1-c, 2-a, 3-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Pandyas ruled from Madurai (1-b), the Cheras from Vanji (2-a), and the Early Cholas from Uraiyur (3-c). Option B swaps the Chera and Pandya capitals; C and D misalign all three.

**Trap:** Uraiyur is sometimes loosely called a 'Chera' capital in popular sources; in strict Sangam-period geography it is the Early Chola capital.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.sangam_age.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=10
The five tinais (land divisions) recognized in Sangam literature correspond to:

- A) Five major rivers of Tamil Nadu
- B) Five geographical zones (kurinji, mullai, marudam, neydal, palai)
- C) Five great kings of the Sangam
- D) Five Vedic varnas
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The five tinais are Sangam literary-landscape divisions: kurinji (hill country), mullai (forest), marudam (cultivable plain), neydal (coastal) and palai (desert/wasteland). Each tinai is associated with a typical deity, occupation, musical mood and flower.

**Trap:** Students sometimes confuse the tinais with rivers or varnas. The five tinais are geographical-ecological zones, each linked to a distinct landscape and mood in Sangam poetry.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.sangam_age.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=10
Consider the statements:
1. The Sangam poems include Ettutogai (Eight Anthologies) and Pattuppattu (Ten Idylls).
2. Tolkappiyam was composed by Tolkappiyar and is the earliest Tamil grammatical text.
3. Jainism and Buddhism had some influence on Sangam literature but the dominant religion was Shaiva/Vaishnava Bhakti.
Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. Ettutogai (Eight Anthologies) and Pattuppattu (Ten Idylls) are the two main Sangam poetic compilations. Tolkappiyam by Tolkappiyar is the earliest Tamil grammar. While Jain and Buddhist ideas appear in some Sangam poems, the dominant religious ethos of the period was Shaiva and Vaishnava bhakti, celebrated in hymns like those of the Nayanars and Alvars.

**Trap:** Statement 3 is sometimes rejected by students who overemphasize the Jain-Buddhist presence; in fact the bhakti strand is the defining religious feature of the late Sangam and post-Sangam.

**See also:** [[../../history/10 - Sangam Age.md#Content|Sangam Age — source chapter]]
</details>
