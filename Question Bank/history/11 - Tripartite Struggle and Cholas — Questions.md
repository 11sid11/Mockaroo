# Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas]]

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=11
The Tripartite Struggle for Kanauj was fought among:

- A) Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas
- B) Palas, Cholas and Rashtrakutas
- C) Pratiharas, Cholas and Pallavas
- D) Palas, Pratiharas and Cholas
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Tripartite Struggle (c. 8th-10th centuries CE) was the long rivalry among the three great powers of the period — the Palas of Bengal, the Pratiharas of western India, and the Rashtrakutas of the Deccan — for control of the holy city of Kanauj and the fertile Gangetic heartland. The Cholas were a contemporary South Indian power but did not participate in this northern contest.

**Trap:** Option D substitutes the Cholas for the Rashtrakutas, and C inserts the Pallavas. The Tripartite Struggle is exclusively a Pala-Pratihara-Rashtrakuta contest.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=11
The founder of the Chola Dynasty (in its later phase) was:

- A) Rajaraja I
- B) Rajendra I
- C) Vijayalaya Chola
- D) Karikala Chola
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Vijayalaya Chola (c. 850-871 CE) captured Thanjavur from the Muttaraiyars and founded the medieval (Imperial) Chola line. Rajaraja I and Rajendra I are later great rulers of the dynasty he founded. Karikala Chola belongs to the much earlier Sangam-era Early Cholas.

**Trap:** Rajaraja I is the dynasty's most famous ruler but is not its founder; Karikala Chola belongs to the Early Cholas of the Sangam period, separated by several centuries.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=11
Rajaraja I built which great temple?

- A) Brihadeeshwara Temple at Tanjore (Thanjavur)
- B) Meenakshi Temple at Madurai
- C) Kailasa Temple at Ellora
- D) Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Brihadeeshwara (Rajarajeshwara) Temple at Thanjavur, completed c. 1010 CE, was built by Rajaraja I and is one of the finest examples of Dravidian architecture, with its towering vimana over the sanctum. The Meenakshi Temple at Madurai is associated with the later Nayaka period; the Kailasa Temple at Ellora was excavated by the Rashtrakuta Krishna I; and the Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram is a Pallava monument.

**Trap:** Brihadeeshwara Temple and the Kailasa Temple are both architectural landmarks of the period and are easily confused; their patrons belong to different dynasties.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=11
Rajendra I assumed the title:

- A) Uttama Chola
- B) Gangaikondachola
- C) Mummadi Chola
- D) Vikrama Chola
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Rajendra I (c. 1014-1044 CE) took the title Gangaikondachola ("the Chola who conquered the Ganga") after his celebrated northern campaign that reached the Ganga river. To mark this feat he founded a new capital, Gangaikondacholapuram, where he built a second great Brihadeeshwara temple. Uttama Chola was a ruler between Parantaka I and Rajaraja I; Mummadi Chola is a general epithet for a mid-ranking Chola; Vikrama Chola was a later 12th-century ruler.

**Trap:** Uttama Chola and Mummadi Chola are genuine Chola titles/epithets but do not match Rajendra I. The distinctive conquest title of Rajendra I is Gangaikondachola.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=11
A student reads that a Chola monarch invaded the Chalukyan capital of Manyakheta, sacked the Rashtrakuta heartland, and successfully reached the Ganga. The monarch being described is:

- A) Rajaraja I
- B) Rajendra I
- C) Parantaka I
- D) Aditya I
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Rajendra I inherited a strong realm from his father Rajaraja I and extended it dramatically: he campaigned against the Chalukyas, sacked the Rashtrakuta capital Manyakheta (modern Malkhed), and led the celebrated northern expedition that reached the Ganga — earning him the title Gangaikondachola. Parantaka I fought the Rashtrakutas and Pandyas but did not reach the Ganga; Aditya I defeated the Pallavas and consolidated Chola power but stayed in the south.

**Trap:** Rajaraja I is Rajendra I's father and the dynasty's other great conqueror, but the Ganga expedition was Rajendra I's personal achievement.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=11
Which of the following was a significant administrative reform of Rajaraja I?

- A) Introduction of the Iqta system
- B) Systematic revenue survey and detailed copper-plate records (like the Uttaramerur inscriptions)
- C) Abandonment of standing armies
- D) Transfer of capital from Tanjore to Madurai
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Rajaraja I (and his successors) carried out systematic revenue surveys, maintained detailed accounts in copper-plate inscriptions, and regulated village self-government — practices later exemplified in the Uttaramerur inscriptions. The Iqta system was a Turko-Afghan revenue-military institution of the Delhi Sultanate, unrelated to the Cholas. The Cholas maintained, not abandoned, a powerful standing army and navy; and the Chola capital remained at Thanjavur (and later at Gangaikondacholapuram), never at Madurai.

**Trap:** Option D is tempting because Madurai was a major Chola conquest, but it was never the Chola capital.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=11
The Chola practice of "kadamai" referred to:

- A) A type of military unit
- B) Land revenue / economic obligation
- C) A council of ministers
- D) A musical mode
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Kadamai was the Chola term for the land revenue (or economic obligation/due) owed by cultivators to the state or temple. Its assessment and remission were major concerns of Chola administration, recorded in detail in copper-plate grants and temple inscriptions. Chola military units were generally termed padai or senais; the ministerial council was not called kadamai; and the term has no musical connotation.

**Trap:** Option A is wrong because military terminology under the Cholas uses padai/senai. Kadamai is specifically an economic-revenue term.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=11
Match the following rulers with their association:

| Ruler | Association |
|---|---|
| 1. Rajaraja I | a. Built Gangaikondacholapuram |
| 2. Rajendra I | b. Built Brihadeeshwara Temple |
| 3. Karikala Chola | c. Built Kallanai (Grand Anicut) dam |

- A) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- B) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- D) 1-b, 2-c, 3-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Rajaraja I built the Brihadeeshwara Temple at Thanjavur (1-b). Rajendra I founded Gangaikondacholapuram and built the new Brihadeeshwara temple there to mark his Ganga campaign (2-a). Karikala Chola of the Sangam era is credited with building the Kallanai (Grand Anicut) dam across the Kaveri (3-c). The other options swap or scramble these pairings.

**Trap:** Both Rajaraja I and Rajendra I built Brihadeeshwara temples (at Thanjavur and Gangaikondacholapuram respectively); careful reading of the associations in the table is essential to pick the right pair.

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=11
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the Chola administration?

- A) The kingdom was divided into mandalams (provinces), nadus (districts) and kurrams (villages)
- B) The village assembly was called the ur or mahasabha
- C) The Cholas had a powerful standing army and a navy that projected power overseas
- D) The Chola rulers were primarily feudatories of the Pandyas and never exercised direct rule
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

Statement D is INCORRECT. The Imperial Cholas (especially from Aditya I onwards, and even more powerfully under Rajaraja I and Rajendra I) exercised direct sovereignty over a vast realm; they were not Pandyas' feudatories — in fact, they eclipsed the Pandyas and reduced them to a subordinate role for long stretches. Statements A, B, and C correctly describe Chola administration: the mandalam-nadu-kurram hierarchy, the ur/mahasabha village assemblies, and the formidable Chola army and navy (which raided Srivijaya and the Maldives).

**Trap:** Students familiar with the Sangam Age sometimes wrongly assume all Tamil dynasties had a vague federal relationship. The Imperial Cholas ruled directly from Thanjavur.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=11
Arrange in chronological order:
1. Founder Vijayalaya Chola
2. Rajaraja I
3. Rajendra I
4. Kulottunga I (Chola-Chalukya)

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 1, 3, 2, 4
- C) 2, 1, 3, 4
- D) 4, 3, 2, 1
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Vijayalaya Chola founded the medieval dynasty (c. 850 CE). His successors led to Parantaka I, then Rajaraja I (r. 985-1014 CE), followed by his son Rajendra I (r. 1014-1044 CE). Several later rulers intervene, and then Kulottunga I — a Chola-Chalukya (son of Rajendra I's daughter and a Chalukya prince) — comes to the throne c. 1070 CE. Hence the correct chronological order is 1 (Vijayalaya) -> 2 (Rajaraja I) -> 3 (Rajendra I) -> 4 (Kulottunga I).

**Trap:** Option B swaps Rajaraja I and Rajendra I. The dynasty's founding comes first, then its two great consecutive conquerors, then Kulottunga I.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=11
Consider the statements about the Tripartite Struggle:
1. Pratihara ruler Nagabhata II (c. 805-833 CE) is the earliest notable figure who briefly held Kanauj.
2. The Pala ruler Dharmapala also held Kanauj for a time and tried to install his protege Chakrayudha.
3. The conflict ended decisively with the Rashtrakuta Indra III seizing Kanauj around 916 CE.
Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. Nagabhata II of the Pratiharas was indeed the first of the three rivals to capture Kanauj (c. early 9th century). Dharmapala of Bengal held Kanauj in turn and installed his protege Chakrayudha on the throne there, an act which triggered Pratihara counter-campaigns. The struggle's decisive late phase saw the Rashtrakuta Indra III capture Kanauj c. 916 CE, after which the Pala and Pratihara positions in the north collapsed.

**Trap:** Statement 1 is sometimes missed because Nagabhata II is less famous than Dharmapala or Indra III, but he is the first to hold Kanauj among the three rivals. The Tripartite Struggle is therefore correctly summarised by all three statements together.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.tripartite_struggle_and_cholas.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=11
The Chola "Vellan" and "Padai" inscriptions refer to:

- A) The Chola coinage system
- B) Village-level self-governance and military mobilization records
- C) Sangam poetic collections
- D) Grants to Buddhist viharas
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Vellan inscriptions are Chola records concerning the Vellala (cultivator) community and village-level obligations, often detailing local assemblies (ur/mahasabha), land grants and revenue (kadamai) arrangements. Padai inscriptions relate to the padai (military) — orders mobilising troops, supplying equipment, and maintaining military camps. Together they are evidence of Chola administrative-military reach at the village and garrison level. Chola coinage is discussed under different inscription series; the Sangam literary corpus is the Ettutogai/Pattuppattu; and Chola grants to Buddhist viharas were uncommon (the dynasty was largely Shaiva and Vaishnava in patronage).

**Trap:** Option D misreads Chola religious patronage. The Cholas were predominantly temple-builders for Shiva and Vishnu, and padai/vellan inscriptions are administrative, not religious-grant, records.

**See also:** [[../../history/11 - Tripartite Struggle and Cholas.md#Content|Tripartite Struggle and Cholas — source chapter]]
</details>
