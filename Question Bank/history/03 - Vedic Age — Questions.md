@qhis.vedic_age.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=3
The Rigveda is composed mainly in which language?

- A) Classical Sanskrit
- B) Vedic Sanskrit (an older form)
- C) Pali
- D) Prakrit
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Rigveda is composed in Vedic Sanskrit (sometimes called "Old Indo-Aryan"), distinct from the classical Sanskrit codified later by Panini.

**Trap:** A is the more famous classical form; students default to it. C and D are post-Buddhist literary languages and cannot be the Rigveda's tongue.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vedic_age.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=3
The Battle of Ten Kings (Dasharajna) was fought on the banks of which river?

- A) Saraswati
- B) Ganga
- C) Parushni (Ravi)
- D) Sindhu (Indus)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Battle of Ten Kings was fought on the Parushni (modern Ravi), where the Bharata chief Sudas defeated a confederation of ten tribes.

**Trap:** Saraswati is a sacred Vedic river but not the battle site. Ganga and Indus are far from the Punjab theatre of this battle. Students often guess Saraswati.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vedic_age.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=3
Which vedic text is the oldest, and is primarily a collection of hymns to the gods?

- A) Samaveda
- B) Yajurveda
- C) Atharvaveda
- D) Rigveda
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The Rigveda is the oldest of the four Vedas and contains 1,028 hymns (suktas) arranged in 10 mandalas (books), addressed to deities like Indra, Agni and Varuna.

**Trap:** Samaveda is largely a rearrangement of Rigvedic hymns set to music; Yajurveda has prose sacrificial formulas; Atharvaveda is later and contains spells.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vedic_age.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=3
In the Later Vedic period, which two technological / social changes most enabled Aryan expansion from Punjab into the Ganga plains?

- A) Use of bronze and writing
- B) Use of iron and horses
- C) Use of cotton and seafaring
- D) Use of iron and wheeled chariots only
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Iron (kishku/ayas) tools cleared dense Ganga-valley forests, while horses enabled faster movement and bigger armies — the two factors behind Later Vedic eastward expansion.

**Trap:** A is tempting because Indus Valley people used bronze, but the Aryans did not adopt bronze in significant amounts. C and D over- or under-state the change.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.vedic_age.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=3
A student reads that in early Vedic society, cattle (especially cows) were the chief form of wealth and were frequently raided in cattle-stealing (gaurayitri) wars. The society being described is:

- A) Later Vedic
- B) Early Vedic (Rigvedic)
- C) Mauryan
- D) Indus Valley
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Early Vedic / Rigvedic economy was pastoral and cattle-centric — wealth was reckoned in cows, and tribal wars were often cattle-raids.

**Trap:** Later Vedic had agriculture and larger kingdoms (the answer students jump to). C and D are entirely later/different civilizations.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vedic_age.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=3
The ashrama system of four stages of life (Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, Sanyasa) is formally codified in which text?

- A) Rigveda
- B) Upanishads
- C) Dharma-shastras / later Smriti texts
- D) Bhagavad Gita
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The four ashramas are systematically codified in the later Dharma-shastras / Smriti literature (e.g., Manusmriti), not in the Vedic samhitas themselves.

**Trap:** Students assume the Vedas themselves mention ashramas — but the concept crystallized later. The Upanishads mention some stages but not the formal four-fold system.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vedic_age.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=3
The famous Purusha Sukta of the Rigveda is significant because it:

- A) Describes the cosmic mount Meru
- B) Provides the varna origin myth from the cosmic Purusha's body
- C) Records the Ashvamedha sacrifice
- D) Praises the river Saraswati
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Purusha Sukta (Rigveda 10.90) describes the cosmic Purusha whose mouth became the Brahmin, arms the Kshatriya, thighs the Vaishya, and feet the Shudra — a varna-origin myth.

**Trap:** Students confuse Purusha Sukta with the Hiranyagarbha or Nasadiya Sukta. A and D describe content that is simply not in this hymn.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vedic_age.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=3
Which Vedic deity is "the Purandhi-like destroyer of forts" and considered the chief god of the early Vedic pantheon?

- A) Agni
- B) Varuna
- C) Indra
- D) Soma
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Indra — destroyer of forts (puramdara), rain-bringer, and warrior — receives the largest share of Rigvedic hymns (~250) and is the chief deity of the early Vedic period.

**Trap:** Agni and Soma also receive many hymns but Indra dominates. Varuna is the cosmic upholder of rita but recedes as the chief deity.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vedic_age.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=3
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the political organization of the Early Vedic period?

- A) The basic unit was the "jana" or tribe
- B) The chief was called "rajan" and was elected
- C) The two main assemblies were the Sabha and Samiti
- D) The king had absolute hereditary power with no tribal council
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

The Early Vedic rajan's power was checked by tribal councils (Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha). He did NOT wield absolute hereditary power — that came in the Later Vedic monarchies.

**Trap:** A, B, C are all textbook facts. D is a trap from Later Vedic politics projected onto the early period.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vedic_age.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=3
A passage describes a society that uses iron ploughs, practices settled agriculture, and recognises hereditary varnas including the Shudras. The passage refers to the:

- A) Early Vedic period
- B) Later Vedic period
- C) Indus Valley Civilization
- D) Mauryan period
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Iron ploughs, settled agriculture, larger territorial kingdoms and hereditary varna (including clearly-defined Shudras) are hallmarks of the Later Vedic period.

**Trap:** A is wrong because Early Vedic was pastoral. C used bronze and had no ploughs. D is far later and uses iron but adds many non-Vedic features.

**See also:** [[../../history/03 - Vedic Age.md#Content|Vedic Age — source chapter]]
</details>
