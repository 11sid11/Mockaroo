# Indus Valley Civilization — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md|Indus Valley Civilization]]

@qhis.indus_valley_civilization.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=2
Which Indus Valley site gave the civilization its alternative name "Harappan"?

- A) Mohenjo-daro
- B) Harappa
- C) Lothal
- D) Kalibangan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Harappa, in present-day Punjab (Pakistan), was the first site excavated (1921 by Dayaram Sahni), so the civilization is often called "Harappan."

**Trap:** Mohenjo-daro is more famous but is in Sindh. Lothal is in Gujarat. Kalibangan is in Rajasthan — the namesake comes from Harappa.

**See also:** [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md#Content|Indus Valley Civilization — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.indus_valley_civilization.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=2
The Indus Valley script is generally described as:

- A) Alphabetic, written left to right
- B) Brahmi-derived, read right to left
- C) Pictographic, undeciphered, generally read right to left
- D) Syllabic, written top to bottom
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The ~400 Indus signs on seals are pictographic in nature, mostly read right-to-left, and remain undeciphered despite many attempts.

**Trap:** B is a trap because later Indian scripts (Brahmi, Kharosthi) ARE alphabetic — students assume Indus was the same. A and D describe unrelated scripts (Latin, classical Chinese).

**See also:** [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md#Content|Indus Valley Civilization — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.indus_valley_civilization.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=2
The "Great Bath" of the Indus Valley Civilization was found at:

- A) Harappa
- B) Mohenjo-daro
- C) Lothal
- D) Dholavira
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Great Bath — a watertight tank of finely fitted burnt bricks — was discovered at Mohenjo-daro on its citadel mound.

**Trap:** Dholavira has a sophisticated reservoir/step-well system (often confused), and Lothal had a dockyard — both sound "water-related." Harappa has no Great Bath.

**See also:** [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md#Content|Indus Valley Civilization — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.indus_valley_civilization.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=2
Which Indus Valley site had a dockyard, suggesting maritime trade with Mesopotamia?

- A) Harappa
- B) Mohenjo-daro
- C) Lothal
- D) Kalibangan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Lothal (Gujarat) had a rectangular brick dockyard with a spillway — evidence of seafaring trade with Sumer, supported by Indus seals found in Mesopotamian sites.

**Trap:** Kalibangan has a fire-altar and ploughed field. Mohenjo-daro has the Great Bath. Students confuse Lothal's dockyard with Mohenjo-daro's bathing tank.

**See also:** [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md#Content|Indus Valley Civilization — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.indus_valley_civilization.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=2
A student notes that an Indus Valley site has grid-pattern streets, a citadel, an elaborate water reservoir, and inscriptions on the Harappan script. Which site is this?

- A) Harappa
- B) Lothal
- C) Dholavira
- D) Kalibangan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Dholavira (Gujarat) is unique for its extensive water reservoirs, stadium, and signboard inscriptions — the only Indus site with large carved letter-like signs outside seals.

**Trap:** A and B are more famous Indus sites but lack the carved signboard. Kalibangan has ploughed fields, not water reservoirs. Students default to Harappa/Mohenjo-daro.

**See also:** [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md#Content|Indus Valley Civilization — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.indus_valley_civilization.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=2
The Indus Valley people are known to have cultivated which of the following crops?

- A) Rice and sugarcane only
- B) Wheat, barley, cotton, dates and peas
- C) Tea and coffee
- D) Only millet
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Archaeobotanical evidence shows Indus farmers grew wheat, barley, cotton (earliest known), peas, sesame, dates, and mustard — a wide mix of rabi and kharif crops.

**Trap:** A and D are too narrow. C is wrong — tea and coffee came to India much later (post-Vedic / medieval respectively).

**See also:** [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md#Content|Indus Valley Civilization — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.indus_valley_civilization.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=2
At which Indus site were the remains of a ploughed field found, indicating the earliest evidence of a "furrow" in the subcontinent?

- A) Harappa
- B) Mohenjo-daro
- C) Kalibangan
- D) Chanhudaro
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Kalibangan (Rajasthan) yielded a ploughed field with parallel furrows and a fire altar, suggesting ritual as well as agricultural activity.

**Trap:** Harappa and Mohenjo-daro get all the fame; Chanhudaro is famous for bead-making. Students rarely remember Kalibangan's ploughed field.

**See also:** [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md#Content|Indus Valley Civilization — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.indus_valley_civilization.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=2
Which Indus Valley site is best known as a major centre of bead-making and shell-cutting, and notably LACKS a citadel?

- A) Harappa
- B) Mohenjo-daro
- C) Chanhudaro
- D) Lothal
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Chanhudaro (Sindh) was a specialist craft centre — bead-making, shell-cutting, seal-making and copper working — and is the only major Indus site without a separate citadel.

**Trap:** Students assume all Indus cities have the classic citadel+lower-town plan (Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Kalibangan). Lothal has a citadel; only Chanhudaro is the exception.

**See also:** [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md#Content|Indus Valley Civilization — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.indus_valley_civilization.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=2
The famous "Pashupati seal" of the Indus Valley depicts a horned figure surrounded by animals. From which site was it found?

- A) Harappa
- B) Mohenjo-daro
- C) Lothal
- D) Kalibangan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Pashupati seal (a horned figure seated in yogic posture with wild animals around) was discovered at Mohenjo-daro and is sometimes interpreted as an early "proto-Shiva."

**Trap:** A and D are real Indus sites but did not yield this specific seal. Students confuse it with the "unicorn seal" which is more common across sites.

**See also:** [[../../history/02 - Indus Valley Civilization.md#Content|Indus Valley Civilization — source chapter]]
</details>

Match the following Indus Valley sites with their distinguishing feature:

| Site | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| 1. Mohenjo-daro | a. Ploughed field and fire altar |
| 2. Kalibangan | b. Dockyard for maritime trade |
| 3. Lothal | c. Great Bath on the citadel |

- A) 1-c, 2-a, 3-b
- B) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- C) 1-b, 2-c, 3-a
- D) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

1-c (Mohenjo-daro = Great Bath), 2-a (Kalibangan = ploughed field, fire altar), 3-b (Lothal = dockyard).

**Trap:** D swaps Lothal and Kalibangan. B and C scramble the matches — the standard SSC trap.
