@qhis.maurya_dynasty.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=6
Megasthenes was the ambassador sent to which Mauryan king's court?

- A) Ashoka
- B) Chandragupta Maurya
- C) Bindusara
- D) Dasharatha
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Megasthenes was the Seleucid ambassador sent by Seleucus I to the court of Chandragupta Maurya at Pataliputra. His account Indica is one of the most valuable foreign sources on the Mauryas.

**Trap:** A (Ashoka) hosted later Greek emissaries like Dionysius. C (Bindusara) — the Greek sources note friendly relations but no recorded ambassador of Megasthenes stature. D (Dasharatha) had no known Greek envoy.

**See also:** [[../../history/06 - Maurya Dynasty.md#Content|Maurya Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.maurya_dynasty.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=6
The Kalinga War was fought by Ashoka in which year (BCE)?

- A) 261 BCE
- B) 232 BCE
- C) 273 BCE
- D) 250 BCE
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Kalinga War (c. 261 BCE) is dated by Ashoka's own 13th Major Rock Edict. The massive bloodshed and his remorse converted him to Buddhism and triggered his Dhamma policy.

**Trap:** B (232 BCE) is a common confusion with Ashoka's death year. C (273 BCE) is too early — predates Ashoka's reign. D (250 BCE) is too late and overlaps with the Third Buddhist Council.

**See also:** [[../../history/06 - Maurya Dynasty.md#Content|Maurya Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.maurya_dynasty.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=6
Which edict of Ashoka primarily espouses his Dhamma (dhamma) policy and moral values?

- A) Major Rock Edicts
- B) Minor Rock Edicts
- C) Pillar Edicts
- D) Cave Inscriptions
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Minor Rock Edicts (including the separate Kalinga / Tosali / Rupa minor rock edicts and the Bhabru edict) directly carry Ashoka's personal commitment to Dhamma — most famously the proclamation "I have achieved the supreme conquest through love of Dhamma".

**Trap:** A (Major Rock Edicts) are administrative and policy edicts. C (Pillar Edicts) include some Dhamma content but are not the primary moral-policy series. D (Cave Inscriptions) are mostly donations to Ajivikas.

**See also:** [[../../history/06 - Maurya Dynasty.md#Content|Maurya Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.maurya_dynasty.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=6
A student reads that a Mauryan official was responsible for the registration of births, deaths, and sales of immovable property. The official being described is:

- A) Sannidhata
- B) Samaharta
- C) Pautavadhyaksha
- D) Sitadhyaksha
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Samaharta (the chief collector / accountant-general) recorded vital statistics — births, deaths, sale of immovable property — and managed accounts.

**Trap:** A (Sannidhata) was the treasurer / custodian of the king's treasury. C (Pautavadhyaksha) oversaw weights and measures. D (Sitadhyaksha) supervised crown lands (royal farms).

@qhis.maurya_dynasty.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=6
Which of the following was NOT a feature of the Mauryan administration?

- A) Espionage system (gupta-charas)
- B) Thirty-four members of the king's council (Mantriparishad)
- C) Provincial governors (kumara / vyaprtas)
- D) Permanent large standing army of one million
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

A standing army of one million is a Megasthenes figure often quoted but it is NOT corroborated as a feature of Maurya administration in standard accounts. The actual Maurya features were: gupta-charas (spies), Mantriparishad (per Arthashastra, six but also loosely 30-34), and kumara/vyaprtas as provincial governors.

**Trap:** A, B, C are all genuine features of Mauryan administration. D is the trick — the "million army" is a Megasthenes exaggeration often repeated but not a recognised administrative feature.

**See also:** [[../../history/06 - Maurya Dynasty.md#Content|Maurya Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.maurya_dynasty.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=6
The Buddhist scripture "Divyavadana" describes Ashoka sending emissaries to which foreign ruler?

- A) Antiochus II of Syria
- B) Ptolemy II of Egypt
- C) Julius Caesar of Rome
- D) Darius III of Persia
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Divyavadana (Ashokavadana) narrates Ashoka sending Buddhist missionaries to Antioch to the court of Antiochus II Theos of Syria (the Seleucid king). The Greek historian Strabo also confirms this tradition.

**Trap:** B (Ptolemy II) and several other Hellenistic kings are mentioned in Ashoka's Major Rock Edicts II (13th year) — but Divyavadana specifically pairs Ashoka with Antiochus II. C (Caesar) post-dates Ashoka. D (Darius III) was destroyed by Alexander long before Ashoka.

**See also:** [[../../history/06 - Maurya Dynasty.md#Content|Maurya Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.maurya_dynasty.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=6
The Arthashastra was written by:

- A) Panini
- B) Kautilya (Chanakya / Vishnugupta)
- C) Megasthenes
- D) Patanjali
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Kautilya — also known as Chanakya and Vishnugupta — is traditionally credited as the author of the Arthashastra, the Mauryan-era treatise on statecraft and political economy.

**Trap:** A (Panini) wrote the Ashtadhyayi (Sanskrit grammar). C (Megasthenes) wrote Indica (a Greek account), not Arthashastra. D (Patanjali) wrote the Mahabhashya (commentary on Panini) and the Yoga Sutras.

**See also:** [[../../history/06 - Maurya Dynasty.md#Content|Maurya Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.maurya_dynasty.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=6
Consider the statements:

1. The Mauryan empire had a centralised administration with a vast spy network.
2. Ashoka's pillar edicts are found across the subcontinent.
3. The Arthashastra advocates war and conquest as the primary state policy.

Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the Mauryas ran a centralised empire with the famous gupta-charas spy network; and Ashoka's pillar edicts are found at sites from Delhi-Topra and Allahabad-Kosam to Lauriya-Araraj and Rampurva. Statement 3 is incorrect — the Arthashastra outlines six-fold policy (shad-gunya): sandhi (peace), vigraha (war), yana (marching), asana (staying), samshraya (seeking shelter), and dvaidhibhava (double policy). It does NOT advocate war as the primary policy; peace is often preferred when beneficial.

**Trap:** B adds the wrong Arthashastra claim. C reverses 2 and 3 (incorrect). D accepts all three and falls for the incorrect Arthashastra caricature.

**See also:** [[../../history/06 - Maurya Dynasty.md#Content|Maurya Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.maurya_dynasty.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=6
The last Mauryan ruler was:

- A) Ashoka
- B) Dasharatha
- C) Brihadratha
- D) Jalauka
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Brihadratha (sometimes called the last "official" Mauryan king) was assassinated by his general Pushyamitra Shunga in 185 BCE, who then founded the Shunga dynasty. Note: while Ashoka is by far the most prominent ruler, his successors (Kunala, Dasharatha, Samprati, Shalihadra, Devavarman, Satadhanvan and finally Brihadratha) ruled for about 50 years after him.

**Trap:** A (Ashoka) is the most famous but is not the last. B (Dasharatha) was Ashoka's grandson and immediate post-Kalinga successor — sometimes confused with the last ruler. D (Jalauka) is legendary, the son of Ashoka in Kashmir tradition. The killer trick is Brihadratha as the actual last.

**See also:** [[../../history/06 - Maurya Dynasty.md#Content|Maurya Dynasty — source chapter]]
</details>
