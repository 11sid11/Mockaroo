# Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom]]

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=13
The Vijayanagara Empire was founded in 1336 CE by:

- A) Krishna Deva Raya
- B) Harihara and Bukka
- C) Prataparudra
- D) Saluva Narasimha
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Vijayanagara Empire was founded in 1336 CE by two brothers, Harihara I and Bukka I, initially with support and encouragement from the Sangama clan. Tradition holds that they were inspired/guided by the sage Vidyaranya to set up a new Hindu kingdom in the south as a bulwark against the Deccan sultanates. Krishnadevaraya (r. 1509-1529 CE) was the empire's most famous ruler but came much later. Prataparudra was a ruler of the Gajapati dynasty of Odisha. Saluva Narasimha was a later Vijayanagara king who founded the Saluva dynasty within the empire.

**Trap:** Distractor A (Krishnadevaraya) is the empire's most celebrated ruler, but he is the empire's great ruler, not its founder. Prataparudra and Saluva Narasimha are real Deccan-era rulers but are unrelated to the founding moment.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=13
The Bahmani Kingdom was founded by:

- A) Hasan Gangu (Ala-ud-din Hasan Bahman Shah)
- B) Mahmud Gawan
- C) Muhammad Shah I
- D) Ahmad Shah Wali
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Bahmani Kingdom (also called the Bahmani Sultanate) was founded in 1347 CE by Ala-ud-din Hasan Bahman Shah, popularly known as Hasan Gangu, who broke away from the Delhi Sultanate and established an independent Muslim state in the Deccan with its capital at Gulbarga. Mahmud Gawan was a famous Bahmani vizier much later, not its founder. Muhammad Shah I and Ahmad Shah Wali were later Bahmani sultans who moved the capital to Bidar and presided over the kingdom's decline.

**Trap:** All four names are real Bahmani-era figures, but only Hasan Gangu is the dynasty's founder; the others held power long after the founding moment.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=13
The Bahmani capital was shifted from Gulbarga to Bidar by:

- A) Ala-ud-din Hasan
- B) Mahmud Gawan
- C) Ahmad Shah Wali
- D) Muhammad Shah I
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Ahmad Shah Wali (Ahmad Shah I Bahmani, r. 1422-1435 CE) shifted the Bahmani capital from Gulbarga to Bidar around 1427-1428 CE because Bidar offered a more defensible hill-fort position and was better suited to project power across the Deccan. Ala-ud-din Hasan had originally founded the kingdom with Gulbarga as its capital; Mahmud Gawan was a vizier, not a sultan; Muhammad Shah I (Muhammad Shah Lashkari) came later and is associated with Gawan's execution.

**Trap:** Muhammad Shah I (option D) is associated with other dramatic Bahmani events (Mahmud Gawan's execution) but not the capital shift. Hasan Gangu (option A) is the dynasty's founder and is firmly tied to Gulbarga, not Bidar.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=13
The capital of the Vijayanagara Empire was:

- A) Hampi (Vijayanagara)
- B) Madurai
- C) Tanjore
- D) Mysore
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The imperial capital of the Vijayanagara Empire was the city of Vijayanagara on the south bank of the Tungabhadra river, whose ruins lie in and around present-day Hampi (a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Karnataka). Madurai was the base of the earlier Pandyan kingdom and a region the Vijayanagara rulers controlled but never made their main capital. Tanjore (Thanjavur) was the centre of the Chola power and later a Nayaka kingdom. Mysore became a major princely state only in the post-Vijayanagara period under the Wodeyars.

**Trap:** Madurai, Tanjore and Mysore are all famous south-Indian centres but were not the Vijayanagara capital; students often confuse regional power centres with the imperial capital.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=13
A student reads that a Vijayanagara king wrote the Telugu poem Amuktamalyada, maintained diplomatic relations with the Portuguese, and bore the titles "Andhra Bhoja" and "Gajapati Trayambaka". The king being described is:

- A) Krishnadevaraya
- B) Bukka I
- C) Devaraya II
- D) Rama Raya
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Krishnadevaraya (r. 1509-1529 CE), the most celebrated Vijayanagara ruler, authored the Telugu poem Amuktamalyada (a devotional work on Lord Venkateshwara), was the first major Indian king to welcome and engage with Portuguese envoys (including Domingo Paes and Barbosa), and was honoured by the titles "Andhra Bhoja" (a Bhoja-like patron of Telugu learning) and "Gajapati Trayambaka" (after his victories over the Gajapati rulers of Odisha). Bukka I co-founded the empire in 1336; Devaraya II was a 15th-century Vijayanagara ruler who fought the Bahmanis; Rama Raya was the regent/minister in the 1540s who effectively ran the empire and fell at Talikota.

**Trap:** Bukka I, Devaraya II and Rama Raya were all real Vijayanagara-era figures with military roles, but only Krishnadevaraya combines the literary (Amuktamalyada), diplomatic (Portuguese) and titular (Andhra Bhoja/Gajapati Trayambaka) clues.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=13
Which battle marked the decisive defeat of Vijayanagara by the Deccan Sultanates in 1565?

- A) Battle of Talikota (Rakkasa-Tangadi)
- B) Battle of Khanwa
- C) Battle of Panipat
- D) Battle of Plassey
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Battle of Talikota (also known as the Battle of Rakkasa-Tangadi), fought in January 1565 near the village of Talikota on the Krishna river, saw a combined army of the five Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar and Berar) decisively defeat the Vijayanagara forces under Rama Raya. The defeat led to the sack and ruin of the city of Vijayanagara/Hampi and the end of Vijayanagara as a great imperial power. The Battle of Khanwa (1527) was Babur vs Rana Sanga; the First Battle of Panipat (1526) ended the Delhi Sultanate; the Battle of Plassey (1757) established British power in Bengal.

**Trap:** Distractors B, C and D are all famous Indian battles, but none involved the Deccan Sultanates or Vijayanagara.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=13
The Portuguese traveller Domingo Paes visited the Vijayanagara court of:

- A) Bukka I
- B) Devaraya I
- C) Krishnadevaraya
- D) Sadasiva Raya
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Domingo Paes, a Portuguese horse-trader and chronicler, visited the Vijayanagara capital during the reign of Krishnadevaraya (around 1520 CE) and left a vivid account of the city, court and the king's personality. His description, together with that of Duarte Barbosa, is one of the most important eyewitness sources for Vijayanagara's glory before Talikota. Bukka I ruled in the 14th century, well before any Portuguese presence in India. Devaraya I was a 15th-century Vijayanagara king. Sadasiva Raya ruled in the 1540s-1560s (with Rama Raya as regent) but is not the king Paes described.

**Trap:** Distractors A, B and D are all real Vijayanagara rulers, but the Portuguese chroniclers were contemporaries of Krishnadevaraya, not the earlier or later rulers.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=13
Match the following:

| Ruler | Event |
|---|---|
| 1. Krishnadevaraya | a. Wrote Amuktamalyada |
| 2. Sadasiva Raya | b. Deposed by Rama Raya |
| 3. Rama Raya | c. The real power behind the throne in the 1540s |

- A) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- B) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- D) 1-a, 2-c, 3-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

(1-a) Krishnadevaraya is the author of the Telugu poem Amuktamalyada. (2-b) Sadasiva Raya, the nominal ruler of the empire in the late 1540s-1560s, was sidelined and effectively deposed by his regent Rama Raya, who ran the empire and commanded the army at Talikota. (3-c) Rama Raya (also called Rama or Ramaraja of the Aravidu line) was the all-powerful regent behind Sadasiva Raya's throne from the mid-1540s onward. All three pairings in option A are correct; options B, C and D each scramble at least one pairing.

**Trap:** Option D reverses the roles of Sadasiva Raya and Rama Raya, which is exactly the swap students make when they confuse the nominal king with the de facto ruler.

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=13
Mahmud Gawan, a famous Bahmani vizier, was executed by:

- A) Ahmad Shah Wali
- B) Muhammad Shah III (Lashkari Shah)
- C) Hasan Gangu
- D) Hussain Nizam Shah
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Mahmud Gawan (also called Mahmud Gavan), a Persian nobleman who served the Bahmani sultans as a brilliant administrator, general and patron of learning, was executed in 1481 CE by Muhammad Shah III (Muhammad Shah Lashkari) on trumped-up charges of treason after falling out with court factions hostile to foreign (Afaqi) ministers. Ahmad Shah Wali had moved the capital to Bidar but was long past the throne when Gawan fell. Hasan Gangu had founded the Bahmani state in 1347, more than a century earlier. Hussain Nizam Shah was the founder of the post-Bahmani Ahmadnagar Sultanate, not a Bahmani sultan.

**Trap:** Distractors A, C and D are all real Bahmani/Deccan-era figures but each belongs to a different era; only Muhammad Shah III was the sultan on the throne at the time of Gawan's fall.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=13
Arrange in chronological order:

1. Founding of Vijayanagara
2. Founding of Bahmani Kingdom
3. Formation of the five Deccan Sultanates
4. Battle of Talikota (1565)

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 2, 1, 4, 3
- C) 1, 4, 2, 3
- D) 4, 3, 2, 1
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct chronological order is:

1. Founding of Vijayanagara (1336 CE) by Harihara I and Bukka I.
2. Founding of the Bahmani Kingdom (1347 CE) by Hasan Gangu, breaking from Delhi.
3. Formation of the five Deccan Sultanates (Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Golconda, Berar and Bidar) around the 1490s as the Bahmani state broke apart under Afaqi-Deccani feuds and weak successors.
4. Battle of Talikota (1565 CE), in which the combined Deccan sultans routed the Vijayanagara forces under Rama Raya.

Option B reverses Vijayanagara and Bahmani (placing Bahmani before 1336); option C places Talikota before the sultanates formed; option D reverses the entire sequence.

**Trap:** The common error is treating Bahmani (1347) as earlier than Vijayanagara (1336) because the Bahmanis were Islamic and predate, in popular memory, the "Hindu revival" of Vijayanagara; in fact, Vijayanagara was founded eleven years before the Bahmani state.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=13
Consider the statements:

1. The Vijayanagara Empire had a powerful navy that controlled the Bay of Bengal trade.
2. Krishnadevaraya defeated the Gajapatis of Odisha and the Bahmani Sultans in campaigns that made the empire the dominant power in South India.
3. The ruins of Hampi today are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Statements 2 and 3 are correct. (2) Krishnadevaraya's military campaigns against the Gajapati rulers of Odisha (consolidating control over the eastern coast up to the Godavari-Krishna doab) and against the Bahmani/Deccan sultans (most famously the campaign over Raichur) made the Vijayanagara Empire the dominant power in South India during his reign. (3) The ruins of Hampi, the capital city of Vijayanagara, were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986, covering the Sacred Centre, royal and sacred complexes, and over 1,600 surviving remains.

Statement 1 is incorrect or at best debatable: the Vijayanagara Empire was essentially a land-based power whose economy depended on agricultural surplus and inland trade; its maritime activity was limited and it did not "control the Bay of Bengal trade" in the way that, for example, the Cholas or later the European trading companies did.

**Trap:** Statement 1 is the kind of assertion students half-accept because the Cholas (just before) had a famous navy; the Vijayanagara Empire, however, was a successor inland power, not a maritime one.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.vijayanagara_and_bahmani_kingdom.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=13
The Bahmani Sultanate gave way to the five Deccan Sultanates primarily because of:

- A) Defeat by the Vijayanagara empire at Talikota
- B) Internal wars of succession and weakening of central authority
- C) Invasion by the Mughals
- D) Conversion of the Bahmanis to Christianity
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Bahmani Sultanate fractured into the five Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Berar and Bidar) during the 1490s because of internal decay: bitter Afaqi (foreign, mainly Persian/Arab) vs Deccani (local, Indian-born Muslim) factionalism, weak and short-reigned sultans, succession wars among the governors, and the collapse of central authority. These centrifugal forces broke the state apart before the end of the 15th century. The Battle of Talikota (1565 CE) happened AFTER this breakup, when the now-independent sultanates combined against Vijayanagara, so option A confuses cause and effect. There was no Bahmani conversion to Christianity (D), and Mughal invasions of the Deccan (C) occurred only in the 17th century under Akbar, Shah Jahan and Aurangzeb, well after the Bahmani state was gone.

**Trap:** Option A is the most attractive wrong answer because Talikota is the most famous Vijayanagara-vs-sultanates battle; but the sultanates were already independent states by the time of Talikota, so the breakup cannot have been caused by it.

**See also:** [[../../history/13 - Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom.md#Content|Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdom — source chapter]]
</details>
