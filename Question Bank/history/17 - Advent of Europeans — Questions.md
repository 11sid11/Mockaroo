# Advent of Europeans — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md|Advent of Europeans]]

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=17
Who among the following European explorers was the first to reach India by sea in 1498?

- A) Christopher Columbus
- B) Vasco da Gama
- C) Bartholomew Diaz
- D) Pedro Alvares Cabral
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Vasco da Gama reached Calicut (Kozhikode) on the Malabar coast on 20 May 1498 after rounding the Cape of Good Hope, becoming the first European to sail directly to India since the time of the ancients. Columbus (A), famously, landed in the Caribbean in 1492 and never reached India. Bartholomew Diaz (C) rounded the Cape of Good Hope in 1488 but turned back, and Cabral (D) was the Portuguese captain who, while heading to India, accidentally landed in Brazil in 1500 and then continued to Calicut.

**Trap:** Many students confuse Columbus with Vasco da Gama because both 1492 and 1498 are early-1490s voyages to "Asia". Columbus never reached India; Vasco da Gama did, in 1498.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=17
The Portuguese captain who captured Goa in 1510 was:

- A) Vasco da Gama
- B) Alfonso de Albuquerque
- C) Francisco de Almeida
- D) Pedro Alvares Cabral
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Alfonso de Albuquerque (c. 1453-1515), the second Portuguese governor of India, captured Goa from the Adil Shahi sultan of Bijapur in 1510 and made it the capital of Portuguese India in Asia, a status it retained for over 450 years until 1961. Vasco da Gama (A) came to India three times but never captured Goa. Francisco de Almeida (C) was the *first* Portuguese governor, more famous for his naval doctrine and his death in a skirmish with the Khoi-Khoi at Table Bay. Cabral (D) opened the factory at Calicut in 1500 but did not capture Goa.

**Trap:** Vasco da Gama and Albuquerque are often blurred together because both were early Portuguese governors in India, but Goa 1510 is unambiguously Albuquerque's signature conquest.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=17
The Dutch East India Company (VOC) was founded in:

- A) 1600
- B) 1602
- C) 1623
- D) 1650
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The **Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie (VOC)** was chartered by the States-General of the Dutch Republic on 20 March 1602, making it the world's first joint-stock, permanent, share-issuing company and the prototype of all later trading companies. 1600 (A) is the founding year of the English East India Company, its English rival. 1623 (C) is the year of the Amboina massacre (when the Dutch executed English traders), not the founding of VOC. 1650 (D) is far too late.

**Trap:** The English EIC (1600) and the Dutch VOC (1602) were founded almost simultaneously, and it is easy to mix up the dates by one year. The Dutch VOC came *after* the English EIC by exactly two years.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=17
The Battle of Plassey (1757) was fought between Robert Clive and:

- A) Siraj-ud-Daulah
- B) Mir Qasim
- C) Mir Jafar
- D) Shuja-ud-Daulah
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Battle of Plassey was fought on 23 June 1757 at Palashi (Plassey) in Bengal between the forces of the English East India Company under Robert Clive and the Nawab of Bengal, **Siraj-ud-Daulah**, with the treachery of Mir Jafar (C), the Nawab's commander-in-chief, who secretly defected to the British side during the battle. Mir Qasim (B) was Siraj-ud-Daulah's successor (after Mir Jafar), who lost the Battle of Buxar in 1764. Shuja-ud-Daulah (D) was the Nawab of Awadh, who fought the British at Buxar in 1764, not Plassey in 1757.

**Trap:** The most common confusion is between Siraj-ud-Daulah and Mir Jafar, both associated with Plassey. The enemy commander was Siraj-ud-Daulah; Mir Jafar was his treacherous general who switched sides. Clive installed Mir Jafar as Nawab after the battle.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=17
A student reads that a European trading company set up its first factory at Masulipatnam (1611) and later at Surat, with factories at Hooghly, Pulicat, and Nagapattinam. The company being described is:

- A) English East India Company
- B) Dutch East India Company (VOC)
- C) French East India Company
- D) Portuguese India
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The cluster of early-17th-century factories at Masulipatnam (Machilipatnam, 1611), Surat, Hooghly (Satgaon), Pulicat and Nagapattinam (Nagapattinam) all belong to the **English East India Company**, which established its first Indian factory at Masulipatnam in 1611 under William Hawkins and later expanded under Sir Thomas Roe (Embassy to Jahangir, 1615-1619) to Surat as its western-Indian headquarters. The Dutch VOC (B) also had Pulicat and Nagapattinam, but Masulipatnam-1611 and Surat are characteristic English footholds. The French (C) only arrived much later, in the second half of the 17th century. Portuguese India (D) was already in decline by 1611.

**Trap:** Pulicat and Nagapattinam were held by *both* the Dutch and the English at different times, so a question listing these two ports can mislead students into choosing the Dutch VOC. The decisive clue is Masulipatnam-1611 + Surat, both of which are signature English factories.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=17
The English East India Company was founded in:

- A) 1599
- B) 1600
- C) 1602
- D) 1612
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The English East India Company was chartered by Queen Elizabeth I on 31 December 1600 by a Royal Charter, making 1600 its official founding year. 1599 (A) is the year a group of London merchants first petitioned the Queen for a charter; that petition led to the December 1600 charter. 1602 (C) is the founding year of the Dutch VOC. 1612 (D) is the year the Company first set up factories on the east coast of India (Masulipatnam) and secured trading privileges from the Mughal court.

**Trap:** Students often answer 1599 because it is when the petition was signed, but the *company* in legal terms was chartered on 31 December 1600. SSC and UPSC both consistently treat 1600 as the founding year.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=17
Which of the following is INCORRECT about European trading in India?

- A) The Dutch concentrated on the spices of the Spice Islands and later on Indian textiles
- B) The French had their Indian headquarters at Pondicherry
- C) The Portuguese were the last European power to leave India
- D) The Danes had their Indian centre at Tranquebar
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Statement C is incorrect: the **Portuguese were the *first*** European power to arrive in India (Vasco da Gama, 1498) and, in fact, were the *last* to leave: Goa remained Portuguese until it was annexed by India in December 1961. France (B) is correct: Pondicherry (Puducherry) was the French Indian capital, with Chandernagore, Mahe, Karikal and Yanam as subordinate territories. France ceded these to India only in 1954 (Pondicherry, Karikal, Mahe, Yanam) and 1956 (Chandernagore). The Danes (D) had their main Indian centre at Tranquebar (Tharangambadi) on the Tamil coast from 1620 until 1845, when Denmark sold all its Indian possessions to Britain. Statement A is correct: the Dutch VOC was founded primarily to break the Portuguese monopoly of the spice trade of the Moluccas (Spice Islands) and shifted to Indian textiles (calico, muslin, chintz) only later.

**Trap:** "Last to leave" is the trap word: many students remember the Portuguese as the *first* to come and so will not pick (C). The correct way to read it is that the Portuguese were the *first to arrive*, while the French were the *last to leave European political control* (1954) *except for* the Portuguese, who held on until 1961.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=17
Match the following European powers with their principal settlements in India:

| Power | Settlements |
|---|---|
| 1. Portuguese | a. Daman, Diu, Goa |
| 2. Dutch | b. Cochin, Masulipatnam, Pulicat |
| 3. French | c. Pondicherry, Chandernagore, Mahe |

- A) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- B) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- C) 1-a, 2-c, 3-b
- D) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct match is: Portuguese (1) — Daman, Diu, Goa (a); Dutch (2) — Cochin, Masulipatnam, Pulicat (b); French (3) — Pondicherry, Chandernagore, Mahe (c). Option B reverses Portuguese and Dutch (incorrect: Pulicat and Masulipatnam are well-documented Dutch factories, while Daman/Diu/Goa were never Dutch). Option C reverses Dutch and French. Option D reverses all three.

**Trap:** Masulipatnam, Pulicat and Cochin are the trickiest items because the English EIC and the Dutch VOC *both* had factories there at different times. In this question, the trio is correctly assigned to the Dutch, who held Cochin from 1663 to 1795 and Pulicat and Masulipatnam at various periods in the 17th century.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=17
Who among the following became the first Portuguese Viceroy of India in 1505?

- A) Alfonso de Albuquerque
- B) Francisco de Almeida
- C) Vasco da Gama
- D) Pedro Alvares Cabral
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

**Francisco de Almeida** (c. 1450-1510) was appointed the first Portuguese Viceroy (Governor) of India by King Manuel I and arrived at Cochin in October 1505. He is famous for the *blue-water policy* (the belief that Portuguese mastery of the seas would be enough to keep them in India, rather than building inland fortresses). Albuquerque (A) was the second Governor, succeeding Almeida in 1509 after Almeida's death at Table Bay. Vasco da Gama (C) came to India three times but held no permanent viceregal office before his death in 1524. Cabral (D) led the 1500 fleet that discovered Brazil on the way but was never a viceroy.

**Trap:** Albuquerque is far more famous than Almeida because of his conquests (Goa 1510, Malacca 1511, Hormuz 1515), so many students instinctively pick (A) for "first viceroy". But Almeida was the *first* governor/viceroy; Albuquerque was the *second*.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=17
Arrange the following events in correct chronological order:

1. Vasco da Gama reaches Calicut
2. Portuguese capture of Goa
3. Founding of the English East India Company
4. Battle of Plassey

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 1, 3, 2, 4
- C) 2, 1, 4, 3
- D) 4, 1, 3, 2
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct chronological order, with dates, is: (1) Vasco da Gama reaches Calicut, 20 May 1498 — (2) Portuguese capture of Goa, 1510 — (3) Founding of the English East India Company, 31 December 1600 — (4) Battle of Plassey, 23 June 1757. So the order is 1, 2, 3, 4 (option A). Option B incorrectly puts the English EIC (1600) before the capture of Goa (1510). Option C reverses the first two events. Option D begins with the latest event (Plassey, 1757), which is absurd chronologically.

**Trap:** The English East India Company (1600) and the Battle of Plassey (1757) both belong to the British phase, and a student who organises by "European power" rather than by date may try to put them adjacent to each other. The test is purely chronological: 1498 — 1510 — 1600 — 1757.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=17
Consider the following statements:

1. The English East India Company began as a purely commercial body but later acquired political and military power.
2. The Dutch ceded most of their Indian possessions to the English in the 17th-18th centuries.
3. The French East India Company founded Chandernagore and Pondicherry, but lost political influence after the Battle of Wandiwash (1760).

Which of the above are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. (1) The English East India Company was founded in 1600 as a purely commercial joint-stock venture, but from the mid-18th century, especially after Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764), it exercised de facto sovereign functions: it maintained armies, minted currency, administered Bengal, and eventually, after 1858, ruled all of India directly. (2) The Dutch ceded their Indian holdings to the British by a series of treaties: Cochin was ceded to the British in 1795 during the Revolutionary Wars, and the remaining Dutch posts in India were eventually transferred to the British in the 19th century. (3) The French East India Company founded Chandernagore (1673) and Pondicherry (1674), but its political ambitions in India were crushed by the British in the Carnatic Wars, especially at the Battle of Wandiwash (1760) and the fall of Pondicherry (1761); French India thereafter remained a handful of trading posts without political influence.

**Trap:** Statement 2 can be mis-remembered: students often think it was the Portuguese (not the Dutch) who lost to the English first. In fact, the Dutch VOC was driven out of the Indian mainland by the Anglo-Dutch conflicts and the Napoleonic Wars; the Portuguese lost to *India* in 1961, not to the English.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.advent_of_europeans.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=17
The "Subsidiary Alliance" system in India was introduced by:

- A) Robert Clive
- B) Warren Hastings
- C) Lord Wellesley
- D) Lord Cornwallis
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Subsidiary Alliance system was introduced by **Lord Wellesley** (Governor-General 1798-1805), largely through treaties with the Nizam of Hyderabad (1798), the Nawab of Awadh (1801), the Maratha Peshwa Baji Rao II (1802, Treaty of Bassein), and several smaller states. Under a subsidiary alliance, an Indian state surrendered its foreign affairs to the British, paid for the upkeep of a British army stationed within its territory, and disbanded its own military. Robert Clive (A) won Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764) and laid the groundwork for British political power, but did not systematise it. Warren Hastings (B) consolidated Bengal but is associated with the Rohilla War and the Regulating Act. Lord Cornwallis (D) introduced the Permanent Settlement (1793) and civil-service reforms, not subsidiary alliance.

**Trap:** Students confuse Cornwallis and Wellesley because both were late-18th-century Governors-General. Cornwallis = Permanent Settlement (zamindari); Wellesley = Subsidiary Alliance. The "Subsidiary" in Subsidiary Alliance refers to a *subsidiary* (subsidised) British force, not to Cornwallis's Permanent Settlement.

**See also:** [[../../history/17 - Advent of Europeans.md#Content|Advent of Europeans — source chapter]]
</details>
