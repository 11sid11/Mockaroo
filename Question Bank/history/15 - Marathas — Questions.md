# Marathas — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md|Marathas]]

@qhis.marathas.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=15
The Maratha king Shivaji was crowned Chhatrapati at:

- A) Raigad in 1674
- B) Pune in 1670
- C) Torna in 1646
- D) Pratapgad in 1659
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Shivaji was formally crowned Chhatrapati (a sovereign Hindu royal title meaning "`shadow of the supreme`") on 6 June 1674 CE at Raigad Fort, the hill capital he had wrested from the Sultanate of Bijapur. The coronation was performed by the Brahmin scholar Gagabhatt, who was specifically summoned from Varanasi to give it ritual legitimacy. Pune (option B) was Shivaji's later administrative center under the Peshwas but never the coronation site. Torna (option C) was Shivaji's first major fort conquest (1646 CE) at age 16, important but a capture, not a coronation. Pratapgad (option D) was the fort where the famous 1659 encounter with Afzal Khan took place, which is also wrongly conflated with the coronation date.

**Trap:** Options C and D both name real Shivaji-era forts associated with real Shivaji events (the first fort capture and the Afzal Khan encounter respectively), which makes them the strongest distractors; students must keep the coronation separate from these other landmarks and remember 1674 at Raigad.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=15
The Treaty of Purandar (1665) was signed between Shivaji and:

- A) Aurangzeb
- B) Dara Shukoh
- C) Mirza Raja Jai Singh (Mughal general)
- D) Shaista Khan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Treaty of Purandar (June 1665 CE) was signed between Shivaji and Mirza Raja Jai Singh I, the Mughal general (and Rajput noble of Amber/Jaipur) sent by Aurangzeb to subdue the Marathas after Shaista Khan's failed campaigns. Under the treaty, Shivaji ceded 23 of his 35 forts to the Mughals, retained 12, and agreed to serve the Mughal emperor as a vassal. Aurangzeb (option A) was the Mughal emperor but did not personally negotiate at Purandar; his representative Jai Singh did. Dara Shukoh (option B) was Aurangzeb's elder brother, defeated at the War of Succession in 1658 CE and dead by 1659 CE - well before Purandar. Shaista Khan (option D) was the Mughal governor of the Deccan whom Shivaji had famously raided in 1663 CE at Pune (the famous night attack on Shaista Khan), but he was not the treaty signatory.

**Trap:** Option A is the strongest distractor because Aurangzeb was the emperor on whose orders Jai Singh acted; students who remember only "`Mughal emperor`" but not the actual negotiator will pick A. Option D is the trap for students who confuse the famous 1663 Pune raid with the 1665 treaty.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=15
The Third Battle of Panipat (1761) was fought between Marathas and:

- A) Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani)
- B) Abdali and the Nizam
- C) Hyder Ali
- D) The British
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Third Battle of Panipat (14 January 1761 CE) was fought between the Maratha Confederacy, led by Sadashivrao Bhau (with Vishwasrao and the Peshwa's son) along with Ibrahim Ali Gardi, and Ahmad Shah Abdali (also called Ahmad Shah Durrani), the Afghan ruler from Kabul, who was supported by Rohilla Afghans and the Nawab of Awadh, Shuja-ud-Daula. The battle was a catastrophic defeat for the Marathas and effectively halted their northward expansion. Option B (Abdali and the Nizam) is partially true in that the Nizam of Hyderabad, Nizam Ali (Salabat Jung), was on the Abdali side, but the core opposition to the Marathas was led by Abdali, not the Nizam. Hyder Ali (option C) was the ruler of Mysore in the south and was not involved at Panipat 1761. The British (option D) were not involved at the Third Battle of Panipat; their role in defeating the Marathas came in the three Anglo-Maratha Wars of 1775-1818 CE.

**Trap:** Option B is engineered to confuse students who remember that the Nizam also fought the Marathas at Kharda (1795); at Panipat 1761 the Nizam was only a secondary partner of Abdali, not the main opponent. Option D is the trap for students who conflate the post-1761 Anglo-Maratha wars with the earlier Panipat defeat.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=15
"Chauth" was a tax equal to:

- A) One-eighth of the land revenue
- B) One-fourth of the land revenue
- C) Half of the land revenue
- D) One-tenth of the land revenue
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Chauth (literally "`one-fourth`") was a protection/protection-cum-tribute tax levied by the Marathas, mainly the Peshwas and their sardars, equal to one-fourth (25%) of the land revenue of a given territory, collected from areas not directly under Maratha administration in return for "`protection`" from Maratha raids. Its companion levy was Sardeshmukhi, an additional 10% claimed from the same territories by the Maratha chieftain who held the sardeshmukhi (headship) right. Together, the "`Chauth-Sardeshmukhi`" became the financial backbone of the Maratha state and paralleled a military fiscal system. Option A (one-eighth) is wrong. Option C (half) is wrong and is the trap for students who assume a "`heavy tribute.`" Option D (one-tenth) is closer to Sardeshmukhi, the sister tax, not Chauth itself.

**Trap:** Option D is the strongest distractor because Sardeshmukhi (10%) was the parallel Maratha levy and students frequently confuse the two; the question specifies Chauth, which is specifically one-fourth. Option A is the trap for students who half-remember "`a small share of revenue`" without recalling it was 25%.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=15
A student reads that the Maratha revenue and military system was the "Chauth-Sardeshmukhi" with a parallel military finance through deshmukhs and watandars. The system being described is:

- A) The mansabdari system of the Mughals
- B) The Maratha revenue-cum-military system
- C) The iqta system
- D) The samanta system
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The system being described is the Maratha revenue-cum-military system, in which (i) Chauth (1/4 of land revenue) and Sardeshmukhi (an additional 1/10) were the two taxes collected by the Marathas from territories not directly under their administration; and (ii) deshmukhs and watandars (hereditary local officials holding watan/jagir lands) financed and provided the cavalry and infantry contingents that made up the Maratha army. The mansabdari system (option A) was the Mughal ranking system of nobles by zat and sawar ranks, not a tax levy. The iqta system (option C) was the Delhi Sultanate's land-revenue-cum-military assignment. The samanta system (option D) was the early medieval Indian practice of vassal chieftains paying tribute; it is conceptually related but refers to an earlier period and not to the specific Maratha combination.

**Trap:** Option A is tempting because the Mughals and Marathas co-existed and both had ranking systems, but mansabdari was specifically Mughal and did not feature Chauth-Sardeshmukhi. Option D is the trap for students who think all Indian "`vassal + tribute`" arrangements are the samanta system; the Maratha version is distinctive enough to have its own name.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=15
Match the following:

| Peshwa | Associated event |
|---|---|
| 1. Balaji Vishwanath | a. Baji Rao I succeeded his father |
| 2. Baji Rao I | b. Treaty of Bassein |
| 3. Baji Rao II | c. Became Peshwa by eliminating rivals |

- A) 1-c, 2-a, 3-b
- B) 1-b, 2-c, 3-a
- C) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- D) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct matching is 1-c, 2-a, 3-b. Balaji Vishwanath (1), who became Peshwa in 1713 CE, is famous for becoming Peshwa by outmaneuvering and eliminating his rivals (notably Kanhoji Shirke and the influential Maratha noble families) and for being the father of the celebrated warrior Peshwa Baji Rao I; that rivalry-elimination is event c. Baji Rao I (2), who succeeded his father Balaji Vishwanath in 1720 CE and served until 1740 CE, was a brilliant military Peshwa who expanded Maratha influence across north India up to Delhi and into Rajasthan and Malwa; that direct father-to-son succession is event a. Baji Rao II (3), Peshwa from 1795 to 1818 CE, signed the Treaty of Bassein with the British in 1802 CE, an act that effectively made him a British client and began the end of Maratha independence; that is event b. Options B, C and D scramble one or more of these pairings.

**Trap:** Option D is the strongest trap because it puts Baji Rao II with the "`succeeded his father`" claim, which is confusing since Baji Rao II DID succeed his father (Raghunath Rao / Raghoba) but his more famous association is with Bassein, so the right pairing of 3 with b makes A correct. Option C scrambles all three pairings.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=15
After the Third Battle of Panipat (1761), the Maratha confederacy was reconstructed under whose leadership?

- A) Madhavrao Peshwa
- B) Balaji Bajirao (Nanasaheb)
- C) Bajirao II
- D) Mahadji Shinde
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

After the devastating Maratha defeat at the Third Battle of Panipat (1761 CE), the Maratha confederacy was reconstructed under the leadership of the young Peshwa Madhavrao I (Peshwa 1761-1772 CE), son of Balaji Bajirao (Nanasaheb) and grandson of Baji Rao I. Madhavrao restored Maratha finances, reasserted Peshwa authority over the fractious sardars (Holkar, Scindia, Bhonsle, Gaekwad), and recovered many of the territories lost at Panipat. Balaji Bajirao (Nanasaheb), option B, was Madhavrao's father but is associated with the period immediately before and during Panipat (he was Peshwa 1740-1761 CE and died shortly after the battle, reportedly of shock). Bajirao II (option C) was the last Peshwa (1795-1818 CE) who signed the Treaty of Bassein and lost the remaining Maratha independence. Mahadji Shinde (option D) was the Scindia chief who recovered Delhi for the Marathas in 1771 CE - an important action, but he served UNDER Madhavrao's broader reconstruction, not as the primary leader.

**Trap:** Option B is the strongest trap because Nanasaheb was the Peshwa at the time of Panipat; but the reconstruction after the defeat was led by his SON Madhavrao. Option D is the trap for students who remember Mahadji Shinde's dramatic recapture of Delhi in 1771 but forget that the broader confederacy-wide reconstruction was led by the Peshwa.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=15
Who among the following Peshwas signed the Treaty of Bassein (1802) with the British?

- A) Balaji Vishwanath
- B) Baji Rao I
- C) Baji Rao II
- D) Nana Sahib
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Treaty of Bassein (also called the Treaty of Vasai, signed on 31 December 1802 CE) was signed between Baji Rao II (Peshwa 1795-1818 CE) and the British East India Company (represented by the Governor General, Lord Wellesley). Under its terms, Baji Rao II ceded territory and accepted a subsidiary alliance, which in effect made the Peshwa a British client and triggered the Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803-1805 CE). Balaji Vishwanath (option A) was the Peshwa who concluded the Treaty of Purandar-style settlements in the early 1700s and made the Peshwa hereditary, not Bassein. Baji Rao I (option B) was a famous military Peshwa (1720-1740 CE) who expanded Maratha power northward to Delhi and into Rajasthan and the Deccan, but lived a century before Bassein. Nana Sahib (option D) was the adopted son of Baji Rao II and led the 1857 rebellion at Kanpur, well after the Peshwa's downfall in 1818.

**Trap:** Option B (Baji Rao I) is the most attractive wrong answer because Baji Rao I is the most famous Peshwa and his name is associated with territorial expansion; but it was his descendant Baji Rao II who signed Bassein. Option A is the trap for students who confuse Balaji Vishwanath's earlier Purandar-era diplomacy with Baji Rao II's Bassein signing.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=15
Which of the following is INCORRECT about Shivaji's administration?

- A) The "Ashtapradhan" was a council of eight ministers
- B) The revenue system included "Kathi" and "Ryotwari"
- C) The military was organized into infantry (Mavla), cavalry, and navy
- D) Shivaji abolished the Hindu festival of Diwali
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

Statement D is INCORRECT: Shivaji did not abolish Diwali. Diwali is the Hindu festival of lights, celebrated across India, and was actively promoted by Shivaji; the very coronation at Raigad in 1674 CE was timed to coincide with a Hindu auspicious festival. Far from abolishing festivals, Shivaji used Hindu religious identity to mobilize the Maratha identity against Mughal pressure. Statements A, B and C are all correct: the Ashtapradhan was indeed a council of eight ministers headed by the Peshwa and others (Amatya, Sachiva, Mantri, Senapati, Sumant, Nyayadhish, Panditrao, and Dandadhikari); Shivaji's revenue system did include "`Kathi`" (a rough area measurement unit) and "`Ryotwari`" was the mode of settlement directly with the cultivator; and the Maratha military under Shivaji had Mavla infantry, the cavalry under his sardars (notably the "`Bargi`" or "`Silahdar`" horsemen), and a navy commanded by Kanhoji Angre and others.

**Trap:** Option D is designed to test whether students know that Shivaji was a champion of Hindu identity, not its opponent; the absurdity of "`abolished Diwali`" is the giveaway that this is the INCORRECT statement. Options A, B and C each contain real Maratha-administration terms.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=15
Arrange in chronological order of Shivaji's life events:

1. Treaty of Purandar
2. Agra visit to Aurangzeb's court
3. Coronation at Raigad
4. Death

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 1, 2, 4, 3
- C) 2, 1, 3, 4
- D) 3, 1, 2, 4
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct chronological order of Shivaji's life events is 1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 4. (1) The Treaty of Purandar was signed on 11 June 1665 CE between Shivaji and Mirza Raja Jai Singh, under which Shivaji ceded 23 forts. (2) Shivaji's visit to Aurangzeb's court at Agra happened in 1666 CE, after he went ostensibly as a Mughal vassal and was placed under house arrest, escaping dramatically in a fruit basket. (3) The coronation at Raigad took place on 6 June 1674 CE. (4) Shivaji died on 3 April 1680 CE at Raigad. So the order is Purandar (1665) -> Agra visit (1666) -> Coronation (1674) -> Death (1680), which is option A. Option B places death before coronation, which is impossible. Option C swaps Purandar and the Agra visit. Option D begins with coronation, which is clearly wrong because both Purandar and Agra pre-date 1674.

**Trap:** Option C is the most attractive wrong answer because students may half-remember "`Shivaji went to Agra`" and "`Treaty of Purandar`" but reverse which came first; in fact, Purandar (1665) preceded the Agra visit (1666) by about a year. Option B is a memory slip that places death before coronation.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=15
Consider the statements:

1. The Peshwas were originally Prime Ministers of the Maratha king, but became the de facto rulers.
2. The Battle of Kharda (1795) saw the Maratha Peshwa defeat the Nizam.
3. The Maratha confederacy was finally defeated by the British in the Third Anglo-Maratha War (1818).

Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. (1) The Peshwas were originally the Prime Ministers (literally "`foremost`" in Persian) under the Chhatrapati (Maratha king); over time, beginning with Balaji Vishwanath (1713 CE), the Peshwa became hereditary and from Baji Rao I onwards exercised de facto control over the Maratha state even though the Chhatrapati remained the nominal sovereign. (2) The Battle of Kharda (11 March 1795 CE) was fought between the forces of the Peshwa (under the broader Nana Fadnavis-led campaign and Baji Rao II's army) and the Nizam of Hyderabad; the Marathas won a decisive victory. (3) The Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817-1818 CE) culminated in the defeat of the Peshwa Baji Rao II at the Battle of Khadki and the Battle of Koregaon, the surrender of Baji Rao II, and the formal end of Maratha political power - the "`final defeat`" of the confederacy. Therefore, all three statements (1, 2, and 3) are correct, making option D the right answer.

**Trap:** Option A (1 and 2 only) and Option B (2 and 3 only) are both traps that omit one true statement; the test is whether students can verify all three independently. Option C is the trap for students who wrongly believe the confederacy ended earlier (e.g., at Bassein 1802 or after Panipat 1761) rather than at the Third Anglo-Maratha War 1818.

**See also:** [[../../history/15 - Marathas.md#Content|Marathas — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.marathas.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=15
The Maratha sardars under the Peshwas included:

- A) The Scindias of Gwalior, Holkars of Indore, Gaekwads of Baroda, Bhonsles of Nagpur
- B) The Rajputs of Amber, Jaipur, Mewar, Marwar
- C) The Nawab-Wazirs of Awadh, Bengal, Arcot, Hyderabad
- D) The Sikh Misls of Punjab
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The five great Maratha sardar families (also called "`the five houses of the Maratha Confederacy`") were: (i) the Peshwas of Pune (the de facto prime ministers who led the state), (ii) the Scindias (Shindes) of Gwalior under Mahadji Shinde and Daulat Rao Scindia, (iii) the Holkars of Indore under Malhar Rao Holkar and Tukoji Holkar, (iv) the Gaekwads (Gaikwads) of Baroda under Damaji Rao Gaekwad and later Sayaji Rao, and (v) the Bhonsles of Nagpur under Raghoji Bhonsle and later Appa Sahib Bhonsle. So option A correctly lists four of the five major Maratha houses (excluding the Peshwas of Pune themselves). The Rajputs (option B) were a different warrior class and polity, mainly in Rajasthan. The Nawab-Wazirs (option C) were Muslim provincial rulers (Awadh, Bengal, etc.) and not Maratha sardars at all. The Sikh Misls (option D) were the 12 Sikh warrior bands of Punjab under Ranjit Singh's later unification - not Maratha.

**Trap:** Option B (Rajputs) is the strongest trap because Rajputana and Maratha territory overlapped (parts of Rajasthan were under Maratha Chauth by the 1750s) and the Rajputs are also a famous warrior class; but they were not Maratha sardars. Option C is the trap for students who confuse "`regional Indian rulers of the 18th century`" with Maratha houses.
