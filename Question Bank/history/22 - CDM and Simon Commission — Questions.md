# CDM and Simon Commission — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md|CDM and Simon Commission]]

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=22
The Simon Commission arrived in India in:

- A) 1927
- B) 1928
- C) 1929
- D) 1930
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Indian Statutory Commission, popularly known as the Simon Commission (after its chairman Sir John Simon), was constituted by the British Government in November 1927 under the Government of India Act of 1919 to review the working of the constitutional reforms of 1919 and to propose further reforms. The seven-member commission arrived in India on 3 February 1928 and was met everywhere by hostile demonstrations. The reason for the hostility was that the commission had no Indian member - Indians demanded that Indians themselves should have a say in framing their constitution. The protest slogan "Simon go back" became one of the most famous rallying cries of Indian nationalism of the late 1920s.

**Trap:** A (1927) is the year the commission was *constituted* in London, not the year it arrived in India. C (1929) is the year of the Lahore session of the Congress where Purna Swaraj was declared. D (1930) is the year of the Dandi March.

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=22
The Civil Disobedience Movement began with:

- A) The Salt March (Dandi March, 1930)
- B) The Quit India Resolution
- C) The Khilafat Movement
- D) The Champaran Satyagraha
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM), launched in 1930, was inaugurated by the Salt March (Dandi March), in which Mahatma Gandhi led 78 satyagrahis from the Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March 1930 to the coastal village of Dandi (about 385 km away), arriving on 5 April 1930; on 6 April 1930 Gandhi broke the British salt monopoly by picking up a handful of natural salt from the seashore. This symbolic act of breaking the 1882 Salt Act sparked a nationwide movement of civil disobedience in which Indians broke the salt law, boycotted foreign cloth, refused to pay the chaukidari tax in Bihar, and raided government forest lands.

**Trap:** B (Quit India Resolution) was the August 1942 call for immediate British withdrawal - two entirely different movements, separated by twelve years. C (Khilafat Movement) was the 1919-24 pan-Islamic agitation against the dismemberment of the Ottoman Empire, unrelated to salt law-breaking. D (Champaran Satyagraha) was the 1917 indigo-planters' campaign in Bihar, which was a satyagraha but not a civil-disobedience movement (the latter involves deliberate law-breaking).

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=22
Lala Lajpat Rai died after being injured during:

- A) A protest against the Simon Commission (lathi charge at Lahore, 1928)
- B) The Dandi March
- C) The Chittagong Armoury Raid
- D) The Quit India protests
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Lala Lajpat Rai (1865-1928), one of the three leaders of the "Lal-Bal-Pal" trio of the Extremist phase of the Congress and a major Hindu leader of the Punjab, was fatally injured on 30 October 1928 when the police brutally lathi-charged an anti-Simon Commission protest march in Lahore, leaving him with injuries from which he died on 17 November 1928. The British officer in charge of the lathi charge was later identified as police superintendent James A. Scott; Rai's death led to a nationwide outrage and inspired revolutionaries like Bhagat Singh (whose group assassinated police officer John P. Saunders in December 1928 in mistaken retaliation).

**Trap:** B (Dandi March) was the 1930 Salt March, two years after Lala Lajpat Rai's death. C (Chittagong Armoury Raid) was the 1930 raid led by Surya Sen in Bengal, also unrelated. D (Quit India protests) was the 1942 movement, fourteen years later.

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=22
The Gandhi-Irwin Pact was signed in:

- A) 1930
- B) 1931
- C) 1932
- D) 1929
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Gandhi-Irwin Pact (also called the Delhi Pact) was signed on 5 March 1931 between Mahatma Gandhi (on behalf of the Indian National Congress) and Lord Irwin (the Viceroy of India, later Earl of Halifax) at Viceroy's House in Delhi. By its terms, the Congress agreed to suspend the Civil Disobedience Movement and to participate in the second Round Table Conference; in return the British Government agreed to release all political prisoners not convicted of violence, to permit the free collection of sea-salt along the coast, to remit the fines on confiscated lands, and to withdraw the prosecutions against those who had manufactured or sold contraband salt. The pact was followed by Gandhi's attendance at the second Round Table Conference in London (September-December 1931).

**Trap:** A (1930) is the year the CDM was launched, not the year the pact was signed. C (1932) is the year of the third Round Table Conference and of the Poona Pact. D (1929) is the year of the Lahore session of the Congress at which Purna Swaraj was declared.

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=22
A student reads that the Simon Commission provoked a wave of nationalist protests because all its members were British (no Indian representation). The protest slogan adopted by the Indian National Congress was:

- A) "Simon Go Back"
- B) "Vande Mataram"
- C) "Jai Hind"
- D) "Inquilab Zindabad"
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The slogan adopted by the Indian National Congress and the wider Indian political class against the Simon Commission was "Simon Go Back" (sometimes rendered "Simon, Go Back!"). Wherever the commission's members went - Bombay, Calcutta, Lahore, Madras, Allahabad and other cities - they were greeted with black flags, hartals, and shouted slogans of "Simon Go Back." The Muslim League, the Hindu Mahasabha, and the Congress alike united in boycotting the commission. The slogan encapsulated the central Indian objection: that a body to recommend India's constitutional future could not legitimately be composed entirely of British members.

**Trap:** B ("Vande Mataram") was the late 19th-century Bankim Chandra Chatterjee hymn that became a patriotic slogan during the Swadeshi Movement of 1905-08 - a generation before the Simon Commission. C ("Jai Hind") was a slogan popularised by Subhas Chandra Bose and the Indian National Army in the 1940s, more than a decade later. D ("Inquilab Zindabad", meaning "Long Live the Revolution!") was a slogan associated with Bhagat Singh and the revolutionaries of the late 1920s, but it was not the specific Congress slogan against the Simon Commission.

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=22
The Indian National Congress declared Purna Swaraj (complete independence) on:

- A) 26 January 1930 (at the Lahore session)
- B) 15 August 1947
- C) 1 January 1927
- D) 26 November 1949
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Purna Swaraj (complete independence, as opposed to the previously demanded dominion status or self-government within the British Empire) was declared as the goal of the Indian National Congress by Jawaharlal Nehru at the historic Lahore session of the Congress held in December 1929 under the presidency of Jawaharlal Nehru (himself). The session resolved that 26 January 1930 should be celebrated all over India as the first "Independence Day" (Purna Swaraj Day), and on that day Indians took a pledge of independence. The 26 January date was later chosen as the date for the Republic of India after independence, when the Constitution of India came into effect on 26 January 1950.

**Trap:** B (15 August 1947) is the date of actual independence from British rule, but it is not the date of the *declaration* of Purna Swaraj - that was the December 1929 Lahore session. C (1 January 1927) is unrelated and was not a date of any major Congress declaration. D (26 November 1949) is the date on which the Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly, again unrelated to the Purna Swaraj declaration.

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=22
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the Civil Disobedience Movement?

- A) It began with the Dandi March (March-April 1930)
- B) It led to the Round Table Conference in London
- C) It was withdrawn after the Gandhi-Irwin Pact of March 1931
- D) It achieved complete independence for India
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

Statement D is incorrect. The Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-34) did not achieve complete independence for India; India became independent only on 15 August 1947, seventeen years after the CDM began. The CDM did, however, achieve important intermediate political gains: it forced the British to negotiate, produced the Gandhi-Irwin Pact of March 1931, and led to the Round Table Conferences. After the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, Gandhi attended the second Round Table Conference (September-December 1931) in London. The movement was resumed in January 1932 after the failure of the second RTC, and was finally withdrawn in May 1934.

**Trap:** A, B and C are all correct. A correctly identifies the Dandi March (12 March to 6 April 1930) as the inaugural event of the CDM. B correctly states that the CDM was the immediate cause of the Round Table Conference (the first RTC was held in November 1930 - January 1931 in London, although the Congress boycotted the first RTC). C correctly states that the CDM was suspended in March 1931 under the Gandhi-Irwin Pact. Only D is the incorrect statement.

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=22
Match the following:

| Event | Year |
|---|---|
| 1. Simon Commission | a. 1931 |
| 2. Salt March begins | b. 1928 |
| 3. Gandhi-Irwin Pact | c. 1930 |

- A) 1-b, 2-c, 3-a
- B) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- C) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- D) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct matching is 1-b, 2-c, 3-a. (1) The Simon Commission arrived in India in February 1928, so event 1 matches year (b) 1928. (2) The Salt March (Dandi March) began on 12 March 1930 from Sabarmati Ashram and ended at Dandi on 6 April 1930, so event 2 matches year (c) 1930. (3) The Gandhi-Irwin Pact was signed on 5 March 1931 between Mahatma Gandhi and Lord Irwin, so event 3 matches year (a) 1931.

**Trap:** B reverses the Simon Commission and Salt March (placing Simon in 1930 and Salt March in 1928) — clearly impossible, because the Simon Commission preceded the Salt March by two years. C assigns the Simon Commission to 1931, which is the year of the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, not the Simon Commission. D places the Gandhi-Irwin Pact in 1928, the year the Simon Commission arrived, which is three years too early.

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=22
The Round Table Conferences were held in:

- A) London (1930, 1931, 1932)
- B) Calcutta (1928)
- C) Lahore (1929)
- D) Delhi (1935)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The three Round Table Conferences were all held in London at the Royal Gallery of the House of Lords and the Council Chamber of India Office. The First Round Table Conference was held from 12 November 1930 to 19 January 1931 (boycotted by the Indian National Congress but attended by the Muslim League, the Hindu Mahasabha, the Liberal Federation, and various princes and zamindars). The Second RTC ran from 7 September 1931 to 1 December 1931 and was attended by Gandhi on behalf of the Congress. The Third RTC ran from 17 November 1932 to 24 December 1932 and was attended by the Congress after the unconditional release of political prisoners.

**Trap:** B (Calcutta, 1928) is the site of the All-Parties Conference (with the Nehru Committee on constitutional principles), not of a Round Table Conference. C (Lahore, 1929) is the site of the historic Congress session where Purna Swaraj was declared, not a Round Table Conference. D (Delhi, 1935) is unrelated - in 1935 the British Parliament passed the Government of India Act of 1935, but it was not a conference held at Delhi.

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=22
Arrange in chronological order:

1. Simon Commission arrives
2. Lahore Session — Purna Swaraj declared
3. Dandi March begins
4. Gandhi-Irwin Pact

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 2, 1, 3, 4
- C) 1, 3, 2, 4
- D) 4, 1, 2, 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct chronological order is 1, 2, 3, 4: Simon Commission arrives (February 1928) —> Lahore Session and declaration of Purna Swaraj (December 1929) —> Dandi March begins (12 March 1930) —> Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931). All four events belong to the tightly-packed period 1928-1931 of late nationalist history: the Simon Commission triggered the widespread protest; the Lahore session of the Congress (December 1929) raised the demand for Purna Swaraj in response to the British refusal to concede dominion status; the Dandi March (March-April 1930) inaugurated the Civil Disobedience Movement; and the Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 1931) suspended the CDM.

**Trap:** B places the Lahore session before the Simon Commission (1929 before 1928) - impossible, because the Lahore session was a response to the Simon Commission. C places the Dandi March before the Lahore session (March 1930 before December 1929) - impossible, because the Dandi March was launched *because* of the Purna Swaraj declaration. D places the Gandhi-Irwin Pact first (1931 before 1928) - clearly impossible.

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=22
Consider the statements:

1. The CDM involved the breaking of the salt law, the boycotting of foreign cloth, and the burning of British goods.
2. The Poona Pact (1932) was between Gandhi and Ambedkar regarding separate electorates for Depressed Classes.
3. The First Round Table Conference was attended by the Indian National Congress.

Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Statements 1 and 2 are correct; statement 3 is incorrect. (1) The Civil Disobedience Movement of 1930-34 was marked by the deliberate breaking of the salt law (culminating in the Dandi March and its offshoots along the entire Indian coast), the boycotting of foreign cloth (in favour of khadi), the refusal to pay land revenue and chaukidari taxes in selected provinces, the raiding of government forest lands in Central Provinces, and the public burning of foreign cloth. (2) The Poona Pact of 20 September 1932 was indeed an agreement signed between Mahatma Gandhi (representing the Indian National Congress and the Harijan cause) and Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (representing the Depressed Classes, who had been awarded separate electorates by the Communal Award of August 1932). By the pact, Ambedkar accepted joint electorates with reserved seats in lieu of separate electorates for Depressed Classes, and in return Gandhi agreed to a larger number of reserved seats and to certain other safeguards.

(3) is incorrect: the Indian National Congress *boycotted* the First Round Table Conference (November 1930 - January 1931). The reason was that the Congress objected to the conference being held at all, in the context of the ongoing Civil Disobedience Movement and the British refusal to discuss Purna Swaraj. The Congress attended only the Second RTC (September - December 1931) via Gandhi and the Third RTC (November - December 1932) with a full delegation.

**Trap:** B (2 and 3 only) wrongly accepts the incorrect statement 3 and drops the correct statement 1. C (1 and 3 only) wrongly drops statement 2 (the Poona Pact is a textbook fact) and accepts statement 3. D (all three) wrongly accepts statement 3. The only choice that keeps both correct statements and rejects the incorrect one is A.

**See also:** [[../../history/22 - CDM and Simon Commission.md#Content|CDM and Simon Commission — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.cdm_and_simon_commission.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=22
The Communal Award of 1932 was associated with:

- A) Separate electorates for minorities including Depressed Classes
- B) The partition of Bengal
- C) The Rowlatt Act
- D) The Civil Disobedience Movement
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Communal Award, announced by British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald on 16 August 1932, was the formal extension of the separate-electorates principle (already conceded to Muslims in 1909 and now extended) to the Depressed Classes (Scheduled Castes), to Indian Christians, to Sikhs, to Anglo-Indians, and to certain other minorities. The Award retained separate electorates for Muslims and gave separate electorates to the Depressed Classes. The extension to the Depressed Classes provoked Mahatma Gandhi to fast in September 1932 (the famous Yerawada jail fast), which led to the Poona Pact of 20 September 1932 between Gandhi and Ambedkar, by which separate electorates for Depressed Classes were abandoned in favour of joint electorates with reserved seats.

**Trap:** B (partition of Bengal) refers to the 1905 partition by Lord Curzon (and the 1947 partition of India at the time of Independence) - unrelated to the Communal Award. C (Rowlatt Act) was the 1919 Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, which authorised detention without trial, an entirely different kind of legislation. D (Civil Disobedience Movement) was the 1930-34 mass movement launched by the Salt March; while the Communal Award was issued during the CDM, the Award itself is associated with the separate-electorates question, not with the CDM as such.
