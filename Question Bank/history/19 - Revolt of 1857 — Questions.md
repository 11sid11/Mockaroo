# Revolt of 1857 — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md|Revolt of 1857]]

@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=19
The revolt of 1857 started at:

- A) Lucknow
- B) Meerut on 10 May 1857
- C) Kanpur
- D) Jhansi
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The 1857 revolt began at Meerut on 10 May 1857, when Indian soldiers (sepoys) of the Bengal Army refused to use the new Enfield rifle cartridges allegedly greased with cow and pig fat, and shot their British officers. The mutiny then spread rapidly to Delhi, Lucknow, Kanpur, Jhansi and many other cantonments and towns across north and central India.

**Trap:** A (Lucknow), C (Kanpur) and D (Jhansi) all became major centres of the revolt but none of them was the place where it started. Meerut was the trigger; Delhi, where Bahadur Shah Zafar was proclaimed emperor, was the second major outbreak on 11-12 May 1857.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=19
Who fired the first shot of the 1857 revolt?

- A) Mangal Pandey
- B) Bahadur Shah Zafar
- C) Begum Hazrat Mahal
- D) Tatya Tope
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Mangal Pandey, a sepoy of the 34th Bengal Native Infantry, attacked his British officers (Major-General John Hearsey and Sergeant-Major Hewson) at the Barrackpore parade ground on 29 March 1857. He is generally credited with firing the first shot of the chain of events that became the 1857 revolt, though the actual cantonment-wide uprising only broke out later at Meerut on 10 May 1857.

**Trap:** B (Bahadur Shah Zafar) was proclaimed the symbolic emperor by the rebels at Delhi but was a frail political figurehead, not a combatant. C (Begum Hazrat Mahal) and D (Tatya Tope / Tantia Tope) became important rebel leaders at Lucknow and Kanpur/Gwalior respectively, but the first shot is attributed to Mangal Pandey at Barrackpore.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=19
The Doctrine of Lapse was introduced by:

- A) Lord Wellesley
- B) Lord Dalhousie
- C) Lord Curzon
- D) Lord Canning
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Lord Dalhousie (Governor-General 1848-1856) introduced the Doctrine of Lapse, by which the British annexed several Indian states whose rulers died without a natural heir, including Satara (1848), Jaitpur and Sambalpur (1849), Baghat (1850), Udaipur (1852), Jhansi (1853) and Nagpur (1854). Annexation of Oudh/Awadh (1856) used a different legal pretext but followed the same political logic and is widely counted as the last major annexation under Dalhousie.

**Trap:** A (Wellesley) is associated with the Subsidiary Alliance system. C (Curzon) is associated with the Partition of Bengal (1905) and the Archaeological Survey of India. D (Canning) was the first Viceroy after the 1857 revolt. None of them introduced the Doctrine of Lapse.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=19
The Government of India Act of 1858 transferred power from:

- A) The Mughal emperor to the East India Company
- B) The East India Company to the British Crown (Viceroy)
- C) The Crown to the Viceroy
- D) The Viceroy to the Governor-General
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Government of India Act 1858, passed by the British Parliament in the aftermath of the 1857 revolt, transferred all governmental powers over India from the East India Company to the British Crown. The office of Governor-General of India was retitled "Viceroy of India" and the Crown's authority was exercised through a Secretary of State for India (sitting in London) and the Viceroy (sitting in India).

**Trap:** A reverses the direction of transfer (Company to Crown, not emperor to Company). C is wrong because there was no transfer from the Crown to the Viceroy; the Crown became the supreme authority and the Viceroy merely represented it. D is wrong because the Governor-General (later Viceroy) was the top executive, not a subordinate office that received power from the Viceroy.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=19
A student reads that an Indian leader of the 1857 revolt at Lucknow proclaimed the son of the deposed Nawab Wajid Ali Shah as the new ruler. The leader being described is:

- A) Begum Hazrat Mahal
- B) Rani Lakshmibai
- C) Kunwar Singh
- D) Bahadur Shah Zafar
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Begum Hazrat Mahal, the wife of the deposed Nawab Wajid Ali Shah of Awadh, led the revolt at Lucknow in 1857. With the support of the rebel sepoys she proclaimed her minor son, Birjis Qadr, as the Nawab of Awadh and herself acted as regent. After the British recaptured Lucknow in March 1858 she refused to surrender and eventually took refuge in Nepal.

**Trap:** B (Rani Lakshmibai) led the revolt at Jhansi and then at Gwalior, not Lucknow. C (Kunwar Singh) led the Bihar revolt centred on Jagdishpur. D (Bahadur Shah Zafar) was the symbolic emperor proclaimed at Delhi, not a Lucknow-specific leader.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=19
The leader of the 1857 revolt at Jhansi was:

- A) Rani Lakshmibai
- B) Begum Hazrat Mahal
- C) Tantia Tope
- D) Nana Saheb
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Rani Lakshmibai (1828-1858), the queen of Jhansi, led the revolt at Jhansi in 1857 after the British refused to recognise the claim of her adopted son Damodar Rao and annexed Jhansi under the Doctrine of Lapse. She defended Jhansi against the British assault under Sir Hugh Rose in March-April 1858, then escaped to Gwalior where she died fighting on 17 June 1858.

**Trap:** B (Begum Hazrat Mahal) is associated with Lucknow, not Jhansi. C (Tantia Tope) was a close associate of Nana Saheb who led rebel forces at Kanpur and later in central India, but he was not the leader at Jhansi itself. D (Nana Saheb) was the leader at Kanpur, not Jhansi.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=19
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the 1857 revolt?

- A) It began at Meerut on 10 May 1857
- B) It was supported by soldiers and civilians across north and central India
- C) It was purely a military uprising with no civilian participation
- D) The Mughal Emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar was proclaimed the symbolic head
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The statement that the 1857 revolt was "purely a military uprising with no civilian participation" is INCORRECT. In reality, the revolt was a broad-based movement supported by sepoys, deposed rulers, peasants, zamindars, religious leaders, artisans and townspeople across north and central India. Begum Hazrat Mahal, Rani Lakshmibai, Kunwar Singh, Nana Saheb and many civilian notables were central leaders, not just soldiers.

**Trap:** A, B and D are all correct factual statements about the revolt, which is why C is the false one to pick. Many textbooks still debate whether 1857 was a "sepoy mutiny" or a "national uprising", but even the narrowest account acknowledges substantial civilian participation, especially in Awadh, Bundelkhand and Bihar.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=19
Match the following centres of the 1857 revolt with their leaders:

| Centre | Leader |
|---|---|
| 1. Lucknow | a. Begum Hazrat Mahal |
| 2. Kanpur | b. Nana Saheb |
| 3. Bihar (Jagdishpur) | c. Kunwar Singh |

- A) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- B) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- D) 1-a, 2-c, 3-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

1-a: Lucknow was led by Begum Hazrat Mahal, who proclaimed her son Birjis Qadr as Nawab. 2-b: Kanpur was led by Nana Saheb (the adopted son of the exiled Peshwa Baji Rao II), with military support from Tantia Tope and the famous actions at Satichaura Ghat and the Bibighar. 3-c: Bihar was led by the 80-year-old zamindar Kunwar Singh of Jagdishpur, one of the most tenacious rebel leaders until his death in April 1858.

**Trap:** B swaps 1 and 2 (a common error because both Lucknow and Kanpur are large Awadh-region cities). C swaps 1 and 3, attaching Kunwar Singh to Lucknow. D pairs Kunwar Singh with Kanpur, the most frequent mix-up because the Kanpur and Bihar revolts both involved heavy fighting in 1857-58.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=19
After the failure of the 1857 revolt, the British Crown's authority over India was embodied by:

- A) The East India Company
- B) The Viceroy appointed by the British Crown
- C) The Mughal Emperor
- D) The Governor-General appointed by Parliament
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

After the 1857 revolt, the Government of India Act 1858 ended the East India Company's rule and placed India directly under the British Crown. The senior Crown representative in India was titled the Viceroy (also still Governor-General), who was appointed by the Crown on the advice of the British government and reported to the Secretary of State for India in London.

**Trap:** A (East India Company) is the body whose rule was ended by the Act of 1858, so it cannot be the embodiment of the new Crown authority. C (Mughal Emperor) is wrong: the last Mughal, Bahadur Shah Zafar, was deposed, deported to Rangoon and died there in 1862. D (Governor-General appointed by Parliament) is wrong: under the 1858 Act the Crown appointed the Viceroy, not Parliament directly, and Parliament's role was only to pass the enabling Act.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=19
Arrange the following in chronological order:

1. Doctrine of Lapse
2. Mangal Pandey's attack at Barrackpore
3. Outbreak at Meerut
4. Government of India Act 1858

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 2, 1, 3, 4
- C) 1, 3, 2, 4
- D) 4, 1, 2, 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct chronological order is: (1) Doctrine of Lapse, introduced by Lord Dalhousie starting in 1848 and applied to Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur and others through 1854-56, with Awadh annexed in 1856 $\rightarrow$ (2) Mangal Pandey's attack on his officers at Barrackpore on 29 March 1857, the symbolic first shot $\rightarrow$ (3) Outbreak at Meerut on 10 May 1857, the actual start of the cantonment revolt $\rightarrow$ (4) Government of India Act 1858, passed by Parliament in August 1858 to transfer power from the East India Company to the British Crown. So the order is 1, 2, 3, 4.

**Trap:** B places Mangal Pandey (March 1857) before the Doctrine of Lapse (1848 onwards), which is chronologically backwards. C places the Meerut outbreak (May 1857) before Mangal Pandey's attack (March 1857), also backwards. D places the 1858 Act first, which is impossible because the Act is a consequence of the revolt, not a cause.

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=19
Consider the following statements:

1. The 1857 revolt was triggered by the use of greased cartridges with cow and pig fat.
2. The revolt led to the end of the Mughal dynasty.
3. The revolt was suppressed by 1858 but became a foundational myth for later nationalist movements.

Which of the above are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. (1) The immediate trigger of the sepoys' anger was the new Enfield Pattern 1853 rifle whose cartridges had to be bitten at the greased end; a rumour spread that the grease was made of cow and pig fat, offending both Hindu and Muslim sepoys. (2) The last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, was captured after the fall of Delhi in September 1857, deposed, deported to Rangoon and died there in 1862, formally ending the Mughal dynasty. (3) Although militarily defeated by mid-1858, the revolt was retrospectively christened the "First War of Indian Independence" by V. D. Savarkar (1909) and became a foundational nationalist myth invoked by later movements including the Congress-led freedom struggle.

**Trap:** C drops statement 2, but the deposition and exile of Bahadur Shah Zafar is uncontested as the end of the Mughal dynasty. A drops statement 3, but the nationalist canonisation of 1857 (from Savarkar through the Ninetieth Anniversary celebrations in 1947-48) is a standard textbook point. B drops statement 1, but the greased-cartridge controversy is the textbook "spark" of the revolt, even if the underlying causes were deeper (annexations, Doctrine of Lapse, Awadh, etc.).

**See also:** [[../../history/19 - Revolt of 1857.md#Content|Revolt of 1857 — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.revolt_of_1857.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=19
The first Indian to call the 1857 revolt the "First War of Indian Independence" was:

- A) Dadabhai Naoroji
- B) Bal Gangadhar Tilak
- C) V. D. Savarkar (in his 1909 book "The Indian War of Independence")
- D) Subhas Chandra Bose
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

V. D. Savarkar popularised the label "First War of Indian Independence" through his 1909 book "The Indian War of Independence, 1857", originally written in Marathi ("Kalnirnaya" / "1857 che Swatantrya Samar") and published in English from exile. He argued that the revolt was a planned national uprising rather than a mere "sepoy mutiny", and his framing was later adopted by the Indian National Congress and became mainstream Indian historiography.

**Trap:** A (Dadabhai Naoroji), B (Bal Gangadhar Tilak) and D (Subhas Chandra Bose) all invoked the legacy of 1857 in their own ways — Naoroji in his economic critique of British rule, Tilak in his public celebrations of Shivaji and nationalist festivals, Bose in the Indian National Army's symbolism — but none of them was the first to coin or popularise the "First War of Independence" label. That honour goes to Savarkar specifically through his 1909 book.

</details>
