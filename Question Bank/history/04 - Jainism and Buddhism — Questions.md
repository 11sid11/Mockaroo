@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=4
The founder of Buddhism was:

- A) Mahavira
- B) Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha)
- C) Adi Shankaracharya
- D) Gosala Maskariputra
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Siddhartha Gautama, born in Lumbini (c. 563 BCE), became the Buddha ("Enlightened One") after attaining Nirvana at Bodh Gaya.

**Trap:** A is the founder of Jainism. C is the 8th-century Advaita philosopher. D was the leader of the Ajivika sect — a rival, not founder.

**See also:** [[../../history/04 - Jainism and Buddhism.md#Content|Jainism and Buddhism — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=4
The Jain concept of "Anekantavada" refers to:

- A) Non-violence (ahimsa)
- B) Plurality of viewpoints / many-sidedness of truth
- C) Strict non-attachment to possessions
- D) Five-fold path of conduct
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Anekantavada (literally "many-sidedness") is the doctrine that truth has multiple aspects and no single viewpoint captures it fully.

**Trap:** A is ahimsa. C is aparigraha. D is the Pancha-Mahavrata. Students confuse these often-quoted Jain concepts.

**See also:** [[../../history/04 - Jainism and Buddhism.md#Content|Jainism and Buddhism — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=4
Buddha's first sermon at Sarnath is known as:

- A) Mahaparinirvana
- B) Dharmachakrapravartana (Setting the Wheel of Law in Motion)
- C) Mahabhinishkramana
- D) Kshetrajna-pratipatti
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

Buddha's first sermon at the Deer Park in Sarnath, delivered to his five former companions, is called the Dharmachakrapravartana — the "Turning of the Wheel of Dharma."

**Trap:** A refers to his death at Kushinagar. C is the "Great Renunciation" — leaving his palace. D is a Jain term for right knowledge.

**See also:** [[../../history/04 - Jainism and Buddhism.md#Content|Jainism and Buddhism — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=4
A question asks: where did Mahavira attain Kaivalya (omniscience)? The correct answer is:

- A) Rajgriha
- B) Sarnath
- C) Pawapuri (Pava)
- D) Vaishali
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

Mahavira attained Kaivalya at Pawapuri (modern Bihar), and he also passed away (Nirvana / moksha) there. His death anniversary is celebrated as Diwali by Jains.

**Trap:** A is the First Buddhist Council. B is Buddha's first sermon. D is the Second Buddhist Council — all real Buddhist sites, easy to confuse.

**See also:** [[../../history/04 - Jainism and Buddhism.md#Content|Jainism and Buddhism — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=4
The Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marg) of Buddhism is recommended to end suffering because:

- A) It prescribes ritual sacrifices to please the gods
- B) It is a path of mental and ethical discipline to attain Nirvana
- C) It relies on caste-based ritual purity
- D) It demands extreme asceticism and self-mortification
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Eightfold Path (right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration) is the middle way (Madhyam Marg) of mental-ethical discipline that ends suffering.

**Trap:** A describes Vedic ritualism, which Buddhism rejected. C is contrary to Buddhism's rejection of caste ritual. D describes the extreme asceticism Buddha rejected before enlightenment.

**See also:** [[../../history/04 - Jainism and Buddhism.md#Content|Jainism and Buddhism — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=4
The Buddhist text "Milindapanho" records the dialogue of:

- A) Buddha and Ananda
- B) Nagasena and the Indo-Greek king Menander (Milinda)
- C) Moggaliputta Tissa and Ashoka
- D) Bimbisara and the Buddha
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Milindapanho (Questions of Milinda) is a Pali text in which the Indo-Greek king Menander (Milinda) questions the monk Nagasena on Buddhist doctrine.

**Trap:** A, C, D are all real historical pairings — students default to Buddha's famous companions (Ananda) without noticing the Indo-Greek context.

**See also:** [[../../history/04 - Jainism and Buddhism.md#Content|Jainism and Buddhism — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=4
The "Three Jewels" (Triratna) of Buddhism are:

- A) Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha
- B) Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya
- C) Right speech, right action, right livelihood
- D) Karma, Rebirth, Nirvana
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Triratna ("Three Gems") are the Buddha, the Dhamma (his teachings), and the Sangha (monastic community) — every Buddhist takes refuge in these three.

**Trap:** B is the first three vows of Jainism. C is part of the Eightfold Path. D are Buddhist doctrines but not the Three Jewels. Students mix the categories.

**See also:** [[../../history/04 - Jainism and Buddhism.md#Content|Jainism and Buddhism — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=4
The Jain doctrine of "Syadvada" (or Saptabhangi) states that:

- A) Every truth can be expressed in seven conditional / partial ways
- B) The soul is identical with the body
- C) All material existence is illusion
- D) Knowledge comes only from scriptural authority
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Syadvada (the doctrine of "may be") and Saptabhangi assert that every assertion about reality is true only conditionally — there are seven modes (e.g., "in some way it is, in some way it is not").

**Trap:** B describes Charvaka. C is Advaita Vedanta. D is the wrong epistemic stance — Jainism stresses perception, reasoning and testimony.

**See also:** [[../../history/04 - Jainism and Buddhism.md#Content|Jainism and Buddhism — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=4
Consider the following statements about Jainism and Buddhism:

1. Buddhism rejects the authority of the Vedas; Jainism accepts Vedas as authoritative.
2. Both religions believe in karma and rebirth.
3. The First Buddhist Council was held at Saptaparni caves in Rajgriha after Buddha's death.
4. Mahavira was called "Jina" (conqueror) and his followers Jains.

Which of the above are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2, 3 and 4
- C) 1, 3 and 4
- D) All of the above
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

2 is true (both accept karma and rebirth). 3 is true (First Council at Saptaparni caves in Rajgriha, presided by Mahakassapa). 4 is true (Mahavira = Jina, followers = Jains). Statement 1 is wrong because Jainism also rejects Vedic authority and the Brahminical caste order.

**Trap:** C and D accept statement 1, which mixes Buddhism with Brahmanism. A drops two true statements.

**See also:** [[../../history/04 - Jainism and Buddhism.md#Content|Jainism and Buddhism — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.jainism_and_buddhism.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=4
Match the following correctly:

| Buddhist Council | President |
|---|---|
| 1. First | a. Moggaliputta Tissa |
| 2. Second | b. Mahakassapa |
| 3. Third | c. Vasumitra |

- A) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- B) 1-b, 2-c, 3-a
- C) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- D) 1-c, 2-a, 3-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

1-b (Mahakassapa at Rajgriha), 2-c (the Second Council at Vaishali was presided by Sabbakami), 3-a (Moggaliputta Tissa at Pataliputra under Ashoka).

**Trap:** The "Vasumitra" option is a real name from the Fourth Council but gets placed at position 2 here. A and D scramble the presidencies.

</details>
