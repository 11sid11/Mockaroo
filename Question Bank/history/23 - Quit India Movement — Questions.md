# Quit India Movement — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md|Quit India Movement]]

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=23
The Quit India Movement was launched by Gandhi on:

- A) 8 August 1942
- B) 9 August 1942
- C) 15 August 1942
- D) 26 January 1930
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Quit India Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi on 8 August 1942 at the All India Congress Committee session at Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay. The resolution, demanding an end to British rule in India, was moved by Jawaharlal Nehru and seconded by Maulana Abul Kalam Azad; the AICC authorised Gandhi to take charge of the non-violent mass struggle. The very next morning (9 August 1942), the British arrested virtually the entire Congress leadership - Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Azad, and hundreds of others - and the AICC was declared an unlawful association.

**Trap:** B (9 August 1942) is the day after the launch, when the mass arrests of Congress leaders began. C (15 August 1942) is irrelevant to the launch - 15 August 1947 is the date of actual Independence. D (26 January 1930) was the first Independence Day declared by the Congress at Lahore, where Purna Swaraj was pledged.

**See also:** [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md#Content|Quit India Movement — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=23
The slogan associated with the Quit India Movement was:

- A) "Do or Die" (Karo ya maro)
- B) "Jai Hind"
- C) "Inquilab Zindabad"
- D) "Swaraj is my birthright"
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The slogan "Do or Die" ("Karo ya maro" in Hindi) was given by Mahatma Gandhi at the AICC session at Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay on 8 August 1942, immediately after the Quit India Resolution was passed. The slogan captured the spirit of total non-violent resistance - either India would free itself from British rule or would die in the attempt. It became the rallying cry of the underground movement that followed the arrests of the senior Congress leaders.

**Trap:** B ("Jai Hind") was coined by Subhas Chandra Bose and was used as a greeting by the Indian National Army (INA), not by the Quit India Movement. C ("Inquilab Zindabad" - "Long live the revolution") was coined by the Urdu poet Hasrat Mohani and was widely used by revolutionaries like Bhagat Singh. D ("Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it") was Bal Gangadhar Tilak's 1916 slogan from the Home Rule Movement, more than two decades earlier.

**See also:** [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md#Content|Quit India Movement — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=23
The Quit India Resolution was first adopted at:

- A) Wardha (AICC Working Committee, 14 July 1942)
- B) Bombay (8 August 1942)
- C) Calcutta
- D) Delhi
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Quit India Resolution was first adopted on 14 July 1942 by the AICC Working Committee at Wardha (Maharashtra), where Mahatma Gandhi had his ashram (Sevagram Ashram). The Working Committee, fearing imminent British repression and an offer from Stafford Cripps, drafted the resolution demanding the immediate withdrawal of British forces from India. The Wardha resolution was then ratified and formally launched at the AICC plenary session at Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay on 8 August 1942, accompanied by Gandhi's "Do or Die" slogan. Both events are part of the same Quit India campaign but the resolution text itself originated at Wardha.

**Trap:** B (Bombay, 8 August 1942) is the date of the launch and the public mass rally, but not of the resolution's *first* adoption - that happened earlier at Wardha. C (Calcutta) is wrong - the famous Calcutta session was the 1928 session (Motilal Nehru Report) and the 1929 session was held in Lahore. D (Delhi) had no Quit India resolution in 1942.

**See also:** [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md#Content|Quit India Movement — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=23
Who led the underground radio (Secret Congress Radio) during the Quit India Movement?

- A) Usha Mehta (in Bombay)
- B) Subhas Chandra Bose
- C) Jayaprakash Narayan
- D) Aruna Asaf Ali
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Secret Congress Radio (also called the Underground Congress Radio) was operated from a concealed location in Bombay during the Quit India Movement by a small group led by Usha Mehta (later Usha Mehta Ganguli, b. 1920). A postgraduate student of philosophy, Usha Mehta began broadcasting in August 1942 from a flat in south Bombay, transmitting news of the movement, Congress directives, and patriotic songs. The broadcasts were conducted for about three months until the British tracked down the radio station on 12 November 1942 through direction-finding equipment; Usha Mehta was arrested and sentenced to four years' rigorous imprisonment. She was later awarded the Padma Vibhushan (1998) and the BBC declared her one of the hundred most inspiring women of all time.

**Trap:** B (Subhas Chandra Bose) was in Germany in 1942 and later led the INA from Southeast Asia - he had nothing to do with the Secret Congress Radio. C (Jayaprakash Narayan) was an underground Congress leader but operated in north India, especially Bihar, not Bombay; he did not run the radio. D (Aruna Asaf Ali) was the underground Congress leader who hoisted the Congress flag at the Gowalia Tank Maidan after the arrests, but she did not operate the radio.

**See also:** [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md#Content|Quit India Movement — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=23
A student reads that all senior Congress leaders were arrested within hours of the Quit India launch, yet the movement spread as a mass uprising via strikes, hartals, and sabotage. The movement being described is:

- A) The Quit India Movement
- B) The Civil Disobedience Movement
- C) The Khilafat Movement
- D) The Non-Cooperation Movement
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The description fits the Quit India Movement (August 1942 onwards) precisely. Within hours of the launch at Bombay on 8 August 1942, the British arrested virtually the entire senior Congress leadership - Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Azad, Rajendra Prasad, and many others - and declared the AICC an unlawful body. Despite (and in some ways because of) the absence of central leadership, the movement exploded into a mass uprising: railway strikes, post office and telegraph disruptions, cutting of telegraph wires, attacks on police stations and government buildings, hartals, and acts of sabotage by ordinary people and local leaders. The violence was most intense in Bihar (especially the Patna Secretariat explosion by Jayaprakash Narayan's comrades), UP, Bengal, Bombay, and Maharashtra, and the movement acquired a strongly anti-British, anti-colonial character.

**Trap:** B (Civil Disobedience Movement, 1930-34) was a non-violent, mostly disciplined mass movement that did not witness the immediate mass arrests of the entire leadership at the outset. C (Khilafat Movement, 1919-24) was a pan-Islamic agitation over the Ottoman Empire, led mainly by the Ali brothers, and is unrelated. D (Non-Cooperation Movement, 1920-22) was also called off by Gandhi after the Chauri Chaura incident and did not begin with the arrest of all leaders.

**See also:** [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md#Content|Quit India Movement — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=23
The Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind Sarkar), headed by Subhas Chandra Bose, was formally proclaimed in:

- A) Japan
- B) Singapore (and later moved to Rangoon, Burma)
- C) Germany
- D) Afghanistan
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind Sarkar) was formally proclaimed on 21 October 1943 in Singapore, with Subhas Chandra Bose as the Head of State, Prime Minister, and Minister of War. The government was recognised by nine Axis-aligned states - Japan, Germany, Italy, Thailand, Burma (under Ba Maw), the Philippines, Manchukuo, Croatia, and the Free Thai government. Bose hoisted the Indian tricolour (the INA flag) at the Municipal Hall in Singapore. Later, as the Japanese advance into Burma reversed, the Provisional Government moved to Rangoon (Burma) in early 1944 and then collapsed with the Japanese defeat in 1945.

**Trap:** A (Japan) is incorrect - Japan hosted the INA but the formal proclamation of the Provisional Government happened in Singapore, not Tokyo. C (Germany) is wrong - Bose had formed the Free India Centre in Berlin in 1941 and the Indian Legion there, but the Provisional Government was declared in Singapore, not Berlin. D (Afghanistan) is wrong - Bose passed through Kabul briefly in early 1941 on his way from Calcutta to Berlin, but nothing was proclaimed there.

</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=23
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the Quit India Movement?

- A) It was the last major mass movement before independence
- B) Gandhi gave the slogan "Do or Die"
- C) The Muslim League fully supported the Quit India Movement
- D) The British refused to negotiate with Congress during the movement
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Muslim League did NOT support the Quit India Movement - this is the INCORRECT statement. The League, under Muhammad Ali Jinnah, was hostile to the Congress-led Quit India campaign, calling it a "Hindu" agitation and a Congress imposition on Muslim India. The Quit India period coincided with the League's growing demand for Pakistan, articulated in the Lahore Resolution of March 1940 and reinforced during the wartime period. The British exploited the League's opposition to argue that Congress did not represent all Indians, which was a major factor in the eventual partition of India in 1947.

**Trap:** A (last major mass movement before independence) is correct - Quit India 1942 was the last all-India mass movement before Independence on 15 August 1947. B (Gandhi's "Do or Die" slogan) is correct - the slogan was given at Bombay on 8 August 1942. D (British refused to negotiate during the movement) is correct - the British refused to negotiate with the Congress while the movement continued; only after the war ended in 1945 did talks resume (Wavell talks, 1945; Cabinet Mission, 1946).

**See also:** [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md#Content|Quit India Movement — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=23
Match the following leaders to their roles during the Quit India Movement:

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| 1. Aruna Asaf Ali | a. Underground Congress leader |
| 2. Jayaprakash Narayan | b. Underground Congress leader |
| 3. Usha Mehta | c. Operated the Secret Congress Radio |

- A) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- B) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- C) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- D) 1-a, 2-c, 3-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

All three were leading figures of the Quit India Movement once the senior leaders were arrested. Aruna Asaf Ali (1909-1996), an AICC member from Delhi, presided over the remaining AICC meeting at Gowalia Tank Maidan on 9 August 1942 and famously hoisted the Congress flag after the arrests; she then went underground and edited the underground newspaper "Inquilab" - she is correctly matched as an underground Congress leader (a). Jayaprakash Narayan (1902-1979), popularly known as JP, escaped arrest and went underground in Bihar, where he organised the Patna Secretariat attack (the explosion of the Bihar Legislative Assembly building on 12 August 1942) and led the underground Congress movement in north India - he too is correctly matched as an underground Congress leader (b). Usha Mehta (1920-2000) led the Secret Congress Radio from a hidden flat in Bombay and broadcast Congress news and patriotic songs for about three months before being tracked down and arrested on 12 November 1942 - she is correctly matched as the operator of the Secret Congress Radio (c).

**Trap:** B swaps Usha Mehta (radio) with Aruna Asaf Ali (underground leader). C swaps Aruna Asaf Ali and JP. D swaps JP and Usha Mehta. The distinguishing feature is that Usha Mehta is the ONLY radio operator among the three; the other two were underground political organisers.

</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q9 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=23
The Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj) was primarily formed from:

- A) Indian soldiers captured by Germany
- B) Indian soldiers captured by Japan (after the fall of Singapore)
- C) Civilian volunteers recruited inside India
- D) Muslim volunteers from Punjab and Bengal
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Indian National Army (INA, or Azad Hind Fauj) was primarily formed from Indian soldiers of the British Indian Army who had been captured by the Japanese after the Fall of Singapore on 15 February 1942. About 60,000-70,000 Indian soldiers of the British garrison at Singapore surrendered to the Japanese in the largest surrender of British-led troops in history. Captain Mohan Singh (of the British Indian Army's 1/14th Punjab Regiment), under Japanese encouragement, organised these POWs into the First INA in 1942. After a disagreement with the Japanese, the First INA was dissolved, and Subhas Chandra Bose (who arrived in Singapore in July 1943) reorganised it into the Second INA in September 1943. Bose also recruited Indian civilians from the Indian diaspora in Southeast Asia (Malaya, Burma, Thailand) and formed the Rani of Jhansi Regiment (women's infantry) and the Azad Hind Fauj fought alongside the Japanese against the British in the Burma Campaign (1944).

**Trap:** A (Germany-captured soldiers) is incorrect - Germany did not capture large numbers of British Indian soldiers. Bose did form the Indian Legion (later the 950th Infantry Regiment of the Wehrmacht) in 1941 in Germany from Indian POWs held in North Africa and from civilian volunteers, but this was a much smaller force (about 3,000) and never fought. C (civilian volunteers in India) is incorrect - the INA operated outside India. D (Muslim volunteers) is incorrect - the INA had Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, and all communities among its ranks.

**See also:** [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md#Content|Quit India Movement — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=23
Arrange the following events in chronological order:
1. Wardha AICC resolution (Quit India)
2. Quit India launch at Bombay
3. Formation of the Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind)
4. End of World War II

- A) 1, 2, 3, 4
- B) 2, 1, 3, 4
- C) 1, 2, 4, 3
- D) 1, 4, 2, 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The correct chronological sequence is 1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 4, i.e., Wardha AICC resolution (14 July 1942) -> Quit India launch at Bombay (8 August 1942) -> Provisional Government of Free India / Azad Hind Sarkar proclaimed in Singapore (21 October 1943) -> End of World War II (September 1945). The Wardha resolution came first because the AICC Working Committee at Wardha (under Gandhi's chairmanship) drafted and adopted the Quit India text on 14 July 1942; the AICC plenary session at Bombay on 8 August 1942 ratified and launched it with the "Do or Die" slogan; the Provisional Government of Free India under Subhas Chandra Bose was proclaimed in Singapore on 21 October 1943; and World War II ended in September 1945 with the surrender of Japan.

**Trap:** B reverses Wardha (1) and Bombay (2) - both are in 1942 but Wardha came first by nearly a month. C places WWII (4) before Azad Hind (3), which is impossible because the Azad Hind Sarkar was proclaimed in 1943 during the war. D places WWII (4) before the Bombay launch (2) in 1942, again impossible.

**See also:** [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md#Content|Quit India Movement — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q11 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=23
Consider the statements:
1. The Quit India Movement saw the rise of parallel governments in several parts of India.
2. The movement was violently suppressed, but did not derail India's path to independence.
3. The INA trials at the Red Fort (1945-46) became a rallying point for Indian nationalism, especially among ex-INA soldiers.
Which are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. (1) During the Quit India Movement, especially after August 1942, parallel governments (prati sarkars) emerged in several places: in Satara (Maharashtra) under Y. B. Chavan and Nana Patil; in Tamulkar's Jatiya Sarkar in Midnapore (Bengal); in Ballia (UP) under Chittu Pandey who briefly seized the town in August 1942; and in forest-based pockets of Bihar, Andhra, and Orissa. (2) The British suppressed the movement with mass arrests, police firings, flogging, collective fines, and air raids (e.g., the bombing of the Bihar Secretariat), but the movement's very intensity convinced the British that India could no longer be governed against the Indians' will - leading to the post-war Cripps Mission failure, the Cabinet Mission, and ultimately Independence on 15 August 1947. (3) The INA Red Fort Trials (November 1945 - May 1946), in which Shah Nawaz, Prem Kumar Sahgal, and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon were charged with "waging war against the King-Emperor", became a massive rallying point - Congress and even the Muslim League united to defend the accused, the INA soldiers' slogan "Jai Hind" entered common usage, and the Royal Indian Navy Mutiny of February 1946 was triggered directly by the trials.

**Trap:** A (1 and 2 only) wrongly drops statement 3, which is a major and well-documented event. B (2 and 3 only) wrongly drops statement 1, which is also textbook. C (1 and 3 only) wrongly drops statement 2, which is the central political consequence of the movement. The only option that keeps all three is D.

**See also:** [[../../history/23 - Quit India Movement.md#Content|Quit India Movement — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.quit_india_movement.q12 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=23
After the Quit India Movement, the most important post-war development was:

- A) The Cripps Mission (1942) and Gandhi-Jinnah talks in 1944
- B) The Cabinet Mission (1946) and the path to a divided India
- C) The Wavell Plan (1945)
- D) All of the above
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All the listed events were important post-Quit India developments. (a) The Cripps Mission (Sir Stafford Cripps, March 1942) - although its offer came during the early phase of Quit India, it failed because Cripps offered dominion status after the war (not immediate freedom), which Congress and Gandhi rejected. (b) The Gandhi-Jinnah talks in September 1944 in Bombay, where the two leaders met 14 times over two weeks, failed because Jinnah insisted on Pakistan as a precondition. (c) The Wavell Plan (June 1945), announced by Viceroy Wavell, proposed an executive council with a Muslim League/Congress parity, which was rejected by Congress because it gave the League effective veto power. (d) The Cabinet Mission (March-June 1946) by Cripps, Pethick-Lawrence, and Alexander proposed a united India with a federal structure; Congress accepted it initially but Jawaharlal Nehru's statement on 7 July 1946 ("we are free to modify the Cabinet Mission plan") led Jinnah to withdraw the League, opening the door to Direct Action, the Great Calcutta Killing of August 1946, and finally Partition in 1947.

**Trap:** A, B, and C each name only one of several important developments. While each of them is in itself correct, the question asks for the *most important* development, and the only option that captures the entire post-Quit India trajectory (from Cripps through Cabinet Mission to Partition) is D - all of the above.
