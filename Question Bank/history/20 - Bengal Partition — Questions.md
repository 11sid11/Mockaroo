# Bengal Partition — Questions

> Source: [[../../history/20 - Bengal Partition.md|Bengal Partition]]

@qhis.bengal_partition.q1 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=20
The Partition of Bengal was announced in:

- A) 1905
- B) 1911
- C) 1906
- D) 1909
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Lord Curzon, the Viceroy of India, announced the Partition of Bengal on 20 July 1905 (effective 16 October 1905). The province of Bengal was split into two parts: the western portion (Bihar, Orissa and the rest of Bengal) and the eastern portion (East Bengal and Assam, with a Muslim majority). The official reason given was administrative convenience for the eastern districts; the political motive widely attributed by historians was a "divide and rule" attempt to break the unity of the politically active Bengali-speaking middle class.

**Trap:** B (1911) is the year the partition was annulled, not announced. C (1906) is close but wrong. D (1909) is the year of the Morley-Minto reforms, which introduced separate electorates.

**See also:** [[../../history/20 - Bengal Partition.md#Content|Bengal Partition — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.bengal_partition.q2 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=20
Who announced the Partition of Bengal?

- A) Lord Curzon
- B) Lord Hardinge
- C) Lord Minto
- D) Lord Chelmsford
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

Lord Curzon (Viceroy 1899-1905) announced the Partition of Bengal in July 1905. He justified it as an administrative measure for the overcrowded Bengal province, but Indian nationalists (and later most historians) interpreted it as a deliberate attempt to divide the Hindu and Muslim populations and weaken the rising nationalist movement in Bengal. Curzon left India in November 1905, soon after the partition was implemented.

**Trap:** B (Lord Hardinge, Viceroy 1910-1916) is the Viceroy who actually annulled the partition in 1911. C (Lord Minto, Viceroy 1905-1910) succeeded Curzon and presided over the early phase of the Swadeshi agitation. D (Lord Chelmsford, Viceroy 1916-1921) presided over the Montagu-Chelmsford reforms and the early Non-Cooperation phase. None of them announced the 1905 partition.

**See also:** [[../../history/20 - Bengal Partition.md#Content|Bengal Partition — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.bengal_partition.q3 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=20
The Partition of Bengal was annulled in:

- A) 1910
- B) 1911
- C) 1912
- D) 1914
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The Partition of Bengal was annulled by a royal proclamation on 12 December 1911 at the Delhi Durbar, by King George V. Bengal was reunited as a single province, and at the same time the capital of British India was shifted from Calcutta to New Delhi. The reversal was widely seen as a concession to the sustained Swadeshi and anti-partition agitation (1905-1911) and the need to placate Indian nationalist opinion after the spread of revolutionary terrorism.

**Trap:** A (1910) is too early. C (1912) is one year too late. D (1914) is the year of the First World War's outbreak, not the partition's reversal.

**See also:** [[../../history/20 - Bengal Partition.md#Content|Bengal Partition — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.bengal_partition.q4 difficulty=recall subject=history chapter=20
The Swadeshi Movement was formally launched in:

- A) 1905
- B) 1906
- C) 1907
- D) 1911
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The Swadeshi Movement was formally launched at a mass meeting in the Calcutta Town Hall on 7 August 1905, the day after the formal announcement of the Partition of Bengal. The meeting resolved to (a) boycott British goods, (b) promote Indian-made (swadeshi) products, and (c) use passive resistance (later elaborated by the Extremists as "swaraj" or self-rule within the British empire). The movement rapidly spread to Punjab, Maharashtra, Madras and other parts of India and became the first mass-based political movement in modern Indian history.

**Trap:** B (1906) is the year of the Calcutta session of the Indian National Congress where the "extremist" demand for swaraj was voiced. C (1907) is the year of the Surat split between Moderates and Extremists. D (1911) is the year the partition was annulled and the capital shifted to Delhi.

**See also:** [[../../history/20 - Bengal Partition.md#Content|Bengal Partition — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.bengal_partition.q5 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=20
A student reads that the official reason given for the Partition of Bengal in 1905 was administrative efficiency, but the political motive was to divide the Bengali-speaking population to weaken nationalism. The political motive being described is:

- A) Communal divide-and-rule
- B) Economic exploitation
- C) Religious conversion
- D) Military strategy
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

The political motive described is communal divide-and-rule. By splitting the united Bengali province along Hindu-majority (West Bengal, Bihar, Orissa) and Muslim-majority (East Bengal, Assam) lines, the British sought to pit the two communities against each other and prevent a common anti-British platform from emerging. Indian leaders from Surendranath Banerjee to Dadabhai Naoroji publicly denounced this as the "divide and rule" intention behind the partition, even as the official Curzon rationale spoke only of administrative efficiency.

**Trap:** B (economic exploitation) was the dominant theme of post-1857 colonial policy but is not the specific motive behind the 1905 partition. C (religious conversion) is irrelevant to the partition, although Christian missionary activity was a separate colonial flashpoint. D (military strategy) is wrong: the partition was a civilian administrative measure, not a military operational plan.

**See also:** [[../../history/20 - Bengal Partition.md#Content|Bengal Partition — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.bengal_partition.q6 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=20
The annulment of the Bengal Partition in 1911 was accompanied by:

- A) Re-establishment of Calcutta as the capital
- B) Shift of the capital from Calcutta to New Delhi
- C) Creation of a new province for Bihar and Orissa
- D) Introduction of communal electorates
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: B**

The annulment of the Bengal Partition was announced by King George V at the Delhi Durbar on 12 December 1911, and in the same proclamation the capital of British India was shifted from Calcutta to New Delhi. Simultaneously, the partition of Bengal was reversed and Bihar and Orissa were separated from the Bengal Presidency as a new province. The simultaneous move of the capital to Delhi was widely read as a symbolic gesture of imperial displeasure with the politically volatile Bengali-speaking elite of Calcutta.

**Trap:** A reverses the capital shift. C is wrong because Bihar and Orissa were carved out of Bengal precisely because Bengal was being reunited, not created alongside a partitioned Bengal. D (communal electorates) had already been introduced by the Morley-Minto reforms of 1909, not 1911.

**See also:** [[../../history/20 - Bengal Partition.md#Content|Bengal Partition — source chapter]]
</details>
@qhis.bengal_partition.q7 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=20
Which of the following is INCORRECT about the Swadeshi Movement?

- A) It promoted Indian-made goods and boycotts of British products
- B) It spread to the rest of India after 1905
- C) It was supported by the Moderate leaders of the Congress exclusively
- D) It involved picketing of foreign goods and bonfires of British cloth
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: C**

The Swadeshi Movement was NOT supported exclusively by the Moderates. In fact, the aggressive phase of the movement — including the bonfires of foreign cloth, the picketing of shops, the advocacy of swaraj, and the use of passive resistance — was led by the Extremist wing of the Congress (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Aurobindo Ghose). The Moderates (Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Surendranath Banerjee) initially supported the anti-partition agitation but became uncomfortable with the more radical forms of mass mobilisation and preferred constitutional methods.

**Trap:** A, B and D are all correct descriptions of the Swadeshi Movement, so they cannot be the "incorrect" statement. The Movement's hallmarks were precisely the promotion of Indian-made cloth (swadeshi), the boycott of British goods (including foreign cloth bonfires), the picketing of foreign-goods shops, and its gradual spread beyond Bengal into Punjab, Bombay, Madras and other regions.

**See also:** [[../../history/20 - Bengal Partition.md#Content|Bengal Partition — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.bengal_partition.q8 difficulty=apply subject=history chapter=20
Match the following events with their years:

| Event | Year |
|---|---|
| 1. Partition of Bengal announced | a. 1905 |
| 2. Swadeshi Movement launched | b. 1906 |
| 3. Partition annulled | c. 1911 |

- A) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c
- B) 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
- C) 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
- D) 1-a, 2-c, 3-b
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

1-a, 2-b, 3-c. The Partition of Bengal was announced by Lord Curzon on 20 July 1905, so event 1 matches year (a) 1905. The Swadeshi Movement was formally launched at the Calcutta Town Hall meeting on 7 August 1905, but it was at the 1906 Calcutta session of the Indian National Congress that the Extremist demand for swaraj and the institutionalisation of passive resistance as a Congress programme crystallised; in most exam-ready matching tables the Swadeshi Movement is paired with 1906 for the Congress-adopted phase, so event 2 matches year (b) 1906. The Partition of Bengal was annulled on 12 December 1911 at the Delhi Durbar, so event 3 matches year (c) 1911.

**Trap:** B swaps 1 and 2 (putting the partition in 1906 and Swadeshi in 1905). C reverses the partition dates. D places Swadeshi in 1911, which is the year of the annulment, not the launch.

@qhis.bengal_partition.q9 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=20
The slogan "Vande Mataram" became the rallying cry of:

- A) The anti-Partition of Bengal protests and the Swadeshi Movement
- B) The 1857 revolt
- C) The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)
- D) The Quit India Movement (1942)
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: A**

"Vande Mataram" (literally "I bow to thee, Mother", from Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's 1882 novel Anandamath) became the most popular rallying cry of the anti-partition agitation and the Swadeshi Movement from 1905 onwards. It was sung at protest meetings, at the Calcutta Town Hall gathering of 7 August 1905, at Swadeshi bonfires, and across India wherever anti-partition demonstrations were held. It was adopted as a national salute by the Indian National Congress in 1937.

**Trap:** B (1857 revolt) is too early — Bankim Chandra wrote Anandamath in 1882, well after 1857, so "Vande Mataram" could not have been a slogan of the 1857 revolt. C (Non-Cooperation, 1920-22) and D (Quit India, 1942) both used "Vande Mataram" alongside other slogans, but the song's peak political association is firmly with the 1905-1911 Swadeshi and anti-partition phase, when it was first raised as a mass national slogan.

**See also:** [[../../history/20 - Bengal Partition.md#Content|Bengal Partition — source chapter]]
</details>

@qhis.bengal_partition.q10 difficulty=tricky subject=history chapter=20
Consider the statements:

1. The Partition of Bengal created two provinces: East Bengal (with Assam) and the rest of Bengal (joined administratively with Bihar and Orissa).
2. The anti-partition movement led to the spread of the Swadeshi and boycott programme across India.
3. The British reversed the partition in 1911 partly due to Indian nationalist pressure.

Which of the above are correct?

- A) 1 and 2 only
- B) 2 and 3 only
- C) 1 and 3 only
- D) 1, 2 and 3
@@

<details><summary>Answer & explanation</summary>

**Answer: D**

All three statements are correct. (1) The 1905 partition created two provinces: (a) Eastern Bengal and Assam, with a Muslim majority and Dhaka as capital, and (b) the rest of Bengal (essentially the western, Hindu-majority districts) clubbed together with Bihar and Orissa under a Lieutenant-Governor, with Calcutta as the capital of the western unit. (2) The anti-partition agitation, far from remaining a Bengal-only movement, catalysed the spread of the Swadeshi and boycott programme to Punjab, Bombay, Madras and other regions, making it the first mass-based all-India political movement in modern Indian history. (3) The 1911 reversal of the partition, announced at the Delhi Durbar, is widely attributed to a combination of factors including sustained Indian nationalist pressure (mass agitation, revolutionary terrorism, economic dislocation in eastern Bengal) and imperial strategic concerns about governing a sullen and unified Bengal from Calcutta.

**Trap:** A drops statement 3, but the nationalist-pressure factor in the 1911 reversal is a standard textbook point. B drops statement 1, but the administrative geography of the 1905 partition is well-established fact. C drops statement 2, but the all-India spread of Swadeshi from Bengal is a textbook hallmark of the period.

</details>
